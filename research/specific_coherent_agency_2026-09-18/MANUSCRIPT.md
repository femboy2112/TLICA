# Specific Coherent Agency
## Toward a TLICA-Normalized Comparison of Reality-Coupled, Self-Resonant Intelligence

**Research-tier application precursor — v0.1.0 — 2026-09-18**

### Abstract

Ordinary intelligence language compresses several separable capacities into one unstable judgment. A person can model a situation accurately while lacking an executable route; generate novel abstractions while misattributing evidence; preserve commitments while failing to act; act efficiently while pursuing a false model; or revise beliefs rapidly while never committing long enough to actualize anything. TLICA already contains distinctions that make this collapse visible.

This paper develops a candidate comparative object, **Specific Coherent Agency (SCA)**: a benchmark-relative measure of how much reality-coupled, self-resonant, correctable agency a person can generate relative to an operational estimate of the effective dimensional load of the integrated self being coordinated. It does not identify intelligence with agency and does not propose a measure of human worth. The intended use is narrower: compare persons, within declared task families and horizons, on their ability to construct faithful models, discriminate rivals, find and execute routes, revise under error, preserve relevant self-invariants, and retain future option value.

A second construct, **update fidelity**, formalizes a phenomenological claim from the originating conversation. The author often experiences herself as "right about something" even while expecting the current idea or frame to change. The proposed tracked object is not the frozen proposition but a relation among felt structure, current representation, intervention, and reality-coupled consequences. If revisions systematically reduce transport defect on held-out perturbations while preserving declared invariants and provenance, then replacing the current frame is not epistemic surrender; it is increased resolution of the relation that motivated the frame.

The paper separates the useful vector profile from any optional scalar projection, defines candidate normalization schemes, specifies benchmark and adversarial controls, and leaves the main comparative claim UNVERIFIED.

---

# 1. The motivating failure: "smart" changes meaning mid-proof

A recurrent self-evaluation can take the form

\[
\text{novel success}
\Rightarrow
\text{"genius"}
\Rightarrow
\text{basic failure or poor outcome}
\Rightarrow
\text{"idiot"}
\Rightarrow
\text{reference-class repair}.
\]

The apparent contradiction is often created by **metric substitution**.

The first judgment may be driven by generativity, abstraction, compression, transfer, or discovery. The second may be driven by execution, life-state, sequencing, accumulated resources, social navigation, or an elementary knowledge gap. The third may compare the self with a population using yet another implicit metric.

No contradiction follows unless all of these are measurements of one latent scalar with stable meaning.

TLICA makes that assumption especially unattractive because it already distinguishes objects that ordinary self-evaluation tends to fuse: contact, verification pathway state, source-map adequacy, identity/commitment integration, probe availability, live-option construction, action access, execution, ownership, and correction.

The immediate application-level lesson is:

\[
\boxed{\text{do not let "intelligence" become an undeclared sum of heterogeneous coordinates.}}
\]

This is structurally similar to the sensor-to-governor problem developed in Moreish: a locally useful signal is silently promoted into a global evaluator.

# 2. Intelligence is upstream of actualization, not identical to it

Suppose a person's trajectory is schematically decomposed as

\[
\text{representation}
\to
\text{option construction}
\to
\text{route availability}
\to
\text{selection}
\to
\text{execution}
\to
\text{world response}
\to
\text{correction}
\to
\text{accumulated state}.
\]

Strong performance at an upstream stage does not logically certify strong downstream performance.

The existing traversability work gives a finite disclosure of this point. If the only admissible action from state \(a\) returns \(a\), then an observer can know the state perfectly and still be unable to reach \(b\). Adding one action can make \(b\) reachable without changing the observer's informational capacity.

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\text{observation/model quality}\not\Rightarrow\text{control}
}
\]

without additional assumptions about the action set and dynamics.

The reverse separation matters too. Successful action does not certify model fidelity: imitation, luck, coercive structure, cached routine, or environmental scaffolding may produce the outcome.

A comparative intelligence-like measure should therefore be built from multiple explicitly typed components.

# 3. The capability vector

Let a benchmark episode for person \(m\) produce the profile

\[
\mathbf Q_m
=
(E,M,D,R,X,C,S,O).
\]

The letters are provisional and application-level.

- \(E\): **epistemic calibration** — confidence tracks observed correctness under declared scoring.
- \(M\): **model fidelity** — the explicit representation predicts relevant observations and consequences.
- \(D\): **discrimination** — live rivals are separated by probes rather than rhetoric or retrospective fit.
- \(R\): **route construction / reachability** — useful paths are represented and actually admissible under the task dynamics.
- \(X\): **execution** — selected admissible routes are implemented reliably.
- \(C\): **correction** — residual error produces useful revision rather than denial, random switching, or unbounded audit.
- \(S\): **self-resonance / invariant preservation** — declared commitments or ownership constraints that were meant to survive the transition do survive, subject to their own corrigibility.
- \(O\): **option preservation** — successful action does not gratuitously destroy future reachable possibilities.

This vector is the primary object. It prevents the scalar from hiding why two people achieved the same aggregate value.

No component is proposed as a TLICA foundation coordinate. The vector is an application-level measurement interface.

# 4. Coherent reachability

For person \(m\), current state \(x\), horizon \(H\), admissible action repertoire \(\mathcal U_m\), and declared constraints \(K\), use the existing reachability shape

\[
\mathrm{Reach}_{H,m}(x;\mathcal U_m,K)
=
\left\{
z:
\text{an admissible path from }x
\text{ reaches }z\text{ within }H
\right\}.
\]

A merely reachable state may be reached by accident, false belief, external preemption, or a route the person would not endorse. Define a stricter application-level set:

\[
\mathcal R^{\star}_{m,H}(x)
=
\left\{
z\in \mathrm{Reach}_{H,m}:
\begin{array}{l}
q_{\rm epistemic}(z)\ge q_0,\\
q_{\rm correction}(z)\ge c_0,\\
q_{\rm resonance}(z)\ge s_0,\\
C(x\to z)\preceq B
\end{array}
\right\}.
\]

Here \(C\) remains a **cost tuple**, not an undeclared scalar. The existing traversability dossier explicitly warns that latency, reported effort, sleep displacement, semantic loss, and other effects do not arrive with common units.

The set \(\mathcal R^{\star}\) is called the **coherently reachable set**.

The adjective coherent does not mean socially conventional, calm, internally simple, or conflict-free. It means that the path satisfies the declared fidelity, correction, ownership/invariant, and cost conditions of the benchmark.

# 5. Why "per unit self" is not nonsense

The originating phrase "intelligence per unit of coherent self" was intended as a normalization enabling cross-person comparison.

The naive version

\[
\frac{\text{intelligence}}{\rho}
\]

is not well typed. TLICA's \(\rho\) is an identity-correlation/integration quantity, not an extensive amount of self.

The underlying intuition can be preserved by introducing a new **operational denominator**:

\[
d_{\rm self}(m;B),
\]

the effective dimensional load of self-relevant structure that must be coordinated in benchmark family \(B\).

One candidate definition is:

> the smallest effective number of approximately independent self-relevant degrees of freedom required to predict, to a declared accuracy, the person's stable commitment rankings, vetoes, ownership judgments, and value-sensitive behavioral reversals across the benchmark contexts.

This is not "how much self the person has." It is a complexity estimate for the coordination problem.

If a covariance/kernel representation of the benchmarked self-profile has normalized spectral weights \(p_i\), a candidate effective-rank statistic is

\[
d_{\rm self}^{(H)}
=
\exp\!\left(-\sum_i p_i\log p_i\right).
\]

Another candidate is the participation ratio

\[
d_{\rm self}^{(2)}
=
\frac{1}{\sum_i p_i^2}.
\]

Neither is privileged at this stage. They answer related but nonidentical questions and require stable operational feature construction before interpretation.

The denominator must not reward impoverished self-representation or fragmentation. If reducing the represented self to one coordinate mechanically improves the ratio, the benchmark has failed. This is why predictive adequacy of the self-model is a precondition, not a free simplification knob.

# 6. Specific Coherent Agency

Let \(\mathcal V_B(\mathcal R_m^\star)\) denote a benchmark-declared measure of usable coherent reachability. A schematic form is

\[
\mathcal V_B
=
\sum_{z\in \mathcal R_m^\star}
w_B(z)
P_{\rm reach}(z)
Q_{\rm epistemic}(z)
Q_{\rm correction}(z)
Q_{\rm resonance}(z)
Q_{\rm option}(z).
\]

The weights \(w_B\) belong to the benchmark and must be declared before outcomes are inspected.

Then a candidate **Specific Coherent Agency** is

\[
\boxed{
\eta_m(B,H)
=
\frac{
\log\!\left(1+\mathcal V_B(\mathcal R^{\star}_{m,H})\right)
}{
d_{\rm self}(m;B)
}
}
\]

with the logarithm included only to reduce domination by benchmarks whose raw route counts expand combinatorially.

This scalar is **not canonical**. It is a declared projection of the primary vector profile. Its value is only interpretable relative to:

- the benchmark family;
- horizon;
- route-value rule;
- thresholds;
- self-dimension estimator;
- calibration population;
- missing-data policy.

A responsible report should show \(\mathbf Q_m\), \(d_{\rm self}\), and the scalar together.

# 7. The deeper phenomenological claim: "I am right about something"

The author's second clarification changes the epistemic object.

The claim is not:

\[
M_t = \text{truth},
\]

where \(M_t\) is the current explicit model.

The reported experience is closer to:

> There is a real relation among the feeling, the current idea/frame, and their coupling to reality. The endpoints may change. If changing either endpoint increases fidelity and the intervention produces the predicted/resonant transformation, the central tracked object has not been abandoned.

Introduce:

- \(S_t\): current self-configuration relevant to the episode;
- \(F_t\): felt/phenomenal structure being tracked;
- \(M_t\): explicit frame/model;
- \(W_t\): contacted world state, only partially observed;
- \(a_t\): chosen intervention;
- \(Y_t\): declared observable consequences.

The person forms a predicted transformation

\[
\widehat{\Delta Y}_{t+1}
=
P(M_t,F_t,S_t,a_t),
\]

acts, and observes

\[
\Delta Y_{t+1}.
\]

Define a **transport defect**

\[
\epsilon_t
=
d_Y\!\left(
\widehat{\Delta Y}_{t+1},
\Delta Y_{t+1}
\right).
\]

The term transport is used cautiously: this is an application-level common-basis comparison only when the observable basis and orientation are explicitly declared. Without that declaration it is merely prediction error, not a phasor/parallel-transport claim.

The key proposal is that a person can be wrong about \(M_t\) while tracking a useful relation if revisions generated from residuals reduce future defect on held-out perturbations.

# 8. Update fidelity

Let \(U_m\) be the person's update operator in the benchmark:

\[
(M_t,F_t,S_t,\mathcal E_t)
\xrightarrow{U_m}
(M_{t+1},F_{t+1},S_{t+1}),
\]

where \(\mathcal E_t\) is the evidence/residual record.

A good update operator should do more than lower in-sample error. It should:

1. preserve provenance and uncertainty;
2. reduce error on fresh perturbations;
3. improve rival discrimination where possible;
4. avoid unnecessary destruction of live options;
5. preserve the self-invariants that were explicitly intended to remain;
6. permit those invariants themselves to be revised through a separately justified process.

A benchmark-level update score could be based on held-out improvement:

\[
UQ_m
=
\frac{
\mathbb E[\epsilon_{\rm before}-\epsilon_{\rm after}]
}{
\text{declared update cost}
},
\]

reported with the raw numerator and cost tuple rather than only the ratio.

A **specific update-fidelity** statistic can then be explored:

\[
\eta^{\rm update}_m
=
\frac{UQ_m}{d_{\rm self}(m;B)}.
\]

This is closer to the author's original intuitive target than an IQ-like problem-set score:

\[
\boxed{
\text{How rapidly can this coherent self learn the shape of an unfamiliar reality
and acquire truthful, corrigible control through it?}
}
\]

# 9. Why meta-correctability can coexist with strong felt conviction

Ordinary descriptions make strong conviction and corrigibility look opposed. They need not be.

If identity/commitment is attached primarily to a proposition \(M_t\), then revision threatens the protected object:

\[
M_t\to M_{t+1}
\quad\text{looks like}\quad
\text{loss}.
\]

If the protected object is instead something like

\[
\mathcal J_t
=
\text{the best currently accessible relation among }F_t,M_t,W_t
\]

plus a commitment to improve its fidelity, then replacement of \(M_t\) can preserve \(\mathcal J\)'s governing aim.

This produces a distinctive pattern:

\[
\text{high local conviction}
+
\text{high willingness to rewrite the sentence}
\]

without contradiction.

The benchmarkable question is not whether the person reports such an orientation, but whether model replacement under adverse evidence actually lowers held-out residuals more reliably than rival update policies.

This also prevents a self-sealing interpretation. "I was right at a deeper level" is not accepted merely because the person can narrate continuity after failure. The claimed deeper relation earns status only when it makes prospective discriminating predictions that survive mutation and holdout.

# 10. Self-resonant actualization

Prediction alone is insufficient. A person may correctly predict that an environment will change her in a way she does not endorse.

Let \(\mathcal I_t\) denote the declared self-invariants intended to survive an intervention. These can include commitments, ownership conditions, protected relationships, epistemic constraints, or refusal boundaries.

Define, schematically,

\[
R_t
=
\operatorname{Compat}(S_{t+1},\mathcal I_t),
\]

and separately

\[
A_t
=
\operatorname{Align}(
\Delta_{\rm endorsed},
\Delta_{\rm realized}
).
\]

The first concerns preservation of declared invariants; the second concerns whether the realized change resembles the change the person meant to cause.

The distinction matters:

- a predicted but unendorsed transformation can have high prediction fidelity and low self-resonance;
- an endorsed transformation can be self-resonant but reality-wrong;
- a lucky success can have high outcome value but low attributable control;
- a painful correction can lower immediate affective resonance while increasing long-horizon epistemic fidelity.

No single term substitutes for the others.

# 11. Comparative use

A comparison between persons \(m\) and \(n\) is licensed only under a shared benchmark family \(B\), comparable opportunity/action conditions, and common scoring rules.

A report should look like:

\[
(\mathbf Q_m,d_{\rm self,m},\eta_m)
\quad\text{versus}\quad
(\mathbf Q_n,d_{\rm self,n},\eta_n),
\]

not simply

\[
\eta_m>\eta_n\Rightarrow m\text{ is globally smarter}.
\]

The scalar can support statements such as:

> Under benchmark family \(B\), horizon \(H\), and normalization \(d_{\rm self}^{(H)}\), participant \(m\) produced more held-out coherent reachability per effective self-dimension than participant \(n\).

It cannot support:

> participant \(m\) is a better person, more conscious, or universally more intelligent.

The benchmark must also report which dimensions drove the difference.

---

# 12. Relation to ordinary psychometrics

This project should not pretend existing psychometrics do nothing useful. Standardized cognitive tests estimate narrower latent constructs under controlled conditions and have the advantage of large calibration literatures.

SCA asks a different question.

It attempts to include:

- novel-model construction;
- probe selection;
- causal interaction;
- route discovery;
- actual execution;
- correction under residuals;
- ownership/invariant preservation;
- option preservation;
- learning slope across an unfamiliar environment.

That breadth is also a liability. A broader construct is easier to contaminate with environment, motivation, resources, culture, task familiarity, language, disability, and benchmark design.

Therefore a successful SCA program must demonstrate **incremental validity**, not merely conceptual richness.

# 13. Main failure modes

## 13.1 Hidden scalarization

If the benchmark silently chooses weights after seeing results, the scalar is uninterpretable.

## 13.2 Rewarding simple selves

A denominator that can be lowered by representing fewer commitments will perversely reward impoverishment. Self-dimension needs an adequacy constraint.

## 13.3 Environment masquerading as person

Reachability is jointly determined by person and action environment. Comparative tasks must equalize or explicitly model resource access.

## 13.4 Verbal sophistication masquerading as model quality

Participants should be scored on prospective consequences and interventions, not explanatory eloquence alone.

## 13.5 Retrospective self-sealing

"I was right about the relation" must fail when the proposed relation does not improve held-out prediction or discrimination.

## 13.6 Correction as random movement

High revision frequency is not high corrigibility. Updates should be directional with respect to residual reduction and preserved evidence.

## 13.7 Productivity capture

The benchmark must allow refusing, waiting, preserving options, or concluding that no transition is warranted. Output maximization is not the target.

# 14. Candidate benchmark ideal

The cleanest benchmark class is not a trivia battery. It is a family of **unfamiliar interactive systems**.

Participants repeatedly cycle through:

\[
\text{observe}
\to
\text{model}
\to
\text{predict}
\to
\text{choose}
\to
\text{act}
\to
\text{observe consequence}
\to
\text{revise}.
\]

Systems should contain:

- multiple live but distinguishable hypotheses;
- interventions with different information values;
- at least one tempting but misleading correlation;
- at least one useful indirect route;
- explicit costs and irreversible actions;
- opportunities where the correct move is to preserve option value;
- held-out regime changes;
- self-relevant preference constraints supplied or elicited before action.

The benchmark then measures not only terminal score but learning curves:

\[
\frac{d\epsilon}{dn},
\qquad
\frac{dP_{\rm control}}{dn},
\qquad
\frac{d\mathcal V(\mathcal R^\star)}{dn}.
\]

The strongest result would be reliable out-of-sample prediction of real-world adaptation after controlling for standard cognitive measures. That result does not currently exist.

# 15. What this says about the originating self-evaluation

The framework dissolves rather than answers the question "genius or idiot?"

A person can be:

- high in generative abstraction;
- uneven in stored knowledge;
- high in update fidelity;
- low in some execution domains;
- high in self-model complexity;
- locally constrained in reachability;
- strong at adversarial correction;
- poor at cumulative sequencing.

Those can all be true simultaneously.

The relevant empirical task is not to pick the flattering or insulting label. It is to estimate the profile and test whether the proposed normalization predicts anything beyond ordinary alternatives.

---

# 16. Epistemic status

**Disclosed:** in stipulated finite systems, perfect observation can coexist with zero target reachability when the action graph lacks a route.

**Observed:** the originating author explicitly seeks a quantitative cross-person normalization and reports a form of conviction attached to the relation among felt state, frame, and reality rather than permanently to the current proposition.

**Conjectured:** effective self-dimension is a useful denominator for comparing complex reality-coupled agency.

**Conjectured:** update fidelity captures part of the author's reported meta-correctability more faithfully than proposition-level confidence.

**UNVERIFIED:** any proposed scalar has psychometric reliability, predictive validity, fairness, or cross-person interpretability.

**Dark:** whether there is a natural invariant normalization across people independent of benchmark family. The lamp is a multi-benchmark, multi-implementation study with standard psychometric comparators and held-out ecological outcomes.

**Boundary:** this framework is not a clinical instrument, moral ranking, consciousness meter, or proof of extraordinary intelligence.
