# Shared Reality, Different Lives

## Cultural interpretation, conflict localization, and respectful coexistence

**Research draft v0.1.0.** This is an application-level proposal, not a new foundation axiom or an empirically validated intervention. References S1–S7 are documented with access limits in [SOURCES.md](SOURCES.md).

## Abstract

Two people may be embedded in the same historical and institutional world while encountering it through different local relationships, learned expectations, practical repertoires, affective associations, and material constraints. The same expression can therefore evoke different situations, and different expressions can sometimes encode comparable relations. Music supplies one revealing observation channel, but the engineering goal is broader: recover enough of each participant's intended meaning and lived constraints to distinguish misunderstanding from factual disagreement, incompatible commitments, scarce resources, damaged trust, procedural exclusion, and genuinely conflicting interests. The proposed system is a task-indexed cultural atlas coupled to participant-correctable models, provenance-aware reality checks, and consent-gated option search. Its target is not assimilation or compulsory consensus but arrangements participants can endorse while retaining legitimate differences. This bootstrap proves several finite-model distinctions, implements a small diagnostic instrument, and specifies empirical tests. It reports no real mediation outcome.

## 1. The object is an interaction, not a type of American

Regional maps can organize evidence about cultural variation. Louf and colleagues' 2023 lexical analysis supplies one aggregate method, with a selected social-media population rather than a representative account of every American [S1]. Such a map does not identify the interpretation, preferences, or history of a particular resident. The present proposal therefore starts with a concrete episode: who acted, what each person noticed, what each thinks happened, what consequences matter, what resources and authorities are involved, and what decision must actually be made.

An eventual atlas would index places, cohorts, practices, institutions, relationships, and interpretive conventions. Its entries would be overlapping, time-stamped, provenance-bearing claims with explicit populations and counterexamples. They would suggest questions, not fill in a person's mind from a demographic label. A person may participate in several contexts, reject local conventions, acquire new repertoires, or interpret the same sign differently at different times.

“Fully connecting” is best treated as an expanding, auditable network of task-specific intelligibility and coordination pathways. It does not mean total psychological transparency, a single national worldview, or the disappearance of genuine disagreements. Each edge must specify what it transports, for whom, under which conditions, with what remaining loss.

## 2. Why music was a productive opening

Fox's ethnography connects country music and ordinary speech to working-class life in one Texas community [S2]. McLeod examines how authenticity is organized through discourse in hip-hop communities in the 1990s [S3]. Armstrong's 1993 comparison identifies a bounded set of rap/country resemblances around trouble, toughness, and violence; it is not evidence that the genres or their audiences are generally equivalent [S4].

These sources motivate an application hypothesis: a cultural expression can feel real when its relational, affective, and socially indexical organization is legible through structures a listener has acquired. They do not establish that this mechanism explains commercial success, exhausts musical enjoyment, or requires the listener to have lived the described biography. Craft, imagination, aspiration, novelty, affiliation, and sound itself remain live alternatives.

The broader idea applies to a joke, a workplace instruction, a family ritual, a silence, an accent, a complaint, or the word “respect.” Eckert's indexical-field account is especially pertinent: social meanings of linguistic variation are not fixed demographic labels but contextually activated possibilities [S5]. Swidler's toolkit account likewise treats culture as providing practical repertoires rather than simply a uniform list of ultimate values [S6]. Neither framework is identical to TLICA, and neither is evidence for TLICA's full apparatus.

## 3. Four connections that must not be confused

**Causal connection:** two lives are affected by related institutions, markets, migrations, histories, or direct interactions. Establishing a particular connection requires an actual causal account.

**Structural correspondence:** selected relations in two episodes match under a declared mapping. This depends on what the analyst preserves and omits; a sufficiently coarse encoding can manufacture similarity.

**Interpretive correspondence:** a participant or listener reconstructs a relevant situation adequately. Correct nouns alone are insufficient; counterfactual expectations and salient consequences may still differ.

**Experiential similarity:** participants report related affective or phenomenal organization. Neither a graph match nor common history establishes identical experience.

A proposed transport must preserve these distinctions. Two people can have similar material constraints but conflicting goals. Two people can use very different language yet already agree on a practical arrangement. Conversely, they can agree on a slogan while anticipating incompatible actions.

## 4. A layered diagnosis of “bumping heads”

Before interpreting a dispute as cultural, eliminate ordinary mismatches: wrong event, missing information, stale plans, uncommunicated changes, mistaken identity, contradictory instructions, and unrecognized practical limitations. The first question is what actually needs deciding now.

The reconstruction then keeps several potential obstructions separate. A **semantic** obstruction concerns what a sign, action, or situation means. An **epistemic** obstruction concerns what happened or what a proposed action will cause. A **commitment** obstruction concerns what each person refuses to sacrifice. A **resource or opportunity** obstruction concerns the available options, costs, time, space, or capabilities. A **relational** obstruction concerns trust, recognition, prior breaches, or perceived humiliation. A **procedural and institutional** obstruction concerns voice, authority, enforcement, and unequal ability to refuse. These can coexist; their separation is a diagnostic design, not an established exhaustive taxonomy.

Do not infer a person's developmental cause from a current reaction alone. Different histories can produce the same observable response. Ask for relevant history voluntarily, retain alternatives, and distinguish a participant's account from independent historical verification. A flattering explanation is not necessarily an accurate one.

## 5. Get perspectives; do not merely invent them

The system should construct provisional, editable models and ask each person to correct them. Eyal, Steffel, and Epley's 2018 experiments are a useful caution: instructions to take another's perspective did not consistently improve accuracy, while their final conversation-based experiment improved accuracy through obtaining new information [S7]. That result supports testing information acquisition; it does not establish the effectiveness of this proposed mediation workflow.

A participant-confirmed reconstruction records the situation they believe they face, their desired outcomes, unacceptable costs, relevant uncertainties, and how they predict representative options would affect them. Confirmation means “this describes my position sufficiently for this task,” not “all my factual claims have been verified.” Neither participant's approval proves a causal prediction. Agreement with the model can also be shallow; held-out paraphrase and counterfactual probes should test the reconstruction rather than rely on one agreeable response.

Understanding, approval, affection, trust, consent, and practical cooperation are separate outputs. The system must allow a person to understand the other accurately and still decline an arrangement.

## 6. From improved understanding to a joint option space

The direct repository ancestor is *Shared Reality, Divergent Maps*, especially its distinction among shared vocabulary, representation, agreement, and truth, and its constraint-closed compromise/opposition-checksum proposal. The extension here is developmental cultural context plus explicit computational and empirical interfaces.

For a concrete task, construct a catalog of actions or procedures. Preserve each party's important, adequately supported consequential variables rather than optimizing within one person's frame and translating the sales pitch afterward. Candidate generation can explore timing, sequencing, separate spaces, resource substitution, reversible experiments, shared infrastructure, or a different procedure. These are search operators, not universally appropriate remedies.

Evaluate candidates against independently tracked reality conditions, declared standing and safety requirements, relevant third-party constraints, each participant's minimum acceptability conditions, and explicit permission for a specified next step. Preferences order the surviving candidates; they do not purchase exemptions from hard constraints. A non-dominated option is not thereby fair, endorsed, truthful, or safe. Those conditions must have their own checks.

The prototype returns a set of supported, non-dominated candidates under stipulated ordinal ranks. It does not sum people's happiness or select the objectively best compromise. In real work, preferences may be incomplete, unstable, or incommensurable; collecting complete numeric ranks is a calibration convenience, not a theory of persons.

## 7. Reality is not whatever the parties can agree to

Maintain separate records for empirical claims, value commitments, permissions, and procedural rules. Empirical statements require appropriately scoped evidence. A normative floor requires its stated basis and a legitimate process for contestation; a popularity vote cannot turn it into a physical fact. A signature or claim of consent must be checked for scope, voluntariness, duration, and ability to withdraw.

The proposed system must not create apparent harmony by suppressing adverse measurements, redefining someone else's commitments, hiding material burdens, or exporting the disagreement to an absent third party. Nor should cultural interpretation excuse threats or coercion. High-stakes or unsafe disputes require suitable human support and domain competence, not automatic negotiation. This research program is not a legal, clinical, or emergency service.

When evidence is missing, the appropriate result may be a discriminating probe. When no candidate has been constructed, it is a search gap. When a finite catalog is fully evaluated and every option violates a required condition, the incompatibility is disclosed only within that catalog. A new resource, a new option, or a freely revised commitment can change the result. Some conflicts remain real after every misunderstanding is repaired.

## 8. How coexistence can succeed without convergence

A successful outcome can leave the participants' identities, narratives, tastes, and many factual disagreements different. They may share a reliable schedule or boundary rather than a worldview. The desired evidence is each participant's freely reported acceptability, task-relevant predictive accuracy, independently checked consequences, implementation fidelity, and persistence without coercion or hidden externalities.

Satisfaction should be assessed separately for every affected participant over time; a good average can hide a persistently worse-off person. The project aims to increase the available mutually acceptable space while preserving refusal and correction. Consent is not identical to happiness, and short-term happiness is not durable success.

## 9. From two people to a connected society

A network of successful dyadic arrangements does not automatically compose. Three participants can have pairwise overlapping acceptable sets with an empty three-way intersection. Shared resources, incompatible promises, or different contracts across contexts can create additional obstructions.

The national-scale research object must therefore include multi-party and institutional compatibility, not just pairwise semantic translation. It must track where an agreement was valid, what obligations it created, who bears its consequences, and when it expires. This gives a concrete motivation for later local-to-global mathematics. It does not establish that culture itself is a sheaf or that any mathematical obstruction is a social destiny.

## 10. Present claim boundary

The strongest delivered result is a disciplined problem formulation and an executable finite instrument with counterexamples and controls. The ordinary conceptual neighbors are established literature; this particular integration's predictive advantage and practical benefit are **UNVERIFIED**. No real people, cultural corpus, audio, or mediation outcomes were studied in this bootstrap.

The next decisive step is not a more elaborate taxonomy. It is testing whether explicit, participant-corrected relational reconstruction improves held-out understanding and viable option discovery without sacrificing reality checks, consent, or standing. A result in which a simpler structured interview performs equally well would limit the value claimed for the atlas and TLICA-specific machinery.
