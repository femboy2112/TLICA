# Source ledger and literature boundary

Checked 2026-09-22. This is a targeted primary-source pass, not an exhaustive review or novelty search. Publisher summaries and original article abstracts identify arguments; they are not substitutes for reading every method and result. The prior chat is hypothesis provenance, not an external scholarly source.

## Sources used in the draft

**S1 — Louf, Thomas; Goncalves, Bruno; Ramasco, Jose J.; Sanchez, David; Grieve, Jack (2023).** “American cultural regions mapped through the lexical analysis of social media.” *Humanities and Social Sciences Communications* 10, 133. DOI `10.1057/s41599-023-01611-3`.
https://www.nature.com/articles/s41599-023-01611-3
Access: original article's indexed abstract/introduction inspected; a subsequent direct open returned an error. Supports a lexical/social-media approach to aggregate cultural regions and disagreement among earlier regional schemes. It does not validate a complete cultural ontology or person-level cultural diagnosis. No figure or numerical reanalysis was performed here.

**S2 — Fox, Aaron A. (2004).** *Real Country: Music and Language in Working-Class Culture*. Duke University Press. DOI `10.1215/9780822385998`.
https://www.dukeupress.edu/Real-Country
Access: publisher description and table of contents. Supports the book's stated ethnographic focus on speech, music, feeling, place, and working-class life in Lockhart, Texas. Full ethnography not read in this bootstrap; do not generalize its community to all rural Americans.

**S3 — McLeod, Kembrew (1999).** “Authenticity within hip-hop and other cultures threatened with assimilation.” *Journal of Communication* 49(4), 134–150. DOI `10.1111/j.1460-2466.1999.tb02821.x`.
https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1460-2466.1999.tb02821.x
Access: original article abstract. Supports analysis of discursively organized authenticity in mid/late-1990s hip-hop communities. The online page's later posting date is not the issue year. No audience causality or contemporary universality inferred.

**S4 — Armstrong, Edward G. (1993).** “The Rhetoric of Violence in Rap and Country Music.” *Sociological Inquiry* 63(1), 64–78. DOI `10.1111/j.1475-682X.1993.tb00202.x`.
https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1475-682X.1993.tb00202.x
Access: original article abstract and bibliographic metadata. Supports a bounded semiotic comparison focused on trouble, toughness, and violence. Does not establish equivalence of populations, histories, genres, or motivations. It is not the organizing model for this broader project.

**S5 — Eckert, Penelope (2008).** “Variation and the indexical field.” *Journal of Sociolinguistics* 12(4), 453–476. DOI `10.1111/j.1467-9841.2008.00374.x`.
https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1467-9841.2008.00374.x
Access: original article abstract, cross-checked against an indexed author-hosted copy. Supports context-sensitive potential social meanings rather than fixed demographic semantics. Full methodological interpretation remains to be read.

**S6 — Swidler, Ann (1986).** “Culture in Action: Symbols and Strategies.” *American Sociological Review* 51(2), 273–286. Stable record `2095521`.
https://www.jstor.org/stable/2095521
https://web.mit.edu/curhan/www/docs/Articles/15341_Readings/Culture_and_Identity/Swidler-1986.pdf
Access: PDF text opened; printed page 273 (PDF page index 1) visually inspected. Supports the toolkit/strategies-of-action framing and settled/unsettled distinction. Only the opening argument is relied on here. Cultural repertoires are not identified with TLICA's verification-tool closure.

**S7 — Eyal, Tal; Steffel, Mary; Epley, Nicholas (2018).** “Perspective mistaking: Accurately understanding the mind of another requires getting perspective, not taking perspective.” *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology* 114(4), 547–571. DOI `10.1037/pspa0000115`; PMID `29620401`.
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29620401/
Access: original abstract via PubMed search; direct open supplied no additional text. The abstract reports 25 experiments and a final conversation-based comparison. Supports caution about imagined perspective-taking and the value of acquiring information in the studied tasks. Not evidence that our intervention resolves cultural conflict, and not a universal theorem that imagination is useless.

## Provenance families and claim scope

S1: aggregate spatial/lexical inference. S2: situated ethnography. S3/S4: cultural-discourse and textual comparison. S5/S6: conceptual accounts of language/cultural action. S7: experimental interpersonal-accuracy work. This is methodological diversity, but not seven independent tests of the same hypothesis. Citations from this list may share intellectual provenance; no numeric independence or truth score is assigned.

Repository documents belong to one theory family and supply definitions, not external confirmation. The synthetic fixtures belong to one authoring family even when they contain several source ids. Leave-one-family-out tests therefore withdraw the stipulated support rather than simulate independent empirical replication.

**Novelty boundary:** the reviewed work motivates neighboring questions. No claim is made that cultural translation, mutual gains, partial structural comparison, or any elementary mathematics here is new. The proposed integration must earn usefulness against simpler baselines.
