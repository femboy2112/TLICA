# When the Cage Becomes Common Sense

## Authority-Compatible Frame Dynamics, Constraint Reification, and Positive Self-Resonance

**Working paper seed — research tier — 2026-09-28**

### Abstract

This paper develops a candidate account of a familiar but poorly specified social phenomenon: people subject to a hierarchy sometimes do more than comply with it. They may re-describe its contingent constraints as reality itself, attribute the consequences of coercion to those who could foresee them, enforce the hierarchy against peers, and experience successful resisters as unfair "cheaters." The paper calls the broader family **authority-compatible frame dynamics** rather than assuming a unitary personality type.

The first contribution is analytic. Four propositions that are often collapsed are separated: a regime can be predictable without being invariant; retaliation can be foreseeable without being authored by its target; an action can be locally unaffordable without being globally causally irrelevant; and a rule can be enforceable without being legitimate. From these distinctions the paper defines candidate mechanisms of **constraint reification**, **foreseeability laundering**, **captured fairness**, **retrospective necessity**, and **resistance-visibility error**.

The second contribution is dynamical. Building only at application level on existing TLICA machinery, the paper hypothesizes that authority-compatible frames may become self-sustaining when they acquire **positive self-resonance**: self-perceptions, interpretations, attentional selections, priorities, and actions consistent with the frame elicit positive substrate-mediated valence. Such valence need not appear as conscious pleasure; it may present as relief, certainty, familiarity, righteousness, belonging, safety, pride, or simple "this is how things are supposed to be." If valence affects salience, retention, action, and social selection, the frame can partly manufacture the evidence surface that later seems to justify it.

The proposal is explicitly not a diagnosis, a moral score, or a claim that all compliance is internalization. Compartmentalized survival under coercion is a primary rival and limiting case. The empirical mechanisms remain CONJECTURED / UNVERIFIED.

## 1. The target phenomenon

The colloquial word **bootlicking** bundles together behaviors that should not be treated as one mechanism.

A worker who follows a dangerous or absurd rule because rent is due may outwardly resemble a worker who sincerely believes that rule-breaking demonstrates moral defect. A person may defer to expertise because they lack domain knowledge, obey an emergency command because coordination matters, comply with a threat because the alternative is catastrophic, or identify deeply with the institution issuing the command. Behavior alone underdetermines the frame.

The narrower phenomenon of interest begins when practical knowledge of power changes epistemic or normative structure.

A subject learns:

\[
P(H\mid a,S_t)\approx 1,
\]

where \(a\) is some act of noncompliance, \(H\) an adverse consequence, and \(S_t\) the current state of the institution.

That knowledge may be excellent. The mistake begins when it is silently upgraded into one or more stronger claims:

\[
P(H\mid a,S_t)\approx1
\not\Rightarrow
\forall S\;P(H\mid a,S)\approx1,
\]

\[
\text{foresee}(H)\not\Rightarrow\text{author}(H),
\]

\[
\text{enforced}(r)\not\Rightarrow\text{legitimate}(r),
\]

and

\[
a\notin\mathcal A^{\mathrm{feas}}_i
\not\Rightarrow
\Delta W(a)=0.
\]

These non-equivalences are the analytic spine of the paper.

The proposal is not that prudence is contemptible. It is that **prudence can be re-described as ontology**.

"I cannot afford to fight this" becomes "fighting this cannot work."

"They will fire you if you do that" becomes "you caused your firing."

"This is how management behaves" becomes "this is how workplaces are."

"Everyone had to pay this cost" becomes "the cost is fair."

The same predictive competence that helps someone survive a hierarchy can therefore become raw material for reifying it.

## 2. Constraint reification: from model to supposed invariant

A model can be both accurate and parochial.

If a person repeatedly observes that violating a rule leads to punishment, they may correctly infer a stable local conditional. What does not follow is that the relation is structural across all reachable institutional states.

The distinction is between:

\[
\text{model of the current regime}
\]

and

\[
\text{law of the system}.
\]

The reification error occurs when the first is treated as evidence for the second.

This matters because institutions are not passive environments. Their response-functions depend on policies, incentives, expectations, coordination, records, norms, staffing, law, and the actions of other agents. A regime can be stable precisely because people treat its current response-function as fixed.

That generates a candidate reflexive loop:

\[
\text{expect persistence}
\rightarrow
\text{adapt to persistence}
\rightarrow
\text{reduce perturbation}
\rightarrow
\text{institution persists}
\rightarrow
\text{expect persistence}.
\]

Call the resulting configuration a **self-confirming domination equilibrium** when the stable expectation concerns a coercive or asymmetrically imposed relation.

This name is deliberately narrower than "self-fulfilling prophecy." The claim is not that belief creates arbitrary reality from nothing. The institution already has material powers and constraints. The conjecture is that **agents' adaptation can become one of the causal inputs preserving the state they correctly predict**.

The belief may therefore be locally accurate and dynamically constitutive at the same time.

## 3. Foreseeability laundering

Consider a coercive policy:

> If you perform action \(a\), actor \(J\) will impose consequence \(H\).

The target knows this policy. The target performs \(a\). \(J\) imposes \(H\).

A common rhetorical transformation is:

> You knew what would happen.

followed by:

> Therefore this is on you.

The first proposition can be true while the second remains unsupported.

Foreseeability changes the target's decision problem. It does not erase the other actor's policy, discretion, or causal contribution. In particular, if \(J\) could have refrained from imposing \(H\), the target's knowledge that \(J\) would probably act does not make \(J\)'s act disappear from the causal graph.

The paper calls the shift from **predictive access** to **transferred authorship** **foreseeability laundering**.

The term is not intended to settle a complete theory of moral responsibility. Existing TLICA agency work correctly separates structural attribution-inputs from normative sufficiency. The narrower point here is that a conditional prediction alone cannot carry all the normative weight often assigned to it.

This distinction becomes especially important under threat.

A mugger can make the victim's decision environment highly predictable. Predictability does not convert the mugger's threatened act into a natural force or the victim's authorship.

The same form can occur in less extreme institutions without making every workplace rule equivalent to a mugging. The structure of the inference is the object, not an equivalence of severity.

## 4. Slack, the edge, and local impossibility

The statement

> Nothing I do will change anything

often mixes two questions:

1. Is there an action capable of perturbing the system?
2. Is there an action whose expected cost I can presently absorb?

These are not the same.

Let \(C_i(a;S_t)\) be the cost to agent \(i\) of action \(a\) in state \(S_t\), and let \(L_i(t)\) denote the agent's available loss-bearing slack: not merely money, but whatever margin remains before an unacceptable boundary is crossed.

Define the feasible action set

\[
\mathcal A^{\mathrm{feas}}_i(t)
=
\{a:C_i(a;S_t)\le L_i(t)\}.
\]

As \(L_i(t)\to0\), the feasible set may collapse.

That collapse can be morally and strategically decisive. A person supporting children, dependent on medication, facing deportation, imprisonment, violence, or homelessness may have extremely little usable resistance capacity. The model therefore rejects the cheap moralism that treats every act of compliance as cowardice.

But a small feasible set is a fact about **the agent's current constraint surface**. It is not a proof that all resistance pathways are causally null.

This yields another candidate reification:

\[
\text{my survivable action set is narrow}
\rightarrow
\text{there exists no effective action}.
\]

The paper treats that implication as a hypothesis to detect, not as a universal feature of constrained agents.

## 5. Resistance visibility error

Resistance is frequently evaluated by one macroscopic variable:

> Did the institution visibly lose?

If not, the action is called useless.

For a dynamic system this criterion is too coarse.

Represent an intervention's effect as a vector

\[
\Delta(a)
=
(
\Delta_{\mathrm{macro}},
\Delta_{\mathrm{cost}},
\Delta_{\mathrm{information}},
\Delta_{\mathrm{coordination}},
\Delta_{\mathrm{reachability}}
).
\]

A resistance attempt can leave the immediate macrostate unchanged while changing enforcement cost, revealing a hidden rule, creating a record, exposing allies, changing coordination structure, altering future options, or teaching the institution that a particular boundary is costly to enforce.

None of those changes guarantees eventual success. Some resistance really is ineffective, mistargeted, or counterproductive.

The error is narrower:

\[
\Delta_{\mathrm{macro}}=0
\not\Rightarrow
\Delta(a)=0.
\]

Call the collapse of the full vector into the first component **resistance-visibility error**.

This provides a direct empirical route: define in advance which state variables count, then test whether observers systematically call an intervention "pointless" despite measurable changes in non-macro components.

## 6. Captured fairness and the resister as cheater

Why can a successful resister provoke anger from people who themselves paid the cost imposed by the system?

One candidate mechanism is **captured fairness**.

Suppose a rule \(R\) imposes cost \(c\) on members of a group. An agent evaluates fairness only after conditioning on \(R\) as fixed:

\[
F_{\mathrm{within}}
=
F(\text{distribution among players}\mid R).
\]

Inside that frame, an individual who escapes \(c\) appears to have received an unfair exemption.

But the question under dispute may be:

\[
F_{\mathrm{meta}}
=
F(\text{players},R),
\]

which includes whether \(R\) itself should impose \(c\).

The fairness faculty need not disappear. Its **reference frame can be captured**.

This helps explain a familiar inversion:

> I had to do it, so why shouldn't you?

The statement treats prior payment as a claim on future payers.

If the rule is arbitrary, the opposite inference is also available:

> I paid an unnecessary cost; therefore others should not have to.

What selects between those interpretations is an empirical question.

### 6.1 Information deletion

Captured fairness becomes more likely if the live representation no longer distinguishes:

\[
\text{principled resistance}
\]

from

\[
\text{self-serving defection}.
\]

If both are compressed into

\[
\text{noncompliance},
\]

the resister can only appear as someone seeking benefits without paying the required price.

This is the **resister-as-cheater** frame.

The information may have been actively rejected, lost through repeated environmental conditioning, or never represented at sufficient resolution. Those possibilities must remain separate.

## 7. Retrospective necessity and sunk suffering

Prior suffering can become evidence in a person's self-story.

A stable autobiographical interpretation may be:

\[
\text{I paid }c
\quad\text{because}\quad
c\text{ was necessary}.
\]

A successful resister introduces a counterexample:

\[
\exists a:\text{noncompliance}(a)\land\text{survival or success}(a).
\]

That counterexample does not prove the original person's sacrifice was avoidable at the time. Their slack, information, dependents, or opportunities may have been different.

But it can make a previously hidden counterfactual salient:

> perhaps the cost was contingent rather than necessary.

That threatens more than a proposition. It can threaten an autobiographical justification.

The dossier therefore distinguishes **sunk cost** from a stronger candidate mechanism: **sunk suffering as an epistemic commitment**.

The conjectured transition is:

\[
\text{I suffered}
\rightarrow
\text{my suffering must have been necessary}
\rightarrow
\text{others who avoid it are cheating}
\rightarrow
\text{punishing them protects the necessity story}.
\]

Call the middle operation **retrospective necessity**.

This is not asserted as the explanation for all hazing, gatekeeping, seniority norms, or resentment. It is a falsifiable rival among several.

## 8. From survival emulator to authority attractor

The phrase "bootlicker" is too coarse unless the internal dynamics are separated.

### 8.1 Compartmentalized survival emulation

An agent can carry a local policy:

> In this context, model what authority expects and minimize friction.

The agent may simultaneously maintain:

> This rule is contingent, stupid, or unjust.

The authority model is being **run**, not necessarily **believed**.

The clean discriminator is what happens when the threat or context is removed. If endorsement and peer-enforcement collapse with the coercive condition, survival emulation remains a strong explanation.

### 8.2 Internalized authority compatibility

Internalization begins when the local emulator generalizes:

- the response pattern persists after immediate threat removal;
- enforcement is redescribed as legitimacy;
- noncompliance becomes character evidence;
- the agent exports the same logic to peers or new contexts.

This is no longer merely "I know how the boss thinks." The boss's point of view has become part of the agent's normative world-model.

### 8.3 Positive self-resonant reproduction

The strongest candidate regime adds positive self-valence.

The relevant phenomenology is broader than pleasure. Let

\[
V_t(x\mid S_t,C_t)
\]

denote an application-level valence functional over represented content \(x\), current self-state \(S_t\), and context \(C_t\).

Positive self-resonance occurs when a represented relation, action, interpretation, self-image, or priority yields a positive "keep/approach/good" signal.

For an authority-compatible frame, examples might include:

\[
V(\text{I am responsible})>0,
\]

\[
V(\text{I understand how the world works})>0,
\]

\[
V(\text{authority recognizes me as useful})>0,
\]

\[
V(\text{rule-breakers receive deserved consequences})>0,
\]

or simply

\[
V(\text{order restored})>0.
\]

The crucial point is not the particular contents. It is that **valence can attach to the act of inhabiting the frame itself**.

At that point, criticism of the hierarchy can impose an identity-level cost even when no material sanction follows.

## 9. Valence-weighted sampling

A frame that feels good can influence the evidence that later appears to support it.

TLICA already treats substrate mediation as lossy and osmotic imprinting as capable of retuning salience and somatic-affective association through repeated co-occurrence. The present dossier adds only an application-level candidate loop.

Let \(W_t\) be the available world-information and let

\[
O_t(z\mid W_t,S_t)
\]

be an observation/salience kernel determining which represented input \(z\) becomes live enough to guide explicit reasoning.

The conjecture is not that positive valence mechanically determines observation. It is that valence may alter the kernel:

\[
O_t
=
O(W_t,S_t,V_t,\ldots).
\]

Then explicit reasoning receives a **sampled world**, not the whole world:

\[
W_t
\rightarrow
O_t
\rightarrow
z_t
\rightarrow
\text{reasoning}.
\]

If authority-consistent representations repeatedly acquire positive valence while dissonant representations acquire threat or aversive valence, the resulting evidence surface can become systematically skewed before propositional evaluation occurs.

This gives a stronger model than simple conscious motivated reasoning.

The agent may reason coherently over a distorted sample.

The paper therefore insists on:

\[
\text{coherence within sampled evidence}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{adequate sampling of the world}.
\]

This is directly compatible with the repository's standing separation of resonance from truth.

## 10. Ecological self-reinforcement

Perception is only half the loop.

Agents act.

If an authority-compatible frame encourages someone to reward compliant peers, punish challengers, choose authority-affirming social groups, repeat institutional explanations, or avoid dissonant environments, the frame modifies its future input distribution.

The loop becomes:

\[
\text{valence bias}
\rightarrow
\text{salience / interpretation}
\rightarrow
\text{behavior}
\rightarrow
\text{environmental modification}
\rightarrow
\text{new validating inputs}.
\]

The world seen later is therefore not merely selected; it is partly **constructed by earlier frame-consistent action**.

This is the point at which external hierarchy can acquire a distributed internal replication mechanism.

The institution no longer has to directly enforce every relation. Some enforcement is performed by agents for whom doing so is itself identity-consistent or positively resonant.

No group mind is required. The existing TLICA institutional framing is sufficient: distributed people and relations can realize a stable macro-pattern without postulating a super-agent.

## 11. A diagnostic vector, not a scalar score

To avoid turning "bootlicking" into a single moral number, the dossier proposes four separable application-level axes:

\[
B=(s,i,r,e),
\]

where:

- \(s\): **scope** — how many contexts activate the authority-compatible frame;
- \(i\): **internalization** — degree to which the authority model is treated as the agent's own normative ontology rather than a local emulator;
- \(r\): **positive self-resonance** — degree to which frame-consistent states carry positive self-valence;
- \(e\): **externalization** — degree to which the agent imposes or propagates the frame onto peers.

This is not a truth score, diagnosis, moral ranking, or person-level measurement instrument.

Its immediate use is conceptual.

Two agents can have identical behavior under threat while occupying different regions of this space.

A survival emulator may show high local compliance with low scope, internalization, resonance, and externalization. A cross-context authority attractor could exhibit much higher values on some or all axes.

The point is to make rival explanations distinguishable.

## 12. What resistance threatens

Under the strongest regime, the resister threatens three things at once:

1. **institutional stability** — by perturbing the rule;
2. **epistemic stability** — by demonstrating that the predicted invariant may be contingent;
3. **identity stability** — by making costly autobiographical counterfactuals live.

This predicts heterogeneous reactions.

The survival compartmentalizer may admire a resister while declining to imitate them.

The internalizer may judge the resister irresponsible.

The sunk-suffering case may experience successful resistance as cheating.

The positive self-resonant case may experience the resister as an attack on order, responsibility, realism, or the self.

No single ranking of "which resister is hated most" should therefore be expected across all cases.

The mechanism profile matters.

## 13. Normative boundary: spine without romanticizing ruin

The author's motivating stance places high value on refusing domination. A serious paper nevertheless has to separate two propositions:

\[
\text{resistance can matter}
\]

from

\[
\text{every person is obligated to pay every possible cost of resistance}.
\]

The latter does not follow.

A person close to an unacceptable boundary can rationally comply while preserving epistemic and normative dissent:

\[
C=1,\qquad N=0,\qquad E=0,
\]

where \(C\) denotes behavioral compliance, \(N\) normative assent, and \(E\) epistemic reification.

The notation is schematic, but the distinction is load-bearing.

One can bend without declaring gravity.

This is especially important for the paper's moral target. If all submission is condemned equally, the analysis collapses the difference between a person protecting dependents under coercion and a person who converts that coercion into a moral law and enforces it on others.

The paper is interested in the conversion.

## 14. Falsifiability and rival explanations

The theory earns nothing merely by sounding psychologically plausible.

The following rivals must remain live:

- ordinary risk aversion;
- strategic conformity without internalization;
- status seeking;
- norm conventionalism;
- simple preference for coordination or order;
- effort justification / dissonance reduction;
- just-world reasoning;
- learned helplessness;
- identity-protective cognition;
- social learning without positive self-resonance;
- material incentives for peer enforcement.

These names are a **prior-art search queue, not a literature claim**. The dossier has not yet run the primary-source literature pass needed to establish overlap, precedence, or novelty.

The formal probe plan therefore prioritizes dissociations.

If threat removal eliminates authority endorsement, survival emulation wins over deep internalization.

If prior suffering does not increase punitive responses to successful evasion when legitimacy information is held constant, the sunk-suffering mechanism weakens.

If positive self-valence adds no predictive power beyond incentives, status, and risk, the self-resonance extension should be reduced or removed.

If resistance judgments track only macro-outcomes and ignore experimentally explicit information-state changes, the resistance-visibility hypothesis gains support; if observers naturally integrate the full effect vector, it does not.

The project should prefer the weakest mechanism that survives.

---

## 15. Conclusion

The central philosophical error examined here is a collapse of levels:

\[
\text{constraint on me}
\longrightarrow
\text{constraint on reality}.
\]

From that collapse, several familiar statements follow:

> That's just how it works.

> You knew what would happen.

> Everyone has to do it.

> Fighting it is pointless.

> Why should you get to avoid what I had to endure?

Each statement may contain a local truth. The danger lies in the conversion.

A coercive relation can be predictable without being necessary. A punishment can be foreseeable without being self-authored. A person's action set can be brutally constrained without the wider system being immutable. Equal suffering can be equal without being justified. And a frame can become most durable when inhabiting it causes the organism to reward itself for seeing the world in ways that preserve it.

The strongest conjecture of this paper is therefore:

> **An authority-compatible frame becomes especially durable when it does not merely explain the world, but causes the organism to positively value perceiving, inhabiting, and reproducing the world in that frame.**

If that conjecture survives hostile probes, "bootlicking" will turn out to be less a single vice than a family of transitions by which external constraint becomes internally replicated structure.

For now, that remains a hypothesis with substantial truth debt.
