# When the Map Becomes a Mandate
## Attention, self-interpretation, and moral authorization in the writings of Ted Kaczynski: a TLICA-guided research study

**Version:** 0.1.0 — Phase 1, source-critical and hypothesis-generating  
**Date:** 15 September 2026  
**Status:** AI-assisted working paper; not peer reviewed; causal reconstruction unverified.

## Abstract

How can a critique of social arrangements become a justification for harming people without every observation within that critique being false? This paper develops a source-critical research design around the writings of Ted Kaczynski. It uses the Two-Layer Identity-Correlation Architecture (TLICA) to distinguish contact, verification-pathway state, identity integration, source attribution, probe selection, and preservation-ranking. Its object is neither retrospective diagnosis nor a deterministic biography. It examines the potentially consequential relations between experienced constraint, repeated attention, self-interpretation, evaluation of alternatives, and moral authorization. An initial documentary pilot establishes a chronological exclusion: a wilderness episode retrospectively assigned to 1983 cannot explain the onset of a bombing campaign beginning in 1978. Other materials prevent a simple replacement story. The public manifesto is evidence of an articulated justification, not transparent evidence of its developmental origin; prosecution-selected private writings raise a revenge-first account, while their political content complicates a revenge-versus-ideology dichotomy. The paper therefore compares focus-mediated foreclosure, revenge-first rationalization, reciprocal development, audience-dependent narration, substrate contributions, and knowing authorization of harm. Its principal analytical contribution is to distinguish failures of factual representation from failures to recognize others as constraints on action. Accurate knowledge does not itself supply moral permission. Phase 1 yields an auditable corpus pilot, correction ledger, and prospective discrimination protocol, not a validated causal model. A subsequent study must establish temporal ordering, preserve provenance dependencies, seek disconfirming cases, and test whether TLICA adds discrimination beyond ordinary ideological and moral-psychological analysis.

**Keywords:** TLICA; political violence; phenomenological reconstruction; attention; agency; ideology; moral authorization; source criticism.

## 1. The problem: explanation without inevitability

A tempting account of destructive political action begins with an injury, follows its conversion into a worldview, and ends with violence. Once the ending is known, every earlier disappointment can appear preparatory. This paper treats that narrative convenience as a methodological hazard. The question is not which childhood event secretly contained the adult outcome. It is which documented transitions, if any, distinguish competing accounts of how a person interpreted experience, allocated attention, evaluated alternatives, and authorized action.

The concrete historical boundary is serious and non-metaphorical. The FBI dates the beginning of Kaczynski's bombing campaign to 1978 and attributes three deaths and approximately two dozen injuries to it. It records his arrest on 3 April 1996. These are the consequences to be explained, not dramatic scenery for an intellectual biography [S01]. Our unit of explanation must therefore remain capable of distinguishing radical criticism from the decision to injure people.

This inquiry separates three questions. First, what claims did Kaczynski advance, and what evidence would support or defeat them? Second, through what history did those claims acquire significance for him? Third, what additional premises or commitments permitted him to treat violence as an acceptable response? A philosophical criticism can answer the first without settling the second. A developmental account can address the second without answering the third. A moral judgment can reject an action without establishing the psychology that produced it.

Nor does a legitimate concern license its proposed remedy. Even a completely sound diagnosis of an institutional harm would not, by itself, establish that a particular person caused that harm, that attacking that person would reduce it, that no less harmful alternative exists, or that the attack is permissible. These are separate inferential obligations. The paper calls their unjustified compression a **truth-to-permission gap**: a proposed analytical distinction, not a newly discovered psychological mechanism.

The contribution sought is deliberately narrower than a comprehensive explanation of Kaczynski. It is a reproducible way of tracking where experiential reports, explanatory claims, identity commitments, and action permissions come apart. TLICA is a candidate instrument for that task. It does not receive empirical confirmation merely because its vocabulary can redescribe the case.

## 2. Position in the literature

This project enters an existing field rather than claiming that serious scholarship has ignored Kaczynski. Moen's philosophical analysis criticizes the premises of his ethics while distinguishing that criticism from the assessment of particular technological concerns [S08]. Fleming's forensic ideology analysis uses archival materials to challenge familiar classifications of Kaczynski and, importantly for this study, argues that personal revenge and political motivation are not mutually exclusive [S07]. These precedents limit both the novelty claim and the permissible simplicity of the proposed explanation.

Two other research traditions supply comparison models. McCauley and Moskalenko distinguish radicalization of opinion from radicalization of action [S10]. Bandura examines mechanisms by which moral self-sanctions can be disengaged from harmful conduct [S09]. Their concepts are not treated as validated diagnoses of this individual. They establish that a TLICA account must do more than rename familiar distinctions between beliefs, motives, self-regulation, and action.

A modest potential addition is the joint bookkeeping of source attribution, identity integration, verification access, and the evolving availability of alternatives. Its value would lie in preventing particular analytical substitutions: conviction for verification, attention for contact, integration for valuation, and coherence for ethical justification. Whether this bookkeeping produces better explanations, more reproducible coding, or more discriminating predictions than a simpler account remains an empirical and methodological question.

The literature search here is bounded and exploratory, not a systematic review. Fleming's earlier intellectual-origins article was located bibliographically but its full text was not acquired in this session; it is a Phase 2 reading lead, not an independently inspected evidentiary foundation [L01]. The study uses the accessible 2024 article for the limited claims stated above. It does not inherit that article's clinical judgments or every historical inference.

## 3. Framework, version control, and operational restraint

### 3.1 What TLICA supplies

TLICA distinguishes three formal coordinates: contact, written κ; toolkit-relative truth-indistinguishability, written φ; and identity-correlation, written ρ. Source attribution σ, probe machinery μ, toolkit closure, and preservation-ranking Π have different roles and are not additional interchangeable truth scores. The repository identifies the identity-correlation profile as a structural object and distinguishes integration from valuation [T01, T02, T03]. The following translation is specific to this project.

| Construct | Question in this study | What the documents do not supply |
|---|---|---|
| κ: contact | What encounter or bodily/environmental relation is reported? | A retrospective narrative does not measure historical contact intensity. |
| φ: pathway state | What tests, warrants, and admitted uncertainties are articulated? | Confidence, fluent argument, and repetition do not estimate φ. |
| σ: source attribution | What source or causal referent does the actor assign to an experience? | A compelling attribution is not thereby an adequate one. |
| ρ: identity integration | Is revising a commitment represented as losing or betraying the self? | Topic frequency does not reconstruct a max-flow network or a numerical ρ. |
| μ: probe selection | Which counterarguments are considered, rejected, or not encountered? | An omitted objection does not prove it was unavailable or suppressed. |
| Π: preservation-ranking | What does the actor say should be protected, sacrificed, or refused? | Identity integration alone does not determine this ranking. |

This table specifies interpretive questions, not a psychometric instrument. There are no estimated κ, φ, σ, ρ, or μ values in the pilot. In particular, an analyst's lack of evidence about someone's verification pathway is **unknown to the analyst**, not **undefined φ for the subject**. Undefined φ is an architectural claim about a missing constructible pathway, not a label for every archival gap.

The same restraint applies to clinical language. A profile-shaped description is not a diagnosis. An account of attentional reinforcement is not evidence of a disorder. No psychiatric condition, traumatic event, or neurodevelopmental characteristic is inferred from radical opinions, solitude, mathematical ability, or the eventual crimes.

### 3.2 The current dynamical correction matters

The pinned v5.5.0 foundation names a slow lived-I structure G, with an identity-load reading ρ = R(G) and a field-reading f = F(G, A). Its fast baseline law is

\[
\dot b = \gamma(f-b),\qquad \gamma>0.
\]

The foundation treats the shared G construction and its reading maps as named posits. In this law, ρ is not an extra force multiplying baseline motion. Reflexive imprinting can affect the baseline through changes in G that change the target f; changing an identity metric alone does not establish that target change. The current formulation also distinguishes a bounded driven regime from a perpetually increasing felt magnitude [T01, T02].

Consequently, the earlier conversational sketch must not be read as “identity intensity increases, therefore violent motion follows.” The most that is proposed here is a candidate dependency chain:

\[
\text{selected attention}
\longrightarrow G\text{-update}
\longrightarrow\text{changed interpretation or option evaluation}
\longrightarrow\text{possible action change}.
\]

Every arrow requires an application-specific evidentiary bridge. None is measured in this case. The manuscript's argument can also be read without accepting the experimental dynamical layer: the documentary distinctions and causal exclusions do not depend on fitting that law.

The choice-as-filter application supplies a further conjecture: self-directed attention can reshape later possibilities through the architecture's existing update mechanisms [T04]. Here **filter** means a selective orientation of attention, not a hidden homunculus or a newly posited mental faculty. The process can be deliberate, habitual, ambiently learned, or mixed. TLICA's always-operating osmotic machinery does not imply that every moment produces a durable change, nor that everything learned was chosen [T02].

### 3.3 Terms that must not outrun the evidence

**Explanatory foreclosure** means treating rival explanations as no longer genuinely examinable; it is stronger than preferring one explanation. **Option foreclosure** means a represented or practical loss of alternatives; it is stronger than evaluating them negatively. **Moral authorization** means an actor's acceptance of a permission to harm; it is distinct from our endorsement of that permission. **Identity bottleneck** is a proposed concentration of self-integration through a narrow commitment, not an observed brain structure. **Attractor** is used, when necessary, as a metaphor for persistence unless an explicit state space and dynamics are supplied. This study estimates no attractor.

The term **phenomenological reconstruction** denotes disciplined interpretation of reported experience. We do not have first-person access to Kaczynski. The available objects are statements, recollections, public arguments, legal selections, and institutional records. Statements about experience and the experiences themselves are not identical data.

## 4. Method: a documentary pilot with explicit rivals

### 4.1 Corpus and provenance

The Phase 1 corpus is purposive. It contains an institutional chronology, a Berkeley employment record, selected passages of the 1995 manifesto, a published 1999 interview, a reproduced prosecution memorandum, and a judicial opinion. Research literature supplies comparisons and challenges, not new independent observations of the same underlying event. Exact references, access levels, and limitations are recorded in [SOURCES.md](SOURCES.md); individual observations are in [data/evidence.json](data/evidence.json).

Sources have different evidentiary jobs. A university can document an employment date without explaining why someone resigned. A manifesto can establish an articulated position without establishing the position's sincerity, genesis, or historical efficacy. An interview can preserve a reported experience without verifying its external cause. A prosecution document can identify evidence and an adversarial interpretation without making that interpretation a judicial finding. A court opinion can document a competency ruling without establishing complete psychological health or retrospective agency at every earlier event.

Multiple web hosts do not create multiple witnesses. The Washington Post text and its archive reproduction belong to the same manifesto family. Two interview reproductions share an underlying interview family. A later article quoting a prosecution-selected diary passage inherits that selection dependency. The source registry distinguishes publication venue, document family, and underlying actor provenance so that these dependencies remain visible.

An important access correction emerged during inspection: the Ted K Archive interview page combines material attributed to a journal and a later documentary. It is not treated as a pristine 1999 transcript [S05]. The pilot prefers the separately presented interview article [S04] and uses the composite only for a provenance warning. Original audio, a facsimile of the original publication, and manuscript exhibits were not acquired. Source pages were inspected through browser text; original web response bytes were not preserved or hashed.

### 4.2 Events, reports, and interpretations

Each historical row separates an event date from a report date and identifies whether the date is exact, annual, retrospective, or unknown. The following sequence is intentionally sparse.

| Period | Documentary anchor | Permissible use |
|---|---|---|
| 1967–1969 | Berkeley employment and resignation effective 30 June 1969 [S02] | Institutional chronology; no inferred motive. |
| April 1971 | Journal material selected in the later prosecution memorandum [S06] | A mediated early statement of motive and uncertainty, not an unfiltered diary corpus. |
| 1978 | FBI beginning of the bombing campaign [S01] | Earliest campaign date used in the chronological test. |
| Summer 1983 | Wilderness-road episode recalled in the 1999 interview [S04] | Retrospective event claim; not an independently verified construction date. |
| September 1995 | Public manifesto [S03] | Public argument and justification after violence had already begun. |
| April 1996 | Arrest [S01] | Chronological endpoint for the pre-arrest campaign. |
| 1998 | Competency proceedings and sentencing-related records [S06, S11] | Bounded legal and documentary context. |
| June 1999 | Interview setting identified in the published article [S04] | Date of narration, not contemporaneous access to earlier states. |

Early-life hospitalization, school experiences, and the Henry Murray research episode are not inserted as explanatory causes. Their relevant primary records were not inspected. A compelling biography can be assembled from such episodes; a defensible causal ordering cannot be assumed from their emotional plausibility. They remain separately documented acquisition leads, with no claim that they were absent or inconsequential.

### 4.3 Competing accounts

We retain six accounts, which can overlap rather than partition all possibilities. **R1** proposes that repeated attention and self-integration progressively narrowed interpretation and options before important violent decisions. **R2** proposes that revenge commitments were already driving action and ideology chiefly supplied later rationalization. **R3** proposes reciprocal development of grievance, ideas, attention, and action. **R4** emphasizes changes in audience and genre rather than changes in underlying commitment. **R5** allows clinical or substrate contributions that this corpus cannot identify. **R6** proposes knowing authorization of harm without the decisive step being factual confusion or an inability to imagine alternatives.

These are a comparison set, not six exhaustive personality types. No posterior probabilities are assigned. A flexible reciprocal account can absorb almost anything; it does not win by accommodation alone. It must eventually specify temporal and documentary predictions that differ from the simpler accounts.

## 5. Phase 1 results: constraints on the reconstruction

### 5.1 A chronological exclusion, not an origin story

The 1983 episode is later than the campaign onset used here. Therefore it cannot be that onset's preceding cause. This conclusion does not depend on deciding whether the road existed exactly as described. If the report is correct, it is too late; if incorrect, it cannot establish the proposed origin either. It remains possible that the recalled episode marked increased commitment, a change in activity, or a later narrative anchor. The present record does not choose among those possibilities [S01, S04; C01, C02, C03].

This small result is methodologically valuable because it removes a vivid, emotionally economical story before that story can organize the entire paper. It also illustrates the difference between refuting a particular chronology and refuting every environmental contribution. The former is supported; the latter is not.

### 5.2 An articulated justification is not a developmental mechanism

The manifesto's paragraph 33 presents its goal–effort–attainment account, with a qualified role for autonomy. Paragraphs 93–96 connect freedom to concrete control and present violence as a means of securing attention. These passages document claims and a public justification; they do not disclose the causal route by which the author adopted them [S03].

For analysis, the distinction is decisive. A statement written after years of violence may be sincere, strategic, retrospective, or some combination. Identical words could be produced by an actor who first reasoned toward violence, an actor who first sought revenge, or an actor whose reasons and actions co-developed. The passage alone does not identify which history occurred.

Nor does dismissing ordinary publication show that the option could not be represented. The argument itself represents a nonviolent alternative and evaluates it negatively. A claim of **unavailability** would require additional evidence beyond a claim of **insufficient expected impact**. The manuscript therefore withdraws the earlier conversational suggestion that the option space had demonstrably collapsed [C07].

### 5.3 The revenge-first rival cannot be ignored—or simplified

The reproduced prosecution memorandum includes an April 1971 passage foregrounding revenge while acknowledging uncertainty about beneficial and harmful public consequences. Its provenance is layered: attributed journal writing, selected exhibit, prosecutorial framing, and later web reproduction [S06]. This is substantial counterpressure against a simple account in which a mature public argument first generated the willingness to harm.

However, revenge and ideology are not logical opposites. An actor can resent a social arrangement through an ideological understanding of what has been lost. Fleming explicitly challenges the exclusion of political motivation from the relevant material [S07]. This prevents treating the prosecutor's framing as the settled explanation. The pilot supports keeping R2 and R3 live; it does not establish either as dominant.

A further distinction follows. Admitting uncertainty about beneficial consequences does not eliminate intentional action. It can instead reveal that anticipated benefit was not the only governing consideration. If later claims of necessity conflict with an earlier acknowledgment of uncertainty, possible explanations include changing beliefs, changing priorities, genre differences, selective presentation, or rationalization. Selecting among these requires dated sequences, not one rhetorically powerful contrast.

### 5.4 Awareness of harm challenges the purely epistemic account

The published interview also attributes to Kaczynski explicit recognition that collapse would cause extensive suffering [S04]. This is important counterevidence to a hypothesis in which the decisive failure was simply an inability to represent harmful consequences. It does not establish complete knowledge of every victim or outcome. It does show why awareness, valuation, and restraint need separate treatment.

The research question thus becomes more demanding: did a particular action arise from inadequate knowledge, from distorted causal attribution, from discounting known alternatives, from moral disengagement, from openly subordinating others' interests, or from several of these? A source-map correction would not necessarily reverse an action whose actor already understood the relevant harm and accepted it.

### 5.5 What the pilot has not shown

There is no measured longitudinal contraction of probing. No numerical identity profile has been reconstructed. No independently established transition from ordinary criticism to a totalizing belief system has been demonstrated across the whole life. No stable relationship between solitude and diminished correction has been identified. No full clinical assessment has been conducted. No intervention has been tested. The corpus was selected after the motivating hypothesis was formulated, so it is development material, not a fresh holdout.

## 6. Candidate mechanism: self-reinforcing interpretation

The following is an application-level hypothesis, not a biographical finding. Suppose that an experience of constraint becomes central to someone's self-understanding. Repeatedly attending to a preferred explanation might increase the availability of related memories, questions, and interpretations. Subsequent choices could then be made from a field increasingly organized by that explanation. The important possibility is not that the initial observation must have been false. It is that the observation's scope could be expanded without independently earning the stronger claims.

A possible sequence is: an encountered constraint is interpreted; an explanation becomes identity-bearing; new events are preferentially assessed through it; alternatives are evaluated under an increasingly demanding standard; and a particular remedy becomes privileged. This sequence contains at least three unproven transitions. Attention must actually change later interpretation; identity integration must matter beyond simple repetition; and changed interpretation must alter response selection rather than merely public language. Phase 2 must look for evidence at each transition.

The vocabulary of **misguided focus choices** is useful only when its agency assumptions are explicit. A person can decide to read one argument, rehearse one grievance, or reject one criticism. That does not mean the person selected the initial experience, every subsequent association, the available cognitive capacity, or the full consequences of the attentional act. Conversely, acknowledging involuntary learning does not erase deliberate actions. The attribution must be local to a documented choice opportunity rather than assigned wholesale to a life.

A hypothetical identity bottleneck would make revision costly because many commitments depended on one interpretive link. But costly revision can encourage either defensiveness or unusually rigorous scrutiny. High ρ is therefore not a monotonic risk factor. A commitment to proving oneself wrong could itself be deeply integrated. A useful model must specify when integration supports correction and when it impedes it, rather than treating intensity as pathology.

The account also needs a rival direction of causation. Action can reshape interpretation: a harmful act may create a need for subsequent consistency, concealment, or justification. A record of increasing rhetorical certainty after violence is compatible with action-driven rationalization. That is why an adequately dated pre-action corpus has greater discriminatory value than a later polished explanation.

Finally, the account cannot infer epistemic isolation from geographical solitude. A solitary person may encounter demanding arguments; a socially connected person may occupy an intellectually uniform group. The proposed variable is the availability and uptake of discriminating correction, not the number of people nearby. Absence of correspondence in a selected archive is not evidence that corrective relationships did not exist.

## 7. The map, the other person, and the permission to harm

### 7.1 Three distinct failures

The earlier discussion described a person being treated as a representative of a system as a source-map catastrophe. That description is incomplete. We distinguish **attribution error**, **representation failure**, and **authorization failure**.

Attribution error occurs when a particular person is assigned causal responsibility they do not have. Representation failure occurs when a person's distinct interests and experience are not adequately represented. Authorization failure, under the ethical premise adopted here, occurs when another person's bodily security is subordinated to an asserted project without sufficient justification. The third can occur while the first two are absent. Someone can know exactly who another person is, know that person is not responsible for the grievance, anticipate the injury, and still choose to use the person instrumentally.

This distinction is not an empirical claim that one of the three explains every Kaczynski attack. It is a logical correction to the analytic framework. A model that forces every harmful decision into misinformation would become unable to describe knowledgeable wrongdoing. TLICA's distinction between identity integration and preservation-ranking helps keep that possibility visible [T03, T05].

### 7.2 The missing premises

Let Q denote a grievance claim, A a causal attribution, E an efficacy claim, and J a proposed justification. The inference from Q to a harmful action requires more than Q's truth. Here Q is a proposition, not the lived-I structure G of Section 3. It needs evidence for A, evidence for E, consideration of alternatives, and a defensible J. A deficiency in any bridge remains a deficiency even if the other bridges hold.

For example, the proposition that an institution imposes a genuine constraint does not specify who bears responsibility. Correctly identifying a responsible institution does not establish that injuring a person associated with it will remedy the constraint. Establishing some causal effect does not establish necessity. Even necessity relative to a selected objective does not establish that the objective outranks others' rights and interests.

This is not a derivation of ethics from TLICA. The paper explicitly adopts a normative commitment against instrumental attacks on nonconsenting persons for the sake of advancing the attacker's message. Describing someone's internally coherent permission structure neither endorses that structure nor refutes this commitment. TLICA's explanatory vocabulary and the manuscript's ethical judgment occupy different levels.

The distinction also prevents an inversion: condemning the violence does not, by itself, disprove every descriptive proposition in the manifesto. The truth of a proposition and the permissibility of an action require their own reasons. Maintaining both standards is neither endorsement nor neutrality about the victims.

### 7.3 Agency must be assessed locally

Planning, articulate writing, and ownership of an action are evidence relevant to some agency questions; they do not settle every component. Conversely, psychological suffering or constrained development does not automatically remove all agency. The judicial record inspected here documents a competency-to-stand-trial determination in 1998 [S11]. That bounded legal finding is not a retrospective measurement of the six-condition agency account across an eighteen-year period.

Accordingly, the paper does not announce that agency was fully intact, absent, or captured. It distinguishes represented alternatives, evaluative engagement, selection, execution, reflective access, and ownership as questions requiring different evidence [T05]. The moral seriousness of the crimes does not license an unsupported total psychological verdict.

## 8. Discriminating the accounts

The principal next probe is a provenance-controlled, dated analysis of previously unexamined pre-action materials. The protocol in [PROTOCOL.md](PROTOCOL.md) must be frozen before selecting or coding a confirmatory set. Anything already viewed in Phase 1 remains development material. An archival restriction is an access gap, not negative evidence.

R1 gains support if dated materials show corrective alternatives being available, considered, and progressively excluded before specific decisions, with identifiable identity costs attached to revision. It is weakened if openness persists while actions occur, or if the alleged narrowing appears only afterward. R2 gains support if revenge-oriented commitments consistently precede the purported ideological rationale and later justifications fail to predict earlier choices. It is weakened by contemporaneous evidence that ideological distinctions constrained action before that rationale was public. R3 needs evidence of changes in both directions, not merely compatibility with both vocabularies.

R4 requires comparisons of near-contemporaneous texts addressed to different audiences. If a shift follows audience rather than time, apparent development may be rhetorical. R5 requires appropriate, ethically obtained clinical evidence and qualified interpretation; a humanities coding exercise cannot decide it. R6 gains support where harm and nonviolent alternatives are explicitly recognized yet knowingly subordinated, without the proposed factual error. It weakens a **purely epistemic** explanation, not every contribution from attention or identity.

The available accounts do not become numerically weighted simply because a table can be drawn. Agreement among several readings of the same passage is not independent confirmation. A second model supplied the first model's interpretation is not a blinded witness. The protocol requires independent first-pass annotations, preservation of disagreement, and a plain-language baseline before TLICA terms are introduced.

A leave-one-family-out analysis is specified as a sensitivity check, not a truth estimator. Removing the 1999 interview asks whether the sanctuary-centered account survives without its central retrospective source. Removing the public manifesto asks whether private materials still support the same articulated rationale. Removing prosecution-selected material asks whether the revenge-first inference rests entirely on that selection. These are proposed comparisons; they have not yet been run as a full empirical analysis.

Negative comparisons are indispensable. Nonviolent critics of technology and people who explicitly rejected violence despite comparable grievances would test the specificity of the proposed mechanism. They must not be selected merely because they make a preferred contrast easy. A one-case study selected on a notorious outcome cannot estimate the probability that a person with certain interests or experiences will become violent.

## 9. Ethics, limitations, and scope of the claim

Victims are not secondary data points in the perpetrator's self-realization. The analysis excludes device construction, concealment, target-selection procedures, and practical violence guidance. It does not reproduce an extremist document in full or present the subject as a model to emulate. Direct quotations are minimized; locators allow critical verification without turning the package into a propaganda anthology.

The public availability of a record does not establish that every personal detail should be republished. The judicial opinion itself documents redactions protecting third-party and irrelevant private information [S11]. This project will not seek sealed material, reproduce intimate details without analytical necessity, or infer pathology from sexuality, gender, unconventional life choices, or dissent. Any later clinical component requires a distinct justification and appropriate expertise.

The largest evidentiary limitations are retrospective narration, selective preservation, adversarial document assembly, missing early records, incomplete edition verification, and the absence of independent coding. Their effects differ. Missing records reduce access. Shared provenance reduces independence. Genre differences complicate measurement. The lack of a discriminating comparison leaves rivals unresolved. These are not interchangeable defects, and none is repaired by declaring the resulting prose coherent.

TLICA itself remains an unvalidated explanatory instrument for this application. Its formal definitions do not establish a measurement model linking passages to latent structures. The proposed use may prove to be useful bookkeeping without explanatory advantage. A serious test must allow that result. The study also cannot determine that a particular conversation, social contact, or reframing would have prevented the crimes. Counterfactual prevention claims require evidence beyond a reconstruction of what happened.

## 10. Conclusion

The defensible Phase 1 result is a set of constraints, not a completed psychological history. Chronology excludes one prominent origin narrative. Genre and provenance prevent treating a public justification as direct access to developmental causation. Early revenge material and its political content keep competing causal accounts alive. Recognition of harm prevents a purely informational account from becoming the default explanation.

The proposed TLICA contribution is to hold distinctions apart that a persuasive biography can too easily merge: contact and truth, truth and identity, identity and valuation, negative evaluation and unavailable alternatives, accurate representation and moral restraint. These separations make the inquiry harder but more falsifiable. They also place the framework under the same discipline as its subject: an internally integrated interpretation does not acquire authority over reality by becoming compelling.

The title names the problem, not a proven sequence. A map becomes a mandate only through further commitments about responsibility, efficacy, alternatives, and permission. The research task is to identify those commitments where the record supports them, leave them unresolved where it does not, and test whether their ordering helps explain action. Phase 2 must earn that ordering with new evidence. Until then, the causal reconstruction remains **CONJECTURED / UNVERIFIED**.
