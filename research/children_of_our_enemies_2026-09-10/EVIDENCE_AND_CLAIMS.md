## 2. External sources and exact scope

### E1. Collective victimhood — conceptual predecessor

Daniel Bar-Tal, Lily Chernyak-Hai, Noa Schori, and Ayelet Gundar. 2009. “A sense of self-perceived collective victimhood in intractable conflicts.” *International Review of the Red Cross* 91(874): 229–258. DOI: 10.1017/S1816383109990221. [Author article hosted by ICRC](https://www.icrc.org/eng/assets/files/other/irrc-874-bartal-chernyakhai-schori-gundar.pdf).

**Read:** abstract and opening conceptual discussion, including group identification and historical memory; title page visually checked. **Used for:** conceptual precedence and the distinction between injury and its shared interpretation. **Not used for:** a causal effect size, a diagnosis of all group members, or proof that actual victimization is imaginary. This article's interpretations are not ICRC legal determinations.

### E2. Child exposure and aggression — longitudinal observational evidence

Paul Boxer, L. Rowell Huesmann, Eric F. Dubow, Simha F. Landau, Shira Dvir Gvirsman, Khalil Shikaki, and Jeremy Ginges. 2013. “Exposure to violence across the social ecosystem and the development of aggression: a test of ecological theory in the Israeli-Palestinian conflict.” *Child Development* 84(1): 163–177. DOI: 10.1111/j.1467-8624.2012.01848.x. [PubMed record and abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22906188/).

**Read:** bibliographic record and abstract. **Reported design:** three annual waves and three starting-age cohorts across Palestinian, Israeli Jewish, and Israeli Arab samples. **Used for:** the relevance of interconnected social environments and aggression. **Limit:** observation and aggression are not experimental proof of political radicalization, intergenerational transmission, or a TLICA-specific pathway. Full methods and replication data were not audited; a PMC full-text attempt encountered an access challenge.

### E3. War exposure and local cooperation — counterweight to global moral depletion

Michal Bauer, Christopher Blattman, Julie Chytilova, Joseph Henrich, Edward Miguel, and Tamar Mitts. 2016. “Can War Foster Cooperation?” *Journal of Economic Perspectives* 30(3): 249–274. DOI: 10.1257/jep.30.3.249. [Publisher record and abstract](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/jep.30.3.249).

**Read:** publisher abstract and bibliographic details. **Used for:** reported increases in local cooperation and qualified indications of parochial effects. **Limit:** no conclusion that war improves welfare or that local cooperation yields peace. This is a synthesis and reanalysis of studies; its constituent studies must not be counted again as automatically independent evidence.

### E4. Contact after war — localized behavioral change

Salma Mousa. 2020. “Building social cohesion between Christians and Muslims through soccer in post-ISIS Iraq.” *Science* 369(6505): 866–870. [Author's publication page and abstract](https://www.salmamousa.com/research).

**Read:** author-hosted abstract. **Used for:** a randomized intervention with improvements toward Muslim peers and limited generalization beyond the intervention's social context. **Limit:** no universal contact effect, no identification of TLICA routing, and no inference from better peer behavior to changed national political commitments. Publisher full-text retrieval failed; replication files were not analyzed.

### E4b. Contact and empathy education — a difficult adjacent result

Salma Mousa, Lennard Naumann, and Alexandra Scacco. “Intergroup Contact, Empathy Education, and Refugee-Native Integration: Evidence from a Field Experiment in Lebanon.” **Version actually read: May 2, 2025 draft.** [Author-hosted PDF](https://www.salmamousa.com/s/empathy-contact-lebanon-2May25.pdf).

**Read:** abstract and opening design/context text; PDF pages 1 and 10 visually checked. **Used for:** reported limited contact benefits, some adverse behavioral responses, selected modest empathy-education benefits, and no detected parent spillover. **Limits:** not a slack test. No numerical mixed effect is computed or certified from the abstract; full-cell estimates and uncertainty require another pass. The draft's introductory sample counts and program-composition percentages do not reconcile as written. None is used here. Publication updates and a full statistical audit remain outstanding.

**Provenance:** E4 and E4b involve different settings and designs but share an author. Their metadata on the same page are not additional witnesses.

### E5. Restraint is organizational as well as individual

International Committee of the Red Cross. 2018. *The Roots of Restraint in War*. [Official publication overview](https://www.icrc.org/en/publication/4352-roots-restraint-war).

**Read:** official overview. **Used for:** its research across eight case studies in five countries, focused on formal/informal norms and organizational socialization. **Limit:** the overview supports the relevance of organization, not the efficacy of a particular unexamined program or a quantified causal effect of exhaustion. No complete report audit was run.

### E6. Collective penalties — narrow legal boundary

*Geneva Convention relative to the Protection of Civilian Persons in Time of War*, 12 August 1949, Article 33. [ICRC treaty collection](https://www.icrc.org/sites/default/files/external/doc/en/assets/files/publications/icrc-002-0173.pdf), printed page 162, PDF page 166.

**Read and visually checked:** Articles 31–34 on that page; Article 33 is used only for its prohibitions concerning protected persons and collective penalties. **Limit:** this is not a full legal opinion on a conflict. A relative's indirect suffering is not automatically a collective penalty. Applicable legal categories, intent, conduct, and operation-specific facts require separate analysis. No legal conclusion is inferred from a TLICA diagnosis.

### E7. Reconstruction — institutional persistence rather than mere private resentment

National Park Service. “A Short Overview of the Reconstruction Era and Ulysses S. Grant's Presidency.” Updated December 16, 2021. [Official historical overview](https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/a-short-overview-of-the-reconstruction-era-and-ulysses-s-grant-s-presidency.htm).

**Read:** overview. **Used for:** emancipation and citizenship protections, organized resistance and violence, and erosion of rights after Reconstruction. **Limit:** an official secondary synthesis, not an original archival proof of the proposed operator genealogy. It does not establish an uninterrupted two-culture explanation of contemporary politics. Archival and historiographical work remains required.

## 5. Diagnostic and provenance discipline

Keep kappa/contact, phi/toolkit-relative indistinguishability, sigma/source-map adequacy, rho/identity coupling, mu/probe availability, toolkit closure, independence, coherence, and discrimination distinct. The model of another subject in T1 is not sigma itself, and its affective routing channel is not evidence attribution. Do not recast the paper as one truth, hatred, empathy, or goodness score.

A phi-resolved account can remain sigma-unresolved. A compelling emotional account may register real harm without establishing its correct causal scope. High identity coupling motivates stronger source blinding; it does not increase a claim's truth probability. “No current discriminator” is a declared access/probe limit, not proof of falsehood or final contextual saturation.

The sources are organized into theory, longitudinal observation, field experiment, synthesis, organizational research, legal text, and historical overview. They were selected to constrain different parts of the proposal, not to cast votes. E4/E4b share authorship. E3 aggregates other research. E1, E5, and E6 share a host but are respectively an authored conceptual article, organizational research, and a legal text; the shared host is neither identity of method nor independent corroboration.

## 6. Next discriminating work

Before human research, freeze the requested outcome, independent capacity measures, policy boundary, and rival predictions. Preserve all four cells of any 2-by-2 intervention design; no mixed-effect claim from missing cells. Essential support and basic protection are not experimental withholding conditions. Begin with consenting adults in safe contexts; any later child-related work requires appropriate specialist oversight, consent/assent, and protection against coercion and retaliation.

A successful slack manipulation followed by adequately precise null or contrary target effects weakens the specified slack-mediated hypothesis. A failed manipulation is ambiguous. Improved group attitudes without corresponding protection cannot be called durable peace. Better held-out prediction by a material-threat model without the proposed added mechanism weakens the TLICA-specific explanatory claim. Genuine disagreement after good-faith communication is a possible finding, not an error to erase.

The strongest cultural-operator probe would measure composition and transition effects that a static or averaged model misses, then test against fresh periods with the basis and rule construction frozen. The toy matrices are not those observations.
