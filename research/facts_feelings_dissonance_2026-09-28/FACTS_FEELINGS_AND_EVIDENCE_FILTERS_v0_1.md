# Facts Do Not Care About Your Feelings; Your Evidence Filter Might

## Dissonance Avoidance, Identity-Coupled Belief, and the Mathematics of Epistemic Self-Sealing

**Standalone research draft — v0.1**  
**Date:** 2026-09-28

---

## Abstract

The maxim that facts do not depend on one's feelings expresses a sound ontological point: whether a proposition is true is not ordinarily determined by whether accepting it is pleasant. It does not follow, however, that a person who identifies with this maxim processes evidence independently of affect. The relevant question is not whether truth depends on feeling, but whether **evidence admission, weighting, source evaluation, and belief revision** depend on feeling.

This paper develops a structural account of a particularly ironic epistemic failure: an agent may experience herself as increasingly rational precisely because she reduces the affective discomfort generated by contradiction, while the mechanism by which she reduces that discomfort is selective loss of world-information. Cognitive dissonance is treated neither as evidence that an incoming claim is true nor as noise to be eliminated, but as a signal that some relation among current commitments, observations, interpretations, and action tendencies is under strain. The epistemically relevant operation is therefore not *reduce dissonance at minimum cost*, but *preserve contact long enough to determine what should change*.

A compact formal model makes the point exact. Consider a binary hypothesis tested by diagnostic evidence. If evidence favorable to a current commitment is always admitted while sufficiently much contrary evidence is suppressed, then an otherwise correct likelihood-ratio updater can acquire increasing confidence in a false hypothesis. The threshold is explicit. Under the model, subjective coherence rises while epistemic accuracy falls. This is not a theorem about human psychology; it is a theorem about filtered evidence streams. Psychological research on cognitive dissonance, selective exposure, biased assimilation, motivated reasoning, myside bias, and actively open-minded thinking supplies independent empirical constraints showing that the relevant filtering phenomena are not merely imaginary.

To describe why filtering becomes especially attractive when beliefs are identity-bearing, the paper distinguishes present contact ($\kappa$), toolkit-relative truth-testing ($\phi$), identity-correlation ($\rho$), source-map adequacy ($\sigma$), probe availability ($\mu$), and practical slack ($S$). These quantities must not be collapsed into a single "rationality" score. In particular, high identity-correlation is not evidence of truth; it is a reason to require stronger blinding. High subjective coherence is not calibration. Low felt dissonance is not successful inference.

The conclusion is intentionally modest and therefore difficult to evade: **"facts over feelings" is a valid norm only when the procedure used to implement it preserves disconfirming information precisely when that information produces unwelcome feeling.** Otherwise the slogan does not describe transcendence of emotion. It describes emotion's promotion from participant to evidence-admission officer.

## 1. The slogan and the hidden variable

"Facts do not care about your feelings" can mean at least two importantly different things.

The first is an ontological thesis:

> Whether a proposition is true is generally independent of whether I like it.

Call this **truth-independence**. It is unobjectionable.

The second is an autobiographical thesis:

> I, the speaker, am unusually good at allowing facts rather than feelings to govern my beliefs.

Call this **self-certified objectivity**.

The first statement can be true while the second is false.

Nothing about the truth-independence of propositions gives a reason to believe that a particular nervous system, institution, ideology, subculture, or reasoning procedure is unbiased. A declaration of objectivity is not an audit of the process producing the declaration. It is testimony from the instrument currently under examination.

This paper studies the gap between those two theses.

The central claim is not that emotion should replace evidence. It is nearly the opposite. The claim is that a system can become *less* answerable to evidence while sincerely experiencing itself as *more* fact-driven if it treats the unpleasant affect generated by contradiction as a reason to alter the information channel rather than the model.

The relevant contrast is therefore not

\[
\text{facts} \quad \text{versus} \quad \text{feelings}.
\]

It is

\[
\text{world-contact and error-correction}
\quad \text{versus} \quad
\text{affect-regulation by evidence control}.
\]

This distinction matters because one may regulate affect using cognition. Rationalization is not made non-emotional by occurring in complete sentences.

A system does not become objective by naming its preferred affective state "objectivity."

## 2. Scope: what is and is not being claimed

Three boundaries are necessary.

First, this is **not a claim about conservatives, liberals, religious people, atheists, scientists, skeptics, populists, technocrats, or any other political or cultural population as a class**. The architecture described here is available to all identity-bearing worldviews. Political examples are useful because political commitments are often strongly identity-coupled, but the mechanism is domain-general.

Second, dissonance is **not** treated as evidence that the proposition producing it is true. A flat-earther can feel dissonance when presented with a correct argument and a physicist can feel dissonance when presented with a bad one. Dissonance marks conflict. It does not adjudicate the conflict.

Third, the formal model below is a **toy model of evidence censoring**, not a fitted model of human cognition. Its theorem is mathematical. Its application to psychology is a structural hypothesis constrained by empirical literature, not a claim that people literally perform the equations.

The target is therefore an epistemic procedure, not a demographic group:

> **The self-sealing fact-first posture:** a reasoning style in which the agent treats her own low felt conflict as evidence of objectivity while allowing identity-threatening or affectively costly observations to receive systematically lower probability of admission, retention, scrutiny, or evidential weight.

The phrase "facts over feelings" will be used as a convenient ideal type for the self-description associated with this posture. No claim is made that everyone who has ever used the phrase instantiates the mechanism.

## 3. Dissonance is not the enemy

Classical cognitive-dissonance theory begins from a simple observation: inconsistency among relevant cognitions can produce an aversive motivational state, and people are motivated to reduce that state. Later action-based accounts emphasize that dissonance is especially consequential when conflicting cognitions carry incompatible action implications. The important point for present purposes is that **dissonance reduction admits multiple routes**.

Suppose a person holds a model $M$ and encounters evidence $E$ that is difficult to reconcile with it. Let

\[
D(M,E) \ge 0
\]

denote the experienced conflict generated by their joint presence.

There are many ways to reduce $D$.

One is model revision:

\[
M + E \longrightarrow M'.
\]

Another is reinterpretation of the evidence:

\[
M + E \longrightarrow E'.
\]

Another is evidence downgrading:

\[
w(E) \downarrow.
\]

Another is source attack:

\[
\operatorname{credibility}(\operatorname{source}(E)) \downarrow.
\]

Another is selective non-exposure:

\[
\Pr(E \text{ encountered}) \downarrow.
\]

Another is trivialization:

\[
\operatorname{importance}(E) \downarrow.
\]

All of these can reduce experienced conflict. Only some improve the model.

The epistemic mistake is therefore to optimize directly for

\[
\min D.
\]

An agent interested in accuracy should instead optimize something more like

\[
\min \operatorname{prediction\ error}
\]

subject to enough psychological and practical stability to continue investigating.

Dissonance is thus best treated as an **interrupt**, not a verdict:

\[
D>0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\text{a discriminating probe is needed}.
\]

The probe may vindicate the existing model. It may vindicate the incoming evidence. It may reveal a source error, a category mistake, an ambiguity, a bad inference, or a model that is only locally valid.

The decisive rule is:

> **Do not let the feeling generated by disagreement decide which side of the disagreement receives scrutiny.**

If discomfort is itself allowed to lower the admission probability of the information causing it, then feeling has not been removed from reasoning. It has been assigned control of the gate.

## 4. A minimal architecture of contact, truth-testing, identity, provenance, probes, and slack

To make the failure mode precise, it helps to keep several variables separate. The vocabulary in this section is adapted from the author's broader Two Layer Identity Correlation Architecture (TLICA), but all terms needed here are defined locally. No prior reading is required.

### 4.1 Contact: $\kappa$

Let $\kappa(E,t)$ measure **present contact** with an item of evidence: roughly, the degree to which the agent is actually causally encountering it at time $t$.

High $\kappa$ does not mean the content is true. A hallucination may be experientially forceful. A live propaganda stream may have high contact. Contact answers only:

> Is this actually pressing on the system now?

### 4.2 Truth-indistinguishability: $\phi$

Let $\phi(E,t)$ denote **toolkit-relative truth-indistinguishability**: how well a content survives the agent's currently available verification procedures.

It is not confidence.

It is not identity.

It is not popularity.

It is not rhetorical fluency.

And when no suitable verification pathway is constructible, $\phi$ may be **undefined** rather than low. Failure to know how to test a claim is not evidence that the claim is false.

### 4.3 Identity-correlation: $\rho$

Let $\rho(E,t)$ denote **identity-correlation**: how tightly a content or commitment is integrated into the agent's experienced self-structure.

High $\rho$ means that revising the content is not represented merely as changing a proposition. It may require changing a story about who one is, who one's people are, what prior sacrifices meant, which authorities were trustworthy, or whether years of confident judgment must be reinterpreted.

Crucially:

\[
\rho \not\Rightarrow \phi.
\]

Identity-correlation is not evidence.

If anything, high $\rho$ is a reason to strengthen anti-bias procedure.

### 4.4 Source-map adequacy: $\sigma$

Let $\sigma$ represent the adequacy of the **source map** connecting a content to its actual provenance.

A person may know the name of a source while misunderstanding the causal route by which the belief formed. Repetition, prestige, social reward, selective exposure, memory reconstruction, and ambient cultural association can contribute to a belief without appearing in the autobiographical explanation of that belief.

Thus:

\[
\text{known source label} \not\Rightarrow \text{adequate provenance}.
\]

A highly coherent belief can coexist with a badly wrong source map.

### 4.5 Probe availability: $\mu$

Let $\mu$ represent the availability and weighting of **discriminating probes**: tests capable of producing different expected outcomes under live rival hypotheses.

A debate is not necessarily a probe. A fact-check is not necessarily a probe. A citation is not necessarily a probe. What matters is whether the procedure can force rival models to make different predictions and whether the outcome can actually change the agent's state.

### 4.6 Slack: $S$

Finally, let $S$ denote a family of **practical margins** within which revision can occur: time, safety, cognitive bandwidth, permission to dissent, social room, emotional capacity, and other non-fungible resources.

Slack is not virtue and not intelligence.

A person may possess the abstract capacity to revise while lacking the local room to do so under pressure.

This distinction matters because identity-loaded dissonance can increase precisely when slack is lowest.

## 5. Felt salience and identity-loaded contradiction

Suppose the current model occupies some baseline state and an incoming observation produces discrepancy $\Delta$. A useful structural hypothesis is that the **felt magnitude** of the mismatch increases with both the size of the discrepancy and the identity weight attached to the affected region.

Write, schematically,

\[
V = \tau \|\Delta\|_{\rho},
\]

where $V$ is felt salience, $\tau$ is a sensitivity parameter, and the norm is weighted by identity-correlation.

This expression should be read structurally, not psychometrically. It says:

> The same factual mismatch can be cheap when peripheral and expensive when it lands on something constitutive of self.

If I learn that I misremembered the capital of a small country, revision may cost almost nothing.

If I learn that a central political belief, religious belief, scientific commitment, moral identity, or personal life-story is badly wrong, the same formal operation — "update the model" — may require reconstruction across a large identity-linked network.

A simple pressure model is then

\[
S = M - P(V),
\]

where $M$ is available control margin and $P(V)$ is pressure increasing with felt salience.

Again, this is a modeling proposal, not a validated scale. The qualitative hypothesis is enough:

\[
\rho \uparrow
\Rightarrow
V \uparrow
\Rightarrow
\text{revision cost} \uparrow
\]

unless compensating slack or procedure intervenes.

This yields an immediate epistemic prescription:

> **The more identity-bearing the claim, the less one should trust introspective confidence that one's evaluation of it was impartial.**

High identity-correlation calls for blinding, rival generation, source rotation, precommitted update rules, and independent probes.

It does not call for greater self-trust.

## 6. The evidence-censoring model

We now isolate the core mechanism.

Let the world have a binary state

\[
\theta \in \{0,1\}.
\]

An agent is evaluating the hypothesis

\[
H: \theta = 1.
\]

At each time step the world emits an observation

\[
X_t \in \{+,-\}.
\]

The observation is diagnostic with reliability

\[
p \in \left(\frac12,1\right),
\]

such that

\[
\Pr(+\mid \theta=1)=p,
\]

and symmetrically

\[
\Pr(-\mid \theta=0)=p.
\]

Thus $+$ supports $H$ and $-$ supports $\neg H$.

Let

\[
\ell = \log\frac{p}{1-p}>0
\]

be the magnitude of the correct log-likelihood update.

An unbiased updater who sees every observation changes log-odds by

\[
+\ell
\]

after $+$ and

\[
-\ell
\]

after $-$.

Now suppose the agent is currently committed to $H$.

Evidence consonant with $H$ is always admitted.

Contrary evidence is admitted only with probability

\[
q \in [0,1].
\]

Thus:

\[
\Pr(\text{retain }+\mid +)=1,
\]

\[
\Pr(\text{retain }-\mid -)=q.
\]

If an observation is admitted, the agent processes it correctly using the true likelihood ratio. The only defect is that the agent does **not** correct for the selection mechanism.

This is a strong assumption in the agent's favor. The inference engine is competent. The sample is the problem.

### Proposition 1: Dissonance-suppression inversion

Assume the true state is $\theta=0$ while the agent is committed to the false hypothesis $H$.

Then the expected change in the agent's log-odds for $H$ per raw world-observation is

\[
\mathbb{E}[\Delta L\mid \theta=0]
=
\ell\big[(1-p)-pq\big].
\]

Therefore,

\[
\mathbb{E}[\Delta L\mid \theta=0] > 0
\]

whenever

\[
q < \frac{1-p}{p}.
\]

Under this condition, the agent's expected confidence in the false hypothesis **increases** as evidence accumulates.

#### Proof

When $\theta=0$, a misleading consonant observation $+$ occurs with probability $1-p$ and is always admitted, contributing $+\ell$.

A correctly disconfirming observation $-$ occurs with probability $p$ but is admitted only with probability $q$, contributing $-\ell$ when retained.

Hence

\[
\mathbb{E}[\Delta L\mid \theta=0]
=
(1-p)(+\ell)+pq(-\ell),
\]

so

\[
\mathbb{E}[\Delta L\mid \theta=0]
=
\ell[(1-p)-pq].
\]

Because $\ell>0$, the expectation is positive exactly when

\[
(1-p)-pq>0,
\]

that is,

\[
q<\frac{1-p}{p}.
\]

$\square$

### Interpretation

The result is elementary and devastating.

The agent can be excellent at arithmetic.

The agent can know Bayes' rule.

The agent can demand evidence.

The agent can correctly update on every observation she allows herself to process.

And she can nevertheless become progressively more confident in a false belief because the admission mechanism selectively removes the observations that would correct her.

The defect is upstream of inference.

A perfect calculator cannot recover deleted rows from a dataset it does not know was censored.

## 7. Why the system feels increasingly rational while becoming less rational

Let each admitted contrary observation generate dissonance cost $d>0$.

Under the false commitment $H$ with true state $\theta=0$, contrary observations arrive with probability $p$ but are retained with probability $q$.

The expected experienced dissonance burden per raw observation is therefore proportional to

\[
\mathbb{E}[D] = pqd.
\]

As $q$ decreases,

\[
\mathbb{E}[D] \downarrow.
\]

So lowering $q$ does two things simultaneously:

1. it reduces experienced contradiction;
2. below the threshold from Proposition 1, it increases expected drift toward the false belief.

This gives the central inversion:

\[
\boxed{
\text{subjective coherence} \uparrow
\quad\text{while}\quad
\text{epistemic calibration} \downarrow
}
\]

A person can therefore use felt coherence as evidence that a belief system is "working" precisely when the mechanism generating coherence is suppressing corrective input.

The less emotional one feels after throwing away inconvenient observations is not, unfortunately, a recognized criterion of successful inference.

### Corollary 1: reasoning power cannot repair unmodeled censoring

Suppose two agents receive the same censored stream. One is a mediocre reasoner; the other is a flawless Bayesian conditional on the observations retained. If neither models the censoring process, superior inference on the retained sample does not restore the missing evidence.

Thus raw cognitive ability and epistemic openness are distinct resources.

This formal point is consistent with empirical work finding that myside bias is weakly related, or in many paradigms largely unrelated, to conventional measures of intelligence, and with political-reasoning studies in which sophistication can amplify selective counterargument rather than eliminate it.

## 8. The "facts over feelings" self-exemption error

The slogan itself is not the problem.

The self-exemption move is.

Consider the following inference:

1. Facts do not depend on feelings.
2. I identify as a person who privileges facts over feelings.
3. Therefore, when I experience a conflict between my beliefs and someone else's claim, my side is presumptively the factual side and their side is presumptively affective.

The conclusion does not follow.

Step 2 is a self-description, not a measurement of evidence-processing quality.

A slogan cannot perform source criticism on its owner's behalf.

The more subtle failure occurs when affect is used asymmetrically.

Suppose the agent treats another person's anger, fear, humiliation, grief, disgust, or attachment as evidence that the other person's reasoning is contaminated.

Now suppose the agent's own aversive reaction to contradictory evidence manifests not as visible emotion but as:

- immediate source dismissal,
- unusually demanding standards of proof,
- rapid counterargument generation,
- topic-switching,
- trivialization,
- mockery,
- selective non-exposure,
- recoding an observation as "anecdotal" only when it is inconvenient,
- or defining the out-group's testimony as inherently suspect.

The affect has not disappeared.

It has changed jobs.

It no longer appears as a proposition like "I feel threatened."

It appears as an **evidence-routing policy**.

The clinically relevant sentence is therefore:

> **If discomfort predicts which evidence is permitted to count, then feeling is governing the epistemic procedure even when the final prose contains no emotional vocabulary.**

The rhetoric "I am not emotional about this" is immaterial.

The test is behavioral:

\[
\Pr(\text{admit evidence}\mid \text{congruent})
\stackrel{?}{=}
\Pr(\text{admit evidence}\mid \text{incongruent})
\]

after controlling for evidence quality.

If not, the asymmetry needs explanation.

## 9. Empirical constraints

The formal result above does not require psychological evidence. It is a result about selective sampling. But if the mechanism is to illuminate human reasoning, empirical constraints matter.

### 9.1 Cognitive dissonance

Festinger and Carlsmith's classic induced-compliance experiment helped establish that inconsistency between behavior and prior attitude can lead to attitude change under conditions where external justification is insufficient. Contemporary action-based accounts treat dissonance as an aversive state connected to cognitive conflict and action readiness.

The relevant lesson here is not that dissonance always produces distortion. It is that inconsistency is motivationally active.

### 9.2 Motivated reasoning

Kunda's influential review argued that directional goals can influence which cognitive strategies, beliefs, and inferential resources are recruited, while remaining constrained by the requirement that the desired conclusion be supportable by apparently reasonable justification.

That constraint matters. Motivated reasoning need not feel like fabrication. The reasoner may genuinely experience the selected justification as the best one.

### 9.3 Biased assimilation

Lord, Ross, and Lepper reported that participants with strong prior positions on capital punishment evaluated apparently confirming studies more favorably than disconfirming studies, producing increased polarization after exposure to a mixed evidence set.

The study is old, limited, and should not be inflated into a universal law. Its importance here is structural: equal contact with nominally balanced evidence does not guarantee equal evidential uptake.

### 9.4 Selective exposure

Hart and colleagues' meta-analysis of selective exposure reported a moderate overall preference for congenial over uncongenial information, with the effect moderated by factors related to defense and accuracy motivations.

This is especially relevant to the present model because Proposition 1 requires no corruption of inference *after* exposure. Biased sampling alone is sufficient.

### 9.5 Motivated skepticism in politics

Taber and Lodge found prior-attitude effects, disconfirmation bias, and confirmatory information seeking in experimental studies of political issues. They also reported stronger polarization among participants with stronger prior attitudes and greater political sophistication.

One should not infer that sophistication is bad. The narrower point is that cognitive resources can be recruited in the service of defense as well as correction.

### 9.6 Myside bias and intelligence

Stanovich, West, and Toplak reviewed evidence that myside bias shows little relationship to intelligence across a range of paradigms.

This is exactly what one should expect if the principal defect sometimes lies not in computational horsepower but in the **objective function and evidence policy**.

A faster optimizer does not help if it is optimizing the wrong loss.

### 9.7 Recent experimental identification

Thaler's 2024 experimental work used information-source veracity judgments to distinguish motivated reasoning from ordinary Bayesian updating and reported evidence of politically motivated belief distortion across multiple contested domains.

The relevance is not partisan. The study is another constraint on any theory that assumes people merely update neutrally on whichever information happens to arrive.

### 9.8 Open-minded information acquisition

Haran, Ritov, and Mellers found that actively open-minded thinking predicted information acquisition and, through information acquisition, improved performance in uncertain estimation tasks.

Kahan and colleagues likewise reported evidence that science curiosity was associated with more open-minded engagement with politically uncongenial information.

These findings point toward a positive counterpart to the self-sealing architecture:

\[
\text{epistemic strength}
\neq
\text{absence of discomfort};
\]

rather,

\[
\text{epistemic strength}
\approx
\text{continued information acquisition under discomfort}.
\]

## 10. Identity-coupling makes the problem worse for principled reasons

Why should contradictory evidence be filtered more aggressively in some domains than others?

Identity-correlation supplies one answer.

Suppose a belief $B$ has high $\rho$. Revision then propagates.

Changing $B$ may require changing beliefs about:

- one's intelligence,
- one's moral history,
- one's family,
- one's political tribe,
- one's religion,
- one's profession,
- one's heroes,
- one's enemies,
- one's sacrifices,
- one's credibility,
- or one's past treatment of other people.

The local proposition may be only one node in a large dependency graph.

The cost of updating is therefore not merely

\[
\operatorname{cost}(B\to B').
\]

It is closer to

\[
\operatorname{cost}(B\to B')
+
\sum_{j\in N(B)}
\operatorname{reconstruction\ cost}(j),
\]

where $N(B)$ contains identity-linked commitments downstream of $B$.

This gives motivated resistance a non-mysterious architecture.

The person need not consciously decide:

> I prefer falsehood.

The system may instead experience:

> That source is obviously ridiculous.

> That study has methodological problems.

> That case is an exception.

> Everyone knows that institution is captured.

> This person is too emotional to be credible.

Any one of these judgments can be correct.

The problem appears only when the criteria are applied **asymmetrically as a function of identity-threat**.

The right test is not whether a skeptical counterargument can be generated. Intelligent people are very good at generating skeptical counterarguments.

The test is:

> **Would the same defect have disqualified evidence pointing in the preferred direction?**

If the answer is no, "skepticism" may be functioning as selective permeability.

## 11. The prerogative of consistency

Any functioning self requires enough internal coherence to act.

Call this structural pressure the **prerogative of consistency**: one's current frame is the implicit standard against which mismatch is first registered.

This is not a moral flaw. Without some baseline, nothing could count as surprising.

The epistemic problem begins when a useful first-pass operation becomes a final court.

The sequence

\[
\text{incoming claim}
\to
\text{mismatch}
\to
\text{felt wrongness}
\]

is normal.

The dangerous substitution is

\[
\text{felt wrongness}
\to
\text{therefore false}.
\]

A disciplined epistemic system inserts a probe between them:

\[
\text{felt wrongness}
\to
\text{hold contact}
\to
\text{test}
\to
\text{update or reject}.
\]

The fact that a claim feels absurd is evidence about the relation between the claim and the current frame.

It is not yet evidence about which one is wrong.

## 12. The closure ratchet

Selective evidence loss has dynamics.

Let $M_t$ be the agent's model at time $t$.

Let $A_t(E)$ be the admission rule determining whether an encountered item $E$ reaches serious evaluation.

Then belief change is not simply

\[
M_{t+1}=U(M_t,E_t),
\]

where $U$ is an updater.

It is

\[
M_{t+1}=U(M_t,A_t(E_t)).
\]

But $A_t$ is itself partly determined by $M_t$.

Thus:

\[
M_t
\to
A_t
\to
\text{selected evidence}
\to
M_{t+1}.
\]

This creates a feedback loop.

A model shapes what counts as admissible evidence; admitted evidence then confirms the model that shaped admission.

If the process is sufficiently asymmetric, the agent may enter what can be called an **epistemic cave**.

A **metastable cave** is closed under the procedures currently deployed but contains latent routes to correction: unused tools, untried comparisons, unexamined sources, or probes the agent could in principle construct.

An **absorbing cave** is stronger: no internal operation currently reachable can generate the comparison required to reveal the frame's inadequacy. Escape requires changed contact, a new tool, an external intervention, or a new channel.

This is why aging, institutional insulation, and ideological homogeneity can become epistemically dangerous without any decline in intelligence.

The world changes.

New people appear.

New practices appear.

New evidence appears.

New technologies alter which assumptions remain valid.

If the model updates, the person remains coupled to the changing world.

If the evidence gate tightens instead, reality increasingly presents itself as degenerate:

- people have become irrational,
- institutions have become illegitimate,
- culture has become incomprehensible,
- experts are all captured,
- ordinary disagreement is corruption,
- every new exception proves the rule.

Sometimes those judgments are true.

But a closed system has a problem: it has lost the ability to tell the difference between

\[
\text{the world is wrong}
\]

and

\[
\text{my model is no longer receiving enough world}.
\]

That is a serious diagnostic failure.

## 13. A stronger definition of "fact-first"

If "fact-first" is to mean anything operational, it should not describe affective style.

It should describe **error-correction procedure**.

A fact-first reasoner should be expected to do at least the following.

### Preserve raw input

Do not overwrite the original observation with the interpretation that made it comfortable. Keep enough provenance to reconstruct what was actually encountered.

### Apply symmetric evidential standards

Before accepting "small sample," "biased source," "correlation not causation," "anecdote," "conflict of interest," or "unreplicated" as a defeater, ask whether the same defect would defeat congenial evidence.

### Predeclare update conditions

State in advance what observation would change the belief.

If no imaginable observation counts, the belief is not presently operating as an empirical hypothesis.

### Rotate sources

If identity predicts which source families are trusted, introduce source blinding where possible.

### Preserve rival models

Do not merely collect objections. Maintain at least one live competing model that makes different predictions.

### Prefer discriminating probes

Ask what observation would look different if the preferred frame were wrong.

### Separate affect from verdict

Record the feeling if useful, but do not let the feeling determine evidential weight.

### Increase procedure as identity-coupling rises

The correct response to high $\rho$ is not greater confidence in introspection.

It is stronger anti-bias instrumentation.

This yields a compact principle:

\[
\boxed{
\text{The more a belief is "me," the less "me" should be trusted as its sole auditor.}
}
\]

## 14. Predictions and falsifiers

The present account makes testable predictions.

### Prediction 1: identity-threat should predict evidence asymmetry

Holding objective evidence quality constant, higher identity-correlation with a belief should predict a larger gap between the treatment of congenial and uncongenial evidence.

**Falsifier:** if identity-correlation has no relation to selective admission, counterargument asymmetry, source discounting, or retention across well-powered designs, the proposed $\rho$ pathway is weakened.

### Prediction 2: self-identification as "fact-driven" should not by itself predict calibration

A declarative identity such as "I care about facts more than feelings" should have weak or inconsistent relation to actual calibration unless accompanied by procedural traits such as active open-mindedness, willingness to seek disconfirming information, or source-symmetric standards.

**Falsifier:** if the self-description robustly predicts superior out-of-sample calibration after controlling for cognitive ability, education, ideology, and open-mindedness, the critique of self-certification would require revision.

### Prediction 3: filtering should reduce felt conflict faster than it improves accuracy

Interventions that allow avoidance of incongruent information should reduce experienced dissonance even when they worsen later prediction accuracy.

**Falsifier:** if avoidance does not reduce conflict, or if conflict reduction consistently tracks improved calibration, the proposed inversion loses force.

### Prediction 4: blind evaluation should reduce identity-linked asymmetry

Removing source identity or ideological cues while preserving substantive content should reduce at least some differential scrutiny.

**Falsifier:** if asymmetry remains unchanged under successful blinding, source identity is not a major route in that domain.

### Prediction 5: reasoning ability and correction ability should dissociate

Higher analytic ability should not guarantee lower myside bias; under some conditions it may improve the construction of defensible rationalizations.

**Falsifier:** a strong, stable, domain-general negative relationship between cognitive ability and identity-linked evidence asymmetry would weaken this claim.

### Prediction 6: slack-restoring interventions should sometimes outperform argument escalation

When contradiction is identity-loaded and practical slack is low, reducing threat, increasing time, permitting uncertainty, or separating public identity from private revision should sometimes improve updating more than adding additional arguments.

**Falsifier:** if such interventions never improve evidence uptake relative to matched informational controls, the slack component is unsupported.

These predictions are deliberately stronger than the prose thesis. A framework that cannot specify what would count against it is merely another way to become comfortable.

## 15. What would count as real superiority?

Suppose two people disagree.

Person A is visibly emotional.

Person B speaks flatly, cites data, and repeatedly says that feelings are irrelevant.

Nothing epistemically decisive follows.

Person B's low visible affect may reflect composure.

It may reflect temperament.

It may reflect social style.

It may reflect expertise.

It may reflect dissociation from the stakes.

It may reflect that the conclusion is identity-congruent and therefore cheap.

Person A's emotion may distort reasoning.

It may also be the cost of permitting painful information to remain in contact.

The valid comparison is not presentation style.

It is performance under adversarial testing.

Who:

- states uncertainty accurately,
- preserves contrary evidence,
- applies standards symmetrically,
- predicts novel outcomes,
- updates when predictions fail,
- distinguishes source criticism from source rejection,
- and remains capable of saying "I was wrong" when identity cost is high?

Those are measurements.

"I am the rational one" is branding.

---

## 16. A note on the apparent paradox

The slogan can now be recovered in a stronger form.

Facts do not care about your feelings.

Correct.

But **your access to facts can care very much about your feelings**.

Your attention can care.

Your source selection can care.

Your memory can care.

Your threshold for methodological criticism can care.

Your willingness to run a probe can care.

Your interpretation of ambiguity can care.

Your social network can care.

Your identity can care.

Your search behavior can care.

Your stopping rule can care.

Your decision about what counts as "just an anecdote" can care.

The independence of truth from affect therefore does not imply the independence of **truth acquisition** from affect.

That is the category error.

Truth is a property of the world-proposition relation.

Objectivity is a property, if anything, of a procedure.

The first cannot be borrowed by merely announcing allegiance to it.

---

## 17. The strongest version of the criticism

The harshest defensible conclusion is not that self-described "facts over feelings" people are stupid.

That claim would itself be lazy.

The stronger conclusion is structural:

> A person can possess high intelligence, strong verbal skill, substantial domain knowledge, and genuine respect for truth while operating an evidence-selection policy that systematically protects identity-bearing beliefs from correction.

When that happens, the person's intelligence may improve the local plausibility of the defense.

The failure is therefore not necessarily lack of reasoning.

It can be **reasoning subordinated to a hidden loss function**.

If the hidden objective is

\[
\min \text{dissonance}
\]

while the declared objective is

\[
\min \text{error},
\]

then successful optimization of the hidden objective can masquerade as success at the declared one.

This is why self-certifying rationalist identity deserves suspicion.

The person who says "I do not let feelings affect my reasoning" has reported an aspiration, not a result.

A serious result would require:

- provenance,
- calibration,
- holdouts,
- symmetric tests,
- adversarial probes,
- and evidence that the procedure still works when the conclusion hurts.

The world is under no obligation to make corrective information emotionally convenient.

---

## 18. Conclusion: keep the pain, test the claim

Cognitive dissonance is uncomfortable because incompatible structures are simultaneously active.

That discomfort can be reduced by learning.

It can also be reduced by deleting contact.

These routes are phenomenologically confusable.

The paper's formal model shows why this matters: selective suppression of contrary observations can produce increasing confidence in a false belief even when the retained evidence is processed with mathematically correct likelihood updates.

The empirical literature does not prove this architecture, but it independently supports its key ingredients: selective exposure, biased assimilation, motivated evaluation, myside bias, and the importance of open-minded information acquisition.

The proper lesson is not to worship discomfort.

Dissonance is not truth.

The proper lesson is to **protect the channel that lets dissonance teach you something**.

A mature epistemic procedure asks:

\[
\text{What, exactly, is the contradiction?}
\]

\[
\text{Which rival models survive?}
\]

\[
\text{What observation would distinguish them?}
\]

\[
\text{Am I applying the same evidential rule in both directions?}
\]

\[
\text{What information did I discard before I began reasoning?}
\]

The final irony can then be stated precisely.

A person may insist that facts do not care about feelings while structuring her entire evidence environment around avoiding one feeling in particular: the felt cost of discovering that the current self-model is wrong.

At that point, the slogan remains semantically true.

The epistemic procedure has simply failed to live up to it.

## Provenance and construct-status note

The paper's use of **contact** ($\kappa$), **truth-indistinguishability** ($\phi$), **identity-correlation** ($\rho$), **source-map adequacy** ($\sigma$), **probe availability** ($\mu$), **slack** ($S$), the **prerogative of consistency**, and the **metastable/absorbing cave** distinction is adapted from the author's Two Layer Identity Correlation Architecture (TLICA). They are restated here so that the paper is self-contained.

The evidence-censoring proposition in §6 is a mathematical result internal to the stated toy model. The mapping from TLICA variables to human cognitive-dissonance and motivated-reasoning mechanisms is a research hypothesis. None of $\kappa,\phi,\rho,\sigma,\mu,$ or $S$ should be interpreted as established psychometric scales without independent operationalization and validation.
