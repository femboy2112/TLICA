## Core result

The paper studies a self-sealing epistemic failure in which an agent can reduce felt contradiction by selectively suppressing disconfirming evidence. In the paper's binary evidence model, a false commitment gains expected log-odds whenever contrary evidence is retained with probability

\[
q < \frac{1-p}{p},
\]

despite correct likelihood-ratio updates on every observation that survives the filter.

The mathematical result is internal to the stated toy model. Its mapping to human cognition is explicitly treated as a hypothesis constrained by literature on cognitive dissonance, selective exposure, motivated reasoning, myside bias, and actively open-minded thinking.
