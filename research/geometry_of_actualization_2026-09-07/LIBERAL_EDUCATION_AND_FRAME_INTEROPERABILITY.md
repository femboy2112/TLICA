# 1. Returning to an existing thread

The author linked plural immersion to Leonardo's range across art, engineering, and inquiry, then recognized the connection with her earlier liberal-education proposal:

> What we are talking about now *is* what the ideal of liberal arts education is supposed to enable in people

This is not a newly discovered absence in the repository. The August 9 note already distinguishes intra- and interpersonal functions of education and proposes shared conceptual infrastructure that makes perspectives more mutually inspectable. It explicitly rejects equating credentials, shared vocabulary, agreement, and truth. [T-R1]

The present extension develops the **inhabitation and transition** side of that account. Possessing a conceptual reference is different from being able to work within a method, sustain it long enough to learn, recognize its limits, and carry something warranted into another setting. Shared cultural references can compress communication; trained cross-frame participation may additionally change what one can notice and do.

The proposal should remain an author-level reconstruction of an educational ideal, not an assertion that every institution or historical defender of liberal learning intended precisely this model. A contemporary institutional description provides partial resonance: Harvard describes broad intellectual grounding and general education addressing questions that do not fit one discipline. That is evidence of a stated educational aim, not evidence that this curriculum produces TLICA's proposed capacities. [T-E1]

# 2. Many subjects are not yet many inhabitable frames

The distinction is between collecting terms and learning practices. Someone may know the words used in several disciplines while applying the same favored explanation everywhere. Conversely, a specialist can become skilled at changing methods and recognizing the limits of a representation. Breadth and narrowness of subject matter do not directly identify flexibility of reasoning.

A disciplinary frame includes more than vocabulary. It can involve what questions are admissible, what counts as evidence, what kind of object is being described, what makes an explanation satisfactory, and how errors are found. A historical inquiry about provenance and contingency is not interchangeable with a mechanical calculation or an artistic judgment about form. Each domain is internally plural; the familiar labels “historian,” “engineer,” and “artist” are convenient examples, not homogeneous mental types.

To inhabit a frame is to become competent enough for its questions and corrections to guide work from within. That requires dwell. Immediate comparison from outside can miss what only sustained practice reveals. But immersion becomes limiting when the frame's success at illuminating one aspect is treated as proof that it exhausts the object.

The educational aspiration is therefore neither uninterrupted specialization nor perpetual perspective tourism. It is **depth with revisable jurisdiction**: let a method do the work it can do, and learn how to recognize when another question or another method is required.

# 3. Leonardo as illustration, not psychological evidence

Leonardo serves the conversation as an emblem of plural practice. The museum catalog records inspected here name drawings combining animal and human heads with astronomy and geometry notes, and another anatomical study of a fetus. These catalog titles provide narrow evidence of a range of subject matter. The accessible pages did not supply enough content for a fresh visual or manuscript-level analysis. [T-A1]

That boundary matters. The presence of several subjects on paper does not establish how cheaply Leonardo changed mental states, how much reflection accompanied a transition, what motivated every project, or why a particular work remained unfinished. Nor does one example identify the psychology of Renaissance people in general. Those stronger historical claims are not adopted.

What the example enables is a design question: what would allow knowledge acquired in one practice to become useful in another without simply renaming it? Anatomical observations might constrain a depiction, or a drawing might make an investigative question easier to formulate. These are intelligible routes to investigate, not a claim that every actual connection has been verified in this session.

The proposed notebook analogy is similarly functional rather than biographical. A persistent external record can hold an observation, sketch, question, uncertainty, and next step so that every relation need not be kept active during every transition. Whether such a record reduces burden or improves transfer is a prospective question. A pile of notes can also become inaccessible, misleading, or never used again.

# 4. Transition is more than escape

A transition between frames can involve at least three distinguishable operations:

**Decoupling:** the prior frame stops having exclusive jurisdiction over what the problem can mean.

**Integration:** the episode leaves an accountable record of what was observed, attempted, learned, and left unresolved.

**Transport:** some structure is re-expressed under the methods and requirements of the next frame.

These need not occur in a fixed order or as separately conscious acts. Some transitions should transport very little. Leaving work for music need not convert the work problem into an artistic object. A useful transition can bracket the old concern without resolving it, provided its status is not falsified.

When transport is intended, however, fidelity becomes a separate objective from ease. A metaphor can feel illuminating while changing the meaning of its source. A physical conservation law does not become a law of motivation merely because both discussions use “energy.” An economic incentive, an ethical reason, and a reported motive cannot be silently substituted for one another.

The relevant questions are: what object is being carried; which relation must survive; what assumptions made it valid in the source; what is different in the target; and what result would show the translation failed? Similarity starts inquiry. It does not discharge those obligations.

# 5. A partial transport contract

A modest representation is

\[
T_{ij}:D_{ij}\subseteq R_i\longrightarrow R_j,
\]

where the source representation domain is restricted to cases for which the proposed translation is defined. This does not claim that human cognition literally implements a mathematical function or that every pair of disciplines possesses a lossless map.

For a particular use, declare a relation-preservation requirement \(I\). The statement that \(T_{ij}\) preserves \(I\) is meaningful only after specifying both how \(I\) is expressed in the source and how it is checked in the target. Carrying a source label is not enough. Some information may be deliberately omitted; that loss must be visible rather than mistaken for a refutation of what was left behind.

A practical contract can record the source object, intended target use, assumptions, declared invariant, known losses, uncertainty status, and a reconstruction test. A receiver or later self should be able to explain the load-bearing relation rather than merely repeat its new terminology.

The word transport here is semantic and operational. There is no declared oscillatory common basis, so no physical phase or phasor claim is licensed. A frame is not presumed to be a vector-space basis. A mapping can be partial, many-to-one, provisional, or inappropriate.

# 6. Within-person and between-person interoperability

The earlier repository note emphasizes how people with different histories can share enough conceptual structure to communicate a perspective faithfully without agreeing with it. This extension places the same concern alongside transitions within one person's activities. [T-R1]

Within-person interoperability asks whether the same person can retain a warranted observation while changing the activity through which it is interpreted. Between-person interoperability asks whether another person can reconstruct the relevant relation without having lived the sender's entire history. Neither requires identical mental states.

The two forms can assist each other. Explaining a problem to someone working in another method may reveal an assumption that was invisible inside the original approach. Conversely, having practiced several methods may make another person's explanation easier to reconstruct. These are hypotheses about learning and communication, not automatic consequences of exposure to many subjects.

Both forms also admit shared failure. Two people can fluently exchange the same misleading analogy; one person can carry the same hidden assumption through several vocabularies. Multiplicity of descriptions is not independence of sources. A theory transported through art, engineering, and philosophy does not acquire three independent validations simply because the presentation changes.

# 7. What an educational practice would need to teach

The proposed capacities are acquisition, sustained participation, suspension, transport, and re-entry. Acquisition means learning a method well enough to avoid caricature. Sustained participation lets it reveal what surface familiarity misses. Suspension limits its jurisdiction without destroying what it established. Transport carries declared structure with visible losses. Re-entry tests whether the result can actually support inquiry, creation, communication, or action.

This is an aspiration, not a claim that a course list already implements it. A useful educational task could ask a learner to solve a problem under one method, reconstruct another account, explain what changes, identify what does not translate, and use the result on a new case. An assessment should reward justified non-transfer as well as successful analogy.

An engineer–historian comparison, for instance, should not reward forcing a historical event into a deterministic mechanism. A science–art comparison should not reward claiming that a formal property proves an aesthetic interpretation. The challenge is to preserve what each account actually warrants while making their disagreement more precise.

External records can support this practice. A handoff note might state: the question; what is known and from which source; what remains hypothetical; why work stopped; what the next frame needs; and what should not be imported. This is the same rationale behind the project's research ledgers, but their actual effectiveness must be checked rather than presumed.

# 8. Failure modes that the ideal must survive

**Breadth without depth** produces superficial analogies. **Depth without jurisdictional limits** can produce unwarranted extension of a successful method. Neither failure belongs exclusively to degree holders, autodidacts, specialists, or generalists.

**Shared vocabulary without reconstruction** produces apparent agreement. **Shared schooling without source diversity** can propagate correlated blind spots. **Fluent switching without consequence checks** can produce rationalization: the person changes frames whenever the current one becomes inconvenient.

**Reflection without re-entry** can make every practical step perpetually provisional. **Re-entry without preservation** can lose the very evidence that motivated the transition. **Permanent transition** can prevent completion. **Permanent dwell** can conceal new requirements. The right balance is not a universal time ratio supplied by the model.

The ideal also has material conditions. Time, instruction, access to tools, disability accommodations, financial security, and freedom to question can affect whether a person gets to practice these capacities. Treating interoperability as an individual virtue while ignoring opportunities to acquire it would reproduce the very access error identified in the agency module.

A liberal education is not reducible to this one purpose. Nor is this capacity restricted to institutions called liberal-arts colleges. Apprenticeship, self-directed study, collaboration, work across communities, and artistic practice may supply relevant routes. Those routes require case-specific evidence; a credential is not the construct being measured.

# 9. Proposed empirical distinction

The most informative test would distinguish learning several vocabularies from learning to preserve relations across methods. A low-stakes study could compare time-matched instruction with and without explicit transport practice, then assess unfamiliar tasks using a rubric fixed beforehand.

Keep source-method competence, target-method competence, transfer fidelity, warranted rejection of a bad analogy, completion, and effort separate. Hold out new domains or problem families. A benefit on rehearsed examples does not establish general semantic interoperability.

The simpler rival is ordinary practice: perhaps any structured comparison or extra explanation produces the same benefit. Another is language fluency rather than transport skill. Another is grading bias toward the preferred interdisciplinary story. The proposed model earns explanatory value only by surviving these comparisons. The protocol is expanded in the companion specification; no such study has been run.

# 10. Candidate passage for the manuscript

Plural immersion extends the liberal-education proposal from a shared library of references to a practiced capacity for inhabiting and leaving methods. The value of learning several disciplines is not merely possessing their terms. It is learning what becomes visible within each, what counts as a correction there, and where its jurisdiction ends. A learner must dwell long enough for a practice to teach her something, yet retain the means to place its results in relation to other questions.

The passage between frames is consequently an epistemic task. It can preserve an observation while changing its use, retain an uncertainty without keeping it permanently foregrounded, or reveal that an attractive analogy does not carry the required structure. The outcome need not be agreement or a single synthesis. Sometimes the achievement is a more accurate account of why two perspectives cannot simply replace one another.

On this interpretation, liberal education supports both an inhabited plurality within a person and more faithful exchange between people. The claim is an educational ideal and research proposal, not a credential-based judgment or a demonstrated law of learning. Its governing limit is the same as in the earlier interoperability note: shared intelligibility is not shared truth. What travels smoothly must still answer to what it came from and what it is being used to claim.
