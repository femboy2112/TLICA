## Central result to preserve

The proposed distinction is **shared machinery, differently organized support**, not a contest between rational labor and authentic art. The same activity may be sustained by present significance, a future outcome, an imagined audience, care for another person, opposition, habit, or several of these together.

The three writers supply nonexclusive lenses:

- Bataille: significance not exhausted by useful future return.
- Dostoevsky's Underground Man: self-definition that may remain dependent on the adversary it negates.
- Wallace: an alternative interpretation becoming live and implementable before selection closes.

Do not make these a single scale or three diagnoses. Other-dependence is not automatically servitude; future orientation is not automatically alienation; present enjoyment is not automatically freedom. These safeguards are central to the paper, not disclaimers to remove during stylistic revision.

## Important refinements already made

Past and future labels refer to contents represented **now**; no retrocausal agency is implied. The self/not-self/neutral display is overlapping bookkeeping rather than a disjoint field ontology. An other can be identity-integrated, and a neutral object can have substantial causal importance.

The support graph distinguishes instrumental expectation, identity integration, opposition, association, normative reason, reported motive, and hypothesized operative cause. Conjunction and redundancy are represented explicitly rather than hidden inside ordinary arrows.

The geometry is relational, with no claimed metric or physical phase. The familiar emotional sequence is a motivating pattern, not a universal law requiring high arousal or a verbal thought. The manuscript preserves the gaps between desire, selection, implementation, and sustained action.

The customer-service report is used to expose causal confounding, not to diagnose the author or vindicate making strangers wait. Counterfactual questions are hypothesis-generating reports, not clean mental ablations.
