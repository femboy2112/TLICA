# Abstract

Two people can undertake outwardly similar actions through substantially different relations to themselves and their world. Conversely, activities ordinarily opposed as self-expression and instrumental labor may use the same architecture of attention, affect, evaluation, selection, and implementation. The present paper proposes that the relevant contrast is often neither an action category nor a new faculty of agency, but the organization of the relations through which an action becomes presently intelligible, attractive, necessary, threatening, or sustainable.

The Two-Layer Identity-Correlation Architecture supplies an indexed conscious field, a historically developed lived-I network, self-representations, identity-correlation, focus, verification resources, and an application-level account of choice as endogenous field reweighting. This paper asks how those resources can describe **actualization**: a presently available orientation becoming operative in an episode of action. Actualization here does not mean moral achievement, discovery of an essential self, or metaphysical realization of all human potential.

Bataille, Dostoevsky's Underground Man, and Wallace supply three contrasting interpretive problems: activity whose significance is not exhausted by useful future return; refusal whose experienced independence may depend on what it refuses; and the timely reorientation of an otherwise automatic interpretation. The proposed analysis places these within one architecture without declaring them identical philosophies, a hierarchy of human worth, or three exhaustive personality types.

A typed, context-annotated support graph makes the proposal explicit. All operative content is indexed to the current episode, even when it represents another time. Temporal reference, referent identity, identity-integration, source status, and relation type remain separate. A nine-cell past/present/future by self/not-self/neutral display can assist exposition but is not a literal partition of the field. Counterfactual questions suggest possible dependencies; only appropriately controlled interventions could establish them causally.

The limited contribution is a research framework: preserve the shared agency machinery, expose proposed differences in action-support relations, and specify observations that could defeat the interpretation. The central empirical hypothesis remains untested. The mathematical examples establish only that identical actions can coexist with different stipulated support structures, and that compliance and contrarianism can both depend on an external signal. Neither result establishes a psychology of freedom.

# 1. The question: what makes an orientation become action?

Consider two motivating examples:

> I am going to paint this thing for eight hours.

> I will put up with exhausting work because it improves my résumé.

A tempting first classification calls the first consummatory and the second instrumental, then treats these as different kinds of agency. The author's correction is more economical: either episode can contain an emotionally significant encounter, a self-congruent orientation, stabilization of attention, and sustained action. What differs is the network of relations through which the orientation becomes significant.

In one imagined case, the present activity matters as an activity. In another, its significance is mediated by a represented future opportunity. In a third, either painting or résumé work matters because it refuses an imposed identity. In a fourth, either activity expresses care for another person. The outward action does not settle which account applies.

The thesis is therefore:

> **An orientation's role in actualization depends on how it organizes relations within the present field, including relations involving the self. Action labels alone underdescribe that organization.**

This is an architectural proposal, not a claim that every human act results from an explicit sentence or an overwhelming emotion. The initiating event might be quiet interest, habit, bodily discomfort, a remembered commitment, an encountered object, or a consciously rehearsed thought. A congruent thought can also fail to produce action. Existing TLICA distinctions concerning live options, capacity, implementation, capture, and ownership must remain available rather than disappear inside the word *resonance*. [T1–T3]

## 1.1 The three writers are interlocutors, not diagnostic categories

This comparison does not identify the author or reader with a fictional character. Nor does it infer a psychological diagnosis from literary recognition. Recognition establishes that a reader finds a resemblance; the mechanism of the resemblance remains a further question.

The three names organize three questions, not three kinds of person:

- **Bataille:** Must an activity receive its justification from a useful return beyond itself?
- **Dostoevsky:** Can an assertion of independence become dependent on the resistance through which it is asserted?
- **Wallace:** Can knowledge of an alternative perspective become operative before a default interpretation closes action?

These are selective interpretive entry points. They neither summarize each writer's entire work nor establish historical influence between them. Their usefulness is that they place different demands on a proposed architecture. [B1–B2; D1; W1–W2]

# 2. What the architecture already supplies

## 2.1 The field contains the self, without making every content self

The existing Wallace application explicitly distinguishes consciousness as the I-with-a-field, the indexed core, the acquired lived-I network, and the operating architecture. Its account does not equate the full field with the identity-correlation profile or with autobiographical self-description. The choice-as-filter application likewise locates choice inside existing focus and selection machinery rather than assigning it to an additional inner controller. [T1–T2]

That distinction governs this paper. Three objects must not be substituted for one another:

1. the indexed subject and its operating organization;
2. the developed lived-I network and its integration relations;
3. particular self-representations occurring within experience.

A thought about being an artist is not the whole person. A projected future self is not a second agent literally reaching backward through time. The field's inclusion of selfhood does not mean every object in it is part of the lived-I network. An identity-neutral tool can matter causally without becoming a constitutive part of identity.

The proposal therefore concerns relations within an indexed field. It does not add a homunculus who chooses which self to be.

## 2.2 Self-congruence is not truth, goodness, or total agreement

In this draft, *self-congruence* is a provisional description of an orientation fitting some currently operative commitments, self-representations, or identity relations. It is not defined as agreement with every element of the person. An orientation can fit one salient commitment while conflicting with another.

For example, a hypothetical person might experience revenge as congruent with refusing humiliation while also judging revenge inconsistent with fairness. The coexistence of those relations is part of the object to explain; one should not erase it by declaring the selected action to have been the person's unique true preference.

Several distinctions consequently survive:

- an orientation may be strongly felt but poorly sourced;
- it may be coherent within a frame but false;
- it may be sincerely owned but harmful;
- it may be reflectively endorsed but not implementable;
- it may be implemented without being the person's endorsed option.

The existing Wallace paper already separates resonance, truth, ownership, rightness, and the availability-to-exercise gap. This draft inherits those limits. [T1]

## 2.3 One coarse sequence, not a universal pipeline

For the motivating examples, write schematically:

\[
\text{encounter or endogenous activation}
\rightarrow\text{affective and interpretive organization}
\rightarrow\text{live orientation}
\rightarrow\text{selection and implementation}
\rightarrow\text{feedback}.
\]

The arrows summarize possible dependencies. They are not a proven fixed sequence, nor a new neural model. A bodily response may precede a reportable interpretation; quiet practiced activity may require little conspicuous arousal; reflection may arrive too late. Sustained action is an extended trajectory, not necessarily one decision repeated unchanged for eight hours.

The relevant explanatory question is not simply how intense a thought becomes. It is which relations keep an orientation available and selected, what constraints permit implementation, what feedback changes those relations, and what could interrupt the trajectory. [T2–T3]

# 3. Temporal and identity-relative organization

## 3.1 Every operative representation is present-indexed

Let \(m\) index the modeling subject and \(t\) the current episode. Let \(\mathcal U^m_t\) denote an accessible field in the sense used by the choice-as-filter application. A content \(q\in\mathcal U^m_t\) may represent a past humiliation, current activity, or future interview. Its **occurrence now** and its **temporal reference** differ. [T2; T4]

Use, for example,

\[
q_t^{\mathrm{future}}=\text{a present representation of a possible future event}.
\]

A present expectation can affect present action. This notation does not assert that the future event itself presently acts on the agent. Similarly, a current recollection is not identical with the event recalled. Temporal reference does not establish memory accuracy or forecast reliability.

This distinction is essential to the résumé example. A represented future employer's approval may presently sustain effort even if that employer will never exist or the anticipated approval never arrives.

## 3.2 The nine-cell display is a view, not an ontology

The author's past/present/future and self/not-self/neutral vocabulary suggests a useful display:

| Temporal reference | Self-related content | Other-related content | Identity-neutral treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Past | remembered commitments, prior self-understanding | remembered encounters and judgments | retained information not currently self-defining |
| Present | current commitments and self-experience | people, institutions, expectations | objects and tasks held in a bounded role |
| Future | projected self-configurations | anticipated relationships and assessments | projected circumstances and outcomes |

The columns are deliberately **not disjoint**. Another person can be represented as an independent other and also integrated into the lived-I network. A neutral object can be intensely attended. A future event can connect to all three columns. Neutrality concerns a relation's identity role, not an object's lack of meaning or causal influence. [T5]

For operational work, therefore, separate at least temporal reference, referent kind, identity treatment, and evidence status. Permit multiple tags, unresolved tags, and shifts over time. Do not infer integration from the noun *self*, or lack of integration from the noun *other*.

## 3.3 A context-annotated support graph

For an episode and target orientation, propose

\[
G_{m,t,q}=(V,E,\ell,\operatorname{src},\operatorname{stat}),
\]

where nodes represent relevant contents or state summaries; edges represent explicitly declared relations; \(\ell\) supplies context tags; \(\operatorname{src}\) records provenance; and \(\operatorname{stat}\) distinguishes observation, report, interpretation, and unresolved assignment.

Useful relation types include remembered association, expected instrumental consequence, identity integration, opposition, endorsement, opportunity, implementation constraint, and experienced significance of the activity itself. These must not be treated as one interchangeable edge weight. A declared association is not automatically a causal mechanism; a normative reason is not automatically an operative cause.

Ordinary edges also underrepresent conjunction. An action may require both resources and an endorsed aim. Where that distinction matters, use an explicit AND gate or hyperedge rather than portraying two jointly necessary conditions as alternative routes. Conversely, several independent supports may substitute for one another. [See the formal companion.]

The term *geometry* means relational organization in this draft. No metric, manifold, physical field, Fourier basis, phase, or measured emotional amplitude has been established. *Resonance* remains a phenomenological term unless an operational quantity is separately defined.

# 4. Bataille: a limit on deferred justification

## 4.1 Textual anchor and scope

The inspected Bataille excerpt contrasts labor organized toward future use with enjoyment whose point lies in the present activity. The publisher places sovereignty within a larger account of expenditure, consumption, and utility. These support an encounter with non-instrumental significance; they do not support reducing Bataille's entire project to a preference questionnaire or a self-help doctrine of authenticity. The available excerpt is mediated by a scholarly database, not a fully collated edition. [B1–B2]

The narrow TLICA question is therefore:

> Can an orientation be sustained without deriving all its significance from a represented useful future return?

The answer is structurally representable without introducing a second agency faculty. A present activity can figure as something worth doing within the field rather than merely as a tool that produces something else. Whether a particular case actually has that organization remains open.

## 4.2 Painting and résumé work, reconstructed

Imagine an episode in which painting is sustained by interest in color, the unfolding gesture, and the experience of making. A future finished object may be represented without being the indispensable reason for continuing. Alternatively, imagine painting sustained almost entirely by anticipated praise, sale, or a portfolio requirement. The behavior is similar; the hypothesized support differs.

Résumé work permits the reverse contrast. A person may undertake it exclusively for a future opportunity. Another may also find present satisfaction in accurately representing their work. Neither account follows from the action's public name.

A useful distinction is between **represented consequences** and **justificatory dependence on those consequences**. Most activities have future consequences. That fact alone does not make their significance wholly deferred. A valued present activity can also require extensive planning, resources, and care for future possibilities.

This avoids a false opposition between future thought and actual living. Planning a shared musical performance need not make the performance an instrument of the plan; the plan may serve an activity participants value in its enactment.

## 4.3 Why present closure is only a diagnostic lens

The earlier conversation proposed *present closure*: support that survives the removal of useful future payoff. Retain this only as a conditional test within a specified model. It is not a definition equivalent to Bataillean sovereignty.

A presently gratifying episode can be habitual, compulsive, exploitative, or dependent on humiliating another person. Conversely, caring for someone or keeping a promise can be future-oriented and freely endorsed. Present-centeredness alone therefore cannot certify freedom, rightness, or an interpretation of Bataille.

Nor should the model treat an internalized audience as absent merely because nobody is physically watching. The absence of observers does not delete remembered judgments, imagined appraisal, or a demand to remain a particular kind of person.

## 4.4 A genuine pressure point for PCE

The author's correction defeats the claim that non-instrumental activity obviously requires new agency machinery. It does not automatically settle every question about preservation. TLICA distinguishes identity integration from preservation-ranking and leaves aspects of action-priority arbitration open. [T5]

One could hypothesize that an expenditure preserves a self whose identity includes generosity or art. But simply redescribing every action as preserving the self that wants that action would make preservation difficult to falsify. The interpretation needs commitments specified before the outcome, independently relevant observations, and possible cases it would not classify that way.

There remains room for tension between the framework and stronger readings of Bataille. Representing expenditure is not the same as demonstrating that a preservation architecture explains everything Bataille calls sovereignty. This draft keeps that question open rather than solving it by definition.

# 5. Dostoevsky: dependence inside refusal

## 5.1 Textual anchor

Part I presents a narrator who takes pleasure in troubling petitioners, immediately complicates his own account of spite, rehearses humiliations, and contests the idea that a person's interests can be completely tabulated. The wall and piano-key passages make necessity and resistance central. Part II, especially the relationship with Liza, prevents this from being merely an abstract celebration of contrary choice: the narrator's recognition-seeking can become domination. These are features of a deliberately unstable narrator, not direct statements of Dostoevsky's endorsed doctrine. [D1]

## 5.2 Refusal can preserve a relation to what is refused

A hypothetical orientation can acquire significance through the chain

\[
\text{represented demand}
\rightarrow\text{experienced threat to self-definition}
\rightarrow\text{oppositional response}
\rightarrow\text{experienced separateness}.
\]

The proposed issue is not that opposition is inherently unfree. Refusing coercion may be a defensible expression of considered commitments. The narrower concern is **opposition becoming the indispensable condition for experiencing oneself as an agent**.

The familiar binary toy model makes one limited point. Given an external signal \(s\in\{0,1\}\), both compliance \(a=s\) and contrarianism \(a=1-s\) vary with that signal. Negation does not remove functional dependence. This proves neither that real resistance is servile nor that independence from all external influence is possible or desirable.

The stronger empirical hypothesis would require evidence that the person tracks the demand even when its content changes, at the expense of their otherwise endorsed aims. That prediction competes with principled disagreement, calibrated boundary enforcement, fatigue, habit, and ordinary preference. It is not established by enjoying an act of refusal.

## 5.3 Reflexivity is not automatically liberation

An orientation can also be sustained or disrupted through recursive self-interpretation: what I intend, how I appear intending it, how another person might judge that appearance, and what that imagined judgment says about me.

In this proposed interpretation, reflexive activity can multiply alternatives without enabling selection. Yet neither the presence of reflection nor a delay in action proves pathology. Deliberation may be warranted; implementation may be blocked by resources; a narrated thought sequence may be retrospective. Distinguish unavailable options, unresolved evaluation, late reflection, intentional postponement, and blocked execution.

The existing Wallace application already warns that more reflective content need not mean greater agency. The contribution here is not to discover that warning, but to connect it to the different support organizations being compared. [T1]

## 5.4 Recognition without identity capture

A reader can recognize enjoyment of local power, humiliation sensitivity, or self-observation without becoming a literary type. A useful question is which relation is operating in an episode, not which fictional identity the person permanently possesses.

The opening narrator also reports contrary impulses and retracts the simplicity of his self-description. That detail matters methodologically: a single trait label can fail even within the text being used as a comparison. No population psychology should be inferred from a novel, and no diagnosis of the reader follows from resonance. [D1]

# 6. Wallace: the alternative must become live in time

## 6.1 Textual anchor

Wallace's 2005 address uses ordinary fatigue, traffic, and shopping to examine automatic self-centered interpretation. It introduces alternative accounts of strangers without presenting them as established facts, and connects freedom with attention and repeated care. The delivered-address transcription and Kenyon's shorter publication are different textual witnesses to the same speech, not independent psychological evidence. [W1–W2]

## 6.2 From an available description to an operative orientation

A person may already know that strangers have lives, constraints, and needs. That sentence may nevertheless be absent from the relations determining the current encounter. The existing Wallace application names the transport problem: relevant knowledge must become operative before selection, or prior learning must already have altered the selection process. [T1]

The proposed geometry makes the contrast visible. One graph organizes the stranger predominantly as an obstacle to immediate aims. Another includes the stranger's possible constraints, the person's own commitment to fairness, and a feasible response. The difference is not necessarily new information about that particular stranger. It can be an altered treatment of uncertainty and an altered set of live responses.

Crucially, an imagined charitable explanation remains imagined. Reorientation must not become a license to replace an unsupported hostile story with an unsupported comforting certainty.

## 6.3 Defaults are not the enemy

The author's earlier Wallace work distinguishes the existence of scripts from lack of authorship over them. The practical target is not perpetual conscious supervision of every action. A practiced response can be efficient, owned, revisable, and humane. A deliberate response can be false or cruel. [T1; T6]

The relevant questions concern revisability, provenance, timing, capacity, and control: can the current route become inspectable; does an alternative become live; can the person act on it; and can feedback revise the route? A failure at one stage is not evidence of failure at every other stage.

This also blocks a sentimental reading in which freedom means cheerfully enduring exploitation. A truth-respecting reorientation may support refusal, collective action, leaving an interaction, or setting a boundary. Considering another person's standpoint does not erase one's own legitimate claims.

# 7. A shared architecture without a hierarchy

| Interpretive lens | Proposed action-support relation | Error the lens helps expose |
|---|---|---|
| Bataillean question | significance not exhausted by useful future payoff | treating every valuable present as merely an investment |
| Underground question | separateness supported through opposition to a represented demand | treating negation as proof of independence |
| Wallacean question | a previously unavailable or untimely alternative becomes operative | treating knowledge of alternatives as their actual exercise |

These rows can overlap within one episode. A person may paint because making matters now, because they refuse a imposed occupational identity, because they hope to sell the work, and because they care about someone for whom it is made. The research problem is to identify which supports are operative, substitutable, jointly required, or misattributed—not to award one label to the whole person.

The comparison is asymmetric in another way. The Bataillean lens asks about the role of useful ends; the Underground lens about oppositional dependence; the Wallacean lens about reorientation and timing. These are not three positions on one axis. A future-oriented act may be reflectively reoriented; a present-valued act may remain oppositional; an other-dependent act may be freely endorsed.

No quantity in this paper should be called a universal sovereignty score, authenticity score, or truth score.

# 8. The customer-service case: why source attribution matters

A motivating report described an employee finding some pleasure in making customers wait, but becoming actively helpful toward customers who remained patient and kind. That report has been abstracted here; it is not a controlled observation or permission to diagnose the author.

Several explanations remain live. The employee might be reacting to genuine disrespect, displacing frustration from elsewhere, testing for deference, responding to interpersonal warmth, defending against an imposed emotional-performance role, or moving between these configurations. Selective helpfulness does not prove that prior obstruction was fair. Nor does a neutral facial expression establish mistreatment.

The central methodological problem is intervention-generated evidence. If the employee creates an avoidable delay and the customer becomes irritated, the irritation is partly a response to the employee's conduct. One cannot simply interpret it as a clean measurement of prior entitlement.

A schematic causal distinction is:

\[
\text{customer circumstances and expectations}\rightarrow\text{observed response}
\]

versus

\[
\text{imposed delay}\rightarrow\text{observed response}.
\]

Both may operate. The observer must account for the second before attributing everything to the first. Patient waiting can likewise have several meanings; it need not certify kindness or freely offered respect.

The appropriate research setting is consensual, low-stakes simulation or careful non-manipulative observation. Do not manufacture inconvenience for uninformed customers as an experiment. Basic fairness should not be conditional on passing an undisclosed interpersonal test.

This case illustrates why distinguishing contact, identity, source adequacy, and justification is substantive rather than decorative. The same visible interaction can sustain rival models, and a preferred model can recruit the very evidence it created.

# 9. What can actually be established?

## 9.1 A conditional representational result

Adding annotations can be conservative if forgetting them recovers exactly the already-admissible TLICA states and transitions. The formal companion states the necessary coverage conditions. This is a conditional property of the construction, not an empirical discovery about minds.

A paper that silently introduces new transitions, a hidden selector, a mandatory emotional threshold, or a new action-priority law has exceeded annotation. Those additions would require separate justification.

## 9.2 A non-identifiability result in a stipulated toy model

Two explicitly specified support rules can produce the same action at baseline and different responses under a specified change. Hence baseline behavior alone need not identify the support rule. The finite demonstration is checkable and useful as a warning against inferring sovereignty, resentment, or ownership from activity names.

It does not establish which rule any actual person follows. Nor does a successful retrospective explanation establish predictive discrimination.

## 9.3 The empirical hypothesis

The main empirical proposal is that a pre-specified, source-audited description of support relations will sometimes predict responses to ethical changes in context better than action class, reported intensity, generic liking, or a simpler account of external incentive.

That proposal remains **CONJECTURED / UNVERIFIED**. No human experiment was performed for this draft. A fitted narrative does not validate its own assignments. Tests must allow the graph model to lose to simpler rivals or fail to distinguish them.

# 10. A research program that can lose

The first task is not to ask people whether they are sovereign. It is to specify an action, plausible rival supports, a manipulation that distinguishes them, and observable outcomes before collecting the result.

For the painting/résumé family, compare activities without presuming their motives. A consensual, low-stakes task could vary whether an external useful reward remains available while measuring continued participation, task switching, stated reasons, fatigue, and perceived opportunity. Never vary access to necessities, livelihood, medication, or other consequential goods.

For the oppositional family, distinguish refusal of a coercive demand from preference for the opposite of whatever is suggested. Counterbalance content and source, preserve the option to decline, and include principled disagreement as a rival rather than treating it as an error by definition.

For the Wallace family, distinguish reflection before selection from the same reflection after selection. Measure whether an alternative becomes live and implemented, not merely whether participants can repeat a desirable explanation afterward.

Each case needs null and positive controls, measurements capable of registering the intended change, and outcomes designated **pass**, **fail**, or **ambiguous**. A manipulation that destroys task feasibility does not isolate justification. A removal of audience visibility does not necessarily remove internalized evaluation. A self-report after the event may be informative but does not uniquely reveal the preceding causal organization.

The companion protocol specifies these restrictions, source-family leave-outs, matched interventions, complete factorial contrasts, and representation-sensitive tests. The current state is a proposal with executable logical controls, not calibrated human measurement.

# 11. Limits and open obligations

**Textual adequacy.** The analysis is deliberately selective. Bataille's fuller relation to subjectivity, expenditure, communication, and sovereignty requires direct edition-level work before claims of comprehensive fidelity. A transcript of Wallace is not a recording, and a narrator's assertions are not the novelist's doctrine.

**Predictive completeness.** TLICA does not acquire a complete action-selection law because this paper draws graphs. The missing empirical mapping from reports and observations to operative relations remains central.

**Representation dependence.** Graph length and path count depend on how descriptions are segmented. Counting more future-tagged nodes can reflect more verbose annotation rather than greater dependence on future payoff. Untested depth scores are therefore withheld.

**Normative incompleteness.** A support graph can explain an action's place in a self without justifying the action. Dependence on others is not inherently subordination; present enjoyment is not inherently good; refusal is not inherently freedom.

**Independent evidence.** Conversation, assistant revisions, and earlier TLICA applications are connected provenance, not independent confirmations. Literary examples chosen because they fit are not holdouts.

**Falsifiability.** If graph assignments are always adjusted after outcomes, the model can become a redescription of whatever happened. Commit to assignments and rivals before test results, compare against simpler models, and retain ambiguous verdicts.

# 12. Conclusion

The author's correction is not that painting and résumé work are secretly the same experience. It is that their difference need not reside in two separate mechanisms of agency. The same operating architecture can support distinct configurations of temporal reference, identity integration, opposition, expectation, present significance, and interpersonal relation.

Bataille, Dostoevsky, and Wallace can then be read as placing different questions before that architecture. Where is significance deferred? What does refusal continue to depend upon? When does an alternative understanding become operative? These questions preserve differences that a flat action label or a single measure of emotional intensity would obscure.

The resulting geometry is neither a new physics of the mind nor a proof that TLICA explains the three writers. It is a disciplined representation of possible relations and a program for testing which of them matter.

> **Same agency machinery; potentially different organization of support. The question is how that organization makes a particular orientation actual in a particular encounter.**
