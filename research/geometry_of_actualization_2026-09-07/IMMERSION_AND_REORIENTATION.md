**Read alongside:** [original manuscript](MANUSCRIPT.md), [original formalism](FORMALISM_AND_PROBES.md), [new probes and source/claim ledger](IMMERSION_PROBES_AND_LEDGER.md), and [integration handoff](IMMERSION_INTEGRATION_2026-09-08.md).

# Abstract

The earlier application asks how differently organized temporal and identity-relative support can sustain apparently similar actions. This extension asks how an orientation becomes sufficiently operative for sustained activity without becoming immune to correction. Its governing contribution is the author's proposal that maladaptive immersion belongs to the same broad family as living absorption, passion, and investment: the contrast concerns the relation between a committed orientation and its context, not immersion versus an ideally detached consciousness.

We distinguish practical priority from epistemic certainty; local performance correction from reconsideration of the governing task or interpretation; self-resonance from source adequacy; and inability to reorient from unwillingness to acknowledge evidence. Recursive reflection can itself become an absorbing, self-protecting activity. Consequently, flow and the Underground are not simply opposite ends of one awareness scale.

The proposed target is **correctable commitment**: an orientation may organize action while relevant doubts remain honestly represented and routes to warranted revision remain usable. Flow is a motivating case, not a certificate of truth or goodness. Passion may sustain a relationship to what matters without guaranteeing that the selected method preserves it. Material constraints can prevent revision even when a person recognizes the problem. Literary comparisons and a small logical counterexample clarify these distinctions; prospective, ethically bounded probes are specified separately. No human validation has run.

# 1. The author's correction: recover the parent process

The author proposed:

> To flow, to live, to have passion. To be invested.

And, more specifically:

> To get lost in the sauce is to partially let go of the underground, enough to give your frame momentum.

The motivating contrast was between flow that preserves the main dish and passion that **attempts to** preserve self-resonance in it. The qualification matters: an attempt can be sincere and fail. This is an author-derived conceptual proposal, not a report of experimentally identified mechanisms.

The strongest reconstruction is not that awareness is bad, that immersion always succeeds, or that the person is innocent whenever context is difficult. It is this:

> **The processes that allow an orientation to become lived rather than merely contemplated may also allow it to become self-insulating. Their evaluation requires the task, environment, available corrections, and consequences.**

This extends the original paper's governing premise that **the field contains the self**. An orientation can be sustained by presently operative self-relations, future expectations, other people, practical constraints, learned skill, or their combination. We need not invent a new faculty for immersion and another for maladaptive absorption. But shared coarse descriptions do not yet prove a single underlying neural or computational mechanism.

The metaphor requires its own source map. The **sauce** is the operative interpretive, affective, practical, and social medium. The **dish** is the declared activity or purpose together with relevant commitments and effects. There is no neutral dish outside every context: what counts as preserving it must be stated and may itself require revision. A service target, a mathematical truth claim, a musical performance, and another person's welfare are not interchangeable objectives.

# 2. Vocabulary and scope pins

| Term | Meaning in this extension | Not licensed by the term |
|---|---|---|
| Frame | An operative organization of what is noticed, expected, valued, and treated as actionable. It can be tacit. | A literal coordinate basis, a complete worldview, or a fully verbal theory. |
| Immersion | Sustained involvement in an activity or interpretation, with alternatives and self-description less foregrounded. | Universal inattention, lack of awareness, or proof of expertise. |
| Frame momentum | Persistence of an operative orientation across an episode; any measurement must specify duration, perturbation, and observable. | Physical momentum, a conserved quantity, or a thermodynamic arrow. |
| Self-resonance | Experienced or hypothesized fit with some presently operative identity relations or commitments. | Truth, moral worth, total inner agreement, or literal oscillation. |
| Correction channel | A specified route by which relevant information can change a prediction, action, goal, or frame. | An automatically accurate sensor or a necessarily external/verbal message. |
| Correctable commitment | Practical priority compatible with honest uncertainty and usable, appropriately timed revision. | Guaranteed correctness, compulsory constant doubt, or endless reversibility. |
| Degeneration | Failure relative to named task, evidentiary, implementation, or ethical requirements. | A mathematical singularity, a person's intrinsic defect, or a clinical category. |
| Underground monitoring | An interpretive label for recursive scrutiny that can inhibit another declared activity. | A diagnosis or a claim that Dostoevsky's narrator does nothing. |
| Context | Material, social, informational, historical, and bodily conditions relevant to the episode. | A residual variable that explains every failure after the fact. |

Here *field* retains the application-level indexed domain of accessible contents and relations, including self-representations. It is not a physical field or an algebraic field. *Geometry* means relational organization unless additional structure is explicitly supplied. No phase, Fourier transport, entropy law, or truth probability is being inferred from the metaphors.

# 3. Practical closure is not epistemic closure

Consider a person who keeps several credible explanations of a problem available. Three different commitments can be made:

**Practical commitment:** act provisionally under one plan because something must be attempted.

**Epistemic commitment:** assign a claim a degree of warrant based on the evidence.

**Identity commitment:** treat maintaining a plan, role, or interpretation as important to being oneself.

These may influence each other, but none entails the others. One can provisionally implement a plan without believing it is uniquely correct. One can find a claim well supported while lacking the means to act on it. One can identify intensely with a practice while revising how it is performed.

A compact distinction is

\[
\text{priority for implementation}
\neq
\text{warrant for belief}
\neq
\text{identity integration}.
\]

This corrects the earlier claim that action requires rival frames to be suppressed until one dominates. Some actions use explicit comparison, several cooperating models, randomized exploration, or continuing uncertainty. The modest point is that limited execution requires some allocation of attention and effort; it does not require psychological certainty or global winner-take-all selection.

For example, a programmer can say: “This cause is not established. I will test it first because the test is cheap and distinguishes it from the rival.” That is movement without corruption of the source map. Treating the candidate as a fact because work has begun would be a different operation.

Likewise, temporarily parking a question is not deleting its evidence. A bounded record can retain the objection, its source, its stakes, and the condition that would reopen it. The proposal does not demand perfect memory. It demands that deferral not be silently represented as refutation or resolution.

The goal is therefore not permanent neutrality. It is the ability to say:

> This is the frame I am acting through now; it is not thereby the whole territory or the last word about it.

# 4. What might give a frame momentum?

The proposed explanation is a family of routes, not a universal sequence. Relevance to a goal, learned routines, emotional salience, immediate reward, social demands, or urgency may make an orientation easier to sustain. Repetition and consequences may then reinforce it. These are candidate explanatory variables; none has been measured in this project.

An episode could have the schematic organization

\[
\text{salient orientation}
\rightarrow
\text{selective practical priority}
\rightarrow
\text{action and feedback}
\rightarrow
\text{continuation or revision}.
\]

The arrows declare a hypothesis to investigate, not a proven causal chain. Low-arousal skill and quiet curiosity must remain admissible; otherwise the model merely rediscovers its own high-intensity examples. Habit need not involve an explicit endorsement. A person can also experience intense emotion without sustained action.

The critical comparison is between two kinds of reinforcement. In one, the activity supplies informative consequences that help regulate performance. In another, successful protection of an interpretation becomes rewarding even when the underlying task deteriorates. The same observed persistence is compatible with either. We must inspect what is preserved, what is sacrificed, and which observations could change the course.

Nor is the underlying system guaranteed non-degenerate in some original or ideal state. Skill limitations, learned defensive habits, fatigue, conflicting commitments, misleading environments, and unavailable alternatives are competing explanations. “Context caused it” becomes useful only after specifying which context and predicting what changes when that feature changes.

# 5. Two scales of correction: the move and the game

The earlier answer described flow as immersion with intact feedback. That is useful but incomplete. Feedback about performance inside a frame may be abundant while information challenging the frame is missing or discounted.

Distinguish:

\[
\text{task correction: how should this move change?}
\]

from

\[
\text{frame correction: should this be the task, rule, or interpretation?}
\]

A pianist may adjust timing without deliberating over every finger movement. That does not establish that the performance serves every relevant commitment. A software team may respond rapidly to failing tests while the tests encode the wrong requirement. A worker may meet a speed target while the target ignores service quality. These are constructed examples, not findings about particular people.

Csikszentmihalyi describes absorption and reduced separation between action and awareness, alongside clear goals and immediate feedback. He also explicitly denies that flow guarantees virtue. Those textual points support keeping subjective absorption, local performance, and ethical evaluation separate. They do not establish the specific two-scale mechanism proposed here. [I-F1]

The key implication is logical rather than clinical:

> **A responsive loop can be highly effective at correcting the wrong variable.**

A useful task-level signal is not automatically an adequate source map of the wider activity. Conversely, some tasks offer delayed or internally evaluated feedback. Lack of an immediate external correction does not by itself establish degeneration. The test is whether the declared evidence and correction arrangements are adequate for the actual claim, stakes, and available alternatives.

This is why the author's “perfectly preserves the main dish” is retained as an ideal contrast, not adopted as the empirical definition of flow. A genuine experience of flow need not be globally correctable. A non-flow episode may be carefully and effectively corrected.

# 6. Passion: investment that attempts to preserve a relation

Passion adds an especially important relation: the activity matters to the person, rather than merely occupying attention. The author's formulation emphasizes an **attempt to preserve self-resonance**. The attempt may concern art, care, discovery, belonging, refusal, or another commitment. It need not concern comfort or a flattering self-image.

Two different organizations are possible. A person may preserve a commitment by changing the means of pursuing it. Alternatively, preserving the present means may become a condition of preserving the commitment. The second makes revision costly, but it cannot be inferred merely from intensity or persistence.

A useful question is:

> Would correcting the method be experienced as serving what matters, or as becoming someone who no longer matters?

An answer is a self-report and hypothesis generator, not a clean causal ablation. The research task is to distinguish method attachment, actual values, realistic costs, and the availability of alternatives.

Vallerand and colleagues' dualistic model is a relevant prior comparison, not an independent confirmation of TLICA. Their original abstract distinguishes autonomous from controlled internalization of a valued activity and associates obsessive passion with rigid persistence. The current proposal should be tested against that established vocabulary rather than claim that flexible versus self-pressuring investment is a new discovery. Only the original abstract was inspected here. [I-P1]

High identity integration does not imply defensiveness. Attachment to accuracy can motivate revision. Commitment to a friend can motivate listening rather than refusal. Conversely, low attachment to truth can make a person casually change claims to fit an audience. Ease of switching is therefore not a sufficient criterion either. What matters is **selectivity of revision in response to relevant evidence and constraints**.

# 7. The Underground is not outside the sauce

A direct textual anchor appears in *Notes from Underground*, Part I, Chapter V. The narrator describes reasons giving way to further reasons, emotion temporarily overriding doubt, and renewed self-reproach undoing that commitment. Part I, Chapter II connects heightened consciousness with both distress and disturbing enjoyment. These are the narrator's self-presentations, not established psychological laws or transparent statements of Dostoevsky's position. [I-D1]

The extension's interpretation is that recursive scrutiny can itself become a committed practice. The person may be inactive relative to one declared goal while intensely active at rehearsing, anticipating objections, protecting independence, or refusing premature closure. “Low momentum” is meaningful only after naming the trajectory being measured.

A possible meta-frame says:

> Acting before every doubt is resolved would compromise my integrity.

That rule might resist correction just as strongly as an unreflective certainty. Another might say:

> Since I can expose a motive behind your kindness, your kindness cannot be real.

The additional explanation need not cancel the initial observation. It may instead reveal mixed motives. Detecting a possible interpretation gives it a place in inquiry; it does not give it jurisdiction over the verdict.

This yields a refinement of the author's insight. Letting go of the Underground need not mean endorsing what one suspects is false. It may mean relinquishing the demand that every warranted uncertainty occupy the foreground before any action occurs.

Reflection and action need not simply alternate, either. Background checks, embodied adjustment, collaboration, and occasional deliberate review can coexist. The problem is not too much awareness as a single quantity. It is whether the way awareness is organized makes relevant correction and worthwhile activity more or less possible.

# 8. Context can close the exit without changing a belief

Suppose a person recognizes a failing procedure but cannot change it without losing essential income. The observation “the person continued” does not identify belief, endorsement, comfort, or inability to notice the defect. A proposed correction may be epistemically available but practically unavailable.

A minimal distinction is

\[
\text{detect mismatch}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{can reorient}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{can implement the alternative}.
\]

An intervention that merely supplies more information may fail because information was never the limiting factor. Another may make a feasible route available without changing anyone's stated values. These are discriminable explanations, not licenses to blame all failures on individual attention or all harmful conduct on institutions.

This matters for the conversation's workplace and “jester” framing. A bounded role can be executed without being treated as a theory of reality. Yet private disagreement does not cancel consequences. A role boundary must still permit information about harm, dishonesty, safety, and legitimate obligations to cross it. Compartmentalization is not a moral exemption, and the jester is a personal metaphor here, not a verified historical account of court life.

A better application-level formulation is **role-bounded participation with truth-preserving records and explicit escalation conditions**. A person may perform a harmless convention while retaining a reasoned objection. Where conduct has serious consequences, a private disclaimer is not enough. The analysis must name the effects, the feasible responses, and the actual risks rather than demand either total assimilation or heroic refusal.

The parallel to the shopping-cart example is structural only. Similar outward behavior can arise from indifference, exhaustion, competing responsibilities, physical difficulty, or a badly designed environment. One observation does not identify the cause. No civilizational-collapse theory is established by this extension.

# 9. Emotional direction: a frame can supply the story's momentum

The earlier conversation supplied another author-derived motivation: emotional investment can make a causal interpretation feel obvious enough that alternatives are not considered. The important correction was that an experienced feeling and a naive explanation of its cause are different objects.

For example, these may all need separate records:

1. A person reports feeling misunderstood.
2. They explain that feeling as another person's refusal to understand.
3. The other person may or may not have received the information needed to understand.
4. A reaction to the person's conduct may itself become evidence inside the original interpretation.

None of these observations automatically decides the others. Clarifying what was communicated can distinguish an information-access account from deliberate dismissal, although the two may coexist. Revisiting the direction of explanation is not reversing time or proving the reversed explanation.

The risky pattern is when the frame generates conduct that changes the response it later treats as independent confirmation. To study that, record chronology and intervention effects rather than merely compare retrospective stories. Also retain the adverse case: sometimes the original account of mistreatment is correct, and reframing would misdescribe it. The proposed method must be capable of reaching that verdict.

# 10. Philosophical placement without forced agreement

The original paper's three interlocutors remain distinct.

**Bataille** supplies the question of whether an activity's significance is exhausted by useful later returns. His work should not be reduced to flow, productivity, or a technique for managing attention. This extension inherits the original package's limitation: the relevant sovereignty passage was accessed through a mediated excerpt, not a collated edition. The publisher's account confirms the broader expenditure/utility setting, not equivalence to our model. [Original sources B1–B2; I-B1]

**Dostoevsky's narrator** supplies the recursive demand for grounds and the instability of letting emotion settle them. The present interpretive development concerns whether refusing immersion can itself become an absorbing commitment; it is not a claim that the novella endorses our remedy. [I-D1]

**Wallace** supplies an ordinary-life challenge: a default interpretation may organize attention before alternatives become usable. Kenyon's published text distinguishes possible explanations of strangers' conduct from known facts and connects attention with practical care. Our extension asks what makes such alternatives usable in time; that control vocabulary is ours, not his. [I-W1]

**Dreyfus** is a supplementary interlocutor, not a replacement for the paper's three-part structure. The inspected abstract of his 2006 *Topoi* paper argues for taking nonconceptual embodied coping and expertise seriously. It motivates questioning whether all action requires explicit representation. It does not settle debates about conceptual capacities, reflection during skilled action, or the correctness of TLICA. The fuller 2005 address was not inspected here. [I-H1]

These sources supply different constraints. Their thematic overlap does not make them independent empirical witnesses to a common mechanism.

# 11. A minimal research representation

For a bounded episode, record the current task, time horizon, declared commitments, available actions, information sources, and correction opportunities. Describe the operative frame without pretending it is directly observed in full. Distinguish reported contents from inferred control relations.

Rather than assign a single immersion-quality score, retain separate observations:

- involvement and intrusive self-monitoring;
- task accuracy and response to task-level discrepancies;
- recognition of frame-level discrepancies and resulting belief changes;
- availability and cost of alternative actions;
- implementation of warranted changes and unnecessary switches;
- identity relevance, welfare, and effects on others.

These are proposed observable families, not validated scales. A study must supply operational definitions, time windows, and measurement limitations.

The TLICA diagnostic vocabulary remains separate: κ/contact; φ/toolkit-relative truth-indistinguishability and pathway state; σ/source-map adequacy; ρ/identity integration or commitment coupling; μ/probe availability; toolkit closure; independence; coherence; and discrimination. None receives a numerical value here. High felt coherence is not a high truth probability. A pathway gap is not falsehood. Strong self-resonance does not resolve σ. A lack of accessible correction is not proof that no correction exists.

Especially important: when task feedback stays unchanged but the task's relation to a wider commitment changes, the proposed failure can sit in the **mapping from feedback to purpose**, not in sensory contact. Adding more of the same feedback would then miss the issue. That is a hypothesis about source-map adequacy to test, not a conclusion extracted from the symbol σ.

# 12. A small logical counterexample: local success is insufficient

The following is a stipulated example, not a psychological experiment.

Let an episode have two recorded components:

\[
(s,h)\in\{0,1\}\times\{0,1\},
\]

where \(s=1\) means the declared local target was met and \(h=1\) means a declared wider constraint was violated. Suppose the available task signal is

\[
p(s,h)=s.
\]

Then

\[
p(1,0)=p(1,1)=1.
\]

A procedure observing only \(p\) cannot distinguish those two states from that observation. If preserving the dish requires both local success and no violation, the target set is

\[
D=\{(1,0)\}.
\]

Observing \(p=1\) does not establish membership in \(D\).

**Proof:** The observed value has both \((1,0)\) and \((1,1)\) in its preimage, but only the former belongs to \(D\). Therefore the observation alone underdetermines the declared requirement. No statistical or psychological premise is needed.

This shows why locally corrective immersion need not preserve the full declared task. It does not show that any person actually ignores the second component. If other available probes disclose \(h\), the information obstruction may be repaired. If \(h\) is genuinely unmeasurable, the right result is limited certification, not fabricated safety.

A second stipulated observation is equally important. Both a person who rejects the current procedure and a person who endorses it may perform the same action when the available action set is a singleton. Behavioral agreement then does not identify belief. In actual cases, the available set must be investigated rather than assumed.

# 13. Correctable commitment, not permanent self-surveillance

The desired organization can be described as entry, execution, monitoring, review, and re-entry, with a real option to pause. This is a design sketch rather than a mandatory serial cycle. Review can occur inside the activity; a collaborator or a tool can support it; and the right outcome can be to continue unchanged.

Three constraints prevent the proposal from becoming a new purity demand.

First, **not every discrepancy warrants a full frame switch**. Noise, mistakes, and irrelevant pressure exist. A review can reaffirm the frame on better evidence. Measure false alarms as well as missed corrections.

Second, **the relevant time scale varies**. Immediate hazards and irreversible actions demand different safeguards from a reversible sketch. A practice with delayed feedback may need external checkpoints. There is no universal optimal review interval supplied here.

Third, **monitoring itself has costs and prerequisites**. More reminders can interrupt effective work without improving discrimination. A person with no feasible alternative may need resources or institutional change rather than more introspection. The proposal must permit those results.

The paper's practical principle is therefore:

> **Let an orientation become operative without granting it immunity. Preserve consequential information without requiring every question to remain foregrounded.**

This is not a claim that every context offers such an arrangement. Where adequate correction or safe alternatives are unavailable, the limitation belongs in the result.

# 14. Candidate passage for the application paper

Immersion should not be treated as the negation of reflective integrity. The same narrowing that enables a practice to become lived can, under different conditions, protect an interpretation from its consequences. The issue is not whether the agent has ceased narrating every move to herself, but what remains able to correct the movement. Some corrections concern execution within a task; others concern the task's relation to the purposes and people it affects. Success at the former does not certify the latter.

The Underground offers a further complication. Refusing every provisional closure can become a closure of its own: a commitment to withholding action until no intelligible objection remains. The agent has not necessarily escaped immersion. She may have become absorbed in the work of making every commitment unavailable. Conversely, passion need not secure itself by refusing revision. A person can preserve what matters by changing the method through which she pursues it.

The proposed distinction is thus not awareness against life, nor authenticity against the social world. It is between organizations of commitment with differently available correction, exit, and implementation pathways. An orientation can receive practical priority without receiving final epistemic authority. A context can deny a workable exit even after the limitation of that orientation has become clear. What we call getting lost is consequently not identifiable from absorption alone. We must ask which activity is moving, what gives it continuity, which signals can revise it, and what the person can actually do when revision becomes warranted.

**The target is not to remain outside every frame. It is to inhabit a frame without losing the conditions under which it can responsibly be reconsidered.**
