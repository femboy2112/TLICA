**Status:** Exploratory comparative research paper. Public statements are source-grounded; psychological mechanisms and person-level prevalence claims are not established. This is an application of TLICA vocabulary, not an amendment to its frozen foundation.

**Companions:** [sources and claim ledger](SOURCES_AND_CLAIM_LEDGER.md), [discriminating probes](PROBES_AND_LIMITATIONS.md), and [integration and validation](INTEGRATION_AND_VALIDATION.md). Bracketed source identifiers refer to the ledger.

# Abstract

The most instructive failure of a capable reasoner is not necessarily a lapse into obvious irrationality. A competence can remain effective at its original task while being credited with authority over a different task. This paper develops that distinction through Christopher Hitchens, Daniel Dennett, Richard Dawkins, and Sam Harris: the public intellectual association conventionally called the Four Horsemen. The initial hypotheses locate characteristic hazards in an argumentative arena, a procedure of reconstruction, an intellectual self-conception, and a dominant explanatory model. Research, however, does not support four equally secure psychological portraits. Hitchens supplies a documented conjunction of source error and public correction. Dennett's alleged charitable false balance remains unestablished; a separate, explicitly contested methodological dispute provides a more defensible object of analysis. Dawkins supplies a public distinction between logic and emotion, but also an important counterexample to blanket incorrigibility. Harris's exchange with Bruce Schneier exposes a comparatively well-specified disagreement about transporting threat attribution into operational security policy.

The resulting thesis concerns **warrant substitution**: treating success under one acceptance criterion as sufficient warrant under another. Persuasion is not verification; faithful reconstruction is not equal credibility; generating a rationale is not independently checking it; identifying a relevant cause is not validating a policy. TLICA helps distinguish contact, toolkit-relative verification access, source attribution, identity integration, and probe availability without reducing them to a single score. James Baldwin supplies a literary comparator for understanding without epistemic equalization, not an experimental control. The paper ends with falsifiable research designs and an audit of its own initial overclaims.

# 1. What is being examined

The association has a concrete historical referent: the four recorded a discussion in 2007, subsequently published as a book in 2019. That establishes a shared public object, not a homogeneous doctrine or four interchangeable minds. [P-01]

“Post-mortem” here names a retrospective examination of that intellectual formation and of a conversation about it. It does not announce the death of every participant, the termination of atheism, or the failure of every argument associated with New Atheism. Nor does a mistake about rhetoric, consciousness, or public policy decide whether a particular religious claim is true. The paper's target is an inference practice, not an identity category.

The motivating conversation proposed four diagnoses. Hitchens could mistake the felt success of debate for successful contact with reality. Dennett could become trapped within procedures of reconstruction that grant an opponent excessive standing. Dawkins could trust intellectual rationalization because being an intellectual had become a central self-commitment. Harris could correctly notice the importance of religious ideas but remain too tightly committed to a model centered on them. These are useful search hypotheses. They are not findings merely because they form a memorable sequence.

The initial conversation also contained favorable portraits: moral courage, explanatory reach, charitable understanding, and willingness to examine difficult questions. Those appreciations motivate the inquiry, but this research does not establish who learned courage earliest, who was most honest, or who best integrated different domains. A comparative biography would require a much larger and differently selected evidential base.

This is a **purposive, exploratory comparison**, not a natural experiment. Examples were sought after the hypotheses had been proposed. Consequently, the inspected material belongs to the development set. It cannot simultaneously serve as a fresh holdout. The relevant unit is an identifiable argumentative episode: a proposition, its supporting material, an objection, and the response to that objection. The person is not the unit of truth.

A primary text establishes what its author wrote or reported. It does not by itself establish the accuracy of the report, the author's hidden motives, or the typicality of the episode. An opponent's criticism establishes a live challenge, not that the challenge wins. This distinction is particularly important when the proposed explanation concerns another person's self-conception.

# 2. From inhabited frames to warrant substitution

The recent TLICA work uses **sauce** neutrally: an inhabited organization of attention, significance, expectation, feeling, and action. Immersion is not itself failure. A researcher can remain inside a demanding project for a long time without becoming captive to it. The relevant distinction is whether warranted correction or departure remains available. The related Underground metaphor concerns self-sealing reflection, not reflection as such and not a clinical classification of people. [CTX-02]

For this paper, a frame is **captured** only relative to a specified correction opportunity: an objection that should alter the representation or decision is repeatedly absorbed without an adequate answer. Merely persisting, speaking passionately, or rejecting a weak criticism does not meet that condition. Where the correction opportunity has not been established, capture remains a hypothesis.

**Warrant substitution** is our descriptive term for the following move:

> A result passes criterion A; that success is treated as though it also passed criterion B, without checking the bridge between them.

The bridge can sometimes be justified. A reliable domain expert's judgment may legitimately carry weight. A carefully moderated debate may expose a decisive contradiction. A useful scientific model may support a policy. The mistake is not using such results; it is treating the missing additional conditions as automatic.

The four proposed substitutions are:

| Site | Legitimate achievement | Unlicensed promotion |
|---|---|---|
| Arena | Persuading an audience or defeating an objection | Establishing the larger claim as true |
| Procedure | Reconstructing a position accurately | Granting equal credibility, or exhausting the subject matter |
| Self | Producing an intelligible argument | Treating the producing self as an independent verifier |
| Model | Finding a relevant explanatory variable | Establishing causal dominance or operational adequacy |

These are neither exhaustive categories nor mutually exclusive personality types. An exchange can instantiate several. Calling them four locations is a design choice for analysis, not a discovery that human reasoning has exactly four failure mechanisms.

TLICA supplies distinctions needed to keep the analysis honest. **Kappa** concerns present contact; **phi** concerns truth-indistinguishability relative to available tools and pathways; **rho** concerns historical identity integration. Confidence, eloquence, and emotional intensity do not directly measure any of them. A reconstruction of provenance is fallible: the investigator's attribution is not transparent access to the actual source map, sigma. Probe availability, mu, is also separate from a probe's diagnostic power. [CTX-01]

Accordingly, a document can be source-resolved in the narrow sense that we know who wrote a sentence while the sentence's truth remains unresolved. A compelling public performance does not establish high phi. An inaccessible discriminator does not establish falsehood. No numerical kappa, phi, sigma-adequacy, rho, or mu values are assigned to these thinkers. Nothing in this paper turns coherence into a truth probability.

# 3. Hitchens: the arena and the possibility of repair

## 3.1 A case that cuts both ways

In his 2007 tour account, Hitchens retracts the false story about Orthodox Jewish intercourse through a sheet and promises a reprint correction. He also describes audience enthusiasm. [H-01]

This is a better starting point than a compilation of celebrated rejoinders. The error is identifiable, and its acknowledgment belongs to the same authored record as the presentation of rhetorical success. But the article does not establish that audience reinforcement caused the original error. It also does not independently verify that every subsequent edition was corrected. Those are separate investigations.

The episode supports a narrower research question: **which kinds of correction remain effective when a person is highly successful in an argumentative arena?** Local correction of a factual detail may be easier than reconsideration of the frame that made the detail attractive. That difference is plausible, but this case alone does not establish it.

## 3.2 Winning answers a different question

Consider a deliberately constructed debate. One speaker correctly identifies a contradiction in the opponent's defense of proposition Q. The audience cheers. There are at least three possible worlds consistent with that response: Q is false; Q is true but badly defended; or Q is partly true while the defense confuses its scope. Defeating that defense therefore does not, without further premises, distinguish these worlds.

This is not a claim that debate never supplies evidence. The contradiction may directly refute a carefully specified proposition. The crucial question is the target: a particular sentence, a supporting argument, a person's competence, an institution's conduct, or an entire worldview. Moving among those targets without notice is precisely the bridge failure.

The phenomenological hypothesis is that audience response can be experienced as confirmation broader than its evidential reach. Applause is a real event. Its interpretation as confirmation is the extra step. Contact with the social response can be strong while the attribution of what that response verifies is poor. TLICA makes this distinction available without pretending that the strength of an experience settles its source.

A hostile test must preserve the argument while changing the presentation, and preserve the presentation while changing a checkable premise. If confidence tracks delivery after premise quality is controlled, that would support an audience-level mechanism. It would not retrospectively reveal what Hitchens privately felt during a particular exchange. An archival study of Hitchens requires a different outcome: how he actually used audience response or rhetorical defeat as evidence in subsequent reasoning.

## 3.3 The correction must not disappear from the portrait

Hitchens's waterboarding account distinguishes his recollection from an observer's report and calls the practice torture. It records an encounter capable of challenging an argument, not independently established evidence of a previously pro-torture position. [H-02]

The positive lesson is not that bodily intensity guarantees truth. A vivid encounter can itself be misinterpreted. The useful structure is exposure to something capable of disrupting an argument, followed by attention to discrepancies within one's own account. Nothing here recommends dangerous reenactment as a research method.

**Verdict:** source failure and public correction are observed in an authored record. Arena-driven confidence inflation is a plausible mechanism, not an established predominant cause of Hitchens's errors. His chapter must preserve both propositions. The proposed discipline is to state exactly what was defeated, what was learned, and what remains untested after the applause ends.

# 4. Dennett: understanding is not equalization, but the charge needs evidence

## 4.1 The logical point survives a weaker attribution

The motivating objection to charitable reconstruction is sound as a conditional warning. Accurately representing a position does not determine how much evidence supports it. Neither does understanding why a person holds a belief make the belief as credible as its rival.

Suppose a participant reconstructs two incompatible hypotheses flawlessly. One hypothesis predicts a previously withheld observation; the other contradicts it. Fairness of representation does not erase the difference. Conversely, accurately reconstructing the weaker hypothesis can make its weakness easier to establish. Charity can improve discrimination rather than frustrate it.

There is a second danger: reconstructing a stronger claim than the person actually advanced and then attributing that improvement back to the original speaker. We call this **reconstruction drift**. The improved candidate may deserve consideration, but it needs a new label. A successful repair is not evidence that the unrepaired argument was adequate.

These points do not yet establish a Dennett failure. Dennett explicitly rejects sophisticated theology he regards as indefensible. [D-01] The picture of a thinker committed to equal credibility for every understood position is therefore not a reliable starting assumption. The conversation's Rapoport-rule hypothesis remains an attribution to test, not a settled characterization. The complete book chapter was not checked in an authenticated primary edition during this research pass; this paper does not pretend otherwise. [D-04]

## 4.2 A different, documented dispute

A more substantial dispute concerns **heterophenomenology**: how to treat reports about experience within an inquiry that does not simply accept every report's interpretation as incorrigible. Dan Zahavi argues that Dennett misrepresents philosophical phenomenology, including by associating it too closely with naive introspection. His criticism challenges the fidelity of the reconstruction and the purported neutrality of the method. [D-02]

Dennett's published reply says several proposed alternatives already fall within his methodology. He denies that it assumes subjects are necessarily deluded. He also acknowledges that an insufficiently explicit example might conceal a serious flaw. These are relevant defenses and concessions, not details to omit to preserve our preferred typology. The accessible publisher notes, rather than the entire subscription article, support this account. [D-03]

The live disagreement is therefore not simply “Dennett understands opponents too generously.” It concerns whether a method has retained the distinctions necessary to answer its opponents, or redescribed them in terms that make their challenge disappear. That is a different hypothesis. Substituting it silently for the original charge would be the paper's own source-map error.

## 4.3 The potential Underground mechanism

Here is the general procedural danger, stated independently of the contested attribution. A method M translates every objection into its own vocabulary. If translation preserves the objection's load-bearing distinctions, that may be a genuine achievement. If the translation removes exactly the distinction under dispute, the apparent answer is circular. The method has shown that it can process its inputs, not that it has preserved its target.

The discriminator is not whether an opponent feels dissatisfied. An opponent can demand an incoherent or unjustified distinction. The test is whether the proposed reconstruction accurately states the distinction, gives reasons for retaining or rejecting it, and addresses consequences that differ between the rival accounts. Neither the author's confidence nor the critic's refusal to be satisfied is a verdict by itself.

This is the defensible connection to the Underground: inquiry can become self-sealing through repeated redescriptions that lack an independently specified stopping or correction condition. But persistence in methodological disagreement is not enough to demonstrate that mechanism. The Dennett-Zahavi disagreement supplies material for a fidelity audit; the present paper does not settle consciousness or classical phenomenology.

**Verdict:** the general distinction between understanding and equal credibility is secure. Dennett's characteristic false balance is unestablished. A separate allegation of method-relative closure is documented as a contested philosophical issue, not proved as a psychological diagnosis. The correction is twofold: reconstruct without equalizing, and demonstrate what a reconstruction preserves before claiming that it resolves the dispute.

# 5. Dawkins: a correct logical point can leave another question unanswered

## 5.1 An observable argument rather than an inferred personality

In his July 2014 essay on emotional no-go areas, Dawkins defends the distinction between judging one wrong worse than another and endorsing the lesser wrong. He uses disputed examples involving sexual violence, differentiates misunderstanding his logical point from objecting to his choice of example, and presents the controversy as a problem of emotional restrictions on rational discussion. He also says participation in painful hypotheticals should not be compelled. [W-01]

The elementary logical distinction is valid. If both actions are wrong, ranking their severity does not make the less severe action permissible. A criticism that attributes that implication to him would need correction. A paper interested in his mistakes must not manufacture a logical error where the stated inference is sound.

However, the validity of that distinction leaves further questions open. Is the ranking itself warranted? Does the public context make the example misleading? What precisely was the critic objecting to? Was the contested message received as a hypothetical, a substantive assessment, or a rhetorical provocation? Those are not all questions of formal entailment. A person can accept the logical distinction and still raise an intelligible objection elsewhere.

The interesting possible failure is therefore **scope inflation**: a correct answer to one objection is treated as answering every important objection to the utterance. The author's acknowledgment of different kinds of criticism is counterevidence to the crudest version of that hypothesis. A serious follow-up must examine how he handles each kind, not simply count references to reason or emotion.

## 5.2 What an identity explanation would require

The motivating intuition was stronger: Dawkins may treat his intellect as part of who he is and therefore trust an internally generated rationale too readily. That hypothesis could explain selective resistance to correction. But a public essay cannot by itself distinguish identity protection from impatience, audience selection, a reasonable defense against misquotation, disagreement about communication norms, or genuine error by a critic.

TLICA's identity coordinate cannot be assigned by reputation. Nor does the absence of an explicit admission prove that no revision occurred. The observable target would be a pattern: criticisms addressing the competence of the reasoner are answered differently from equally strong criticisms of a local claim, with domain, evidence quality, and the actual availability of a response controlled as far as possible.

The distinction between generation and verification remains useful even when the identity explanation is unavailable. A person can check their own argument, and such checking can uncover real errors. What it cannot automatically provide is an independent witness. Independence depends on whether the check shares the assumptions, source selection, and failure modes that produced the argument. Repeating the same reasoning more confidently does not diversify its provenance.

Likewise, expertise can rationally influence an initial expectation of reliability without becoming a universal credential. The needed question is not whether an evolutionary biologist may discuss moral philosophy. It is which inference is being made, what competence it requires, and how the inference is checked in that setting. Disqualifying people by profession would merely invert the prestige error.

## 5.3 A genuine counterexample to blanket rigidity

In his 2008 Edge contribution, Dawkins reports changing his assessment of the handicap principle after Alan Grafen's mathematical treatment. He explicitly contrasts the limits of verbal argument with the model that persuaded him. This is an authored report of intellectual revision, not proof that all his later judgments were well calibrated. [W-02]

The counterexample rules out the lazy portrait of a man categorically incapable of revising a cherished view. It does not settle the more selective hypothesis of different correction thresholds across domains or identity-relevant disputes. That surviving hypothesis needs comparative evidence, not a more elaborate story about pride.

**Verdict:** the public logic-and-emotion framing is observed; its adequacy for every criticism is not established. Intellectual-identity coupling remains conjectured and unmeasured. The useful discipline is to ask which objection has actually been answered, and whether the verification route differs meaningfully from the route that generated the answer.

# 6. Harris: the distance between an explanation and a decision rule

## 6.1 Recover the object before criticizing it

The motivating conversation used “Islamophobia” and a simplified picture of Muslims being treated as inherently dangerous. Those cannot substitute for identifying a claim. Criticism of a doctrine, attribution of a specific violent intention, estimation of population risk, and a policy imposing unequal scrutiny are distinct objects. Their ethical and evidential burdens differ.

Harris's April 2012 essay advocates differential airport scrutiny using Muslim identity or apparent possible Muslim identity as a broad guide; the proposed category includes himself. [S-01] In his later Salon-interview text, he distinguishes nominal assent to doctrines from beliefs that actually organize a person's life. [S-04] A faithful critique must preserve these qualifications rather than assigning him a simple essence theory about every Muslim.

This paper also does **not** establish that Islam is “more violent in general.” A textual comparison would need declared corpora, interpretive rules, a unit of analysis, and a comparator. A behavioral comparison would additionally need denominators, populations, contexts, and outcome definitions. The intuition that doctrinal differences can matter supplies none of those measurements by itself.

## 6.2 An unusually useful confrontation with an alternative frame

Harris published a guest objection by Bruce Schneier and then an extended exchange with him. The invitation is itself evidence against total avoidance of opposing expertise. [S-02]

Their May 2012 exchange contains Harris's explicit distinction between reversed conditionals and his base-rate example. Schneier questions proxies, costs, and operational errors. Harris defends intuitive recognition and extends the comparison to intelligence work; Schneier restricts his conclusion to checkpoints. Harris also acknowledges that most profiled people are not terrorists. [S-03]

The exchange is jointly edited public correspondence, not a recording of unfiltered cognition. Schneier's professional authority is not a substitute for checking his analysis. Nevertheless, the exchange makes several otherwise hidden assumptions available for examination.

## 6.3 Four questions that cannot be collapsed

Consider an abstract population and a rare harmful event T. Let G denote membership in a group, O an observable cue, and A a decision rule. The following questions are different:

1. What explains the production of T?
2. What is the association between G and T in a specified population?
3. How reliably does O identify a decision-relevant condition?
4. Does acting on O through A improve the chosen outcomes under actual constraints?

These distinctions do not require denying the causal importance of beliefs. A doctrine could influence some actions while the publicly visible category used to identify adherents is a poor proxy for the relevant operative belief. Even an informative proxy might not justify a particular use once errors, opportunity costs, and rights constraints are included. Conversely, rejecting one proposed classifier does not prove that every form of targeted investigation is useless.

For conditional probabilities with nonzero denominators,

\[
P(T\mid G)=\frac{P(G\mid T)P(T)}{P(G)}.
\]

That identity prevents a simple inversion. It does not by itself decide a screening policy. A small probability can matter when consequences are large; a high relative risk can still yield a poor operational rule; additional information can improve a decision under some costs and worsen it under others. Both blanket approval and blanket rejection can outrun what the displayed quantities establish.

Here is a finite counterexample to the claim that knowing the group composition of harmful cases determines the value of a proxy. The numbers are invented, not estimates about any religious group. In both constructed worlds, a population of 10,000 contains ten harmful cases, all within a group of 100. In world A an observable flag identifies precisely that group: ten true positives and ninety false positives. In world B the flag selects 100 harmless members outside the group: zero true positives and 100 false positives. The group association is identical; the observable's performance differs.

Under the additionally invented utility of twenty units per true positive and a cost of one unit per flagged case, the two rules score 100 and minus 100 relative to flagging nobody. Neither rule is being compared with all feasible alternatives. The example establishes only that group association does not identify proxy utility. The accompanying exact-arithmetic checks verify this finite construction; they do not measure security, Islam, or Harris.

## 6.4 A real empirical rival, with its own limits

Ginges, Hansen, and Norenzayan's 2009 article reports a distinction between prayer frequency and collective religious attendance in predicting support for suicide attacks. Their 2010 response acknowledges that the original work did not directly test specific beliefs such as an afterlife. That limitation matters: a general devotion measure is not interchangeable with every operative doctrine. [E-01] [E-02]

This literature makes alternative decompositions available. It does not establish that beliefs are causally irrelevant, that collective rituals always cause violence, or that survey support measures actual participation in an attack. The research team and its subsequent reply form one linked evidential family, not two independent replications. In this pass the 2009 abstract and the 2010 full response were inspected; participant-level data were not reanalyzed.

For our purposes, the lesson is a requirement on the model: specify what distinguishes belief content, commitment to a group, social organization, and background conflict. A theory can discover a neglected variable and then overstate how much it explains. The opposite theory can discover confounding and then prematurely exclude the original variable. Neither move is licensed by the discovery alone.

## 6.5 Where the criticism lands

The strongest criticism developed here is **unverified operational transport**, not an allegation about an inherent Muslim essence. It concerns movement from a plausible explanatory concern to confidence in a particular way of sorting and acting on people. The debate's recurring disagreement about scope makes that movement inspectable. Whether it establishes an enduring psychological model lock remains a separate, unmeasured question.

The ethical issue should also remain visible. An evidentially weak association can place burdens on innocent people. Rejecting dehumanization does not require pretending all doctrines are identical. Respect for persons does not require equal confidence in propositions. The paper adopts both commitments rather than using either to erase the other.

**Verdict:** a concrete, sustained dispute about operational warrant is observed. The proposal's performance is not demonstrated by the inspected debate. Neither blanket personal bigotry nor the general superiority of every competing security policy is established. The discipline is to demand a validated bridge from explanatory variable to observable indicator to justified action.

# 7. Baldwin: a comparator, not an immunity certificate

In “A Letter to My Nephew,” Baldwin interprets racist belief through history, fear, and threatened identity while refusing its judgment of Black inferiority. He also directs the reader toward lived testimony rather than treating his own voice as sufficient. The text combines an effort to understand with a refusal to equalize what is understood. [B-01]

That combination is the aspect relevant here. It offers a literary example of a distinction the paper needs: a person's belief can become intelligible without becoming true, and a person's humanity can remain nonnegotiable without their explanation becoming credible. This is a reading of a particular text, not evidence that Baldwin always exercised superior calibration.

Calling him “the control,” as the initial discussion did, was incorrect. No matching, randomization, common measurement, or independent comparative sample has been supplied. Nor should the paper use admiration for Baldwin to excuse selective quotation or the attribution of hidden motives. He belongs inside the same evidential discipline.

A further correction to the earlier slogan is necessary. “Preserve the basis, not the posterior” could sound like preserving a speaker's assumptions even when those assumptions are false. What should be preserved is the **faithful representation of the basis**, not its authority. Evidential support is then assessed separately. Understanding, credibility, and human worth require different judgments.

# 8. What the comparison earns—and what it does not

The four cases support a research architecture more securely than they support four diagnoses. The common question is: **which acceptance criterion has actually been satisfied, and which additional criterion is being smuggled in?** This question remains useful even if future work removes one or more names from the table.

The competing explanations must stay live. An arena-shaped exchange may be a format constraint rather than a distorted belief. Procedural disagreement may be genuine philosophical disagreement rather than captivity. Sharp logical rebuttal may answer a real misreading rather than protect identity. Resistance to an expert may reflect a legitimate unresolved tradeoff rather than contempt for expertise. These alternatives are not equally probable by decree; they are unresolved until a suitable discriminator addresses them.

The next research stage must therefore code propositions and responses before assigning types to people. Every episode needs its original claim, source, inference, objection, reply, and unresolved remainder. Evidence that a claim changed is different from evidence that its explanation changed. An explicit counterexample to “never revises” does not determine a revision rate. Silence is not a refusal unless an opportunity to respond has been established.

Provenance also changes the apparent breadth of support. Two websites carrying the same exchange supply two access routes to one document. An author's memoir and later repetition are not independent access to that author's internal state. Multiple domains can inherit one analogy or one selection decision. Removing the Hitchens tour account would remove the observed error-and-repair example. Removing the Harris-Schneier correspondence would substantially weaken the specific scope critique. Removing Zahavi would remove the developed adversarial challenge to Dennett, while leaving the logical distinction between charity and credibility intact. These are dependency observations, not completed quantitative ablations.

TLICA does not gain empirical confirmation simply because its vocabulary redescribes the cases elegantly. Its contribution here is bookkeeping: preventing contact, identity, provenance, and discrimination from silently substituting for one another. The four mechanisms remain candidate explanations. A fresh corpus, blinded coding, and specified contrary outcomes would be needed to support person-level comparative claims. The companion protocol provides those next steps without claiming they have run.

# 9. Conclusion: preserve the strength, audit the promotion

The objective is not a critic without commitments, humor, explanatory confidence, or intellectual pride. Those qualities need not be removed for inquiry to become more corrigible. The objective is to prevent an achievement from acquiring authority beyond the conditions that made it reliable.

After a rhetorical victory, identify the proposition actually tested. After reconstructing an opponent, separate accuracy of representation from evidential support. After generating a rationale, check it through a route that can expose the generator's shared assumptions. After identifying a relevant cause, validate the representation and decision rule built from it.

The initial Four Horsemen portrait was attractive because it was coherent. Research makes it less symmetrical and more useful. One allegation remains weakly attributed; another must be narrowed; public corrections complicate the supposed pathologies. Those changes are not defects to repair with stronger rhetoric. They are the first successful test of the paper's own discipline.

**Victory is not verification. Understanding is not equal credibility. Self-checking is not automatically independent checking. Causal relevance is not operational adequacy.** These distinctions survive the imperfections of the portraits that first made them visible.
