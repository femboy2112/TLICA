# Publication roadmap: Interoperability → Genie → Cave

**Date:** 2026-08-10  
**Status:** Operational publication plan; venue policies and literature judgments must be rechecked before each submission.  
**Sequence:**

\[
\boxed{
\text{Semantic Interoperability}
\rightarrow
\text{The Genie / The Severed Map}
\rightarrow
\text{Out of the Cave}
}
\]

**Primary objective:** Produce three independently defensible philosophy manuscripts in an order that earns external trust incrementally rather than asking a first reader to accept the full TLICA architecture at once.

**First completion boundary:** The first project is complete when *Semantic Interoperability* has been submitted as a policy-compliant, anonymized manuscript to a suitable peer-reviewed venue. Acceptance is a later causal layer, not part of the definition of finishing the manuscript.

**Foundation boundary:** This roadmap proposes no modification to the frozen TLICA foundation. It governs publication-facing reconstruction, source discipline, provenance, and sequencing.

---

## 1. Executive decision

The preferred public order is:

1. **Semantic Interoperability** — earn respect for one bounded, literature-facing argument that does not require prior acceptance of TLICA.
2. **The Genie / The Severed Map** — introduce the minimum TLICA architecture through a thought experiment that forces the distinctions before naming them.
3. **Out of the Cave** — demonstrate the architecture's explanatory and generative power on a canonical philosophical object.

The order is not a ranking of intrinsic importance. It is a ranking of **initial legitimacy dependence**.

| Manuscript | What a new reader must grant at the beginning | What successful review would earn |
|---|---|---|
| Semantic Interoperability | A familiar communication/education problem and a bounded conceptual proposal | Confidence in the author's scholarly method, literature discipline, and ability to make a finite contribution |
| Genie / Severed Map | That the thought experiment identifies real dissociations requiring an architecture | Confidence that TLICA's core distinctions are motivated rather than merely announced |
| Out of the Cave | The prior architecture plus a careful Plato interpretation | Confidence that TLICA transfers to a canonical object and yields explanatory gain |

The strategic logic is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{least architecture-dependent claim}
\rightarrow
\text{architecture introduction}
\rightarrow
\text{flagship application}
}
\]

# Part I — AI provenance and human authorship

## 4. Current provenance classification

The present research corpus was developed through substantial interaction with generative AI systems. That assistance includes some combination of:

- conversational elicitation of the author's ideas;
- structural decomposition;
- literature discovery;
- adversarial objections;
- formal notation;
- drafting and rewriting of prose;
- claim-ledger construction;
- synthesis across conversations and repository documents.

This is not equivalent to ordinary spellcheck or copy editing. Any publication plan that treats the current drafts as purely human-written would be inaccurate.

The correct goal is not to conceal that history. It is to create a manuscript for which Leah can honestly claim:

1. she understands and endorses every sentence;
2. she can reconstruct every argument independently;
3. she personally verified every source and citation;
4. she wrote the submission-facing prose herself;
5. the venue's AI policy permits the actual workflow;
6. the disclosure describes the workflow truthfully.

## 5. How much personal rewriting is required?

For maximum scholarly defensibility and venue flexibility, the first submission should use the following rule:

\[
\boxed{
\textbf{100% of submission-facing prose is written by Leah from a blank document.}
}
\]

This means:

- **no copy-and-paste** from AI-generated or AI-rewritten repository prose;
- **no sentence-by-sentence paraphrase** of the existing draft;
- **no AI-generated replacement paragraphs** during the human drafting pass;
- all claims retained only if Leah can independently reconstruct and defend them;
- all citations opened, read, checked, and entered by Leah;
- all examples restated in Leah's own language;
- all objections answered from Leah's own understanding.

The existing drafts may still serve as:

- an index of questions;
- a list of candidate claims;
- a map of possible sections;
- a hostile-objection inventory;
- a record of prior exploration;
- a source-discovery ledger.

They should not serve as a sentence source for the submission manuscript.

### 5.1 Argument-level rewriting

Sentence-level rewriting alone is insufficient. For each proposed argument, Leah must be able to produce, without consulting AI prose:

1. the thesis in one sentence;
2. the premises;
3. the inference from premises to conclusion;
4. the strongest known objection;
5. the best reply;
6. a condition under which she would revise or abandon the claim.

Any claim that fails this test remains in the research dossier and does not enter the manuscript.

### 5.2 Citation-level rewriting

Every citation used in the manuscript must satisfy:

- Leah accessed the cited work herself;
- Leah read the relevant section, not only an abstract or AI summary;
- exact page or section references are recorded where appropriate;
- the manuscript's characterization is narrower than or equal to what the source supports;
- no reference is included merely because an AI system suggested it;
- DOI, edition, author, title, and publication details are checked against a primary publisher, journal, or library source.

### 5.3 Clean-room rewriting does not erase provenance

A human rewrite is a method of establishing genuine authorship and comprehension. It is **not** a method for pretending AI was never involved.

If the selected venue requires disclosure of early-stage AI brainstorming, organization, or content generation, that use must still be disclosed. If a venue forbids the type of AI use that occurred during development, rewriting the prose does not automatically make the project compatible with that venue.

## 6. AI-use taxonomy for the publication program

Every tool use should be logged under one of these categories:

| Code | Use | Example | Default treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| A0 | No AI | Reading, handwritten notes, Leah-authored outline and prose | Preferred core workflow |
| A1 | Mechanical language aid | Spellcheck, punctuation, formatting, reference-manager cleanup | Often permitted; verify venue policy |
| A2 | Search/navigation aid | Finding candidate sources or current journal policies | Log and independently verify results |
| A3 | Critical aid | Generating objections, checking clarity, suggesting missing rivals | Log; Leah must independently assess and write all resulting prose |
| A4 | Generative intellectual aid | Producing arguments, philosophical conclusions, section structures, or prose from Leah's ideas | Present in existing scaffolds; policy-sensitive and must be disclosed where required |
| A5 | Autonomous or deceptive use | Fabricated sources, hidden AI authorship, submission of text the author cannot defend | Prohibited |

For the first submission, the recommended drafting mode is:

\[
\boxed{A0\text{ for argument and prose},\quad A1/A2\text{ only after venue-policy confirmation}.}
\]

A3 may be used in a separate red-team phase only if the intended venue permits it and the use is logged. A4 should not be used to generate the submission manuscript.

## 7. Human-authorship recovery protocol

The following protocol applies first to *Semantic Interoperability* and then, with modifications, to the Genie and Cave papers.

### Gate H0 — Freeze the AI scaffold

- Give the existing AI-assisted note a fixed commit and checksum.
- Do not edit it into the submission manuscript.
- Treat it as historical research evidence.
- Create a separate human-draft branch and file.

### Gate H1 — Native-language capture

Before academic translation, Leah should state the object in the language most natural to her:

- one-page free explanation;
- voice recording or handwritten note if easier;
- no citations required yet;
- no concern for journal style;
- answer: “What did I notice, why does it matter, and what exactly do I think is missing?”

This preserves the original invariant before disciplinary vocabulary changes its representation.

### Gate H2 — Claim ownership test

For every claim proposed for the manuscript, create a row:

| Claim | Origin | Can Leah explain it without notes? | Strongest objection | Evidence/source | Keep? |
|---|---|---:|---|---|---:|

Origin labels should include:

- Leah prior insight;
- conversation-elicited Leah insight;
- AI proposal;
- literature-derived;
- jointly synthesized;
- uncertain provenance.

Uncertain origin is not automatically disqualifying, but it must trigger stronger independent reconstruction and venue disclosure.

### Gate H3 — Source-first reconstruction

Read the relevant primary and secondary literature before writing the academic draft. Build a page-indexed differential ledger:

| Source claim | Exact page/section | What it already explains | What Leah's proposal adds | Conflict | Verdict-changing probe |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

For *Semantic Interoperability*, the first major object is Douglas Hofstadter and Emmanuel Sander's *Surfaces and Essences*.

The reading objective is not agreement or inspiration. It is to determine:

\[
\boxed{
\text{existing account}
+
\text{unresolved residual}
=
\text{possible contribution}
}
\]

If the book already contains the full thesis, the manuscript must change. That is successful prior-art correction, not failure.

### Gate H4 — Human outline from memory

After reading and ledger construction:

- close the AI-assisted draft;
- write a new outline from memory and source notes;
- no sentence copying;
- each section must have one job;
- each new term must solve a demonstrated problem.

### Gate H5 — Human first draft

Write the manuscript in a new file from the blank outline.

Recommended constraints:

- one active thesis;
- one main line of argument;
- standard disciplinary vocabulary before new terminology;
- no TLICA acronym in the title or abstract unless the argument genuinely requires it;
- equations only where they clarify a structural claim rather than imitate rigor;
- no autobiographical credential defense;
- no broad claim that the paper explains all communication or all education.

### Gate H6 — Independent source audit

For every nontrivial sentence:

- Is it argument, evidence, interpretation, or conjecture?
- If factual, what source supports it?
- Has Leah read that source?
- Is the citation exact?
- Does the conclusion exceed the weakest premise?

### Gate H7 — Adversarial review

Only after the human first draft exists:

- reconstruct the strongest rival view;
- attempt to remove the new terminology;
- test whether the thesis survives without the motivating examples;
- test whether high semantic interoperability can coexist with shared falsehood;
- test whether perspective reconstruction is separable from agreement;
- test whether the proposal collapses into common ground, hermeneutics, moral reframing, deliberative democracy, or ordinary education theory.

AI may assist in generating adversarial questions only if the venue policy permits it and the interaction is logged. Leah writes all responses.

### Gate H8 — Authorship oral defense

Before submission, Leah should be able to explain orally, without the manuscript:

- the thesis;
- the argument;
- the nearest three prior-art objects;
- the strongest objection;
- the limits;
- the exact novelty claim;
- the AI provenance.

Failure means the paper returns to H3–H5.

# Part II — Venue and policy strategy

## 8. Policy matrix as of 2026-08-10

Policies are unstable and must be rechecked immediately before submission.

| Venue/publisher lane | Current official position | Consequence for this project |
|---|---|---|
| **Ergo** | Requires an anonymized PDF and uses triple-anonymous review. Its public submission/review pages do not currently state a specific generative-AI policy. | Potentially attractive for credential-neutral review, but **do not submit until the managing editors answer a procedural AI-provenance query in writing**. |
| **Springer Nature journals** | Authors remain accountable; generative AI used for text, analysis, or content must be declared; AI-assisted copy editing need not be declared; every claim and reference must be verified. Individual journals may add stricter rules. | Potentially compatible with a transparent, fully human-owned rewrite, subject to exact journal fit and policy. Preserve a complete AI-use log and disclosure. |

A clean-room rewrite does not automatically cure the earlier workflow. Do not target JCS without written editorial clarification describing the history accurately. |
| **PhilArchive** | Allows individual users to submit material for archival. | Use only after checking the target journal's anonymity and preprint policy. Do not compromise anonymous review by posting the exact submission prematurely. |

### 8.1 Policy inquiry template

Before submitting to any venue whose policy is silent or ambiguous, send a procedural query rather than guessing:

> Dear Editors,
>
> I am preparing a philosophy manuscript and want to confirm its compatibility with your policy before submission. The originating questions and central ideas are mine, but early research notes and provisional drafts were developed with substantial LLM-assisted brainstorming, structural organization, objection generation, and prose drafting. I am now independently reading the relevant literature, verifying every source, and writing the submission manuscript myself from a blank document without copying AI-generated prose. I would disclose the earlier assistance accurately and remain solely accountable for the argument and text.
>
> Does your journal permit submission under those conditions? If so, what disclosure language and placement would you require?
>
> Sincerely,
> Leah VanDetta

Do not soften the description to obtain permission. A negative answer is a venue mismatch, not a moral judgment.

### 8.2 Candidate disclosure for a permissive venue

Use only if factually accurate and adapted to the selected venue:

> During early-stage development, the author used generative AI systems for conversational brainstorming, structural organization, and adversarial exploration of objections. The submitted manuscript was subsequently written by the author from a blank document. The author independently reviewed the relevant literature, verified every citation and claim, and accepts full responsibility for the manuscript. No AI system is an author.

If AI is used again after the human rewrite, the disclosure must be updated.

## 9. Venue-selection scorecard

Do not choose a venue by prestige alone. Score each candidate on:

| Criterion | Question |
|---|---|
| Scope fit | Does the journal regularly publish the exact kind of philosophy this paper is doing? |
| AI-policy fit | Does the actual development history comply, with truthful disclosure? |
| Review anonymity | Will reviewers see the author's identity or affiliation? |
| Word-limit fit | Can the argument fit without artificial compression or padding? |
| Fee model | Is there a no-fee submission/publication route? |
| Preprint policy | Can or should a preprint be posted before or after review? |
| Literature expectations | Does the paper engage the field at the depth the venue expects? |
| Accessibility | Does the journal permit cross-disciplinary language or require specialist framing? |
| Revision value | Is the venue likely to provide substantive peer criticism if the paper reaches review? |

The first venue should maximize:

\[
\text{scope fit}
+
\text{policy compatibility}
+
\text{anonymous object-level review}
\]

rather than status.

# Part III — Paper I: Semantic Interoperability

## 10. Paper I objective

Produce a standalone philosophy article with a thesis approximately of the following form:

> **A broad shared education can function as semantic infrastructure: by increasing shared conceptual coverage, it can lower the expected translation cost of transporting perspectives among differently situated minds. This value is distinct from agreement and truth, and it can coexist with correlated shared blind spots.**

This is a working thesis, not yet the final novelty claim.

The paper must distinguish:

\[
\boxed{
\text{understanding}
\neq
\text{agreement}
\neq
\text{truth}
}
\]

and:

\[
\boxed{
\text{shared vocabulary}
\neq
\text{shared relational representation}
}
\]

## 11. Narrowing rule

The first submission should **not** attempt to carry all of:

- TLICA foundation;
- David Foster Wallace exegesis;
- culture war;
- American party realignment;
- constraint-closed compromise;
- civic participation dynamics;
- a universal communication theory.

The first paper should carry one central object:

\[
\text{liberal education}
\rightarrow
\text{shared conceptual coverage}
\rightarrow
\text{higher-probability faithful perspective transport}
\]

with the boundary:

\[
\text{interoperability is not truth}.
\]

The political material can appear only as a brief consequence or motivating example unless the literature review shows that the educational thesis cannot stand alone.

## 12. Interop reading and prior-art program

### Core required reading

1. Douglas Hofstadter and Emmanuel Sander, *Surfaces and Essences*.
2. Work on analogy, categorization, and concept formation identified through that book.
3. Work on common ground and communication.
4. Philosophy of education concerning liberal education, shared culture, and civic understanding.
5. Hermeneutics and perspective understanding.
6. Social epistemology and pluralist democratic communication.
7. Adjacent empirical work on moral reframing, perspective taking, and cross-group communication.

This list is a search program, not a claim that all listed traditions say the same thing.

### Differential ledger questions

For each source:

- Does it already model shared conceptual repertoires?
- Does it distinguish transporting a perspective from transmitting a proposition?
- Does it distinguish understanding from agreement and truth?
- Does it treat education as social semantic infrastructure?
- Does it discuss compression cost or common conceptual coverage?
- Does it explain correlated blind spots produced by a shared canon?
- What prediction or distinction would the present paper add?

## 13. Interop manuscript skeleton

A provisional structure:

1. **The communication problem** — Sisyphus and *Weekend at Bernie's* as examples of shared semantic decompression.
2. **Private representational histories** — why ordinary language is not a complete shared codec.
3. **Shared conceptual coverage** — define the bounded structural analogy.
4. **Liberal education as infrastructure** — intrapersonal attention governance versus interpersonal interoperability.
5. **Understanding, agreement, and truth** — preserve the three-way separation.
6. **Correlated blind spots** — why shared education can increase fluency while preserving shared error.
7. **Objections and rivals** — common ground, hermeneutics, canon criticism, credentialism, universality, class exclusion.
8. **Consequences and predictions** — narrowly stated and explicitly UNVERIFIED where empirical.

The paper should introduce no more new terminology than its argument requires.

## 14. Interop acceptance gates

The manuscript is submission-ready only if:

- it stands without TLICA branding;
- its central claim can be stated in one sentence;
- the nearest prior art is named and accurately represented;
- the novelty residual is no larger than the evidence supports;
- at least three serious objections are answered;
- high interoperability with shared falsehood is explicitly handled;
- degree status is rejected as a proxy for conceptual coverage;
- the prose is entirely Leah-authored;
- the bibliography is personally verified;
- venue AI compatibility is confirmed;
- the PDF is fully anonymized, including metadata;
- a cover letter and AI disclosure are prepared.

## 15. Interop completion definition

\[
\boxed{
\text{Interop complete}
=
\text{policy-compliant manuscript submitted}
}
\]

Not accepted. Not praised. Submitted.

That definition creates a finish line controlled by the author.

# Part IV — Paper II: Genie / The Severed Map

## 16. Paper II objective

Use one controlled thought experiment to make the reader encounter the distinctions TLICA needs before introducing TLICA terminology.

Working functional thesis:

> **Several questions ordinarily compressed into “Would I still be me?” can vary independently. A controlled genie scenario can dissociate present contact, grounded truth-status, identity integration, ownership, and preservation priority; TLICA offers one systematic decomposition of those dissociations.**

The final thesis depends on recovery of the exact scenario and prior-art audit.

## 17. Genie recovery gate

Before drafting:

- recover the original thought experiment from repository history, library files, or conversation records;
- preserve the original wording and date as provenance;
- distinguish the original Leah insight from later AI elaboration;
- state exactly what the genie changes and what it leaves unchanged;
- reject any version that changes several variables simultaneously without controls.

## 18. Controlled mutation design

Each variant should alter one load-bearing variable:

- memory retained versus removed;
- factual correspondence preserved versus severed;
- body retained versus transformed;
- affective significance retained versus removed;
- values retained versus rewritten;
- present endorsement retained versus caused by the transformation;
- reversible versus irreversible;
- gradual versus instantaneous;
- transformation versus copying;
- one successor versus two equally continuous successors.

For every case, preregister the question being tested. Do not infer metaphysical conclusions merely from intuitive reaction.

## 19. Minimal architecture rule

The paper should introduce only the TLICA subset forced by the experiment, likely:

- \(\kappa\): present contact;
- \(\phi\): toolkit-relative truth-indistinguishability / verification status;
- \(\rho\): identity integration;
- possibly preservation ranking \(\Pi\) if the experiment shows that identity integration and preservation choice dissociate.

The paper should not reproduce the entire foundation.

## 20. Rival and hostile controls

At minimum compare the proposal with:

- psychological continuity;
- bodily continuity;
- narrative identity;
- bundle or reductionist accounts;
- higher-order endorsement;
- radical skepticism / brain-in-a-vat structures;
- symbol grounding and source correspondence;
- enactivist or sensorimotor approaches where relevant.

The thought experiment must not claim to prove:

- a theory of why consciousness exists;
- a separable soul substance;
- one universally correct answer to personal identity;
- that introspective intuition is infallible;
- that \(\rho\) is moral worth;
- that TLICA uniquely solves duplication.

## 21. Genie completion gate

The paper is ready only when:

- the scenario is recovered and provenance-labeled;
- each mutation has one declared variable;
- the architecture is introduced after, not before, the conceptual pressure appears;
- the paper survives removal of the TLICA name;
- the residual over rival accounts is explicit;
- all prose and argument are Leah-authored under the same clean-room protocol;
- venue policy is confirmed;
- the paper is submitted.

# Part V — Paper III: Out of the Cave

## 22. Paper III objective

Demonstrate that the architecture does explanatory work on Plato's cave and on the general problem of escaping a frame without pretending to leave framehood altogether.

The current manuscript contains promising objects—source-map error, toolkit closure, periagoge, developmental impossibility controls, and open-ended ascent—but must be rebuilt as a standalone publication after the Genie paper has earned the minimum architecture.

## 23. Cave literature and source gate

Before rewriting:

- re-read the relevant passages in Plato using a declared translation;
- compare at least one additional translation where wording is load-bearing;
- map major interpretive traditions relevant to education, conversion, appearance, knowledge, and return;
- distinguish TLICA translation from claims about Plato's intention;
- document where existing scholarship already contains the proposed insight;
- state the exact residual.

## 24. Cave rewrite rule

The publication manuscript must not be a lightly edited version of `out_of_the_cave_v0_1_2.md`.

It should be rewritten from a blank file after:

1. the Genie paper establishes the minimal architecture;
2. the Plato ledger is complete;
3. the source-map claim survives prior-art comparison;
4. the strongest disanalogies are explicit.

The cave paper may cite the Genie paper if published or available under a journal-compatible preprint policy. It must nevertheless summarize the required machinery so that it stands alone.

## 25. Cave hostile controls

The paper must handle:

- whether “source-map error” is merely false belief under a new name;
- whether cave escape is being overformalized;
- whether the developmental reading is textually justified or only a TLICA application;
- whether cofinal ascent adds anything beyond fallibilism;
- whether the architecture assumes the epistemic hierarchy it claims to derive;
- whether political or educational applications exceed Plato's text;
- whether the “larger cave” metaphor becomes unfalsifiable.

## 26. Cave completion gate

- exact Plato/source audit complete;
- TLICA residual over existing scholarship explicit;
- manuscript personally written from blank file;
- no dependence on private repository knowledge;
- all citations verified;
- AI policy confirmed;
- submission package complete and submitted.

# Part VI — Work management under variable energy and attention

## 27. One active manuscript, two parked manuscripts

At any moment:

- **Active:** one paper receives writing effort.
- **Warm:** one paper may receive source collection only.
- **Parked:** all remaining papers receive no expansion.

Initial state:

| State | Paper |
|---|---|
| Active | Semantic Interoperability |
| Warm | Genie recovery only |
| Parked | Out of the Cave rewrite |

This prevents all three projects from remaining perpetually open at once.

## 28. Minimum viable work unit

A productive session may consist of only one of the following:

- read and annotate two pages;
- verify one citation;
- write one claim-ledger row;
- explain one argument aloud;
- write one paragraph from scratch;
- formulate one objection;
- correct one source characterization.

The unit is complete when a durable artifact changes.

Do not require entry into a rare maximal-flow state before work counts.

## 29. Native-language-first workflow

Because Leah's natural cognition is compressed and analogical, academic translation should happen after the object exists in native form:

\[
\text{Leah-language object}
\rightarrow
\text{disciplinary mapping}
\rightarrow
\text{journal prose}
\]

Not:

\[
\text{attempt journal prose immediately}
\rightarrow
\text{lose the object}
\]

For each section:

1. state it naturally;
2. identify the invariant;
3. map existing academic terms onto it;
4. preserve the residual that standard terminology loses;
5. write the academic paragraph.

## 30. Session handoff rule

Every work session ends with one line in a handoff file:

> **Next physically executable action:** [one concrete action requiring no reconstruction].

Examples:

- “Read *Surfaces and Essences*, chapter 2, pages X–Y, and fill three ledger rows.”
- “Rewrite the Sisyphus paragraph without looking at the repository draft.”
- “Verify whether source Z distinguishes understanding from agreement.”

This lowers restart cost after interruptions.

## 31. No acceptance-contingent self-worth

The publication program evaluates manuscripts, not the author's right to exist, intelligence, or identity as an artist.

A desk rejection is evidence about venue fit, priority, framing, or editorial judgment. It is not a global verdict on TLICA or Leah.

A referee objection is a probe. Repeated independent objections to the same load-bearing claim are stronger evidence and must be paid as truth debt.

# Part VIII — Submission and outcome protocol

## 36. Submission package checklist

Before upload or email:

- [ ] manuscript file contains no author name or identifying acknowledgements;
- [ ] PDF metadata is scrubbed;
- [ ] self-citations do not reveal identity unnecessarily;
- [ ] abstract meets venue length;
- [ ] word count meets venue rules;
- [ ] bibliography style meets venue rules where required;
- [ ] every DOI and page citation is verified;
- [ ] no copyrighted long quotation exceeds permission/fair-use limits;
- [ ] cover letter states title, thesis, originality, exclusivity, and fit;
- [ ] AI policy has been rechecked on the submission date;
- [ ] written editor clarification is archived if policy was ambiguous;
- [ ] AI disclosure is complete and factually exact;
- [ ] manuscript is not simultaneously submitted elsewhere;
- [ ] preprint status complies with venue policy;
- [ ] final SHA/checksum is recorded;
- [ ] submission confirmation is saved.

## 37. Cover-letter discipline

The cover letter should not discuss lack of credentials or ask for special consideration.

It should state:

- the bounded thesis;
- why the paper fits the venue;
- that the work is original and not under review elsewhere;
- AI disclosure or reference to the attached disclosure, as required;
- any relevant preprint status.

Affiliation can be listed honestly as **Independent Researcher** where the venue requests one.

## 38. Outcome decision tree

### Desk rejection

Classify first:

- out of scope;
- insufficient novelty;
- insufficient philosophical significance;
- presentation/length problem;
- policy problem;
- no useful reason supplied.

Do not revise the theory globally unless the reason is load-bearing.

### External rejection with reports

Create a referee ledger:

| Referee claim | Accurate reconstruction? | Source/argument affected | Shared with other referee? | Required response | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Repeated independent objections to the same premise receive priority.

### Revise and resubmit

Treat the editor's conditions as the new acceptance contract. Preserve the original manuscript and produce a point-by-point response.

### Prior-art collision

If the core thesis already exists:

- cite it;
- narrow to the residual;
- change the paper's claim;
- or terminate the submission if no meaningful residual survives.

This is successful truth correction.

### AI-policy rejection

Do not conceal or minimize prior use. Select a compatible venue or ask whether a new independently authored manuscript would be eligible.

### Acceptance

Acceptance certifies only that the bounded manuscript survived that venue's process. It does not certify the whole TLICA architecture.
