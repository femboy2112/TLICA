## 2. External background: interoception and allostatic regulation

### E1 — Sennesh, Theriault & Barrett / Quigley line: interoception as modeling, allostasis as control
**Citation:** E. Sennesh, J. Theriault, L. Feldman Barrett, K. S. Quigley. "Interoception as modeling, allostasis as control." *Biological Psychology* (2022). PMID 34942287.  
**URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34942287/  
**PMC:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9270659/

**Relevant support:** explicitly treats allostasis as a control problem and interoception as feedback/modeling of body state.

**Why it matters:** Stickman's substrate-control framing is strongly adjacent. This source is a **rival/neighbor**, not confirmation.

**Debt:** inspect the full mathematical formalism before claiming incremental novelty.

### E2 — Schulkin & Sterling, allostasis
**Citation:** Jay Schulkin, Peter Sterling. "Allostasis: A Brain-Centered, Predictive Mode of Physiological Regulation." *Trends in Neurosciences* 42(10), 2019. DOI 10.1016/j.tins.2019.07.010. PMID 31488322.  
**URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31488322/

**Relevant support:** reviews allostasis as coordinated regulation of physiology and behavior under internal/external demands.

**Limit:** predictive allostasis is richer than base Stickman; do not import its anticipatory machinery into the minimal model by citation.

### E3 — Kleckner et al., human allostatic-interoceptive network
**Citation:** Ian R. Kleckner et al. "Evidence for a large-scale brain system supporting allostasis and interoception in humans." *Nature Human Behaviour* 1, 0069 (2017).  
**URL:** https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-017-0069

**Relevant support:** empirical human evidence for a distributed system associated with interoception and allostatic regulation.

**Limit:** network evidence does not identify Stickman coordinates or dynamics.

### E4 — Quigley et al., functions of interoception
**Citation:** Karen S. Quigley et al. "Functions of Interoception: From Energy Regulation to Experience of the Self." *Trends in Neurosciences* / PMC review, PMID 33378654.  
**PMC:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7780233/

**Relevant support:** reviews ingestion, excretion, energy regulation, affective experience, and self-related functions under interoception.

**Why it matters:** supports the scope ladder from bodily regulation outward.

### E5 — Petzschner et al., computational body regulation
**Citation:** Frederike H. Petzschner et al. "Computational Models of Interoception and Body Regulation." *Trends in Neurosciences* (2021). PMID 33378658.  
**PMC:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8109616/

**Relevant support:** surveys homeostatic reinforcement learning, active inference, predictive coding, and computational body-regulation models.

**Critical prior-art role:** Stickman must show what its Differentiated-Affect state space, response quotient, and common-sense projection add beyond these existing regulatory frameworks.

## 3. External background: affect and action tendency

### E6 — Scherer, emotion process and action tendencies
**Citation:** Klaus R. Scherer. "The Emotion Process: Event Appraisal and Component Differentiation." *Annual Review of Psychology* 70 (2019): 719–745. DOI 10.1146/annurev-psych-122216-011854. PMID 30110576.  
**URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30110576/

**Relevant support:** emphasizes event appraisal, action tendencies, physiological responses, expression, experience, and later semantic labeling as separable stages/components.

**Prior-art pressure:** Stickman's "colloquial gloss is downstream of dynamics" is adjacent to established component-process distinctions. Do not claim that separation as novel.

### E7 — Ridderinkhof/Frijda line, emotion in action
**Citation:** "Emotion in Action: A Predictive Processing Perspective and Theoretical Synthesis." DOI 10.1177/1754073916661765. PMID 29098017.  
**URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29098017/  
**PMC:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5652650/

**Relevant support:** explicitly centers **action readiness**, appraisal, forward modeling, and emotional action.

**Prior-art pressure:** Stickman must distinguish its base non-reflective dynamics from richer predictive/appraisal accounts and cannot claim the action-readiness bridge itself as novel.

### E8 — Lowe & Ziemke, feeling/action-tendency dynamics
**Citation:** Robert Lowe, Tom Ziemke. "The Feeling of Action Tendencies: On the Emotional Regulation of Goal-Directed Behavior." *Frontiers in Psychology* 2:346 (2011). DOI 10.3389/fpsyg.2011.00346. PMID 22207854.  
**URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22207854/  
**PMC:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3246364/

**Relevant support:** reviews feeling/action-tendency relations and discusses a dynamic-systems perspective with prediction-feedback loops.

**Prior-art pressure:** particularly important rival for any claim that coupling affect to action-readiness dynamically is itself a new move.

## 4. External background: primitive defensive responses

### E9 — nociceptive withdrawal
**Citation:** V. Skljarevski, N. M. Ramadan. "The nociceptive flexion reflex in humans — review article." *Pain* 96 (2002): 3–8. DOI 10.1016/S0304-3959(02)00018-0. PMID 11932055.  
**URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11932055/

**Relevant support:** establishes the human nociceptive flexion/withdrawal reflex as a measurable response to painful stimulation.

### E10 — normative human withdrawal-reflex values
**Citation:** "The nociceptive withdrawal reflex: normative values of thresholds and reflex receptive fields." *European Journal of Pain* (2009). DOI 10.1016/j.ejpain.2009.04.010. PMID 19505833.  
**URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19505833/

**Relevant support:** large healthy-volunteer reference sample (N=300) with demographic/covariate analyses.

**Why it matters:** potential Tier-0 calibration literature and a reminder that even reflex thresholds vary.

### E11 — human defensive behavior systematic review
**Citation:** "Brain activation during human defensive behaviour: A systematic review and preliminary meta-analysis." PMID 30611801 (2019).  
**URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30611801/

**Relevant support:** reviews simple threat avoidance and approach-avoidance paradigms in humans; reports substantial paradigm heterogeneity.

**Limit:** heterogeneity argues against casual universality claims.

### E12 — peripersonal/looming defense
**Citation:** Graziano & Cooke. "Parieto-frontal interactions, personal space, and defensive behavior." *Neuropsychologia* 44(6), 2006. PMID 16277998 / republished PMID 17128446.  
**URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16277998/

**Relevant support:** review of near-body/looming representations and defensive-like withdrawing/blocking behaviors.

**Use:** Tier-2 candidate, not proof of population invariance.

## 5. External background: interoceptive urgency and state modulation

### E13 — air hunger
**Citation:** "AIR HUNGER: A PRIMAL SENSATION AND A PRIMARY ELEMENT OF DYSPNEA." PMID 33577128.  
**PMC:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10986303/

**Relevant support:** frames air hunger as an urgent homeostatic sensation that motivates behavior and evokes affective responses.

### E14 — ATS dyspnea statement
**Citation:** "An Official American Thoracic Society Statement: Update on the Mechanisms, Assessment, and Management of Dyspnea."  
**PMC:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5448624/

**Relevant support:** distinguishes sensory mechanisms including air hunger/unsatisfied inspiration and respiratory effort.

**Use:** candidate source for a precisely controlled context→interoceptive response domain.

### E15 — sleep loss and emotion meta-analysis
**Citation:** Cara A. Palmer et al. "Sleep loss and emotion: A systematic review and meta-analysis of over 50 years of experimental research." *Psychological Bulletin* (2024). DOI 10.1037/bul0000410. PMID 38127505.  
**URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38127505/

**Relevant support:** 154 studies / 5,717 participants; experimentally reduced sleep changes positive affect, anxiety symptoms, and emotional arousal measures.

**Use:** supports sleep state as a substrate parameter/modulator rather than a single isolated "sleepy response."

## 6. Major rival frameworks that must be compared before novelty claims

### R1 — Homeostatic reinforcement learning
Petzschner et al. review it; additional primary formal sources still need collection.

**Overlap:** internal-state deficits, drive reduction, action selection.

**Potential Stickman distinction to test:** TLICA-derived affect configuration and response quotient without defining the base system as reward maximization.

### R2 — Active inference / interoceptive inference
Useful entry points:
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29694845/
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26365173/
- https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5062097/

**Overlap:** embodied regulation, interoception, action, prediction.

**Potential Stickman distinction to test:** a deliberately pre-reflective application layer derived from Differentiated Affect plus explicit common-sense population projection.

**Risk:** active-inference formalisms may already subsume much of the claimed mechanics. Do not assume otherwise.

### R3 — Component process / appraisal models
Entry: Scherer 2019.

**Overlap:** event→appraisal→action tendency→physiology/expression/feeling/label.

**Risk:** Stickman's "backward derivation" may be a TLICA-specific reparameterization of a well-developed emotion-process literature.

### R4 — Frijda/action-readiness tradition
Entries E7–E8.

**Overlap:** action tendencies as central to emotion and behavior.

**Risk:** the action-readiness bridge is definitely prior art.

### R5 — generic dynamical/state-space models
Need a more targeted primary-source search before publication.

**Overlap:** latent state, forcing, damping, hysteresis, phase lag, multiscale processes.

**Rule:** the Lagrangian form earns no novelty from using physics notation.

## 7. What may actually be distinctive if it survives

These are **candidate contributions**, not novelty claims:

1. **TLICA/Differentiated-Affect projection:** derive a deliberately non-reflective affective state space from an existing self-architecture rather than begin from standard valence/arousal or reward coordinates.
2. **Response-preserving quotient as the definition of minimality:** Stickman variables survive only when they preserve declared response signatures.
3. **Common-sense abstention rule:** "common sense" is the modal coarse response only with a predeclared dominance margin; otherwise the model explicitly returns no common denominator.
4. **Cross-population admission test:** core Stickman requires conditional transport / low residual population information.
5. **Stickman→StickmanPlus ladder:** add weak projected-action structure only from frozen residuals, preserving the boundary around reflexive Mode B.
6. **Mechanics-vs-null discipline:** Lagrangian affective inertia is a falsifiable stronger model, not the starting metaphysics.

Each must be compared against prior art before promotion.

## 9. Current source verdict

The external literature strongly supports the **research neighborhood**:

- bodily regulation and adaptive behavior are tightly linked;
- interoception/allostasis have mature control-theoretic and computational treatments;
- emotion/action-readiness coupling has substantial prior art;
- reflexive and defensive responses provide measurable calibration domains;
- sleep and respiratory state measurably alter affect/behavior.

It does **not** currently establish:

- the Stickman state coordinates;
- the Lagrangian inertia term;
- the response-preserving quotient's empirical usefulness;
- cross-population invariance;
- derivation of broad colloquial common sense;
- novelty.

Those remain the branch's truth debt.
