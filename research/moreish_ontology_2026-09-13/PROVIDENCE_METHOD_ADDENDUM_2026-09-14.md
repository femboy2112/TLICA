# Providence methodology addendum — phenomenological replication and canonization

**Date:** 2026-09-14  
**Status:** author correction to [`PROVIDENCE_SYNTHESIS_2026-09-14.md`](PROVIDENCE_SYNTHESIS_2026-09-14.md); research-tier autobiographical reconstruction; **this file supersedes any reading of the earlier synthesis that makes the acquisition process simple authority-following.**

## 0. Author correction

The author's childhood/adolescent method was not:

> an admired or religious authority says something, therefore accept it.

It was closer to:

> hear / see / read something that an apparently enlightened or unusually perceptive person noticed; treat it as a candidate; probe lived experience for the same structure; if the pattern is repeatedly felt and noticed, internalize it as another truth.

The authority generated a **candidate invariant**. Phenomenology supplied the author's attempted replication.

A schematic acquisition rule is therefore

\[
q \xrightarrow{\text{candidate from thinker / tradition}}
\operatorname{Probe}(q;\,\text{lived experience})
\xrightarrow{\text{felt + noticed recurrence}}
\operatorname{Canonize}(q).
\]

This is better described as **phenomenological empiricism / replication** than as obedience to authority.

The problem was not that the observations were necessarily false. The problem was that successful local replication often licensed a much coarser global operator than the evidence supported.

## 1. Two canonical examples

### 1.1 Jesus — moral behavior and self-sacrifice

The author did not identify as straightforwardly Christian, despite a Christian-coded upbringing (baptism, early Sunday school, stronger religious exposure through grandparents, a spiritually eclectic mother, and a father the author describes as a spiritually embedded realist).

Nevertheless, the reported actions and moral posture of the Jesus-figure functioned as an idealization. The candidate observation was approximately:

\[
\text{moral seriousness includes care for others and willingness to bear real cost for them}.
\]

Phenomenological and social observation supplied enough apparent confirmation for that principle to be internalized.

The coarse compilation was not simply "Jesus says sacrifice, therefore sacrifice." It was closer to:

\[
\text{self-sacrifice can be morally good}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\text{absorbing moral cost myself is a generally safe direction of correction}.
\]

Coupled to the Golden Rule, causal-perturbation reasoning, God-uncertainty, and anti-privilege intuitions, this helped produce the later **moral error-cancellation / Karmic Accountant** controller.

The useful scoped truth survives:

- moral behavior can demand real sacrifice;
- refusing to externalize every cost onto others matters;
- one's own convenience is not automatically the highest-value term.

The overfit operator does not:

- the self is not a zero-weight coordinate;
- every negative consequence is not a debt that can or must be cancelled;
- willingness to sacrifice does not imply becoming the designated sink for system-wide loss.

### 1.2 Buddha — attachment and suffering

The Buddha supplied a second candidate invariant:

\[
\text{attachment is deeply coupled to suffering}.
\]

The author phenomenologically probed that claim and found enough lived structure corresponding to it that it was internalized as another truth.

Again, the observation contains real scope-sensitive insight. Attachment can create vulnerability to loss, craving, clinging, resistance, and suffering.

The coarse version is much stronger:

\[
\text{attachment contributes to suffering}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\text{attachment itself is globally suspect / should be minimized as such}.
\]

The methodological lesson is therefore not "Jesus and Buddha were wrong." It is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{a locally reproduced truth can still be compiled into an invalid global policy.}
}
\]

## 2. The epistemic ratchet

This correction exposes a stronger mechanism than the previous synthesis contained.

The acquisition channel permitted phenomenology to carry substantial **positive** epistemic weight:

\[
\text{candidate }q
+\text{ felt/noticed recurrence}
\Longrightarrow
q\text{ internalized as truth}.
\]

But after canonization, painful phenomenology did not receive symmetric **negative** authority unless it could refute the proposition itself:

\[
\text{pain while implementing }q
\not\Rightarrow
\neg q.
\]

That logical statement is often correct. The damaging move was using it as an update rule:

\[
\text{pain does not refute }q
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\text{pain does not justify revising the policy compiled from }q.
\]

So the overall learning rule could become asymmetric:

\[
\boxed{
\text{phenomenological resonance can promote a rule,}
\qquad
\text{phenomenological suffering cannot easily demote it.}
}
\]

Call this **phenomenological canonization hysteresis** or, more bluntly, an **epistemic ratchet**.

This is the key reconciliation between two otherwise apparently conflicting autobiographical facts:

1. the author used feeling and lived observation to determine whether candidate truths were real enough to internalize;
2. once a reasoning chain had been canonized, intense emotional suffering could be interpreted as a consequence of following the truth rather than evidence against the truth or against the policy derived from it.

The distinction is between **belief acquisition** and **policy correction**.

## 3. Three levels that the childhood method collapsed

The repaired analysis distinguishes:

### Level A — observation

\[
O: \quad \text{I repeatedly encounter this pattern in lived experience.}
\]

### Level B — scoped proposition

\[
P: \quad \text{under conditions }C,\text{ this pattern is a useful description.}
\]

### Level C — governing policy

\[
\Pi: \quad \text{therefore organize behavior globally according to this rule.}
\]

The childhood method often had decent support for \(O\), plausible support for a suitably qualified \(P\), and insufficient support for the jump to \(\Pi\).

Examples:

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\text{attachment can generate suffering}
&&\not\Rightarrow
&&\text{minimize attachment globally},\\
&\text{self-sacrifice can be morally exemplary}
&&\not\Rightarrow
&&\text{assign the self near-zero moral weight},\\
&\text{models are corrigible}
&&\not\Rightarrow
&&\text{future models deserve negligible control authority},\\
&\text{emotion can misidentify external causes}
&&\not\Rightarrow
&&\text{emotion is useless controller telemetry}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The recurring bug is therefore **scope erasure during compilation**.

## 4. Jesus remains a character in *Moreish*

For the manuscript, Jesus should remain a character because the Jesus-ideal was not merely background theology. It occupied a real controller role.

The character is **not** "Christianity causes pathology" and not "Jesus was wrong." The character is the internally compiled moral sensor:

> If I can bear a cost that prevents another person from bearing it, why should I privilege myself?

That is a legitimate moral probe.

Promoted to governor, it becomes:

> If a negative moral perturbation exists and I can absorb its cost, I should preferentially absorb it.

Combined with an unobservable zero-harm setpoint, that is the route to moral integral windup.

The corrected character rule is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{WWJD is a moral probe, not root access.}
}
\]

Or equivalently:

\[
\boxed{
\text{The Jesus-ideal is a sensor of externalized cost, not the sole objective function.}
}
\]

Buddha is best retained primarily as evidence for the **acquisition methodology** rather than promoted into a fourth comic governor unless later author work shows a distinct dynamical role beyond the shared canonization process.
