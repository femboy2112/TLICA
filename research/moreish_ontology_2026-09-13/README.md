## What this is

An author-written essay on a specific self-reinforcing failure mode: the policy that
**maximizes the lived present while discounting the future's *causal* pull on the ground
that the future is only *epistemically* weak** — a category mistake between uncertainty
and irrelevance. The mature paper develops the mode through **three** comic/idealized
controller figures — Underground Super Hans (adversarial epistemic freedom promoted from
*sensor* to *governor*), Pavlov's Veruca (immediate-reward reinforcement promoted to capital
allocation), and the Jesus/WWJD ideal (sacrificial moral seriousness promoted toward moral
error-cancellation) — names the **Greedy Integral Problem** (maximizing the integrand locally
≠ maximizing the integral when the integrand is dynamically coupled through state), exposes the
**epistemic ratchet** (phenomenological resonance can canonize a rule that later suffering
cannot symmetrically de-canonize), and argues the corrective is not more planning but an
**adaptive feedback policy** with preserved invariants — corrigibility, **option value**,
affective telemetry, proportional moral cost, and suspicion of gratuitous irreversible commitments.
