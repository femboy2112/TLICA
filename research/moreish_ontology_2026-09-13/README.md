## What this is

An author-written essay on a specific self-reinforcing failure mode: the policy that
**maximizes the lived present while discounting the future's *causal* pull on the ground
that the future is only *epistemically* weak** — a category mistake between uncertainty
and irrelevance. It develops the mode through comic/idealized controller figures
(Underground Super Hans = adversarial epistemic freedom promoted from *sensor* to
*governor*; Pavlov's Veruca = immediate-reward reinforcement promoted to capital
allocation; the Jesus-ideal = sacrificial moral seriousness promoted toward moral
error-cancellation), names the **Greedy Integral Problem** (maximizing the integrand locally
≠ maximizing the integral when the integrand is dynamically coupled through state), and
argues the corrective is not more planning but an **adaptive feedback policy** with
preserved invariants — corrigibility, **option value**, affective telemetry, proportional
moral cost, and suspicion of gratuitous irreversible commitments.

The 2026-09-14 Providence continuation adds an author-corrected account of how candidate
truths were acquired: **hear/see/read an apparently enlightened person's observation →
phenomenologically probe it → if the pattern is felt/noticed, internalize it as another
truth**. The identified failure is not simple authority-following; it is **scope erasure
and asymmetric updating**. Phenomenological resonance could canonize a rule, while later
phenomenological suffering often could not de-canonize it unless the suffering refuted the
proposition itself.
