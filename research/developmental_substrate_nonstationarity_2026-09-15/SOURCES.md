# Sources and Evidence Map
## Developmental Substrate Nonstationarity

This source map supports only the **domain constraints** needed by the research note. It does not treat developmental neuroscience as proof of the autobiographical causal reconstruction.

The governing distinction is:

- **domain science can corroborate that the substrate is developmentally nonstationary and that affective regulation changes across development;**
- **it cannot by itself establish that those changes caused this author's specific phenomenology.**

---

## 1. Developmental neural reorganization / plasticity

### Baker, Galván, & Fuligni (2025) — review

Amanda E. Baker, Adriana Galván, Andrew J. Fuligni. "The connecting brain in context: How adolescent plasticity supports learning and development." *Developmental Cognitive Neuroscience* 71 (2025): 101486. DOI: `10.1016/j.dcn.2024.101486`. PMID: `39631105`.

**Useful constraint:** puberty/adolescence involves substantial neurobiological change and plasticity, including synaptic pruning, myelination, neuronal reorganization, and strong interaction with social/environmental input.

**What it supports here:** the broad claim that adolescence is a changing neural regime rather than a fixed substrate merely receiving more data.

**What it does not support:** any specific TLICA mapping or the author's autobiographical mechanism.

### Casey, Heller, Gee, & Cohen (2019 issue / 2017 online) — review

B. J. Casey, Aaron S. Heller, Dylan G. Gee, Alexandra O. Cohen. "Development of the emotional brain." *Neuroscience Letters* 693:29-34. DOI: `10.1016/j.neulet.2017.11.055`. PMID: `29197573`.

**Useful constraint:** emotional development during adolescence is associated with dynamic reorganization of limbic and cognitive-control circuitry; the review describes gradual changes in emotion reactivity/regulation extending into young adulthood.

**What it supports here:** it is biologically reasonable to model the effective affect/regulation plant as changing across adolescence into young adulthood.

**Limit:** review-level synthesis; not a direct test of the moving-machine hypothesis.

### Dipnall et al. (2024) — longitudinal primary study

Lillian M. Dipnall, Joseph Y. M. Yang, Jian Chen, Ian Fuelscher, Jeffrey M. Craig, Timothy J. Silk. "Childhood development of brain white matter myelin: a longitudinal T1w/T2w-ratio study." *Brain Structure and Function* 229:151-159. DOI: `10.1007/s00429-023-02718-8`. PMID: `37982844`.

**Useful constraint:** longitudinal evidence that white-matter/myelin-sensitive measures continue changing through childhood, consistent with ongoing maturation of network communication rather than a static postnatal substrate.

**Limit:** MRI proxies do not uniquely measure one cellular process; do not equate TLICA substrate with myelin.

### Juraska & Willing (2017) — review of pubertal transition

Janice M. Juraska, Jane Willing. "Pubertal onset as a critical transition for neural development and cognition." *Brain Research* 1654:87-94. DOI: `10.1016/j.brainres.2016.04.012`. PMID: `27060769`.

**Useful constraint:** pubertal transition is associated with cortical and white-matter change; white-matter development can extend beyond conventional adolescence while cortical gray-matter measures stabilize later.

**Limit:** combines human and animal evidence; useful for direction and timing, not a direct human phenomenology bridge.

## 2. Why "new blank neurons = noise" is the wrong centerpiece

### Bhardwaj et al. (2006) — human neocortex cell-age study

Ratan D. Bhardwaj et al. "Neocortical neurogenesis in humans is restricted to development." *PNAS* 103(33):12564-12568. DOI: `10.1073/pnas.0605177103`. PMID: `16901981`.

**Useful constraint:** major human neocortical neuron populations are not continuously regenerated in adulthood at detectable levels; the paper supports treating cortical development primarily as organization/maturation rather than a stream of newly born blank neurons.

**Important nuance:** adult human neurogenesis, especially in hippocampal niches, remains a distinct and historically contested literature. Nothing in this dossier requires taking a maximal position on that debate. The only point needed here is that **large-scale continual addition of blank cortical neurons is not the right general mechanism for adolescent phenomenology.**

### Huttner et al. (2024) — review

Wieland B. Huttner, Michael Heide, Felipe Mora-Bermúdez, Takashi Namba. "Neocortical neurogenesis in development and evolution—Human-specific features." *Journal of Comparative Neurology* 532(2):e25576. DOI: `10.1002/cne.25576`. PMID: `38189676`.

**Use here:** modern review context for cortical neurogenesis as a developmental process; reinforces that the more relevant adolescent question is maturation/reorganization of already-formed circuits.

## 3. Development of affective control and emotion regulation

### Schweizer, Gotlib, & Blakemore (2020) — review

Susanne Schweizer, Ian H. Gotlib, Sarah-Jayne Blakemore. "The role of affective control in emotion regulation during adolescence." *Emotion* 20(1):80-86. DOI: `10.1037/emo0000695`. PMID: `31961183`.

**Useful constraint:** affective control develops through adolescence into adulthood, and distinct control components follow non-uniform trajectories.

**What it supports here:** the controller is also developing, so the moving-machine problem is two-sided: plant drift can change while tracking/regulation capacity changes.

**Limit:** does not justify a single monotone "adolescents are less regulated" scalar.

### Galarneau et al. (2026) — longitudinal primary study

Emma Galarneau, Tanja Lischetzke, Xiaomei Li, Kalee De France, J. P. Lougheed, Tom Hollenstein. "Developmental stability and change in emotion regulation strategies and strategy repertoires across adolescence." *Journal of Research on Adolescence* 36(1):e70161. DOI: `10.1111/jora.70161`. PMID: `41721196`.

**Useful constraint:** longitudinal samples show both stability and developmental change in regulation strategies, including increasing transitions toward higher-diversity regulation repertoires with age.

**What it supports here:** regulation is neither absent in youth nor suddenly switched on in adulthood; it changes in repertoire and deployment over development.

## 4. Longitudinal affective dynamics

### Maciejewski et al. (2015) — five-year daily-diary longitudinal study

Dominique F. Maciejewski, Pol A. C. van Lier, Susan J. T. Branje, Wim H. J. Meeus, Hans M. Koot. "A 5-Year Longitudinal Study on Mood Variability Across Adolescence Using Daily Diaries." *Child Development* 86(6):1908-1921. DOI: `10.1111/cdev.12420`. PMID: `26464350`.

**Useful result:** in a community sample followed from ages 13 to 18, day-to-day variability in happiness, anger, and sadness declined across adolescence; anxiety showed a more complex nonlinear course.

**Why it matters:** supports the directional plausibility of increasing affective stability while warning against a one-dimensional story.

**Limit:** population-level trend in one sample; cannot be projected directly onto the author.

### Sun et al. (2025) — two-year EMA longitudinal study

Tingyue Sun et al. "Characterizing emotional dynamic trajectories in adolescents: A two-year longitudinal study with an ecological momentary assessment design." *Journal of Research on Adolescence* 35(4):e70089. DOI: `10.1111/jora.70089`.

**Useful result:** in repeated EMA across two years, positive-affect inertia and variability decreased while negative-affect intensity increased; emotional development was multidimensional rather than a simple global damping process.

**Why it matters:** this is exactly the kind of result the TLICA note should preserve. Maturation may increase stability/flexibility in some dimensions while intensity moves differently in others. "Adult context-resolution" must not be equated with globally weaker emotion.

## 5. Evidence pattern actually licensed by these sources

The source families jointly license a restrained statement:

1. adolescence and young adulthood include ongoing neural/circuit reorganization;
2. white-matter/myelin-sensitive properties continue changing over development;
3. emotion regulation and affective control change across adolescence and into adulthood;
4. longitudinal affect data show developmental changes in variability/inertia/strategy use, but not a single universal monotone decline in emotional intensity;
5. therefore a TLICA model in which both the substrate and controller are changing is biologically plausible;
6. none of this determines whether the author's own baseline-buzz -> context-resolved transition was caused by ordinary maturation, depression/recovery learning, environment, retrospective reconstruction, or their interaction.

That final causal split remains **UNVERIFIED**.

## 6. Source-map hazards

### Review duplication

Baker, Casey, Schweizer, Juraska/Willing, and Huttner are synthesis sources. They should not be counted as independent replications of the primary studies they cite. A mainline literature pass should normalize shared citations before claiming convergence.

### Imaging-to-mechanism overreach

MRI measures are not literal readouts of "myelin amount," synapse count, or TLICA state. Where a source uses a proxy (e.g., T1w/T2w ratio), preserve the proxy language.

### Population-to-person transport

Population developmental trends do not establish an individual's trajectory. The autobiographical application requires its own provenance.

### Age-to-development collapse

Chronological age, puberty, neural maturation, learned regulation, social environment, and autonomy are correlated but distinct. The research note must not use "age" as a hidden composite variable and then call the result mechanism.

### Clinical overreach

The source set is used to constrain a theoretical developmental model. It should not be used to diagnose the author or infer treatment effects.

## 7. Next literature pass required before mainline promotion

A full integration pass should add at least:

- one large longitudinal multimodal neurodevelopment cohort spanning adolescence into the twenties;
- one primary study linking pubertal/endocrine stage to affective-control change;
- one systematic review or meta-analysis of adolescent affective variability/instability;
- one adaptive-control/nonstationary-system-identification source to formalize the moving-plant analogy without reinventing terminology;
- one hostile source or review emphasizing heterogeneity/non-monotonicity, to prevent a maturation fairy tale.

Until that pass is complete, the neuroscience bridge should remain **corroborative context**, not the paper's empirical centerpiece.
