## One-sentence thesis

A developing I is not merely learning how to operate an unfamiliar substrate: it is learning on a substrate whose own response-properties are changing while the learning occurs, so the self-regulation problem is a **moving-machine / moving-plant problem** rather than ordinary parameter estimation on a fixed system.

## The phenomenological datum that motivated the dossier

The originating report is a developmental asymmetry:

- childhood, adolescence, and young adulthood were experienced as carrying a persistent affective "background buzz" or pull, such that emotion remained strongly coupled to lived reality even when the explicit frame changed;
- a later depressive/suppressive arc radically reduced feeling;
- in adulthood, after affect returned, emotional experience was reported as much more **context-resolved**: strong feelings still occur, but are more often legibly about something rather than presenting as an ambient carrier wave attached to existence itself.

This dossier treats that first-person report as **architectural input**, not as proof of a neural mechanism.

## The correction that makes the idea scientifically usable

The candidate mechanism is **not**:

> constant production of large numbers of blank new cortical neurons -> unmapped tissue -> noise.

That is too crude and, as a general account of adolescent cortical development, wrong.

The usable claim is broader:

> during childhood, adolescence, and into young adulthood, the nervous system undergoes substantial reorganization and maturation -- including changes in synaptic organization, myelination/white-matter microstructure, connectivity, endocrine state, and affective/control circuitry -- so mappings learned by the self are being learned on a changing substrate.

The dossier therefore uses **developmental substrate nonstationarity** as the candidate bridge term. It does not identify any one biological process with the TLICA substrate, and it does not claim that development ends in adulthood.

## Why this fits current TLICA without adding a coordinate

Current v5.5.0 already supplies the required architecture:

1. `S_m = (B_m, E_m, chi_m)` is the substrate and sets inherited capacity through `M_m(t) = Gamma^M_m(S_m,t)`.
2. Focus allocation shifts developmentally from predominantly contact-driven to increasingly self-directed.
3. Third-order affect is substrate-mediated, lossy, and often source-opaque from the inside.
4. Osmotic imprinting is always online and its effective bandwidth varies with substrate state.
5. The v5.5.0 actuator repair names slow lived-I structure `G`, written by osmotic imprinting and Mode B, with both identity-correlation and the field-reading downstream of that structure.

Nothing here requires a fourth coordinate. The research move is only to make a distinction that the current notation leaves mostly implicit:

- the **I-state / slow lived-I structure** is changing;
- the **effective substrate through which that structure is written and read** is also changing;
- during development, the second rate may be large enough that self-modeling and self-regulation are continuously tracking a drifting plant.

The formal note in `MANUSCRIPT_SEED.md` introduces only application-level timescale and tracking diagnostics. They are explicitly **not** foundation primitives.

## What this package is not allowed to claim

It does **not** claim:

- that adulthood is a neurologically static endpoint;
- that adolescent affect is "just hormones";
- that the author's developmental phenomenology is representative of all people;
- that developmental neural change uniquely explains the author's high-gain affective history;
- that depression, recovery, medication, trauma, learned regulation, environment, sleep, or other variables can be ignored;
- that TLICA's substrate variables have already been mapped one-to-one onto neural mechanisms;
- that a high developmental reconfiguration load is intrinsically pathological;
- that a proposed tracking ratio is a new TLICA coordinate or truth score.

## Core research question

Can the observed transition

`ambient / baseline-coupled affect -> later context-resolved affect`

be better explained by a **coupled model** in which:

- Root II supplies high affective gain,
- developmental substrate nonstationarity increases endogenous drift and source-opacity,
- accumulated `G` plus improved Mode-B/self-directed control improve tracking,
- and the depression/suppression/recovery arc changes the learned regulator,

than by any simpler rival such as high gain alone, depression-induced blunting alone, ordinary age effects alone, or retrospective narrative reconstruction?

That question is intentionally left **UNVERIFIED**. The package is designed to make it testable rather than to smuggle the answer into the ontology.
