# Public-first release strategy — *The Ethical Quiet Quitting Manifesto*

**Checked:** 2026-08-16  
**Objective:** Maximize public reach while preserving a durable source of record and disclosing the manuscript's real AI-development history.  
**Action status:** Planning only. No page, post, account, pitch, submission, or message was created or sent.

## Executive recommendation

Use a **source-of-record + discovery + canonical mirror + optional archive** stack:

1. **Source of record:** publish the complete release on a personal site, preferably a custom domain backed by GitHub Pages; a free `github.io` address is a workable zero-cost start.
2. **Discovery and audience ownership:** publish a substantial, self-contained companion essay on Substack, then use Substack Notes for several platform-native excerpts that point to the source of record. Do not make the Substack publication a bare link farm.
3. **Searchable mirror:** import the complete article into Medium only after the source-of-record URL exists. Medium's importer automatically backdates the copy and assigns the original URL as canonical. Put the AI disclosure within the first two paragraphs and do not paywall it.
4. **Optional citable archive:** after Leah has completed the human verification gate, deposit a fixed PDF/Markdown release in Zenodo as a publication record. Zenodo supplies a DOI and versioning, but it is an archive, not the principal reading experience or reach engine.
5. **Editorial amplification:** treat later editorial pieces as **materially distinct derivatives**, not undisclosed duplicates. A true reprint is plausible only where the venue expressly accepts reprints and agrees to this provenance.

This public-first choice should be made with eyes open: publishing the full manifesto will very likely close the ordinary *New Labor Forum* route for a substantially overlapping manuscript. Sage says substantially previously published work is generally unacceptable and requires disclosure of prior distribution. A later, genuinely different article is not automatically barred, but the public manifesto must be disclosed and the editor—not the author—would decide whether the overlap is acceptable. Source: [Sage prior-publication policy](https://www.sagepub.com/journals/publication-ethics-policies/prior-publication).

## Why this stack, rather than one platform everywhere

The real target is not merely “post it online.” It is to create one durable identity for the work, then let other platforms distribute it without confusing provenance or making silent copies compete with the source.

| Layer | Recommended role | What it earns | Boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Personal site / GitHub Pages | Complete canonical HTML plus PDF and Markdown downloads | Control, stable source URL, version history, self-canonical metadata | A new site has no built-in audience; GitHub Pages is platform hosting, and a truly portable identity requires a domain Leah controls |
| Substack | Self-contained release companion, email list, Notes excerpts | Email delivery, subscriptions, and discovery across the Substack network | No official author-facing external-canonical control was found; an exact full mirror may create duplicate-source ambiguity |
| Medium | Complete canonicalized mirror | Additional search, direct-link, follower, and publication discovery | Disclosed AI assistance is allowed, but AI-generated content is not eligible for General Distribution and AI-generated writing cannot be paywalled |
| Zenodo | Immutable release archive and DOI | Citation, fixity, explicit versions, preservation | Must be grounded in genuine human-conducted research/analysis; not a general blog host or meaningful launch audience |
| Editorial venues | New derivative, authorized reprint, or commissioned adaptation | Editorial validation and a different audience | Originality, prior-publication, AI, and rights rules vary; silence is not permission |

## Canonical-host options

### 1. Personal domain on GitHub Pages — recommended source of record

GitHub describes Pages as a static-site host that publishes HTML, CSS, and JavaScript from a repository. It is available from public repositories on GitHub Free, supports the default `owner.github.io` address or a custom domain, and supports HTTPS. Sources: [What is GitHub Pages?](https://docs.github.com/en/pages/getting-started-with-github-pages/what-is-github-pages), [custom domains](https://docs.github.com/en/pages/configuring-a-custom-domain-for-your-github-pages-site/about-custom-domains-and-github-pages), and [HTTPS](https://docs.github.com/en/pages/getting-started-with-github-pages/securing-your-github-pages-site-with-https).

**Why it wins:** Leah controls the page structure, disclosure placement, downloads, version note, citation suggestion, and outward links. A custom domain can move to another host later without changing the public identity of the work. The zero-cost fallback is a `github.io` URL.

**AI-policy precision:** GitHub Pages is self-publishing, not editorial review. No GitHub Pages contributor rule specifically deciding this manuscript's AI provenance was located. That absence is not a platform endorsement; it means the operative public safeguards are Leah's disclosure, rights, factual accuracy, and the general GitHub terms rather than a journal-style AI eligibility gate.

**Release page should contain:**

- the full accessible HTML text;
- author, first-publication date, and exact version number;
- the complete AI-development disclosure;
- links to the PDF and Markdown editions;
- a short “cite this version” block;
- a changelog or link to prior versions;
- one stable URL and a self-referential canonical tag;
- descriptive page metadata and a social-preview image, if one is later created;
- a clear license statement. Public access does not itself choose a Creative Commons license.

### 2. Substack — recommended discovery/list layer; alternative launch home

Substack says publishing is free, original content remains the creator's, and posts, subscriber data, and statistics can be exported. A subscriber list can be exported as CSV. A custom domain costs a one-time $50 fee and must use a subdomain; newsletters still send from a Substack address. Sources: [cost](https://support.substack.com/hc/en-us/articles/360037607131-How-much-does-Substack-cost), [post/data export](https://support.substack.com/hc/en-us/articles/360037466012-How-do-I-export-my-posts), [subscriber export](https://support.substack.com/hc/en-us/articles/6314498343700-How-do-I-export-my-email-list-on-Substack), [custom domains](https://support.substack.com/hc/en-us/articles/360051222571-How-do-I-set-up-my-custom-domain-on-Substack), and the [Publisher Agreement](https://substack.com/pa).

Substack's current AI tooling is unusually relevant. Readers can request an AI-text estimate for posts and Notes published from July 21, 2026 onward. Creators can attach a “How I make this” transparency statement, disable detection per item, and dispute detection errors. Substack's official account presents this as transparency and shows creators openly describing AI use in research, drafting, writing, and editing. This is strong evidence that disclosed AI-assisted work can be hosted there, but it is **not** a promise of recommendation, reach, or an acceptance ruling for every use. Sources: [AI-detection and creator disclosure controls](https://support.substack.com/hc/en-us/articles/50891130623508-How-can-I-detect-AI-on-Substack), [Substack's explanation of the transparency launch](https://on.substack.com/p/how-writers-are-reacting-to-substacks), and [Publisher Agreement automated-analysis terms](https://substack.com/pa).

Notes can place links, quotes, images, and comments before subscribers and the wider Substack network. Source: [Getting started on Substack Notes](https://support.substack.com/hc/en-us/articles/14564821756308-Getting-started-on-Substack-Notes).

**Recommended treatment:** make the Substack newsletter a self-contained 1,200–1,800-word companion—why this manifesto exists, its three most actionable claims, one concrete example, and a link to the complete source. This is more useful than a teaser and avoids posting an exact second full copy where no documented external-canonical control was found. Substack's guidelines also say a publication should not exist primarily to drive traffic to a third-party site, so the companion must stand on its own. Source: [Substack Content Guidelines](https://substack.com/content).

**If immediate convenience outranks source control:** Substack can instead be the operational canonical home. Its exportability makes that less fragile than many platforms. That is still platform tenancy, however, unless Leah uses a domain she controls. If Leah chooses this route, make Substack the one complete copy and treat GitHub as a versioned source/archive, not a competing full web article.

### 3. Medium — use as a mirror, not as the primary home

Medium explicitly permits responsible AI-assistive technology, but any story incorporating AI-generated text must be clearly labeled. Its suggested placement is within the first two paragraphs. Medium defines “AI-generated writing” as majority-AI prose with little or no human editing, fact-checking, or changes; such writing cannot be paywalled. Undisclosed AI-generated or AI-assisted writing is restricted to Network Only distribution. Sources: [Medium AI content policy](https://help.medium.com/hc/en-us/articles/22576852947223-Artificial-Intelligence-AI-content-policy).

The reach boundary is stricter than the hosting rule. Medium's curation guidelines say Boost stories should be human-created, while AI-generated content is ineligible for General Distribution. Network-only stories remain accessible by direct link, search engines, social sharing, the author's followers, and a Medium publication's followers. Medium also excludes stories with “prominent or prolific profanity” from General Distribution; one analytically defined term is not necessarily “prominent or prolific,” but Medium gives no guarantee. Source: [Medium Distribution Guidelines](https://help.medium.com/hc/en-us/articles/360006362473-Medium-s-Distribution-Guidelines-How-curators-review-stories-for-Boost-General-and-Network-Distribution).

The reason Medium is still useful is its canonical support. Its importer automatically backdates a republished article and points the canonical link to the original URL; authors can also set that URL manually. Sources: [Importing a post](https://help.medium.com/hc/en-us/articles/214550207-Importing-a-post-to-Medium) and [Set a canonical link](https://help.medium.com/hc/en-us/articles/360033930293-Set-a-canonical-link).

**Recommended treatment:** import from the personal-site URL after that page is live; confirm the canonical in page source; put the full disclosure in the first two paragraphs; do not paywall; assume Network Only rather than counting on Medium curation.

### 4. Zenodo — optional fixed archive after human verification

Zenodo supports publications and other research objects, assigns a DOI when a record is published, and links later versions through separate persistent records. Once published, files and the persistent identifier cannot ordinarily be altered; a new version is the proper way to replace files. Sources: [About records](https://help.zenodo.org/docs/deposit/about-records/), [DOIs](https://help.zenodo.org/docs/deposit/describe-records/reserve-doi/), and [versioning](https://help.zenodo.org/docs/deposit/manage-versions/).

Zenodo's April 2026 AI policy is explicit and compatible only within a real boundary:

- the record must be grounded in research/analysis conducted by its human authors;
- AI may assist but cannot be the source of the research itself;
- AI cannot be listed as author or contributor;
- substantive AI use should be disclosed in the record description;
- literature search/summarization and structuring the author's own work are acceptable when verified;
- a prompted paper with no additional research work, raw AI output, fabricated findings, or core arguments originating in AI rather than the depositor's work is unsuitable.

Source: [Zenodo generative-AI policy for depositors](https://support.zenodo.org/help/en-gb/13-policies/227-what-is-your-usage-policy-for-generative-ai-for-depositors). Zenodo's general terms cover research, educational, and informational content and leave the uploader responsible for rights and open dissemination. Sources: [Terms of Use](https://about.zenodo.org/terms/) and [General Policies](https://about.zenodo.org/policies/).

**Verdict for this manifesto:** potentially eligible if Leah's lived analysis and intellectual framework are the real basis, every source is independently verified, and the AI contribution is fully described. Do not treat Zenodo as eligible merely because disclosure exists. If that human-research basis cannot be certified, omit this layer. If used, manually create a **publication** record rather than relying on Zenodo's GitHub software-release workflow.

## Duplicate-content and syndication rules

| Content form | Safe public-first treatment | Risk to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Complete manifesto | One readable canonical HTML page | Simultaneous unmarked full copies on several hosts |
| Medium copy | Import from the canonical URL and verify the canonical tag | Copy/paste before the original exists, or leave Medium as an ambiguous competing source |
| Substack newsletter | A self-contained companion/adaptation with a prominent source link | An exact full duplicate without documented external canonical control; a publication made only to push clicks off-platform |
| Zenodo record | Fixed release files, DOI, version number, original-site publisher/source link, complete disclosure | Treating an immutable deposit like a draft; using Zenodo if the human research/analysis basis is not real |
| Social/Notes posts | Platform-native propositions, examples, or excerpts that add context and link to the source | Reposting the whole piece in fragments, spammy repetition, or obscuring which URL is authoritative |
| Editorial derivative | New angle, audience, structure, examples, and timely hook; disclose the public manifesto | Sending the public text as “original,” disguising a compression as a new work, or inferring AI permission from silence |
| Authorized reprint | Label it as a reprint, link the canonical original, disclose AI provenance, resolve rights | Assuming every outlet accepts reprints or will preserve the canonical URL |

Medium itself warns that duplicate copies without a canonical link may be penalized in search rankings. Source: [Medium canonical-link guidance](https://help.medium.com/hc/en-us/articles/360033930293-Set-a-canonical-link).

## Recommended release sequence

### Gate 0 — complete before anything becomes public

- Freeze a named release version and make HTML, PDF, and Markdown agree.
- Verify every factual claim, quotation, number, and citation against an original source.
- Remove or consent-clear identifying workplace details and third-party private information.
- Confirm that every image, quotation, and table is owned, licensed, or within defensible quotation limits.
- Choose the public license. Defaulting to “all rights reserved” preserves options; a Creative Commons license is a separate reach/control decision, not an automatic consequence of public access.
- Add the final truthful AI disclosure.
- Make the “fuck-it factor” decision described below. No naming change is authorized by this strategy.

### Release day — establish the source

1. Put the complete version at the canonical personal-site URL.
2. Confirm the HTML, PDF, Markdown, version, date, links, disclosure, and canonical metadata.
3. Publish the self-contained Substack companion and its first Note only if Leah has separately authorized publication. The first Note should state one substantive proposition, not merely “new post.”

### Next 1–3 days — widen discovery without confusing provenance

4. Import the canonical article into Medium. Confirm the external canonical link and AI disclosure; keep it free.
5. Use two or three additional Substack Notes over several days, each with a different unit of value: the repair-first sequence, ethical de-buffering, and the acute overload mechanism.
6. Share the canonical URL through Leah's existing social and professional accounts, using native summaries rather than identical promotional copy. Account-specific AI and moderation rules were not audited here; recheck them before posting.

### After the release is stable

7. If the Zenodo human-research boundary is satisfied, deposit the fixed release and link the DOI from the canonical page; link the canonical page from the Zenodo description.
8. Review which ideas readers actually engage with before building editorial derivatives. Metrics are evidence about attention, not truth, but they can identify the strongest public doorway.
9. Approach at most one editorial venue at a time whenever its simultaneous-submission rule is unknown, unless the venue gives contrary permission. Disclose both the public original and the AI history.

## Protected decision: the “fuck-it factor”

**Nothing in this strategy renames, euphemizes, or deletes the term.** It carries phenomenological weight and names a distinct acute state; distribution friction does not silently outrank that function.

Two actual platform issues must be brought back to Leah before any title or placement change:

1. ZNetwork leaves profanity to contributors but warns that corporate syndication partners may block articles containing certain words. That is a direct reach-versus-fidelity choice.
2. Medium excludes “prominent or prolific profanity” from General Distribution. Its rule does not decide whether one sparingly used analytic term crosses the line.

The pending choice for Leah's pontification is therefore:

- use **“fuck-it factor” in the public title or subtitle** and accept possible filtering;
- keep it as the **named construct and section heading inside the work**, with a distribution-neutral page title/URL; or
- choose a second public-facing label while explicitly preserving “fuck-it factor” as the phenomenological name in the text.

No option should be implemented by default. The current low-regret preparation is to keep the construct and its definition intact while postponing only the title/preview decision.
