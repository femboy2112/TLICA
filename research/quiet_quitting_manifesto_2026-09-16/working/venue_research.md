# Publication-venue audit: *The Ethical Quiet Quitting Manifesto*

**Checked:** 2026-08-16  
**Scope:** New Labor Forum (NLF) first; ranked alternatives if NLF's AI rule is a barrier. No editor was contacted and nothing was submitted.

## Executive verdict

**NLF is an unusually strong subject, audience, genre, and length fit, but the present manuscript cannot yet be certified eligible under Sage's current AI policy.** NLF explicitly wants accessible, pointed, provocative labor arguments in the 3,000–3,500-word range. The proposed adaptation is therefore structurally well aimed. The obstacle is not Leah's authorship: a human author who made the central intellectual contribution, critically revises and approves the final article, and accepts accountability can meet Sage's authorship criteria. The obstacle is the manuscript's disclosed production history: “significant prose drafting” by ChatGPT over an extended prompt sequence plausibly falls within Sage's listed inappropriate use, “generation of data or submission using a series of prompts.” Disclosure is necessary but does not automatically make a prohibited use permissible.

**Claim status:**

- **DISCLOSED:** NLF's format, word limit, style, abstract, citation, submission, exclusivity, and review requirements are stated on its official Sage page.
- **DISCLOSED:** Sage permits assistive AI for language/grammar/structure suggestions and requires disclosure for specified generative uses, but lists generation of a submission through a series of prompts as inappropriate.
- **CORROBORATED:** The manifesto is an excellent thematic fit for NLF's stated mission and current labor-ideas portfolio.
- **UNVERIFIED:** Whether NLF/Sage would accept this particular human-directed but substantially AI-drafted manuscript after full disclosure. The policy does not resolve that borderline application.

**Operational recommendation:** Do not submit the present AI-drafted text to NLF on the assumption that disclosure cures the issue. Before a venue-specific submission, Leah should choose between (1) personally rewriting the final NLF article in her own sentences, using AI only as critic/source-finder/structure adviser, while transparently disclosing the developmental history; or (2) moving first to a venue with an explicit conditional allowance for AI-generated portions. Written editorial clarification would be the cleanest NLF probe, but it was outside this no-contact audit.

## Is the transparently AI-assisted article eligible at NLF?

### Human authorship: probably yes, if the acts are real

Leah supplied the thesis, phenomenology, distinctions, revision direction, and acceptance criteria. If she critically revises the publication version, verifies its sources, approves every claim and sentence, and accepts public responsibility, she can satisfy Sage's human authorship requirements. The lack of an academic affiliation or degree is not made a bar in NLF's public rules.

### Present text provenance: not safely certifiable

The decisive conflict is between two parts of Sage's policy:

1. disclosed generative AI is not by itself grounds for rejection; and
2. using prompts to generate a submission is listed as inappropriate.

The current manuscript is not an unattended one-prompt artifact: Leah originated and iteratively governed the argument. But the handoff candidly states that AI performed significant prose drafting across extensive collaboration. That is more than grammar, language, or structure assistance. The policy supplies no threshold for how much human direction or rewriting moves a manuscript out of “generation ... using a series of prompts.” Therefore:

- **Eligible as a Leah-authored intellectual work:** supported.
- **Eligible under the current NLF/Sage AI-production rule:** **UNVERIFIED**, with a serious adverse textual indicator.
- **Safe to submit merely by adding an acknowledgment:** no.

### Truthful disclosure note for the present provenance

This is suitable as an internal draft, not a guarantee of acceptance:

> **Generative AI disclosure:** The author used OpenAI's ChatGPT during development to test the argument, identify counterarguments and candidate sources, and draft and revise portions of the prose. The substantive thesis, lived-experience framing, and iterative editorial direction are the author's. The author reviewed the complete manuscript, checked factual claims and citations against original sources, approved the final text, and accepts full responsibility for its content.

Do not dilute “draft portions of the prose” into “copyediting” if that is not true. If Leah personally rewrites the final submission, the disclosure may accurately distinguish AI-assisted development from human final drafting, but only after that work has actually occurred.

## The “fuck-it factor”: venue-specific naming issue

No NLF or Sage rule found in this audit bans profanity. NLF simultaneously asks for provocative arguments and warns against rhetoric. That creates an editorial tradeoff, not a demonstrated reason to rename the construct.

For NLF, the defensible version is to introduce it once as **Leah's deliberately colloquial, phenomenological label**, distinguish it from established human-factors constructs, and use it sparingly. The vulnerability is not merely the word “fuck”; it is whether the label seems to substitute vivid rhetoric for a discriminating mechanism. The mechanism in this manuscript—an empty fully compliant action set, forced constraint sacrifice, and impaired ranking under overload—is analytically separable and can support the name.

**Do not rename it automatically.** Before any change, bring Leah the actual choice: preserve phenomenological fidelity and memorability, or choose a distribution-friendly public label while retaining “fuck-it factor” in the body. This becomes especially concrete at ZNetwork: its official submission page leaves profanity to contributors but warns that corporate syndication platforms may block articles containing certain words. That is a reach-versus-fidelity decision for Leah, not an editorial fact that decides itself.

## Venues ruled out for this provenance

These are excellent labor/ideas fits in the abstract but are not eligible targets for the attached AI-assisted manuscript under their current public rules:

- **[Dissent](https://dissentmagazine.org/about-dissent-magazine/submission-guidelines/): REFUTED as a target.** It welcomes political/economic/cultural submissions and first-time writers, but says it will not accept or publish work “generated or modified with artificial intelligence.” It also bars simultaneous submissions. The current manuscript was both generated and modified with AI.
- **[Labor Notes](https://labornotes.org/writerguidelines): REFUTED as a target for this text.** Its writer rules say, “Please don't use A.I. to write your piece.” It normally prefers contact before drafting, concise 700–800-word articles, worker voices, concrete reporting, and practical lessons. A genuinely fresh, wholly human-written article by Leah could be considered later, but the AI-assisted submission draft should not be repackaged for it.

## Publication sequencing consequence

Because NLF forbids simultaneous submission and Sage treats substantial prior publication as generally unacceptable, the publication sequence matters:

1. Keep the canonical v2.0 manifesto private while deciding whether NLF remains the first target.
2. Resolve the AI-eligibility pathway before investing in a final NLF `.docx`.
3. If NLF is chosen, submit only the NLF adaptation and do not publicly post overlapping versions or send them elsewhere during review.
4. If NLF is abandoned or rejects the work, adapt to one fallback venue at a time, rechecking both AI and exclusivity rules.

This preserves options without concealing provenance or accidentally converting the future submission into previously published material.
