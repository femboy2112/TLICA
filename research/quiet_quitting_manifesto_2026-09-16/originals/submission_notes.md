## Abstract — 143 words

Quiet quitting is usually framed as laziness or liberation. This article argues that both frames miss the ethical sequence. Workers should first use reasonable pathways to diagnose constraints, communicate problems, propose repairs, clarify priorities, and escalate proportionately. When those pathways repeatedly fail, however, employees need not keep supplying uncompensated, unsustainable discretionary effort that makes institutional incapacity look like adequate staffing. I call the withdrawal of that hidden subsidy **ethical de-buffering**: competent, safe, honest core performance continues while heroic compensation stops. The article also distinguishes long-term pathway closure from the **“fuck-it factor,”** my term for acute overload when no fully compliant action appears reachable before a response is required. Quiet quitting proper is therefore neither sabotage nor an immediate default. It is a last honest boundary—and sometimes a bridge that preserves enough income, time, and agency to make exit or collective action genuinely reachable.
