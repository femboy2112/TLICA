# Changelog — v1.4 to final v1.5

## Lineage and editorial decision

Version 1.4 is the literary and moral parent of final v1.5. The final manuscript is a rebase onto v1.4, not a continuation of the rejected v2-style direction or the preliminary v1.5 candidate that overcorrected toward bilateral moral symmetry.

That rejected intermediate candidate added contract-like language about an “ordinary wage bargain,” allowed institutional reliance on hidden excess to generate worker duties of notice, staging, or handoff, treated “settled mutual practice” as part of the job definition, and repeatedly made the worker prove institutional causation. Leah rejected that framing because equal human worth does not imply equal workplace power or equal ethical burden.

Final v1.5 keeps the useful evidence, scope, and source repairs while making the governing asymmetry explicit:

- the worker has limited but real power over their labor and the donation of excess;
- economic necessity constrains even that power;
- the employer controls pay, staffing, schedules, rules, discipline, and much of the worker’s action-space;
- the worker owes conscious and responsible use of the choices they actually control; and
- those with greater information, authority, and system-level control bear the greater duty to repair and prevent cascades.

The controlling line remains: **“Humanness is symmetric. Power is not.”**

## Substantive changes in final v1.5

### 1. Unequal power, unequal burden

- Replaced the false-symmetry opening with an explicit account of constrained worker power and concentrated employer control.
- Defined “repair first” as truth-seeking for the worker’s own judgment, not permission-seeking or an employer’s right to endless chances.
- Rejected any institutional moral right to notice, staging, or handoff merely because the institution organized itself around unsustainably resourced labor.
- Made clear that responsible worker power means owning actual choices, not accepting responsibility for conditions controlled elsewhere.
- Assigned system-level investigation, staffing, priority design, and safe-stop planning primarily to management.

### 2. Case anchor without a deservingness test

- Stated that the argument arose from Leah’s experience of chronic under-capacity, attempted repair, and hidden compensating effort in one workplace.
- Kept the work a manifesto rather than converting it into an autobiographical case report.
- Offered the pattern for recognition and criticism, not as a template other workers must prove they fit before they are allowed a boundary.

### 3. Quiet quitting proper

- Preserved the definition of quiet quitting proper as competent performance of the paid and reasonably expected role while withdrawing discretionary, uncompensated, unsustainable, or identity-consuming excess.
- Made the category decision explicit: **quiet quitting proper implies that structural or managerial expectations exceed the defensible job baseline**. That excess condition is necessary but not sufficient; the later pathway and ethical conditions distinguish quiet quitting proper from ordinary boundary-setting, misconduct, or collective pressure.
- Defined the defensible baseline as the compensated, reasonably defined role—including legitimate professional and safety standards—performed competently at a sustainable pace, not the most an employer can compel without immediate consequence.
- Distinguished three ways the gap can arise: incoherence within the codified role; material conflict between the codified role and the lived job; or operating conditions such as volume, pace, staffing, equipment, or incompatible priorities that make competent sustainable performance of the nominal bundle impossible.
- Distinguished correction from assimilation. Correction clarifies or prioritizes the role, resources the work, or negotiates an expansion the worker can actually reject. Assimilation tells the worker to absorb the contradiction and lets hidden compensating labor become the job.
- Clarified the category’s counterfactual: in a healthy, properly resourced workplace, the same baseline conduct simply looks like an average employee doing ordinary work. It becomes legible as “withdrawal” only when the institution had mistaken an invisible subsidy for the job.
- Clarified that quiet quitting may be spoken or unannounced; silence under credible retaliation is not automatically dishonesty.
- Defined the real job without allowing repetition or hidden sacrifice to turn role creep into a permanent duty.
- Preserved the distinction between withdrawing a hidden subsidy and manufacturing failure.

### 4. Collective action as a distinct use of worker power

- Scoped the non-negotiable invariants to **individual quiet quitting proper** rather than presenting them as a universal tactics code.
- Kept strikes, boycotts, slowdowns, and coordinated work-to-rule morally open rather than treating disruption as automatically illegitimate.
- Added Leah’s governing collective-action formulation:

  > **Do what you feel is right—but do not mistake the feeling for proof. Test what is true. Know the consequences as far as you can. Own what you choose.**

- Required factual testing, disconfirmation, honest uncertainty, and ownership of intended pressure without transferring responsibility for an exploitative or deliberately brittle system onto workers.
- Distributed responsibility according to choice, knowledge, power, and control—not fifty-fifty by default.

### 5. “Let them eat cake”

- Preserved the v1.4 framing:

  > **“Why not just get another job?” is often the labor-market version of “If they cannot afford bread here, let them eat cake.”** It answers a constraint by assuming access to the very resources the constraint has already taken away.

- Added a short clarification that the comparison is deliberate hyperbole about constraint-blind reasoning, does not equate the suffering involved, and makes no historical authorship claim.
- Kept the phrase as rhetoric with a defined target rather than apologizing it out of the manuscript.

### 6. Exit and material constraint

- Replaced an unsupported empty-exit-set assertion with the valid nonimplication that formal job availability does not establish a viable exit for a particular worker.
- Preserved the material analysis of wages, benefits, scheduling, transition risk, and the way a bad job can consume the capacity required to leave it.
- Presented quiet quitting as a possible bridge that can recover time and agency while explicitly naming discipline, retaliation, lost advancement, and dismissal as live risks.

### 7. Grind culture and the American Dream

- Preserved the complete grind declaration, the distinction between personal resonance and universal character judgment, and “Passion is real, powerful, and partly contingent. It is not payroll infrastructure.”
- Attributed “identity wages” to Alison R. Buck and delimited Leah’s adapted use.
- Preserved the complete standalone American Dream section and its principal argument: the hard-working American supplies the input; the Dream is the owned future the input is supposed to make reachable.
- Framed the argument as one influential cultural strand rather than the only meaning of the Dream.
- Recast the probability expression as the worker’s own changing estimate, not a fitted population claim.
- Preserved the critical word **here** and the concluding strike: when the work ethic has no reachable Dream-side output, it becomes extraction wearing the language of character.

### 8. Repair, evidence, and causal burden

- Limited repair attempts to pathways that are reasonably safe, useful, and functioning; no ritual vulnerability is owed to a retaliatory hierarchy.
- Kept concrete diagnosis, priority calls, and honest source-mapping as instruments available to the worker.
- Added privacy and protected-information cautions.
- Stated that the worker need not prove a unique cause. Management, with broader system data and control, owns the deeper causal investigation.
- Preserved ethical de-buffering as the withdrawal of counterfeit capacity while assigning the primary planning burden to management.

### 9. The fuck-it factor

- Preserved the name, the full section, and the raw phenomenological sentence:

  > **Fuck it. I cannot preserve everything. I will try to fuck the least important thing.**

- Distinguished the lived perception of an empty compliant action-set from the later factual question of whether a compliant route objectively existed.
- Preserved the phenomenological reality without presenting the term as a validated scale, diagnosis, scientific law, excuse, or legal defense.
- Distinguished intentional departures, unintentional errors, and mixed cascades; an intentional departure is not automatically a mistake.
- Removed unsupported fitted-looking mistake and threshold claims.
- Reframed workaround normalization and nonlinear deterioration as conditional mechanisms with possible interruption or repair.
- Retained the thesis that ethical quiet quitting may be justified and still be causally hazardous while refusing to make the worker forecast a complete cascade from inside a system they do not control.
- Analyzed managerial decisions by their observable reach and scale rather than claiming access to a manager’s private mental state.

### 10. Worker’s Pledge, managers, and conclusion

- Added a preface stating that the Worker’s Pledge is an ethical orientation, not a strict oath, procedural checklist, or employer-facing test of whether a worker has earned a boundary.
- Made good-faith truth-seeking explicit: test what appears true, seek disconfirmation, distinguish knowledge from inference or hope, and revise when better evidence arrives.
- Preserved the pledge’s anaphoric architecture while allowing unsafe or absent repair channels and distinguishing individual quiet quitting from collective pressure.
- Added the pledge line “We will not mistake assimilation for correction.”
- Added the manager-facing principle that humane restraint is not pathological tolerance; greater power requires better discrimination, not thinner skin.
- Added a direct instruction to repair or renegotiate work whose operative demands exceed the defensible baseline, rather than rewriting the description and calling assimilation clarity.
- Made the manager-facing diagnosis concrete: if a competent employee now looks merely average, the defined work has not failed; the invisible subsidy has stopped.
- Restored “Watch for your own fuck-it factor” while grounding judgment in observable decisions and their reach.
- Made the conclusion explicit that responsibility is not divided fifty-fifty: the worker owns chosen acts, while the institution bears the larger systemic burden where it designed or maintained the constraint set.

### 11. Source and disclosure apparatus

- Added eight endnotes covering employee voice and silence, exit constraints, identity wages, American Dream context, human-factors risk, intentional departures versus errors, workaround normalization, and nonlinear capacity analogies.
- Added an evidence/provenance note separating Leah’s synthesis and coined concepts from adjacent empirical research.
- Added a future-facing AI-development disclosure. It accurately states that publication remains contingent on Leah’s personal review, independent source verification, and approval of the final positions and wording.
