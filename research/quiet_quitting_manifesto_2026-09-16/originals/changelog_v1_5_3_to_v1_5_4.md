# Changelog — v1.5.3 to v1.5.4

## Why this is an autobiographical-transparency release

v1.5.4 leaves the v1.5.3 construct, architecture, evidence, and protected positions unchanged. It adds a bounded account of how Leah arrived at the ethic: some earlier conduct bearing the broad public label *quiet quitting* did not meet the later standard of quiet quitting proper; a felt but initially inarticulate sense of structural validity prompted continued reflection; and testing consequences and believed truth against what could later be justified produced the present framework.

The disclosure is **AUTHORIAL / NORMATIVE**. It is not evidence that quiet quitting proper is empirically validated, that other workers share Leah's history or motives, that her earlier choices were retroactively ethical, or that workers must confess past conduct before a boundary can be recognized. No source was added, removed, or changed.

- the error was not the refusal of self-sacrifice, but failure to test believed truth against reality, trace foreseeable effects on other people, and distinguish withdrawal of unowned excess from simply putting herself first;

- *something felt right about quiet quitting that I couldn't put my finger on*, but that feeling is classified as a clue rather than a verdict;
- reflection on consequences and on the gap between believed truth and later-justifiable truth revealed that a worker can respond to a real structural mismatch and still respond unethically;
- the present framework resulted from forcing that intuition through truth-testing, consequence-awareness, and responsibility;
- Leah's responsibility for chosen acts neither places worker and employer on equal footing nor erases the institution's larger structural burden; and
- the disclosure explains Leah's route to the framework and is not a confession other workers owe before the facts of their boundary can be recognized.
