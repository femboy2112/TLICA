## v1.5.4 substantive delta

**Delta verdict: PASS.** The textual delta from v1.5.3 is narrow: version metadata, one chronology clarification, and a three-paragraph autobiographical disclosure.

- **Chronology repaired:** the Preamble now says the argument “finally crystallized” through the principal workplace case rather than implying that no earlier experience preceded it (line 27).
- **Prior conduct distinguished from the stipulated category:** Leah states that, at earlier jobs, she engaged in conduct covered by the broader public label but would not now classify all of it as quiet quitting proper (line 39). This preserves the manuscript's governing distinction instead of laundering prior acts through a later definition.
- **Error named without condemning the boundary:** the disclosure says the mistake was not refusal of self-sacrifice; it was allowing immediate need to settle the whole question without adequate truth-testing, consequence tracing, or differentiation between withdrawal of unowned excess and simply putting oneself first (line 39).
- **Phenomenological origin retained:** “something felt right” is presented as a clue rather than a verdict. Reflection separates the possibility of a real structural mismatch from the possibility of an unethical response to it (line 41).
- **No retroactive absolution:** the text expressly refuses to certify prior choices after the fact and says that some do not survive the manifesto's test (line 39).
- **No equality fiction or confession gate:** Leah owns chosen acts while expressly preserving the institution's larger structural burden; the disclosure explains her route and is not a confession other workers owe before their boundaries can be recognized (line 43).

The addition increases moral seriousness because the framework is shown as a product of self-criticism and revision, not a declaration of the author's prior moral purity. It does not alter the definition, repair-first sequence, unequal-power allocation, collective-action boundary, or conclusion.

## Hostile-reader, privacy, and legal-minded screening

**Textual screening verdict: PASS, subject to the author gate and the residual risk inherent in publishing an autobiographical admission. This is not legal clearance.**

- **Employer-side hostile reading:** an employer could excerpt “at earlier jobs” and characterize Leah as a recurring disengaged worker or the manifesto as a rationalization for self-interest. The surrounding text materially answers that reading: it uses the broader public label, withholds the manuscript's ethical certification, identifies specific failures of reasoning, and denies retroactive absolution (lines 35–45). The manuscript does not ask an employer to treat unlike conduct as identical.
- **Labor-side hostile reading:** a worker or labor critic could read the confession as respectability politics—as though only a perfectly reflective worker earns a boundary. The text blocks that inference by calling the disclosure Leah's route rather than a confession others owe and by refusing to let individual responsibility erase unequal footing or the institution's larger burden (line 43). Section 1 also rejects articulate self-explanation as a condition of structural recognition (line 109).
- **Editorial reading:** the chronology is coherent. Earlier broad-label conduct precedes the ethical framework; the principal workplace later crystallizes it (lines 27, 39–43). The disclosure is placed immediately after the manuscript narrows its personal question and before “quiet quitting” becomes shorthand for quiet quitting proper, so it clarifies rather than interrupts the taxonomy (lines 35–45).
- **Legal-minded reading:** the disclosure is a moral and epistemic self-assessment. It does **not** identify below-baseline work, sabotage, falsification, time theft, a safety breach, a policy or contract violation, unlawful conduct, a specific victim, a quantified loss, or a particular harmful event. “Consequences” and “foreseeable effects” remain nonspecific; they should not be described as proof that no legal or contractual issue exists (lines 39–41).
- **Privacy and identifiability:** no earlier employer, location, date, role, supervisor, coworker, customer, policy, incident, or confidential record is named. The phrase “at earlier jobs” nonetheless discloses a recurring autobiographical fact. A former employer or reader who already knows Leah's work history could draw inferences or quote the passage reputationally. Removing that residual risk would require removing the transparency Leah deliberately requested, not merely redacting an avoidable identifier.
- **Scope boundary:** the manuscript's end note correctly identifies the work as an ethical framework rather than legal advice and recognizes that contracts, agreements, policies, professional duties, and law may impose additional obligations (line 845). It does not transform the hostile-reader screen into legal advice or adjudicate any past employment event.

No textual privacy or hostile-reader blocker remains. Before publication, Leah must still decide knowingly whether the value of the disclosure outweighs its unavoidable reputational risk and consider independent legal review if any identifiable past employment dispute, confidentiality duty, regulated professional obligation, or threatened claim exists.

## Governing definition and power asymmetry

The governing definition and unequal burden allocation remain intact:

- **Labor-refusal category boundary:** quiet quitting proper retains the defensible baseline and withdraws only unowned excess. Whether outright refusal is ethical is a separate unsettled question (line 13). Section 9 still treats strikes, boycotts, slowdowns, and coordinated work-to-rule as a distinct use of power (lines 696–700).
- **Unequal action field:** economic necessity constrains the worker's limited power, while the employer controls pay, staffing, schedules, rules, discipline, and much of the field in which labor acts (line 15).
- **Non-mirrored ideals:** worker responsibility concerns competent, safe, honest baseline labor and chosen acts within reasonable knowledge and control. Employer responsibility includes humane restraint and affirmative repair proportionate to greater authority, information, control, and reach. These are expressly not mirror images (line 17).
- **Materiality and closure threshold:** ordinary imperfection is subthreshold. A material, persistent baseline mismatch and meaningful pathway closure are minimum conditions rather than a complete sufficiency test (line 19).
- **No permission gate:** repair first serves truth and causal legibility; it does not grant endless chances, compel danger or retaliation, or transfer institutional repair to the least powerful person (lines 21–25). No worker must earn permission to stop a genuinely voluntary gift, and silence under credible retaliation is not automatically dishonest (lines 59–61).
- **Precise definition and one-way condition:** quiet quitting proper is competent performance of paid and reasonably expected work while ceasing unowned excess (lines 31–33). It implies structural or managerial expectations above the defensible baseline; the converse is not asserted, and excess is necessary rather than sufficient (lines 47–55).
- **Codified, lived, and operating mismatch:** the gap may lie in vague or contradictory words, unagreed and unresourced lived-role expansion, or operating conditions that make sustainable competent performance impossible. Nominally optional extras enforced through pressure or penalty are not optional in practice (lines 63–65).
- **Correction is not assimilation:** correction clarifies, prioritizes, resources, or genuinely negotiates; assimilation makes the worker absorb the defect as identity and duty (lines 67–69).
- **Healthy-workplace control case:** the same competent, sustainable conduct is ordinary or average in a properly resourced workplace. It appears as withdrawal only where the institution mistook excess for capacity (lines 71–73).
- **Disclosure held inside the asymmetry:** the author's ownership of earlier choices is expressly bounded from the institution's larger structural burden and does not establish equal footing (line 43).

Section 8 remains a worker's self-audit, not an employer form or a fiction of equal power (lines 636–656). Repair attempts remain qualified by safety, usefulness, retaliation, futility, channel viability, cost, and assimilation-only pseudo-correction; management retains system-level foresight, staffing, and safe-stop responsibility. The conclusion again rejects fifty-fifty responsibility and distributes it by choice, knowledge, power, and control (line 831).

**Power-asymmetry verdict: PASS.** The disclosure does not create false symmetry, a worker proof burden, an employer permission gate, a general duty to subordinate worker needs to corporate interests, or an institutional escape hatch.
