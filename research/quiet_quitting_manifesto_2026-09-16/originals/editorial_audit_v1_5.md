## Governing definition and power asymmetry

The revised definition now states the manuscript's exact object without turning a worker's boundary into an employer permission process:

- **Definition:** quiet quitting proper is competent performance of paid and reasonably expected work while ceasing discretionary, uncompensated, unsustainable, or identity-consuming excess that the institution has absorbed into its operating model (lines 35–37).
- **One-way structural condition:** the manuscript asserts only that an ethical quiet quitter proper implies structural or managerial expectations above the defensible job baseline (lines 39–45). It does not assert the converse or label every worker facing a bad workplace a quiet quitter.
- **Necessary-not-sufficient guard:** excess above the defensible baseline is necessary but does not by itself settle the ethical classification; pathway, integrity, proportionality, and action-category conditions still distinguish quiet quitting proper from ordinary boundary-setting, misconduct, and collective pressure (line 47; lines 614–625).
- **No permission gate:** the baseline is not whatever an employer can compel, and no worker must earn permission to stop a genuinely voluntary gift (lines 45–55). Declining a genuinely optional extra may be wholly ethical ordinary boundary-setting without being “quiet quitting proper” (line 57).
- **Three mismatch routes:** the gap can exist in the codified role (vague or contradictory words), the lived role (material unagreed or unresourced extension or contradiction), or operating conditions (volume, pace, staffing, equipment, or priorities that make competent sustainable performance of the nominal bundle impossible). Nominal extras enforced through pressure, evaluation, or discipline are not optional in practice (line 57).
- **Correction versus assimilation:** correction clarifies, reconciles, prioritizes, resources, or negotiates duties the worker can actually reject; assimilation makes the worker absorb the defect as identity and duty (lines 59–61).
- **Healthy-workplace control case:** in a properly resourced workplace, the same competent sustainable conduct is simply ordinary or average work. The phenomenon becomes visible only where an institution had mistaken an unsustainable subsidy for job capacity (lines 63–65).

The responsibilities remain deliberately non-mirrored. The worker exercises limited, local, economically constrained power and owns chosen acts within what they can reasonably know and control; the employer controls pay, staffing, schedules, rules, discipline, priorities, and much of the action-field, so it bears the larger affirmative repair and system-foresight duty (lines 15–21, 424, 570–588, 610, 625). Section 8 is expressly a worker's self-audit, not an employer form or a fiction of equal power; repair attempts are qualified by safety, usefulness, retaliation, futility, channel viability, and the possibility that the offered “repair” is only assimilation (lines 614–625). Managers are instructed to repair or genuinely renegotiate contradictions, role expansion, and impossible operating conditions, while retaining proportionate authority over actual misconduct (lines 767–769).

**Power-asymmetry verdict: PASS.** No load-bearing false symmetry, worker proof burden, employer permission gate, or accidental condemnation of ethical ordinary boundary-setting remains.
