# Rerouting and the existential boundary — 2026-10-11 correction

**Status:** author-origin correction to the AdultClub paper; independent formal/empirical status **CONJECTURED / UNVERIFIED**.  
**Authors and provenance:** Leah supplied the distinction; assistant supplied the mathematical reconstruction, counterexamples and proposed tests. The original author wording appears in [AUTHOR_RECORD.md](AUTHOR_RECORD.md).
**Scope:** model-routing geometry, self-related commitments, boundary affect. **No** clinical inference from someone's reaction.

## 1. Correction to the prior discriminator

The initial [probe program](PROBE_PROGRAM.md) made a cross-domain authority effect appear to distinguish "ordinary deference" from "authority-mediated boundary transport." **That dichotomy is false as a classification of mechanisms.** Ordinary deference can itself be an authority-mediated *rerouting* across compartments, including through an orthogonal domain-specific expert.

The corrected distinction is between:

- **Frame-compliant rerouting:** an observer encounters a local tension and chooses another available route through an established compartment. The route can invoke a teacher, manager, parent, peer, technical expert, or other authority. This may be justified expertise or unjustified deference. In either case, the route ordinarily leaves the observer's root-organizing commitments intact.
- **Root-implicating challenge:** an encounter calls into question an inferential relation that participates in the observer's organizing account of reality and self, so that the observer *understands or experiences* possible revision as "that which you root your existence [in] is flawed." This can be conveyed directly or inferred from what a challenger says, even with no explicit criticism of the self.
- **Non-root boundary challenge:** a transport is questioned but the threatened connection is peripheral; not every cross-context discrepancy is existential.
- **Root-implicating non-authority challenge:** a challenge can threaten organizing commitments without a named authority anywhere in the route.

The word "root" is **relative to this observer and situation**. It does not assert that every such commitment is truly necessary to existence, that an authority is right or wrong, or that there is a singular psyche root node.

The **methodological repair** is to cross-classify authority mediation and root-criticality rather than treating them as competing categories.

## 2. Four schematic cases

| Authority used in transport? | Root-level commitments remain safe | Root-level commitments called into question |
|---|---|---|
| **Yes** | "I don't know that tax rule; I consult a competent accountant." A route through expertise preserves the frame. | "Why does professional success confer the right to define whether your local suffering is real?" Challenges a highly relied-upon link from prestige to epistemic warrant. |
| **No** | An unfamiliar task is solved by a familiar calculation or alternative model without authority. | "If this principle fails, what becomes of the narrative that I earned my standing by always obeying the rule?" A self-conception can be threatened by evidence without an authority cue. |

All four examples are constructed illustrations. The accountant's authority is appropriate only inside demonstrated competence; a root-level critique can be erroneous; and the reaction need not be intense in any cell.

## 3. Path formalization and the distinction from edge centrality

For observer \(i\), define a representation graph or small path category \(\mathcal G_i\) whose vertices are local modeling contexts and whose directed arrows are available interpretation/transport operations. This *is a proposed representation*, not a neurologically measured network.

A route \(p:u\leadsto v\) is a composable chain. \(T_i(p)\) is the corresponding transported inference/output when its maps are defined. The route may contain an authority-supplied operation \(A\) without being globally epistemically warranted.

A question \(q\) can be handled **frame-compliantly** if the observer can select a feasible route \(p'\) whose use does not require them to relinquish or rejustify their currently endorsed core commitments \(R_i\). The route can be indirect:

\[
u \xrightarrow{T_{uw}} w \xrightarrow{A_{wv}} v,
\]

even though the original direct route \(T_{uv}\) is blocked, contested, or inconvenient.

This is **rerouting**, not proof of an internalized hierarchy.

To formalize root impact, predeclare a set \(R_i\) of commitments central to the observer's articulated global orientation and an explicit support relation \(e\rightsquigarrow r\) ("the warrant for \(r\) relies on candidate bridge \(e\)"). Then for challenged bridges \(E(q)\), define the *potential rejustification set*:

\[
\mathcal D_i(q)=\{r\in R_i:\exists e\in E(q)\ \text{with}\ e\rightsquigarrow r\}.
\]

**Interpretation:** if the challenge succeeds, these commitments require independent support or reconsideration; they do not automatically become false. \(\mathcal D_i(q)\) is set-valued and depends on the declared support map. A challenge may be *perceived* to threaten \(R_i\) when it actually does not, or may genuinely require revision without being experienced as threatening; distinguish \(\widehat{\mathcal D}_i(q)\) from \(\mathcal D_i(q)\).

High \(\rho\) in TLICA should attach to **represented self-relevant contents**, not to graph edges by fiat. If the relation \(e\rightsquigarrow r\) is highly self-relevant, its challenge may be associated with high affective salience, but **no mathematical identity or psychological law equates graph centrality, \(\rho\), and response intensity**.

The metaphor of a "load-bearing edge" is not necessarily a graph-theoretic cut edge. Multiple paths may exist while all presently available paths presuppose the same questionable relation; conversely a unique edge may be trivial to replace without identity threat.

## 4. Predictive shape of the phenomenology

**Author's proposed characteristic response:** boundary transport conflict often has the implicit or explicit shape "that which you root your existence [in] is flawed." That does **not** mean a person literally thinks this sentence; it means the question is processed as demanding reconsideration of their organizing orientation.

This suggests two partially independent channels:

1. **Inference/task channel:** Can the person maintain a coherent route through the compartments, possibly by deference to an orthogonal authority?
2. **Root/identity channel:** Does the challenge seem to jeopardize commitments by which the person recognizes who they are and what counts as an intelligible world?

The affective hypothesis concerns the second, modulated by childhood associations, self-relative formation load, context, and slack. The first alone cannot establish the second.

A working candidate:

\[
I_i(q)=F\big(\widehat{\mathcal D}_i(q),\rho_i(\text{relevant contents}),a_i,\ell_i,S_i(t),\text{present threat},\text{history}\big).
\]

This is an unvalidated dependency schema, **not** a monotone law or scalar TLICA diagnosis. Actual cognitive/affective mechanisms could differ substantially.

## 5. Counterexamples we must retain

- **Ordinary deference with authority mediation:** a person who cannot recall a tax regulation asks an accountant. No existential rupture need occur.
- **Unwarranted authority deference without root-threat:** a prestigious but irrelevant speaker is believed by heuristic while the listener remains emotionally neutral. An epistemic problem can exist without felt rupture.
- **Root threat without authority:** a mathematical or autobiographical counterexample challenges an identity-anchoring belief.
- **Root threat without distress:** one can welcome deep revision or experience curiosity rather than aversion.
- **Intense response without root-threat:** present danger, misunderstanding, moral injury, sleep loss, trauma cues or frustration can all be live rivals.
- **Genuine authority expertise that requires fundamental revision:** a warranted expert correction might threaten a person's root model and still be true. Root-threatening feeling does not invalidate expertise.
- **Within-domain root-threat:** a single-domain challenge can expose an identity-defining assumption, so the mere within-/cross-domain split is not a necessary boundary of the phenomenon.

Therefore: **cross-compartment location**, **authority-mediated route**, **root implication**, **felt intensity**, **epistemic validity**, **present material sanction** and **behavioral outcome** are separate variables.

## 6. Corrected experimental discrimination

The previous 16-cell factorial remains useful for measuring authority use across within-/between-domain questions, but it **does not discriminate rerouting from root-threatening transport**.

Run a second, fully populated \(2^3=8\)-cell factorial or equivalent preregistered matched-design study:

- **Reroute available?** safe alternative valid explanation/probe route available vs requiring a direct contested bridge;
- **Root implication?** central endorsement-relevant support relation vs peripheral relation, with centrality independently measured **before** challenge and not inferred from distress;
- **Authority mediation?** route via source with appropriately demonstrated domain expertise vs parallel source-free route, holding content and truth constant.

Measure: choice of route, quality of rationale, inferred revision implications, privately endorsed core propositions before/after, reported affect and behavior. Distinguish a subject's *perceived* threat to commitments from analyst-defined *actual* justificatory dependencies. Match topic stakes and difficulty where possible.

**Rerouting prediction:** authority usage can occur in the root-safe condition and can increase when alternative routes are made available; therefore observed deference alone cannot diagnose "ontological capture."

**Root-impact prediction:** the effect of a warranted challenge on affective/identity-relevant outcomes depends more on *perceived threat to central commitments* than on the bare presence of authority or a cross-domain question. This may be mediated by current context; its existence, magnitude and sign are unverified.

**Contradictory outcomes:** if root centrality, independent of social threat and content valence, contributes no predictive power beyond generic cognitive difficulty and conformity, weaken the distinctive existential-boundary hypothesis. If adding or removing viable reroutes makes no difference to inference route choices, revise the rerouting model. If authority cue effects are pervasive and no route/commitment contrasts discriminate them, prefer simpler explanations.

**Ethics:** use low-stakes, voluntary tasks; never manipulate childhood punishment or put a participant's survival at risk.

## 7. Updated paper verdict

**Disclosed (conceptually):** "authority used in a route" does not entail "root commitments challenged." A route can invoke an orthogonal authority while leaving core commitments intact. Conversely, root-level challenge need not invoke an authority.

**Conjectured:** existentially weighted boundary challenges more reliably explain the author's described high-gain "hit a nerve" phenomenon than authority cues or cross-domain reasoning alone.

**Unverified:** empirical prevalence, developmental mechanism, and strength/shape of any interaction. The distinguishing probe has not run.

**Paper correction:** ordinary deference and authority-mediated transport are **not** opposing causal kinds. The target of the paper is **how social defaults can stand in as warranted bridges, and which challenges to such bridges threaten the global roots of an observer's situated reality**.
