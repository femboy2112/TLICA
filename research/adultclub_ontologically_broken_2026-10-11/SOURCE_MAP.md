## 2. Primary external items checked and their actual scope

1. **Shah, A. K., Mullainathan, S., and Shafir, E. (2012).** "Some Consequences of Having Too Little." *Science* 338(6107), 682–685. DOI: [10.1126/science.1222426](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1222426); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23118192/). **Inspected bibliographic/abstract record:** experimental scarcity was associated with attentional allocation shifts. **Does not show:** fixed cognitive limitation, uniformly poorer reasoning, or specifically authority-mediated cross-compartment bridges.

2. **van der Toorn, J., Tyler, T. R., and Jost, J. T. (2011).** "More than Fair: Outcome Dependence, System Justification, and the Perceived Legitimacy of Authority Figures." *Journal of Experimental Social Psychology* 47(1), 127–138. DOI: [10.1016/j.jesp.2010.09.003](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jesp.2010.09.003). **Inspected publisher abstract:** reports five studies, including experiments and field/panel studies, linking outcome dependence to perceived authority legitimacy and voluntary deference. **Does not show:** that a missing epistemic transport bridge is the cause or that everyone dependent internalizes authority.

3. **van der Toorn, J., Feinberg, M., Jost, J. T., Kay, A. C., Tyler, T. R., Willer, R., and Wilmuth, C. (2015).** "A Sense of Powerlessness Fosters System Justification: Implications for the Legitimation of Authority, Hierarchy, and Government." *Political Psychology* 36(1), 93–110. DOI: [10.1111/pops.12183](https://doi.org/10.1111/pops.12183). **Inspected publisher abstract:** studies link financial/job dependence and experimentally induced powerlessness with perceived legitimacy/system justification in studied settings. **Does not establish:** universal causal effects or the boundary-specific mechanism.

4. **Corriveau, K. and Harris, P. L. (2009).** "Choosing Your Informant: Weighing Familiarity and Recent Accuracy." *Developmental Science* 12(3), 426–437. DOI: [10.1111/j.1467-7687.2008.00792.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-7687.2008.00792.x); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19371367/). **Inspected publisher/PubMed abstract:** preschool children weigh familiar speaker versus observed accuracy. **Important rival:** children can selectively calibrate credibility rather than treating authority as infallible.

5. **Corriveau, K. H., Harris, P. L., Meins, E., et al. (2009).** "Young Children's Trust in Their Mother's Claims: Longitudinal Links With Attachment Security in Infancy." *Child Development* 80(3), 750–761. DOI: [10.1111/j.1467-8624.2009.01295.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-8624.2009.01295.x). **Inspected publisher abstract:** evidence cues and relational developmental factors influence children's judgments. **Does not establish:** that punishment of "why" questions imprints epistemic boundary fear.

6. **"Sensitive Periods in Development and Risk for Psychiatric Disorders and Related Endpoints: A Systematic Review of Child Maltreatment Findings" (2022).** *The Lancet Psychiatry* 9(12), 978–991. DOI: [10.1016/S2215-0366(22)00362-5](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2215-0366(22)00362-5); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36403600/). **Inspected review abstract:** of 118 observational studies, many reported timing effects, but the review did not identify a consistent peak sensitivity period across studied outcomes. **Important constraint:** childhood age-to-later-affect cannot be assumed universal or strictly monotone. This population and outcome differ from boundary transition reactions.

These six works do not provide six independent confirmation routes to TLICA; most concern distinct constructs and are linked through broader cognitive/social psychology traditions. Primary full text, methods, and replication audits remain outstanding.

## 3. Prior art to inspect before novelty claims

- System justification theory and critiques of its explanatory scope.
- Epistemic injustice, testimonial credibility, and social power (Miranda Fricker and critics).
- Pierre Bourdieu on symbolic power/habitus, without treating the paper as an exposition of his theory.
- Developmental social learning, epistemic vigilance, selective trust, and norm internalization.
- Threat learning, generalization, fear extinction, developmental stress timing, interoceptive prediction; distinct from any clinical attribution.
- Situated cognition, cognitive load, scarcity and opportunity costs of complex reasoning.
- Cultural evolution and path-dependent institutions; explicit material incentives versus cognitive representations.
- Social epistemology of testimony, expertise and unequal observation interfaces.
- Mathematical local-to-global frameworks and the **limits** of category/sheaf language in psychology.

**Do not make a novelty claim** until sources are read, competing explanations located, and any TLICA-specific residual mechanism is actually distinguished.

## 4. Outstanding source-map defects

- No study directly measuring boundary-crossing inference with authority cue, slack and bridge-warrant manipulated orthogonally.
- No validated operational measure for "age when this particular model boundary solidified" or for "self-relative environmental load."
- No representative evidence that a large fraction of adults carry this mechanism, and **no** evidentiary basis here for the author's rhetorical "99%".
- No direct measures of TLICA \(\rho,\phi,\sigma,\mu\) against independent psychological scales.
- No empirical population dynamics demonstrating hysteresis or institutional attractor stability.
- No independent cross-lab replication or fresh holdout of the distinctive new hypothesis.

## 5. Source discipline

A published abstract is primary evidence of **what the paper reports**, not independent confirmation of its findings or proof of the manuscript's causal bridges. The author's lived report is primary testimony of that report, not third-party measurement of mechanism. Link families, design differences, source-map adequacy, and potential contradictory findings must be explicitly assessed in the next paper round.
