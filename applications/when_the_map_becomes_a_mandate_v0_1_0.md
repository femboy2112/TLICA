## Abstract

A person can describe a genuine constraint without correctly identifying its causes, and can identify a cause without acquiring permission to harm anyone. This paper applies the Two-Layer Identity-Correlation Architecture (TLICA) to these transitions in the writings and reported experience of Ted Kaczynski. The application separates experiential contact, verification, identity integration, source attribution, the evaluation of alternatives, and moral authorization. It incorporates current TLICA distinctions between structure and field-reading, developmental nonstationarity, modeling and affective routing, locally useful sensors and global policies, and institutions as task-relative macrostates rather than collective minds. The historical analysis is bounded: institutional chronology, a public argument, retrospective interview material, prosecution-selected writing, and archival scholarship constrain a reconstruction without revealing an entire psychological trajectory. Six documentary conclusions survive at different scopes; none establishes a unique developmental mechanism. Four conditional formal results show why identity weighting does not itself drive the baseline law, why rejecting an alternative does not establish an inability to represent it, why a task-preserving institutional abstraction may discard ethically relevant information, and why descriptive agreement does not determine an action permission without additional normative premises. Executed finite witnesses and source-dependency removals test those internal distinctions, not human behavior. The resulting account treats attention-mediated foreclosure as one live explanation among revenge-first rationalization, reciprocal development, audience effects, substrate contributions, and knowing authorization. TLICA's present contribution is an explicit structure of evidentiary and inferential obligations. Its incremental empirical advantage remains to be established.

**Keywords:** TLICA; phenomenological reconstruction; political violence; institutional abstraction; agency; moral authorization; source criticism.

## 1. Introduction: the inference that needs explaining

This application begins with a question about transitions, not a psychological label: how can an account of experienced constraint become a mandate for action against other people? The problem is especially difficult when some concerns in the account are intelligible. Rejecting the action does not identify the error in every premise; recognizing a valid premise does not authorize the action. An explanation that moves too quickly in either direction can protect the very inference that requires examination.

The historical consequence is not figurative. The FBI places the beginning of Kaczynski's bombing campaign in 1978 and records three deaths, approximately two dozen injuries, and his arrest on 3 April 1996 [S01]. These consequences define the seriousness of the inquiry. They are not evidence that every early experience must have led toward them. Nor are they a warrant to turn the perpetrator into an exceptional intellectual protagonist while the people he harmed become incidental.

The paper's central claim is that **explanatory organization, practical selection, and moral authorization must remain distinguishable even when they interact**. An explanation organizes what an actor takes to be happening. A practical evaluation assesses what actions appear possible and effective. An authorization determines what the actor takes themselves to be permitted to do. Each can influence the others, but none is simply another name for them. A sophisticated explanation can accompany a defective permission; a correct refusal can coexist with a poor explanation; and a person can represent an alternative accurately while rejecting it for an objectionable reason.

TLICA supplies a vocabulary for keeping these distinctions attached to a developing, situated subject. Contact, verification, and identity-correlation are nonidentical. The source assigned to an experience is not certified by its intensity. The integration of a commitment into a self is not equivalent to the priority it receives in action. The model of another person can be informative without becoming an effective restraint. These are useful distinctions only if their application constrains what the analyst may infer [T01–T04].

The motivating conversational hypothesis was that repeated focus on autonomy and technological constraint might have progressively reorganized Kaczynski's interpretation and option evaluation. That hypothesis remains worth examining. This paper does not make it the organizing conclusion in advance. A completed application is not a completed causal explanation: the work here is to apply the architecture to actual documentary contrasts, state its conditional implications, and expose the rival histories the record still permits.

Three contributions follow. First, the paper reconstructs several *inference boundaries* in the available material: chronology versus retrospective significance, source genealogy versus validity, represented alternatives versus inadequate alternatives, and awareness of harm versus restraint. Second, it develops an application-level criterion for the legitimate transport of claims across institutional abstraction. Third, it reports what the framework changes in this analysis and what it does not establish beyond ordinary historical and moral-psychological methods.

The argument is therefore neither that violence necessarily requires epistemic collapse nor that epistemology is irrelevant to violence. A defective causal map may matter. A selectively maintained map may matter. A morally unacceptable priority may matter even when the relevant facts are represented. Their combination must be established rather than inferred from the eventual outcome.

## 2. Position among existing explanations

The distinction between radical opinion and radical action is not new. McCauley and Moskalenko explicitly separate these dimensions in their two-pyramids model and emphasize emotional experience in studying both [S10]. TLICA cannot claim discovery merely by declining to equate an unconventional belief with violent conduct. Its possible contribution lies at a finer descriptive level: identifying which transitions within a situated evaluation have evidence and which remain assumptions.

Bandura's moral-disengagement framework already describes ways in which conduct can be justified, responsibility displaced, injury minimized, and victims excluded from ordinary moral concern [S09]. The present account is compatible with these possibilities but does not diagnose them from a single rhetorical expression. In particular, awareness of injury does not eliminate moral justification as an explanation. Conversely, describing a harmful permission does not establish that previously operative moral standards were psychologically disengaged; the actor's standards may have been different, inconsistently applied, or explicitly subordinated.

Moen's philosophical criticism is especially important. He identifies an asymmetry in the evaluation of technological and nontechnological harms and argues that unsupported evaluative premises help carry the practical conclusion. His criticism also separates concerns about technology from acceptance of Kaczynski's overall program [S08, Section 3]. This is substantial prior work on what the Phase 1 manuscript called the truth-to-permission gap. The present paper does not claim that naming this gap is novel. It asks how a situated architecture can keep the factual, evaluative, and temporal parts of that criticism distinct.

Fleming provides a different constraint. His intellectual-origins study traces major components of Kaczynski's argument to Ellul, Morris, and Seligman through archival evidence, including private references and annotated material [S13]. This establishes a scholarly alternative to treating the worldview as an isolated eruption or deducing its sources from thematic resemblance alone. The origins of an idea, the actor's use of it, and the idea's validity are three separate questions. An intellectual influence is neither an endorsement of the later violence by the source author nor a sufficient cause of that violence.

Fleming's later study challenges both an uncomplicated environmentalist classification and an exclusive opposition between personal revenge and political motivation. It also identifies earlier wilderness-related writing that complicates a late-origin narrative [S07]. These findings limit the explanatory privilege that can be given to the later interview. The two Fleming articles are related archival research, not independent witnesses to the same internal state.

This literature positioning is bounded rather than exhaustive. Relevant sections of both Fleming articles and Moen's full-text reproduction were inspected for this draft. Bandura and McCauley–Moskalenko are used only for distinctions established in their available abstracts; their complete empirical arguments have not been reviewed here. No claims about the comparative predictive performance of these theories are made. A proper model comparison would require operational measures, an appropriate corpus, and a common evaluation task.

## 3. Architectural inheritance and the current theory seam

### 3.1 The analyst is not the historical subject

TLICA is indexed to a modeling subject and a time. This index is indispensable in historical application. The analyst now encounters a document; the historical subject once encountered a situation. The present accessibility of an interview is not the analyst's direct contact with the earlier event, and the emotional force of its narration does not measure what that event felt like when it occurred.

We therefore distinguish the record, its attributed content, the event represented, and the analyst's reconstruction. A reliable transcription might establish what was said while leaving the statement's accuracy unresolved. An accurate recollection might establish an event while leaving its causal contribution unresolved. A plausible mechanism might fit that event while failing to explain another part of the same life. These are different achievement levels, not increments on a single confidence scale.

The operational translation used here is deliberately restrained:

| TLICA term | Application question | Prohibited substitution |
|---|---|---|
| κ, contact | What encounter is evidenced, and for whose time and perspective? | Narrative vividness for historical contact intensity. |
| φ, toolkit-relative truth-indistinguishability | What verification pathway and discriminating tests are supported? | Conviction, elegance, or repetition for verification. |
| ρ, identity-correlation | How is a commitment integrated into the actor's self-relation? | Topic frequency for measured identity integration. |
| σ, source map | What causal or referential attribution connects experience to its supposed source? | Intense affect for an adequate attribution. |
| μ, probe machinery | What checking opportunities and probe weightings are evidenced? | An unmentioned objection for a proven unavailable probe. |
| Π, preservation-ranking | What is given priority when interests conflict? | High identity integration for an obligatory action priority. |

Only κ, φ, and ρ are the foundation's three coordinates. The other entries retain their different architectural types. Source-map adequacy, toolkit closure, independence, coherence, and discrimination are tracked separately, not compressed into an added scalar [T01]. No numerical coordinate values are estimated for Kaczynski.

An important asymmetry follows. The historian may not know whether a subject could construct a verification pathway. That is an *analyst access gap*. It does not establish that the subject's φ was undefined. Likewise, a present corpus may contain no counterargument without proving that the subject's toolkit contained none. The absence of a trace is evidence about the accessible archive before it is evidence about a mind.

### 3.2 Identity integration is not an action law

The current v5.5.0 architecture distinguishes slow lived-I structure G from two readings of it: the identity-load reading ρ = R(G), and the field-reading f = F(G, A). The fast occupied baseline follows the posited tracking law

$$
\dot b = \gamma(f-b), \qquad \gamma>0.
$$

Here A abbreviates the co-indexed field argument, not a new availability variable. The notation follows the current dynamical layer only within its stated scope. In particular, the foundation locates the reflexive actuator through changes to G that alter f, not through an additional force multiplying the law by ρ. The common-structure reading and the relevant dependencies remain named posits [T01].

This matters because an identity-loaded grievance is not thereby a mathematical cause of a particular action. The route proposed for investigation is instead:

> focus allocation → durable update of G → changed field-reading or option construction → changed evaluation or selection.

The arrows are an application-level dependency stencil, not fitted dynamics. A focus event may leave no durable update. A durable update may improve discrimination. A changed interpretation may leave behavior unchanged. An action can also occur without the proposed sequence. The theory's allowance for a route is not evidence that the route was taken.

The current bounded-drive result similarly blocks a dramatic but invalid narrative. Under the law's relevant fixed-metric and bounded-parameter assumptions, continuing drive need not make felt salience grow without bound. Multistability, where posited, belongs in the slow structure rather than being inferred from a scalar salience magnitude. Nothing in this apparatus licenses describing the historical trajectory as an inevitable escalation of an ever-increasing internal pressure [T01].

### 3.3 Development changes the apparatus doing the learning

Recent mainline work on developmental substrate nonstationarity refines the Self-Applied Architecture. Its useful distinction is between learning within an apparatus and changes in the apparatus's own response properties. A regulation policy calibrated to one substrate regime may cease to fit a later regime. The refinement preserves the earlier framework rather than turning development into the arrival of blank neurons that inject undifferentiated noise [T05].

For this application, the implication is methodological. It is invalid to take an adult's articulate self-account and project the same effective regulatory capacity backward across childhood, adolescence, and early adulthood. It is equally invalid to infer from development alone that a young person lacked every meaningful reflective contribution. Mechanism-online status, nonzero contribution, stable task-effective availability, and actual exercise are distinct. Neither a fixed birthday nor intellectual accomplishment supplies the missing measurements.

We do not assign the author of TLICA's autobiographical substrate roots to Kaczynski. We do not infer his affective parameters, developmental diagnosis, or maturation curve. The updated premise prevents a fixed-controller assumption; it does not fill the historical gap with a biological story. Adulthood is not treated as a static endpoint, and later changes could have several causes. This is a restriction on inference, not an additional explanation of the crimes.

### 3.4 Modeling, routing, and authorization

The Cold Frame application separates modeling another person from routing that model into one's own affective field [T03]. We denote these channels in prose as *modeling* and *routing*, avoiding confusion with R(G). Adequate modeling is not guaranteed by fluent description; routing is not guaranteed by modeling; and conduct is not determined solely by whether routing occurs.

This paper consequently distinguishes three application questions. Is the other person represented adequately? Does that representation affect the actor? Does it constrain the contemplated action? The third is not introduced as a new coordinate or as a hidden faculty. It concerns the operation of existing evaluation, preservation-ranking, and selection. A person can feel distress and still rank an objective above that distress. A person can lack a vivid emotional response yet act under a stable prohibition against harm.

Only this dissociation is imported. The Cold Frame's clinical categories and treatment claims are not used to classify the subject. No diagnosis follows from finding an instrumental sentence. The extension being proposed is modest: a complete account of harmful authorization cannot require either deficient information or absent feeling in every case.

### 3.5 A useful sensor is not entitled to govern everything

The Moreish application distinguishes a locally informative sensor from a global governor, and identifies a possible asymmetry between the evidence that installs a policy and the evidence allowed to revise it [T04]. This provides a candidate interpretation of the transition from detecting constraints to treating one class of constraints as the decisive explanation of nearly every problem.

The relevant inference is a scope expansion. An observation made under particular conditions becomes a policy applied across different conditions, subjects, and time horizons. Its original warrant is not automatically transported. A signal can continue to identify something real while being badly authorized as a global controller.

The present case is not equated with the autobiographical case that motivated Moreish. The shared object is an inference pattern. Whether Kaczynski's attention actually exhibited asymmetric updating, and whether that updating affected subsequent action, requires dated evidence. The conceptual resemblance opens a hypothesis; it does not validate the transfer.

## 4. Evidence design: what is being reconstructed

### 4.1 A bounded documentary corpus

The preserved Phase 1 package contains ten development observations with separate event and report dates. This draft reuses that material rather than relabeling it as a new holdout. It adds substantive engagement with Fleming's earlier intellectual-origins article and Moen's full-text argument, while retaining explicit limits on edition and archival verification.

The main evidentiary jobs are distributed. The FBI record supplies chronology. The manifesto supplies a public argument. The Kintz interview supplies attributed retrospective experience. The prosecution memorandum supplies adversarially selected private writing. Fleming supplies archival interpretation that can challenge simpler readings of those materials. Moen supplies philosophical criticism. These jobs are complementary but do not make all sources independent.

The archive is selected on a known outcome and on the investigator's current question. It is not a representative sample of the subject's life or writings. There has been no independent coding, exhaustive archival search, or preregistered historical model selection. The results below therefore concern inspected contrasts and formally specified implications, not population-level regularities.

### 4.2 A chronology that resists narrative smoothing

The central temporal anchors are sparse. Campaign onset is placed in 1978 [S01]. The interview assigns a wilderness-road episode to 1983 and was conducted in 1999 [S04]. Prosecution-selected writing is attributed to April 1971 [S06]. The public argument was published in 1995 [S03]. The legal opinion concerns proceedings in 1998 [S11].

These dates do not form a ready-made developmental arc. The 1999 account is evidence of a later narration concerning 1983; it is not a contemporaneous 1983 measurement. The 1995 argument is later than the onset it may seek to explain. The early selected writing does not acquire completeness because it predates the public argument. Temporal priority is necessary for a proposed preceding cause, but priority alone is not causal identification.

This application keeps three different outcomes apart: beginning the bombing campaign, changing the relative priority of activities, and constructing a later explanation of one's life. The same event might be relevant to one and incapable of causing another. Changing the outcome definition after a chronological contradiction would be a repair only if the change were made explicit.

### 4.3 Provenance is part of the evidence

Mirrors are not additional witnesses. The interview reproduction previously used in discussion includes composite source metadata; the separately presented Kintz article is preferred here, still without an original recording or publication facsimile. The prosecution memorandum is a reproduced advocacy document, not a judicial finding that its psychological interpretation is correct. Its selected passages and later scholarship discussing them have shared dependencies [S04–S07].

The source registry also distinguishes browser access from archival possession. We have not acquired the original journal exhibits. The author-hosted Moen PDF was reachable as parsed text, but screenshot requests failed with a cache error; the relevant argument was read in the HTML reproduction. Its metadata contains an abbreviated DOI error, corrected here against the bibliographic record to 10.1111/bioe.12494. The source's dated biographical remarks are not transported into the present [S08].

Such details can appear peripheral to interpretation. They are not. A seamless narrative can be assembled from an edited interview, an advocacy extract, and a later quotation of the same extract while falsely appearing to have three independent supports. The source map of the research itself must therefore be inspected before it is used to criticize the subject's source map.

## 5. Worked application I: experience, explanation, and learned categories

### 5.1 A reported refuge is not a measured identity network

Kintz's account attributes satisfaction with practical wilderness activities to Kaczynski, along with distress over the road episode and a subsequent decision to redirect effort toward reading about social change [S04]. These reports provide a legitimate entry into phenomenological interpretation. They do not directly measure an identity-correlation profile.

One possible TLICA reading is that a setting mattered not simply as a possession but as a realization of a preferred relation between effort, consequence, and autonomy. On that reading, a disturbance to the setting might also disturb the actor's model of a viable life. The relevant hypothesis concerns a relationship among contents: place, competence, self-direction, and the possibility of living acceptably. It is not the generic claim that losing something pleasant produces anger.

This reading has two advantages over an unqualified trait description. It specifies what the environmental event might have meant without declaring that meaning intrinsic to the event. It also allows the same event to have different consequences for people whose projects and commitments are differently organized. Its weakness is substantial: the surviving report is already a representation made after the later trajectory was known.

The hypothesis of an identity bottleneck would require more. A bottleneck means that multiple self-relations depend on a narrow structural connection, not merely that a topic matters greatly. Evidence might include records showing that alternative ways of sustaining autonomy were rejected because they threatened the identity organized around a particular arrangement. No such network has been reconstructed here. High ρ is not itself a diagnosis of fragility, and a deeply integrated commitment can support correction as well as resistance.

The warranted result is consequently a structured question: was a threatened arrangement also a threatened organizing commitment? The answer is compatible with the report but remains conjectural. We must not replace the uncertainty with the imagined sentence that the subject *must* have heard internally. A first-person reconstruction written by the analyst would be an illustration, not recovered testimony.

### 5.2 The source of an explanation can be socially mediated without face-to-face company

Fleming's intellectual genealogy makes a purely isolated-mind account inadequate as a default [S13]. A reader alone with books is still encountering other people's conceptual structures. Geographical solitude does not establish intellectual self-sufficiency, and a large bibliography does not establish independent checking. The question is how sources were taken up, differentiated, and tested.

TLICA helps separate at least three operations that a biography may collapse. An encounter can supply a concept. The concept can organize previously diffuse experience. That organized experience can then appear to confirm the concept. The third step is not necessarily circular: a genuinely new observation may discriminate between theories. It becomes epistemically weak when the purported confirmation is merely the original experience redescribed through the newly learned categories.

Consider a generic example, not a report about Kaczynski. A person learns a theory of institutional domination and recognizes their own frustration in its terms. The recognition may establish phenomenological fit. It does not yet establish that domination is the frustration's only cause, that every institution operates in the same manner, or that abolishing the institution would improve the person's situation. Those are additional claims with additional possible counterexamples.

The difference between a source's authority and the applicability of its model is equally important. An argument can be accurately attributed to a respected author while its transport to a new domain remains unsupported. A result concerning one experimental setting does not become a universal social explanation merely because the receiving reader finds the analogy illuminating. Source genealogy repairs *where the idea came from*; it does not settle *where the idea works*.

This is the point at which the method adopted here—analogy, mathematical articulation, new predictions, and contact with reality—has a strict application boundary. Analogy can nominate a model. Formal articulation can expose what the model entails. Neither stage is its validation. In this manuscript, the same discipline applies to the TLICA analogy: explanatory fit is the start of a test, not its end.

### 5.3 Why the 1983 episode cannot carry the origin claim

Given the stated dates, 1983 is later than 1978. The episode cannot therefore be a preceding cause of campaign onset. This conditional exclusion does not depend on a theory of personality and is not a discovery unique to TLICA. It remains valid even if a later account makes the episode emotionally central.

The architectural gain is the separation of three possible functions for that centrality. It could describe an activation of an already formed disposition. It could describe a reallocation of attention among existing commitments. Or it could be a narrative device that organizes an earlier and more complicated development. These functions make different demands on evidence. A dated decision to change reading priorities would not prove the creation of the underlying motive; a later vivid account would not establish either.

A source can therefore be sincere and still mislead the historian about origin if its stated significance is mistaken for temporal causation. Conversely, a chronology error in an analyst's reading does not prove that the subject's whole self-report was deceptive. The correction must be local. The paper rejects the backward causal ordering while keeping later relevance open.

### 5.4 Early revenge and political interpretation can coexist

The reproduced sentencing memorandum attributes revenge-oriented statements and uncertainty about wider consequences to early private writing [S06]. This is counterpressure against a simple story in which a later public theory first generated the willingness to act violently. It is not sufficient to prove that every later political statement was insincere.

The conceptual reason is straightforward. A desire for revenge has an object, and the way that object is described may itself be ideological. Conversely, an articulated ideology can provide a justification for an affective commitment that is not exhausted by the ideology. A clean separation between personal and political motives is therefore a hypothesis about the case, not a logical necessity.

The TLICA question is not which single label wins. It is whether the same content plays several roles: an explanation of loss, a source of felt grievance, an identity-bearing commitment, and a reason offered in justification. Evidence for one role does not automatically establish the others. The prosecution's selection, the actor's self-description, and Fleming's challenge must remain separately attributable.

The present record supports retaining both a revenge-first and a reciprocal-development account. The latter does not win simply because it can accommodate more material. Unless it specifies a sequence or discriminating prediction, reciprocity risks becoming an explanation flexible enough to fit any result.

## 6. Worked application II: from a restricted option to a claimed necessity

### 6.1 The public passage and its modal structure

Paragraph 96 represents nonviolent publication, discounts its impact, and presents killing as communicatively necessary [S03, paragraph 96]. That sentence states the narrow textual anchor; the following analysis concerns the inference it invites.

A represented alternative and an unavailable alternative are not the same object. To discuss a course of action and judge it ineffective is already to represent it at some resolution. This does not prove that it was practically feasible, emotionally sustainable, or available at an earlier date. It does defeat the claim that this passage alone demonstrates its complete absence from the actor's option construction.

We use three different predicates: *represented*, *practicable*, and *endorsed*. They are analytical predicates over an option in a context, not additions to TLICA's foundation. An option can satisfy the first but not the second. It can satisfy the first two but not the third. A refusal can arise because an option is genuinely blocked, because expected results are judged insufficient, because the goal is non-negotiable, or because the actor values something else more highly.

The distinction changes the research task. Evidence that a person dismissed a lawful route does not by itself show a loss of agency. It may instead show an objectionable evaluation, an inflated demand for impact, inaccurate forecasts, or a moral ranking that gives insufficient force to refusal. These possibilities call for different explanations, even where their practical output is the same.

### 6.2 Modal escalation and the threshold hidden in adequacy

A prediction of disappointing results has a different logical form from a demonstration that no acceptable alternative exists. The first concerns an expected consequence under assumptions. The second quantifies over alternatives and imports a criterion of acceptability. A practical claim of necessity must therefore identify both its comparison set and its standard of success.

Suppose an actor's objective is not merely to express a view but to achieve a particular degree of uptake. Label that threshold θ. This symbol is an illustrative standard, not an estimated parameter of the case. A route that falls below θ can be judged inadequate even when it remains possible and has some effect. Raising θ can make more options fail without changing the options' physical availability. Conversely, lowering a demanded level of impact can change the practical comparison without changing any belief about the world.

The key question then becomes: why is that threshold entitled to govern the treatment of other people? A forecast can inform whether an action achieves a goal, but the goal's priority is not established by that forecast. Nor does the mere existence of an effective action make it permissible. The space of practically effective actions and the space of ethically admissible actions need not coincide.

This analysis does not prove that a harmless route would have obtained the desired result. It removes a false burden. One need not demonstrate an equally effective substitute before rejecting an impermissible action. Refusal, delay, and acceptance of limited impact are themselves candidates in a complete practical analysis. The actor's dissatisfaction with them is part of what needs explaining, not evidence that the world eliminated them.

### 6.3 The sensor-to-governor transition

The Moreish distinction gives a specific candidate reading of this shift. A detector of institutional constraints may perform useful local work. Promoting it into a governor occurs when its output determines not only what constraints deserve attention, but which interests can be overridden and which alternatives count as real. The defect is a transfer of authority across domains, not necessarily a false signal at the detector.

An epistemic ratchet would add an asymmetry: evidence consistent with the grievance strengthens the policy, while adverse consequences are permitted to count only as further evidence of the grievance. This mechanism is not established for the historical subject. It is a testable proposal about how a policy can protect itself against the very outcomes that should prompt its reconsideration.

A genuine probe would examine whether adverse evidence changed the prediction, the policy, the interpretation of the evidence, or none of these. Merely observing a continuing commitment cannot distinguish steadfastness in the face of irrelevant criticism from self-protection against relevant criticism. The content of the challenge matters. So does whether the actor understood it, had the capacity to assess it, and responded before or after a consequential decision.

The application therefore keeps conviction, source-map adequacy, and probe selection distinct. It does not infer high φ from the apparent rigor of the argument. A sophisticated argument can make more assumptions visible, or it can provide more routes for defending a favored conclusion. Which occurred is a question about the use of reasoning, not its verbal complexity.

## 7. Worked application III: institutions, people, and the abstraction error

### 7.1 A system can be real without being a single subject

The current Distributed Institutional Realization proposal represents an institution through indexed people, typed relations, and artifacts, then identifies a task-relative macrostate by its response signature. Let

$$
X=(\mathbf P,\mathbf R,\mathbf D),\qquad
q_{\mathcal T}(X)=[X]_{\sim_{\mathcal T}},
$$

where two realizations are equivalent if their responses agree across the declared situations and inputs relevant to task family $\mathcal T$. The situational field remains external to the micro-realization. The proposal is research-tier; its exact quotient results are formal, not empirical validation of every institution [T06].

This matters for interpreting any global system claim. A large organization may exhibit robust behavior even when its participants have different motives and incomplete local knowledge. Explaining that behavior need not posit a secret common intention or a literal collective mind. Yet robustness of a macro-pattern does not make every participant causally interchangeable for every question.

Kaczynski's systemic analysis should not be caricatured as requiring a conspiracy among all relevant people; Fleming explicitly treats its distributed intellectual structure [S13]. The stronger criticism is task mismatch. A model built to describe broad technological expansion may omit precisely the distinctions required to attribute individual responsibility or assess an action involving a particular person.

The TLICA application must therefore recover the abstraction's declared task. What observations does the macro-model preserve? Which variations does it intentionally discard? What additional facts become necessary when the question changes from describing an institutional pattern to judging treatment of individuals? These are methodological questions that remain pertinent even if the system-level analysis is partly accurate.

### 7.2 Task equivalence is not moral equivalence

Two clerks can realize the same access-control behavior while differing in their knowledge, authority, consent, or role in adopting a policy. Their substitution may preserve the door's response and fail to preserve the facts relevant to responsibility. Similarly, the same institution may operate differently after a relation or rule changes while many of its members remain the same. A task-relative quotient is not a list of people renamed.

This elementary example identifies the mistake in transporting a property across an abstraction without checking invariance. The error need not be dehumanization in the strong psychological sense. It may instead be the application of a representation outside the task for which its equivalence relation was defined. A compressed model can be useful and still be insufficient for the next question.

The ethical point is not that organizational membership never matters. Membership, authority, contribution, knowledge, and responsibility can all be relevant when evidenced. Their relevance does not make them identical. Nor does a correct responsibility judgment by itself establish permission to injure. The abstraction question and the authorization question must each be answered.

### 7.3 Accurate representation can coexist with harmful authorization

Kintz's interview also attributes awareness of severe human consequences to the subject and records an unresolved response to the dilemma [S04]. This does not identify what was understood or felt at each earlier decision. It does make an across-the-board assumption of unrepresented harm untenable as a default interpretation of the later account.

Here the Cold Frame distinction requires an additional caution. A represented person may fail to evoke affect; a represented person may evoke affect that is then overridden; or an actor may adhere to a harmful priority without needing to conceal the person's reality from themselves. The same outward action can be compatible with these different configurations.

The analyst should therefore not infer the absence of empathy from the existence of harm, nor infer adequate moral restraint from evidence of distress. The question is whether the represented interests of another have operative standing in the relevant decision. That is a question about evaluation and constraint, not simply information or emotional amplitude.

The paper explicitly adopts a normative prohibition against instrumental attacks on nonconsenting persons for the purpose of advancing the attacker's message. This stance is not derived from TLICA's identity equations. It is the ethical boundary within which the analysis is conducted. Understanding the actor's permission structure is an explanatory task; accepting that structure is not required for completing the task.

## 8. Four conditional results of the application

### Proposition 1. Identity-metric variation alone does not change the stated baseline law

**Assumptions.** Two model instances have the same b, f, and positive γ, and differ only in the identity-load metric ρ. Their baseline dynamics is the v5.5.0 primitive law.

**Result.** Their instantaneous baseline derivatives are equal.

**Proof.** Both derivatives are γ(f − b); ρ does not occur in the expression. If subsequent identity-structure changes alter f, the assumption of equal f no longer holds, and the conclusion need not persist. This is why a proposed reflexive actuator requires the structure-to-field-reading bridge rather than the bare claim that identity importance increased. At the cogito anchor, b = f = 1 gives derivative zero. □

This is a conditional result in the adopted model, not a claim that changes in human identity never affect behavior. The finite witness checks 32 matched configurations with two different metric weights and obtains no derivative mismatches. A deliberately modified law that inserts a metric factor separates the derivatives in a positive mismatch control. The construction exposes a formal confusion; it does not estimate historical dynamics.

### Proposition 2. Negative evaluation does not entail representational absence

**Assumptions.** An evaluation explicitly takes an alternative a as its object. Representation here means at least the construction needed to perform that evaluation; it does not mean full practical availability.

**Result.** That evaluation is not evidence that a was wholly absent from representation at the time of evaluation.

**Proof.** Under the stated meaning, performing an evaluation of a presupposes the relevant representation. Whether a was practicable, sufficiently detailed, voluntarily retrievable under another condition, or endorsed remains open. □

The distinction is logical and definitional. It does not adjudicate the subject's entire agency. Applied to a historical document, it constrains only what that document licenses, and cannot silently be transported to earlier acts. It also does not prove that every formally named alternative was psychologically live in the stronger agency-theoretic sense.

### Proposition 3. A property descends to an institutional quotient exactly when it is constant on its classes

Let q map micro-realizations X to their equivalence classes under a declared task. A property h can be recovered entirely from the macrostate if there exists a function $\bar h$ such that h = $\bar h\circ q$.

**Result.** Such a function exists if and only if h takes the same value on every member of each equivalence class.

**Proof.** If the factorization exists and q(x) = q(y), then h(x) = $\bar h(q(x))=\bar h(q(y))$ = h(y). Conversely, if h is constant on classes, define $\bar h([x])=h(x)$. Constancy makes the definition independent of the representative. □

This is a standard quotient-factorization result, not a novelty claim in mathematics. Its application is the warning that a task's response signature need not preserve every fact needed by an ethical judgment. The finite witness has eight micro-realizations and two task classes. The task response descends; a consent-related fact deliberately omitted from that task does not. Consent is not itself reduced to a response score, and the example does not assert that an institution with that structure exists in the case.

### Proposition 4. The paper's descriptive premises do not select an action permission without a normative bridge

**Assumptions.** The formal vocabulary includes descriptive premises D and an actor-level permission predicate J. The adopted descriptive axioms place no constraint selecting J for the contemplated action h.

**Result.** D alone does not entail J(h), or its negation, within this formalization.

**Proof.** Complete the same descriptive interpretation with J(h) true in one model and false in another. Both satisfy D because no supplied axiom fixes J(h). A further premise is needed to exclude one completion. □

The premise about the formal language is essential. This does not refute every form of ethical naturalism or prove that facts are irrelevant to moral judgment. It shows that a normative conclusion has not been derived merely by completing a descriptive model whose axioms do not supply that conclusion. The truth-to-permission gap is therefore an explicit inference obligation, not a claim that all moral judgments are arbitrary.

The countermodels represent possible *actor permission assignments*. They are not two equally acceptable moral policies. The paper's stated prohibition remains in force. One assignment can be judged wrong even when its descriptive inputs agree with those of the other.

## 9. Feedback, self-confirmation, and historical nonidentification

A plausible focus-mediated mechanism is a loop, not a line. Experience is interpreted using a learned structure; that interpretation influences attention and action; the resulting encounters may then reinforce the structure. But a loop drawn on paper does not establish that the actor's policy produced the later evidence or that reinforcement was epistemically illegitimate.

We distinguish three kinds of apparent confirmation. **External confirmation** comes from an observation that discriminates among rival models under a declared task. **Selection-conditioned confirmation** comes from seeing a favorable subset of a broader field. **Policy-conditioned confirmation** comes from an outcome partly produced by the policy being assessed. The last can be informative, but its interpretation must include the intervention. It cannot automatically be treated as passive evidence that the policy's original explanation was right.

A generic example makes the distinction precise without explaining the historical case by analogy. Suppose two investigators each report two observations consistent with a theory. One examined a mixed set and retained only favorable observations. The other encountered a uniform set and reported all of it. Their reports match, but their sampling histories differ. The observed report does not identify the procedure that generated it. Further information about selection is required.

The finite supplement includes this deliberately constructed observational-equivalence witness. It does not estimate how often either procedure occurs. Its function is to prevent narrative coherence from being promoted to a measurement of probe quality. An equivalent ambiguity applies when a late explanation is compatible with both pre-action reasoning and post-action rationalization.

For the historical application, a self-reinforcing-loop hypothesis would gain force only if dated records showed the relevant order: a prior interpretation, a consequential allocation or action, a changed encounter field, and a subsequent interpretation that treated the change as confirmation while omitting its dependence on the earlier choice. The current pilot does not supply that full sequence. It identifies where to look and which arrows must not be inferred from a completed narrative.

The same reflexive warning applies to this paper. The original hypothesis directed source selection. A text fitting that hypothesis cannot then serve as a fresh holdout. Nor does adding more TLICA vocabulary make the selected material independent. The paper must allow its own successful redescription to be a product of analyst selection rather than evidence for the historical mechanism.

## 11. What TLICA adds—and what it does not

A fair application should not claim that ordinary historical method was incapable of reading a calendar or that moral philosophy needed TLICA to separate facts and values. The chronology exclusion is ordinary source criticism. The distinction between opinion and action already has an established literature. Moen supplies a substantive ethical criticism, and Fleming supplies archival work far beyond this pilot. The framework's contribution must be stated more precisely.

The first addition is an **indexed access discipline**. It distinguishes the historian's encounter with a record from the subject's encounter with an event, and separates absent evidence about a verification pathway from an absent pathway for the actor. This prevents a specific, recurrent inferential substitution. The present application demonstrates that the distinction changes the wording and scope of its conclusions; it does not yet show that TLICA-trained coders outperform other careful historians.

The second addition is a **typed account of a possible developmental transition**. Rather than saying that a belief grew stronger, the framework asks whether attention changed structure, whether structure changed interpretation or available checking, and whether those changes altered evaluation. It also preserves the reverse direction from action to later explanation. This decomposition supplies more specific places where evidence could defeat the preferred account. It does not supply numerical dynamics for this life.

The third addition is an **explicit separation of integration, evaluation, and response**. A commitment can be central without governing every choice. Another person can be modeled without strongly affecting the actor, can affect the actor without constraining action, or can constrain action through a principle despite limited affective vividness. These differences alter what a proposed correction would have to address. They also block a diagnosis of low empathy from the observation of harm alone.

The fourth addition, sharpened by the current institutional proposal, is a **transport test for system-level explanation**. The quotient criterion says exactly when information needed by a later question has survived abstraction. A macro-model's success on one task is not permission to use it on a different task without checking the omitted distinctions. This is a formal contribution to the application's bookkeeping, not a novel theorem or a demonstrated map of the institutions involved in the case.

The fifth addition is **reflexive exposure of the analyst's own commitment**. A hypothesis that is personally attractive to its author can shape attention as readily as the subject's hypothesized frame. The application therefore retains the initial story as provenance, preserves corrections, and identifies the development corpus as such. It does not count its own repeated readings as independent corroboration.

These additions establish a conceptual advantage over the *particular flattened reconstruction with which the conversation began*: fewer conflated claims and more explicit points of failure. They do not establish an empirical advantage over competent historical, philosophical, or psychological alternatives. The latter claim would require blinded coding, a common task, a fresh corpus, and an outcome criterion chosen before seeing the result.

A possible unfavorable result must remain meaningful. TLICA may provide useful vocabulary while adding no discrimination. It may create extra annotation burdens without improving reliability. It may even tempt an analyst to invent latent structure where plain-language uncertainty would be better. If such outcomes occur, the framework's claimed incremental value should be reduced rather than rescued by redescribing the failure as evidence of its depth.

## 12. Rival explanations and the next discriminating observations

Six accounts remain live at different levels. They are neither exhaustive nor mutually exclusive. The aim is not to select a personality type but to identify which explanatory links are supported.

**Attention-mediated foreclosure** predicts that relevant alternatives or corrective probes were progressively downgraded before consequential choices, with identifiable changes in self-relation. It is weakened if the supposed narrowing appears only afterward, if the actor continued to engage the relevant criticism accurately, or if action is better explained by an explicitly maintained permission despite that engagement.

**Revenge-first rationalization** predicts that a revenge commitment precedes the later public rationale and that the rationale poorly distinguishes earlier choices. It is weakened where contemporaneous ideological distinctions constrained the actor before the public justification. Early revenge language alone cannot settle the matter because the object and interpretation of revenge may already carry political content.

**Reciprocal development** predicts changes in both directions: interpretations modifying conduct and conduct modifying justification or identity. It needs dated evidence for both directions. General compatibility with everything is not support. A model with more routes must earn their inclusion by explaining observations a simpler model cannot.

**Audience and genre effects** predict that near-contemporaneous accounts vary with their intended audience or institutional setting. Private writing, public argument, an interview, and litigation are not interchangeable reporting conditions. If an apparent temporal shift tracks genre instead, a developmental inference weakens. Where date and audience are confounded, the comparison remains ambiguous.

**Substrate or clinical contributions** require appropriate evidence, period-specific interpretation, and qualified clinical methods. The developmental update makes a fixed-apparatus assumption less defensible but does not identify a particular clinical cause. A historical application of TLICA cannot substitute for a clinical assessment, and the paper neither adjudicates diagnostic disputes nor makes personal attributes into violence indicators.

**Knowing authorization without decisive factual confusion** predicts that relevant harm and alternatives are represented yet subordinated to another commitment. It challenges a purely informational repair account. It does not exclude an earlier influence of attention or an error elsewhere. Evidence of a specific factual correction reversing the permission would weaken its application to that decision.

The highest-value next observation is therefore not simply another vivid story. It is a contemporaneous pre-action record that distinguishes an unavailable route from a rejected one, or that separates prior reasoning from later rationalization. New evidence must be sampled under a rule declared before confirmatory coding. The present materials, including the newly inspected scholarship, remain development material.

Comparison cases should include nonviolent critics and explicit refusals of violence under relevant grievances. They must be selected by a defensible rule rather than by how neatly they dramatize the thesis. A single notorious case cannot estimate how frequently a pattern occurs or forecast what a person with superficially similar interests will do. No causal prevention claim follows from identifying a possible point at which another path might have existed.

## 13. Ethical stance, limitations, and the application boundary

Explanation and exemption are different questions. Unchosen experiences can shape later capacities without removing responsibility for every subsequent act. Deliberation can be present without guaranteeing a good decision. The 1998 opinion records a competency determination in a specific legal proceeding; it is not a global measure of mental health or a retrospective certification of every component of agency [S11].

This study does not treat technological criticism, solitude, mathematical interest, trauma, sexuality, gender, or unconventional living as evidence of dangerousness. It contains no individual risk score and no route for operationalizing one. Its purpose is to analyze inferential and normative transitions in a known documentary case, not to produce a screening profile.

The privacy boundary is equally explicit. Public availability does not make every intimate detail analytically necessary. The paper does not reproduce clinical records, import the TLICA author's private autobiographical material into the subject's history, or infer protected characteristics as causes. Early-life episodes not supported by inspected primary records remain excluded from the causal argument, not declared irrelevant in principle.

Nor does the paper provide tactical content, target-selection analysis, device information, evasion, or advice for making violent communication effective. The action-policy discussion is critical and nonoperational. The victims' status as people with their own interests does not depend on how much phenomenological detail survives about them in a perpetrator-centered archive.

The principal limitations remain retrospective access, selective preservation, adversarial document assembly, dependence among sources, and an unvalidated mapping from text to TLICA constructs. The external literature review is not systematic. The formal witnesses establish consequences of stated assumptions, and the dependency audit records what this selected set currently supports. Neither supplies new independent evidence about the subject's inner history.

Accordingly, the work is not contextually saturated. Known archival and probe gaps remain. Proposition-level clarification and source-map resolution are separate: some inferences are excluded even though the developmental source map remains unresolved. A complete explanation would require additional evidence, not merely a more integrated version of this prose.

## 14. Conclusion

The most defensible TLICA application to this case is not a single diagnosis or an inevitable developmental arc. It is a structured distinction between what was experienced, how it was interpreted, how the interpretation was checked, how it became integrated, what alternatives were represented, and what permissions were accepted. The documents constrain those questions unevenly. Their unevenness is part of the result.

Current TLICA updates improve the formulation in concrete ways. The G-to-field-reading correction blocks identity intensity from being treated as a direct action force. Developmental nonstationarity blocks the projection of one fixed regulatory apparatus across a life. The Moreish distinction identifies an unearned transfer from a locally informative signal to a global policy. The institutional quotient supplies a criterion for when an abstraction preserves the facts needed by a new question. Modeling and routing remain distinct from operative moral restraint.

The paper's central historical hypothesis is therefore conditional: repeated ways of attending may have helped organize the field from which Kaczynski interpreted constraints and evaluated action. That hypothesis remains in competition with other accounts. The strongest completed results are narrower: a chronology exclusion, several documentary distinctions, an explicit institutional transport condition, and the non-entailment of a moral permission from the supplied descriptive premises.

A map becomes a mandate only through additional commitments. Some concern causation and efficacy; others concern whose interests count, which losses are acceptable, and whether refusal remains an admissible response. The task of an application paper is not to make these commitments disappear inside a compelling story. It is to expose them clearly enough that the explanation, including its use of TLICA, can be challenged.
