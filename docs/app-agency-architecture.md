## What it builds

Where *Free Will* defines free choice, *Agency Architecture* treats free choice as the
**canonical case** and derives *everything else about agency* as a structural variation of
it. Agency stops being a yes/no property and becomes an **I-relative profile** across several
dissociable axes: live-option construction, evaluative engagement, operator selection,
substrate coupling, [Mode B](modes-of-development.md) availability, ownership, and
[identity-correlation](three-coordinates.md). The will is handled strictly as a *function*,
not a faculty or a thing — there is willing, there are will-acts, but no "will-substance."

## The orders of willing

The foundation's three [imprinting pathways](substrate-focus-and-imprinting.md) describe how
the world reaches *in*; this paper turns them around to describe willing reaching *out*:

- **First-order** — directing focus and attention.
- **Second-order** — directing thought and deliberation.
- **Third-order** — directing action through the body.
- **Constructed orders (4+)** — willing through *other people and structures*: asking,
  coordinating a team, acting through an institution.

It also names three **strategies** of willing: **direct** (push toward the goal),
**configurational** (arrange your future environment so the habit forms itself), and
**slingshot** (time a small precise act to ride an existing gradient) — with explicit nods to
phronesis, kairos, and wu-wei. The hard constraint over all of it: *you cannot will what your
[toolkit](access-to-intrinsic-structure.md) cannot construct as a live option.*

## The taxonomy of variation

The center of the paper is a **seventeen-family taxonomy** of agency configurations, each
read as a specific modification of the six conditions:

- **akrasia** (the bundle selects against its own evaluation),
- **compulsion / addiction** (substrate *capture* — alternatives aren't live from inside),
- **ADHD-like configurations** (an implementation gap at the first two orders),
- **learned helplessness** (the projection toolkit has collapsed),
- **depression / anxiety contraction**, **dissociation / alienation** (ownership fails),
- **apparent choice / confabulation**, **manipulation**,
- **habit** (chosen vs. automatic vs. compulsive vs. skilled),
- **flow and mastery** (which *look* automatic from outside but keep ownership and clean
  sourcing intact),
- and **compounds** — because the clinically and philosophically interesting cases are almost
  always combinations.

A subtle, useful result: **Mode B is one capacity but its objects are many, and its
availability is asymmetric** across orders — which is why "reflective access" can be present
for one kind of content and absent for another in the same person.

## Responsibility, done carefully

Agency without a theory of responsibility would be incomplete, so the paper builds one — and
insists it is **graded and profile-indexed, never zero-sum**. The triad from the companion —
**ownership / responsibility / accountability** — is kept strictly apart, captured in a
ten-component **attribution profile**. Two disciplines recur: *"reduction is not absolution"*
(diminished agency lowers attribution without erasing accountability), and a
**developmental-window** overlay that conditions attribution when the agent's equipment was
still forming. There's even a notion of *responsibility for becoming* — for the profile you
helped shape over time.

## Reading the experiments

A whole section is devoted to the **empirical interface**, and its core move is a discipline:
lab paradigms test *narrow* objects, not "free will." It separates the timing markers that
get conflated — preparation, selection, report, movement, outcome, recognition are all
*different moments* — and concludes that, e.g., Libet measures thin *free selection*, the
readiness potential is *preparation, not preemption*, and Wegner's results sort into five
distinct configurations rather than one global "agency is illusory." It promotes its
operational candidates into **eighteen formal predictions**, with the ownership/sourcehood
dissociation under manipulation flagged as the flagship discriminator.

## What it leaves open

Extensively catalogued (and honestly so): how to *weight* the components of an attribution
profile; where the floor of "no accountability at all" sits; operational tests for
source-status and Mode B (named the highest-priority bottleneck); modeling group agency
*without* a group-mind; and clinical translation, which it explicitly marks as **not yet
validated and not treatment advice**. Some items are flagged as *principled limits* (e.g.
neural implementation of the operator-bundle — welcome to map, but reducing the architecture
to it would be a failure) rather than to-do items.

Its closing line mirrors the companion's: the taxonomy *"does not deliver verdicts on
agency-or-not. It makes the analysis structurally navigable."*
