# Translations

[← Wiki home](README.md) · Source: [File 5, §12](../foundation/5_translations_open_problems_conclusion.md)

---

The architecture **maps onto** many existing frameworks — without **depending** on any of
them. Each correspondence is structural: the theory can be read *into* a prior framework
under a particular interpretation, but it stands on its own posits. Just as importantly,
it marks where it genuinely **clashes** rather than papering over the difference.

This page is a kind of bilingual dictionary. TLICA was built from scratch — from the bare
fact "I am I" outward — but the territory it describes is the same territory that
phenomenology, psychoanalysis, predictive processing, and the no-self traditions have all
walked. So the same landmarks show up under different names. Showing the correspondences
does two jobs at once: it lets a reader who already speaks one of those languages find
their bearings, and it lets the theory be *checked* against bodies of evidence it didn't
itself generate.

Before the table, three pieces of vocabulary that recur below, glossed once:

- **ρ (identity-correlation)** — in plain terms, how much of *you* is bound up in a thing;
  the stuff whose loss would actually change who you are. Your child sits high; the brand
  of pen on your desk sits low. Formally it runs from 0 to 1, with the core "I" pinned at
  **ρ = 1**.
- **φ (phi, evidential force)** — roughly, how *firmly grounded* a content feels, how much
  you could justify or trace it if pressed. A proof you can rebuild has high φ; a hunch you
  can't explain has low or undefined φ.
- **κ (kappa, contact)** — how directly something is touching your experience right now:
  the cold mug in your hand has high contact, a country you've only read about has low
  contact.

> **In plain terms —** these three are just three different questions you can ask about
> anything in your experience: *how much is it me?* (ρ), *how sure am I of it?* (φ), and
> *how directly am I in touch with it right now?* (κ). The whole point of TLICA is that
> these are three separate dials, not one. The frameworks below each tend to grab one or
> two of these dials and call it the self.

## Convergences

Frameworks the architecture translates into cleanly:

| Framework | Correspondence |
|---|---|
| **Self-model theory** (Metzinger, Friston, Seth) | The lived I ≈ the content of the phenomenal self-model |
| **Psychological continuity** (Parfit, Schechtman) | The persistence relation ≈ cross-temporal identity |
| **Phenomenology** (Husserl, Zahavi, Merleau-Ponty) | φ ≈ evidential force; ρ ≈ ego-relevance; κ ≈ embodied contact |
| **Self-through-other** (Hegel, Sartre, Lacan) | [Mode A](modes-of-development.md) ≈ master–slave, "the look," mirror stage |
| **No-self traditions** (Buddhist analysis, Albahari) | The ρ-structure ≈ the *constructed* self |
| **Extended mind** (Clark, Chalmers) | Contents sourced beyond the body can still carry real ρ |
| **Embodied cognition** | [Third-order imprinting](substrate-focus-and-imprinting.md) ≈ body-mediated content |
| **Predictive processing** (info-bottleneck variants) | The lossy-compression pathways ≈ compression hierarchies |
| **Classical / Pavlovian conditioning** | [Osmotic imprinting](substrate-focus-and-imprinting.md) ≈ substrate-level association |
| **Implicit cognition** (IAT, dual-process) | Implicit ≈ osmotic substrate patterns (undefined φ); explicit ≈ verification-pathway construction |
| **Attachment theory** (Bowlby, Ainsworth) | Attachment patterns ≈ osmotically-imprinted third-order patterns |
| **Tacit knowledge / expertise** (Polanyi, Dreyfus) | Know-how ≈ substrate proto-patterns grounding skilled performance |
| **Naturalist epistemology of math** | Tools acquired by encountering a structure-instantiating world |

### Walking through the mappings

The table is dense, so here is each row unfolded — what it agrees on, and, where the
source flags it, where the fit is partial.

**Self-model theory (Metzinger, Friston, Seth).** These thinkers hold that the self isn't a
thing in the world but a *model* the brain builds and then inhabits — you live inside a
representation of yourself the way you live inside a video-game avatar you've forgotten is
an avatar. TLICA's "lived I" — the populated, developed self, as opposed to the bare
cogito — lines up with exactly that modeled content.
*Where it agrees:* the self is built, not given; it is content, not the viewer of content.
*Where it can clash:* see the Tensions section — TLICA still keeps a single un-modeled
anchor (the cogito at ρ = 1) that a pure model-all-the-way-down reading does not.

**Psychological continuity (Parfit, Schechtman).** The classic puzzle: what makes the
person who wakes up tomorrow the *same* person as you tonight? The continuity tradition
answers: overlapping chains of memory, intention, and character. TLICA has a formal stand-in
for "same person across time" — written **∼ᵐ_H** (read it as "the persistence relation
for I number *m*, in the human-scale frame H"), the relation that says these two
time-slices belong to one ongoing self.

> **In plain terms —** ∼ᵐ_H is just the math symbol for "still me tomorrow." TLICA agrees
> with Parfit that this sameness is a real relation you can lose or keep, not a magic
> indivisible soul-token.

**Phenomenology (Husserl, Zahavi, Merleau-Ponty).** This is the tradition that studies
experience *from the inside*, carefully describing what showing-up is actually like. The
three TLICA coordinates each have a phenomenological cousin: **φ** is the felt *evidential
force* of a content (how compellingly it presents itself as so), **ρ** is *ego-relevance*
(how much it concerns *me*), and **κ** is *embodied contact* (the felt nearness of the
body's grip on the world).
*Where it agrees:* both insist these are distinct dimensions of experience, not one blurry
"feeling." *Where it can clash:* TLICA's method is structural — it builds a formal graph
and proves things about it — whereas phenomenology describes. The source notes this for the
mood case specifically: TLICA's prediction *matches* the phenomenological description of
mood, but reaches it by a different (structural) road.

**Self-through-other (Hegel, Sartre, Lacan).** A deep theme across these three: you only
become a self by bouncing off other selves. Hegel's master–slave struggle, Sartre's "the
look" (the jolt of being *seen* by someone, which suddenly makes you an object), Lacan's
mirror stage (the infant recognizing itself in the mirror and the caregiver's gaze) — all
describe the self forming through opposition and recognition. TLICA's **[Mode A](modes-of-development.md)**
— adversarial differentiation, the drawing of the I/not-I boundary by pushing *against*
what isn't you — is the structural cousin. Husserl's I-Thou and Buber's dialogue fit here too.

> **From the inside —** think of the first time a stranger's stare made you suddenly
> aware of your own body, your posture, your face. That snap into self-consciousness *is*
> Mode A doing its work: you find your edge by feeling the other press on it.

**No-self traditions (Buddhist analytical philosophy, Albahari).** These traditions argue
the unified, persisting self is a *construction* — a useful fiction the mind keeps
assembling, with no solid core behind it. TLICA's **ρ-structure** — the whole web of
graded identity-correlation, everything you've integrated into "me" — maps onto exactly
that *constructed* self.
*Where it agrees:* the populated self is assembled and could have been assembled
differently. *Where it can clash:* TLICA does not let the construction go all the way down
— the cogito at ρ = 1 is *not* constructed (see Tensions). So TLICA reads the no-self
insight as true of the lived self but not of the bare "I am."

**Extended mind (Clark, Chalmers).** Their famous claim: your mind isn't trapped inside
your skull — a notebook you rely on, a phone, a trusted friend's memory can be *literal
parts* of your cognitive system. TLICA agrees structurally: a content whose source lies
*outside the body* can still carry **non-trivial ρ** — real identity-weight. Your
wedding ring, your life's manuscript, your home town can be genuinely part of who you are.

> **In plain terms —** losing your phone can feel like losing a piece of your mind because,
> on this view, it sort of is. ρ doesn't stop at the skin.

**Embodied cognition.** This school holds that thinking is shot through with the body —
you understand the world partly *by* having a body that moves and feels. TLICA's
**[third-order imprinting](substrate-focus-and-imprinting.md)** is its specific account of
body-mediated content: emotions and bodily sensations reach the "I" along the longest
substrate pathway (body → cognition → I), and they arrive *lossily compressed* — squeezed
through stages that drop detail. That lossy step is why bodily content so often feels
*intense but inarticulable* — exactly the phenomenological signature embodied-cognition
theorists describe.

> **From the inside —** a wave of dread in your gut that you absolutely cannot put into
> words: huge in force, tiny in resolution. TLICA says that fuzziness isn't a failure of
> attention — it's the compression tax on routing a feeling through the body to the "I."

**Predictive processing (information-bottleneck variants).** A leading brain theory says
perception and thought are *compression hierarchies*: the mind constantly squeezes a flood
of input into compact predictions, discarding what it can. TLICA's **lossy-compression**
observation — that each substrate-mediation stage loses information — maps cleanly onto
this. *Where it agrees:* both treat experience as built through stages of lossy summary.
*Where it stays careful:* TLICA is *consistent with* predictive processing but does **not
depend on it** — the imprinting orders can be written in predictive-processing math or in
other frameworks. (And a *strong* predictive reading of φ clashes outright — see Tensions.)

**Classical / Pavlovian conditioning.** Pavlov's dog learned to salivate at a bell because
bell and food repeatedly co-occurred — association formed below the level of reasoning.
TLICA calls this **[osmotic imprinting](substrate-focus-and-imprinting.md)** — *osmotic*
because the pattern soaks in passively from repeated ambient co-occurrence, with no
deliberate focus required. Pavlovian conditioning is the *canonical pure-substrate case*:
the bell acquires its pull without any "evidence" (φ stays undefined). TLICA predicts the
*structural form* of association without committing to specific learning-rate numbers.

**Implicit cognition (IAT, dual-process theories).** Psychology distinguishes fast,
automatic, often-unowned "implicit" attitudes from slow, deliberate "explicit" ones. TLICA
cashes this out directly: **implicit cognition** runs through osmotically-imprinted
substrate patterns with *undefined φ* (associations you can't justify), while **explicit
cognition** runs through *verification-pathway construction* (reasons you can lay out).
A nice consequence: the theory **predicts** that explicit anti-bias commitments don't
directly rewrite substrate patterns — substrate-level engagement is required — which
matches the well-documented empirical *stubbornness* of implicit attitudes despite sincere
explicit beliefs.

> **In plain terms —** you can genuinely *believe* a group is your equal and still feel an
> automatic flinch you didn't ask for. TLICA says the belief lives on the explicit pathway
> and the flinch lives in the substrate, and just deciding harder doesn't reach down to
> where the flinch is stored.

**Attachment theory (Bowlby, Ainsworth).** Early bonds with caregivers lay down lasting
patterns in how we seek and tolerate closeness. TLICA reads attachment patterns as
**osmotically-imprinted third-order patterns**: in early development, caregiver-presence and
somatic-affective states co-occur over and over, soaking in as body-mediated dispositions.
The combination of third-order pathway operation (body → cognition → I) plus osmotic
imprinting matches attachment theory's core findings about how such patterns *form* and how
stubbornly they *persist*.

**Tacit knowledge / expertise (Polanyi, Dreyfus).** Polanyi's line — "we know more than we
can tell" — and Dreyfus on skill: the expert acts fluently without being able to state the
rules. TLICA maps this onto **osmotically-imprinted substrate proto-patterns** that ground
skilled performance, and its developmental arc — from a pattern merely *held in substrate*
to an *explicitly deployable tool* — matches the felt course of getting good at something
through long exposure.

> **From the inside —** the moment a skill "clicks" and your hands just know what to do
> before you can explain it: that's a substrate proto-pattern carrying the performance,
> verification not yet caught up.

**Naturalist epistemology of mathematics.** TLICA holds that we get our verification tools
(logic, math, reason) by *encountering* a world that already instantiates real structure —
you learn ratio because rivers, levers, and shadows actually embody it. This sits with
naturalist accounts of mathematical knowledge as acquired through contact with a
structure-bearing world, rather than by pure a priori insight. See [the two layers](two-layers.md)
and [access to intrinsic structure](access-to-intrinsic-structure.md).

The source also notes a few quieter convergences worth naming: **foundationalism in
epistemology** (the cogito is TLICA's foundational indexed truth — the one thing you can't
doubt your way past); **modal logic and proof theory** (δ-depth, the count of perspectival
steps, behaves like nested indexing operators); and **developmental psychology** (the
temporal-scale "escalator" predicts the *typical order* in which capacities come online,
while leaving the exact spread across individuals open).

## Tensions — where it takes sides

Translation is real but **bounded**. TLICA holds substantive positions, and those positions
*contradict* some frameworks rather than translating into them. These aren't oversights;
they're the philosophical commitments the theory actually makes.

- **Humean bundle theory.** Hume said the self is just a *bundle* of passing perceptions
  with no owner behind them — look inward and you never catch a "self," only the next
  sensation. TLICA disagrees on one structural point: it posits a uniquely privileged
  cogito pinned at **ρ = 1**, a genuine self-pole at the center of the graph. A pure bundle
  with no self-pole can't host that anchor, so TLICA *cannot* be translated into strict
  Humean terms without losing the cogito's special status. (On this point TLICA is
  frankly rationalist.)

  > **In plain terms —** Hume says "there's no one home, just the furniture rearranging."
  > TLICA says "the furniture is real and mostly assembled, but there is exactly one fixed
  > point it's all arranged *around* — the bare 'I am.'"

- **Strong eliminativism about the self.** Eliminativists treat "the self" as
  folk-psychology junk — a leftover concept science should drop, like phlogiston. TLICA
  treats identity-correlation as a *real structural feature* of conscious experience, not
  residue to be eliminated. You can't keep TLICA's core while throwing the self away.

- **Formalism / fictionalism about mathematics.** These views say math is *just* a formal
  symbol-game, or a convenient *fiction* — useful, but not about anything real. TLICA's
  naturalist-realist *in re* commitment (math is a real structure the world instantiates,
  though not a Platonic heaven of abstract objects — see [the two layers](two-layers.md))
  is incompatible with treating math as mere game or fiction. TLICA sides with realism, in
  the naturalist (not Platonist) sense.

- **Strong predictive-processing readings of φ.** The gentle version of predictive
  processing was a convergence above. The *strong* version — *all* of φ is nothing but
  predictive confidence — clashes. TLICA's φ has a three-tier structure: intrinsic items
  sit *outside* the φ-domain entirely; the cogito sits at a structural **1 − δ** (read:
  near-certainty, docked only by the single perspectival step δ); and ordinary Tier-3
  contents are I-relative. That layered structure won't collapse into one number called
  "prediction confidence."

  > **In plain terms —** TLICA is happy to say *some* of how-sure-you-feel is the brain
  > running predictions. It refuses to say *that's all there is* — because the certainty of
  > "I am I," and the standing of logic itself, aren't just confident guesses.

- **Strong introspective rationalism.** Some philosophers think you can reach deep truths
  (even mathematical ones) by introspection alone — just look inward hard enough. TLICA
  allows real meta-reasoning ([Mode B](modes-of-development.md)) but insists it **can't
  bootstrap from nothing**: it's constrained by the [developmental escalator](access-to-intrinsic-structure.md),
  the staged build-up of tools through prior encounter. So any framework that treats
  introspection as a *self-sufficient* route to mathematical knowledge clashes with it.

  > **From the inside —** you can't simply *think your way* to calculus in a sealed room
  > having never met quantity, shape, or change. Reflection sharpens tools you already have;
  > it doesn't conjure them from an empty hand.

- **Reductions of consciousness to selfhood-strength.** Some views would make consciousness
  just a *matter of degree of self* — the more self, the more conscious. TLICA flatly
  separates the two: consciousness is *presupposed* (the bare fact that there's a field of
  experience at all), while ρ merely *taxonomizes contents within* that field. Equating
  "how conscious" with "how much self" is incompatible with TLICA's
  [consciousness-is-presupposed](predictions-and-exclusions.md) commitment.

  > **In plain terms —** a thing with very little wrapped up in "me" right now (a blank,
  > selfless moment of just *seeing*) is no less conscious for it. Consciousness is the
  > screen; ρ only sorts what's *on* the screen.

The translatability is **real but bounded**. The convergences aren't curated to flatter;
they're genuine structural correspondences, and several of them carry checkable
*predictions* (the stubbornness of implicit bias, the form of attachment patterns, the
inarticulability of bodily feeling). The tensions are equally genuine — they're the
philosophical positions the theory actually holds, stated plainly rather than smoothed over.
