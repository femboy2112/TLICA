## The intuition

Think of a fact you can state in one sentence — "I should probably quit this job," "that
argument is valid," "this person is suffering." Now notice that the *same* sentence can sit
differently in different rooms: trivially light at a kitchen table, strangely heavy in a
meeting. The content didn't change. What changed is everything the sentence is *attached to* —
your standing, who's listening, what saying it would cost.

The hypothesis: that extra weight is a **coupling effect**. There's a slow-moving background —
your learned identity, your institutional alignment, your anticipated consequences — and
different propositions couple to it with different strengths. A strongly coupled one acquires
a larger effective "mass": it takes more to move it, it springs back harder.

## The crucial correction: "mass" is not "bad," and "bullshit" is an *anomaly*

An early version of the note leaned on *bullshit* as a synonym for ordinary interaction
resistance. The author corrected this, and the correction is load-bearing. A nonzero
background and ordinary coupling are **expected and normal** — that's just how any situated
mind works. The analogue of *bullshit proper* is not "this feels heavy"; it is a physicist
noticing **Higgs bosons apparently being produced all over an ordinary room with no mechanism
to produce them** — a *source/detection/model anomaly* that prompts "**why is the field doing
this?**", not "heavy = bad." Heaviness is baseline; an unexplained, gratuitous *excitation* of
the background is the thing worth investigating.

## Three meanings of "heavy" — and why they come apart

The single word "heavy" hides three separable constructs, and the dossier insists they be
measured separately:

| Construct | Toy law | What you'd see if you manipulated it |
|---|---|---|
| **Gap / restoring resistance** (the minimal Higgs analogy) | $K_r = m_{0,r}^2 + g_r^2 h^2$ | smaller steady deviation under the same push; springs *back* toward baseline; energy stored, not burned |
| **Damping / friction** (a different mechanism) | $\Gamma_r \dot q_r$ | energy dissipated; a distinct relaxation signature |
| **Inertial resistance** (optional, *not* implied by the Higgs term) | $M_r \ddot q_r$ | different initial acceleration at the same force; equilibrium maybe unchanged |

The sharp consequence: increasing a Higgs-type restoring term can make a process return
**faster**, not slower. So "it *feels* slow" alone cannot establish a Higgs-type gap — that
confusion is exactly what the three-way split is there to prevent.
