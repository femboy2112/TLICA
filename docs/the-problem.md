# The Problem It Starts From

[← Wiki home](README.md) · Source: [File 1, §1](../foundation/1_foundations.md)

---

Most theories of selfhood run on a **single dimension**. Something is more "self" or
less "self," more "real" or less "real," and these are treated as roughly the same
scale. TLICA's starting observation is that this is wrong, and that ordinary experience
already shows it.

The theory calls this the *diagnostic problem*: classical accounts of the self quietly
fuse together three things that, when you look closely, behave independently. They
*usually* move together, which is why they're easy to mistake for one thing. But "usually
together" is not "the same." A pair of dancers who almost always move in step are still
two dancers — and the moments when they *don't* step together are exactly what proves it.

> **In plain terms —** Almost every theory of the self uses one slider: "how much is
> this *me*?" TLICA says you actually have three sliders that happen to sit near each
> other most of the time, and the whole theory begins by noticing they can be pulled
> apart.

## Three things that usually travel together

Take your own hand. Right now it is:

- **in contact** with you — you can feel it, it's currently interacting with you;
- **undeniably real** — you can't reason it away, it holds up under any test you put to it;
- **part of you** — losing it would change who you are; it's woven into the network of
  things you treat as *me* or *mine*.

These three plain words — **contact**, **real**, **mine** — are the everyday seeds of the
theory's three formal coordinates. You don't need the symbols yet; this is the on-ramp.
But it helps to know where each one is heading:

- **contact** is the seed of **κ (kappa)** — *current* interaction. How live the thing is
  *right now*. Your hand has high contact when you feel it; it drops the instant the
  feeling stops, even if nothing about the hand has changed.
- **real** is the seed of **φ (phi)** — how *truth-indistinguishable* something is, how
  far it holds up when you reason about it, how impossible it is to deny without
  contradicting yourself. Your hand reads as flatly, un-arguably real.
- **mine** is the seed of **ρ (rho)** — *identity-correlation*, how integrated a thing is
  into who you are, the stuff whose loss would actually change you. Your hand is deeply
  yours.

> **In plain terms —** Three plain words do the work for now: is it *touching my life
> right now* (contact), does it *read as real* (real), and is it *part of who I am*
> (mine). The Greek letters come later; the felt distinctions are already here.

Now take a distant rumor about a stranger. It is weakly contacted, weakly certain, and
not part of you at all. In *ordinary* cases the three line up — the hand is high on all
three, the rumor low on all three — which is exactly why they're easy to confuse. When
two needles always point the same way, you start believing there's only one needle.

> **From the inside —** Most of the time you never feel the seam. Your body, your home,
> your name all show up as present *and* real *and* yours in one undivided wash. The
> theory isn't asking you to doubt that. It's asking you to notice the rare moments when
> the wash splits.

## The cases where they come apart

The dissociations are the whole motivation for the theory — they are the wedge that
forces the one slider into three. Each case below isolates a feature that a
single-dimension account is *forced* to treat as the same as the others, and shows it
standing alone. Read the table as: which of the three is doing something the others
aren't.

| Phenomenon | In contact? (κ) | Feels real? (φ) | Part of you? (ρ) |
|---|---|---|---|
| A vivid **hallucination** | no | **yes** | no |
| An **anesthetized limb** | no | no (in the moment) | **yes** |
| **Depersonalization** | yes | yes | **no** |
| A **phantom limb** | no | varies | **yes** |
| A **thought that feels inserted** | varies | **yes** | no |

- A **hallucination** can be vivid and undeniable while being obviously *not you*. High
  "real" (φ), but no "mine" (ρ): the content blares as real yet never registers as part
  of who you are.
- An **anesthetized limb** stops presenting itself as real in the moment, yet remains
  fully *yours*. "Real" (φ) and "contact" (κ) drop to nothing while "mine" (ρ) stays
  pinned high — the clearest case that *mine* is not made of *felt* or *real*.
- In **depersonalization**, contents still present as real but no longer as *mine*. High
  "real" (φ) and live "contact" (κ), but "mine" (ρ) has quietly fallen away.
- A **phantom limb** is gone — no contact — yet stays integrated as part of the body.
  Zero "contact" (κ), shaky "real" (φ), and yet "mine" (ρ) persists with no limb there to
  anchor it.
- An **inserted thought** ("alien clarity," in its psychiatric form, but also milder
  inspiration that doesn't feel like one's own) is undeniable yet *not mine*. High "real"
  (φ), absent "mine" (ρ).

> **In plain terms —** Each row is one needle breaking ranks. The hallucination: real but
> not mine. The anesthetized limb: mine but not felt and not "real" right now.
> Depersonalization: real and present but not mine. The phantom: mine with nothing there
> at all. The inserted thought: undeniable but coming from somewhere that isn't me. No
> single slider can do that.

### Three of these from the inside

The table is dry; the experiences are not. Walking through a few makes the splits
unmistakable.

**The anesthetized limb.** A dentist numbs your jaw, or you sleep on your arm wrong and
wake to a slab of meat hanging off your shoulder. You can lift it with your other hand and
watch it flop. In that moment there is *no contact* — you feel nothing through it — and it
doesn't even present as *real* in the ordinary way; it reads like an object, almost like
someone else's arm. And yet not for one second do you doubt it is **yours**. You would
never say "I have lost an arm." The "mine" coordinate (ρ) didn't so much as flicker, while
"contact" (κ) and "real" (φ) both bottomed out. If *mine* were just *strongly felt* or
*verified real*, the numb arm should feel less yours — and it doesn't.

> **From the inside —** "I can't feel it, it barely seems real, and it is *completely*
> mine. I'm not worried about it for a second." That sentence is impossible on one slider.

**Depersonalization.** This one runs the opposite direction. Everything is in contact and
everything reads as real — your hands are right there, the room is solid, your voice makes
sound — but a strange film has dropped between you and all of it. The contents present as
real; they no longer present as *yours*. People describe it as watching their own life
through glass, or feeling like an actor reciting their own lines. Nothing has gone dim or
unreal. What's drained is the *mine*. Here "contact" (κ) and "real" (φ) are intact and
high, while "mine" (ρ) has quietly withdrawn.

> **From the inside —** "It's all real and it's all happening and none of it feels like it
> belongs to me." The realness is untouched; the ownership has gone hollow.

**The inserted thought.** In its mild, everyday form: a line arrives in your head fully
formed, sharper and cleaner than your usual thinking — a solution, a sentence, a melody —
and it doesn't feel *authored* by you. It feels *given*. The content is undeniable; you'd
defend it. But the *source* doesn't register as you. In its clinical form, "thought
insertion," this same structure becomes distressing: the thought is experienced as placed
there by something else. Either way it's the same dissociation — maximal "real" (φ),
absent "mine" (ρ).

> **In plain terms —** You can be *certain* of a thought and still feel it isn't *from*
> you. Certainty (real) and ownership (mine) are simply not the same dial.

## The move

If "real" and "mine" and "present" were one scale, these cases would be contradictions —
flat impossibilities, the kind of thing that can't happen. They aren't contradictions;
they're *common*. Every one of them is something people actually live through, some of
them on an ordinary afternoon. A theory that calls common experiences impossible has the
wrong number of dimensions.

So the theory makes the obvious-in-hindsight move: it splits the one scale into
[three independent coordinates](three-coordinates.md) — contact, truth-indistinguishability,
and identity-correlation — and rebuilds the self on top of them. "Independent" is the load-
bearing word: none of the three is built out of the others, and any one can move while the
others hold still. The dissociations stop being paradoxes and become exactly what you'd
predict from three free-moving dials.

> **In plain terms —** The fix isn't clever, it's structural: stop pretending there's one
> slider, admit there are three, and the "impossible" cases turn into ordinary readings
> off three separate dials.

Everything else in the architecture — the [two layers](two-layers.md), the
[cogito](the-cogito.md), the [developmental story](modes-of-development.md), the account
of [disturbances](profile-shape-disturbances.md) — exists to make that split precise and
to show what it explains. (In TLICA's terms, the conditions usually called mental illness
turn out to be *profile-shape disturbances* — not a shortage of self, but the three
coordinates falling into an unusual *shape* — which is why the split has to come first.)
