# Choice as Endogenous Filter Application

[← Applications overview](applications.md) · [Wiki home](README.md) · Source: [`applications/choice_as_filter_v0_1_0.md`](../applications/choice_as_filter_v0_1_0.md) · **v0.1.0 · First draft**

> **First draft.** A *conservative refinement* of the existing [free-will](app-free-will.md)
> and [agency](app-agency-architecture.md) papers — it adds **no new foundation primitive**.
> Its internal claim — that "choosing is applying a filter" merely re-describes machinery
> TLICA already has — is **disclosed only conditional on a stated image condition**: an
> internal formal result, not an empirical one. Every *phenomenological and psychological*
> claim it makes — perceptual momentum, control depth, correlated-neighborhood
> amplification, response leverage — is **CONJECTURED / UNVERIFIED**, awaiting discriminating
> probes. Read it as the theory sharpening the *fidelity* of one word it already used:
> **"choose."**

---

## The claim

The paper's whole thesis fits in one line: **choosing is the endogenous application of a
filter to the field of possibilities you are currently in.**

Three load-bearing words. *Endogenous* means it comes from you — the
[self-directed](substrate-focus-and-imprinting.md) part of where your attention goes, not the
part the world grabs. A *filter* is a re-weighting: it turns some regions of what's available
up and some down. The *field* is everything currently live for you — percepts, affordances,
possible understandings, candidate responses — the usable contents your toolkit can reach.

The picture the paper argues *against* is the one most of us carry without noticing: that
choosing is **picking one point off a ready-made menu**. There is no menu sitting there in
advance, it says — there is a *field*, and choosing is the act of **deforming** it: leaning
your focus so some possibilities gain weight, stability, and response-readiness, and others
lose it.

> **In plain terms —** you don't choose the way you pick a dish from a printed menu. You
> choose the way you *lean into a room* — turning toward some things, letting others fade,
> until one way of acting is the one that's actually live for you. The "menu" was never
> lying there; you make it by how you lean.

And the subtle part the paper is most careful about: the move does **not** push choice one
step backwards into a *second, prior* act — some inner chooser who first "selects a filter"
and then applies it. There is no extra chooser. **The self-directed lean of your focus is the
choice-event itself** — not three steps in a row. (Why this matters is
[below](#no-homunculus-choosing-the-filter-is-applying-it).)

## A word on the terms

A few pieces of the theory's vocabulary do all the work here:

- **Focus (Foc)** — where your attention points now. The theory already splits it into a
  **contact-driven** part (what the world seizes) and a **self-directed** part (what *you*
  steer toward); this paper is entirely about the self-directed part. [→](substrate-focus-and-imprinting.md)
- **The field** — the currently usable contents, reachable through your toolkit, under a fixed
  limit on how much you can hold at once.
- **Baseline weighting** — how the field is *already* weighted before you lean (contact,
  salience, habit, mood, [osmotic](substrate-focus-and-imprinting.md) history). Not a new
  coordinate; shorthand for "how things stand before this act of will."
- **The coordinates κ / φ / ρ** — [contact](three-coordinates.md),
  [truth-status](three-coordinates.md), and [identity-correlation](three-coordinates.md). The
  paper's discipline: a filter can raise focus on a content **without** raising any of these —
  attention is not truth.

The only genuinely new word is "filter" — and even that isn't a new *part* of the mind, just a
higher-resolution name for how you steer your own attention.

## What it adds — fidelity, not new machinery

This is the paper's most important self-limitation, and it wants it front and center: **it
introduces nothing new into the foundation.** No new primitive, no new coordinate, no new
causal faculty, no hidden selector. Everything it describes runs on parts TLICA already has —
self-directed focus, [live-option construction, operator-bundle selection, and order-indexed
willing](app-agency-architecture.md), focus-driven weighting and
[imprinting](substrate-focus-and-imprinting.md).

The formal way it says this: the space of "filters" is built so it can only ever re-describe
self-directed focus states the theory *already* permits. A filter reaching a focus state the
old architecture couldn't would not be a refinement — it would be a new mechanism smuggled in
under a new name. So the paper imposes an **image condition**: every filter must land on an
already-admissible focus state, and every already-admissible focus state must be reachable by
some filter. Two filters can even land on the *same* state — the filter language is finer than
what it controls, which is exactly why it supplies fidelity without adding power. Under that
condition, "filter" is a **coordinate chart on an existing part of the theory**.

> **In plain terms —** think of it like giving a familiar map a finer grid. The territory
> doesn't change; you can just describe positions on it more exactly. "Filter" is a finer
> grid over the machinery of self-directed attention — not a new country.

The epistemic status has to be stated carefully, because it is *split*:

- The **internal** claim — that the filter formalism re-describes existing self-directed focus
  without enlarging the theory — is **DISCLOSED, but only *conditional on the image
  condition* holding.** It is an archive-relative formal result, not external validation.
- The **psychological** claim — that human choosing is actually well modeled *as* this filter
  application — is **not** settled by the formal equivalence. It remains empirically
  vulnerable and, throughout, is held as **CONJECTURED / UNVERIFIED**.

The paper is blunt that "the old frozen theory remains correct in shape. This refinement fills
in missing fidelity" — it is a sharper account of *what selection is from the inside*, not a
correction of anything.

## Discrete options aren't wrong — they're coarse-grained

The pivotal conceptual move, made with a physics analogy: the old "pick-a-point-from-a-set"
picture stands to the filter picture as **Newtonian mechanics stands to general relativity.**
Newtonian gravity is not *false* and not *degenerate* — it is a valid description at the scale
it was built for (it lands rockets), simply a coarse-grained limit of a finer theory. In that
exact spirit, the paper insists: **describing choice as selecting one discrete option from a
set is not an error.** It is a valid *macroscopic* description — a quotient that groups the fine
process into equivalence classes and names them.

Why the two descriptions are compatible: many slightly different filters — many
micro-different ways of leaning — funnel into the **same downstream response basin** ("move the
arm," "stay silent," "take the job"). Group all the filters that land in one basin and the
group behaves like a single discrete "option." So the option-set is what you get by
*coarse-graining the filter field by its outcomes*:

$$\text{continuous / high-dimensional filter field} \;\longrightarrow\; \text{response basins} \;\longrightarrow\; \text{discrete live options}.$$

Formally the paper writes the live-option space as a **quotient** of the filter space by
response-equivalence — options *are* equivalence classes of filters. Filters can carve the
field so cleanly that the result is genuinely well described as "picking from a set." The
menu-picture isn't a mistake; it's the low-resolution photograph of a higher-resolution
process — the paper's only claim is that it's not the *underlying mechanism*. This stays a
**CONJECTURED formalization**, needing a declared resolution and empirical recovery of stable
option-classes before it earns more.

> **In plain terms —** "I had three options and picked the second" is a perfectly true
> sentence — the way "the apple fell at 9.8 m/s²" is perfectly true. It's just a zoomed-out
> snapshot. Zoom in and there was no clean list of three; there was a whole landscape of ways
> to lean, and a bunch of them happened to roll into the same valley you're calling "option
> two." The list is real *as a summary.* It isn't what was happening underneath.

## No homunculus: choosing the filter *is* applying it

The most tempting misreading — the one the paper spends real effort killing — is to hear
"choice is applying a filter" and quietly insert an extra step:

> *I → choose which filter → apply it → choose the action.*

That would be fatal — it just relocates the mystery. Now there's an inner "I" who *chooses the
filter*, and we'd have to ask how *that* choice gets made — another filter? another chooser? —
straight into infinite regress, the classic homunculus. The paper refuses it. The intended
identity is flat, not stacked:

$$\text{choosing the filter} \;\equiv\; \text{applying the filter} \;\equiv\; \text{the self-directed part of the present focus-deformation}.$$

There is no further choice hiding behind the choice. The lean *is* the decision.

> **From the inside —** there isn't a little you at a control panel deciding how to decide.
> There's just the leaning itself — and that leaning, the turning-toward, already *is* you
> choosing. Looking for the chooser behind it is like looking for the fist behind the
> clenching. The clenching is the fist doing something.

## Moving and not moving an arm

The paper motivates all of this with the plainest possible case: deciding to move your arm —
and deciding *not* to.

Notice, from the inside, that a voluntary arm movement rarely has a crisp "now!" instant.
What you find is a spread of moments: an emerging orientation toward moving, a felt readiness,
the onset of motion, the recognition that you've chosen, a time you'd *report* if asked. These
need not collapse into one point — which fits a chain the theory already carries, that
*preparing*, *selecting*, *reporting*, *moving*, the *outcome*, and *recognizing* are not the
same instant. The refinement adds only that the *selecting* moment may be better drawn as an
**interval** — an orientation-to-closure stretch — than as a single phenomenal atom.

Now the mirror case: deciding *not* to move. Once the not-moving orientation is stably in
place, nothing moves — not because you re-issue a "stay still" command muscle by muscle, but
because a resting basin is being *held stable*. It gives way only if something shifts the
field: a salience event, a bodily perturbation, a competing aim, a reflex, fatigue, pain. That
asymmetry is the tell. Moving needn't begin with a discrete motor-command token; not-moving
needn't be continuously re-selected. Both are changes in the **weighting and stability of
response basins** — exactly what a filter does.

> **In plain terms —** watch yourself lift your hand. There's no single click where "the
> decision" happened — there's a shading-in, from "maybe" to "moving," and only afterward can
> you name a time. Now sit still on purpose. You're not re-deciding to be still every instant;
> you settled into stillness, and you'll only pop out of it if something jostles you. That
> difference is the clue: choosing isn't flipping a switch, it's tilting a landscape.

The paper is scrupulous about the status here: this phenomenology is **OBSERVED in the
motivating case** — it is what motivates the model, not proof of it.

## Choice as a filter over the field

Kept deliberately light (the [paper](../applications/choice_as_filter_v0_1_0.md) carries the
full version): a volitional filter is a bounded, toolkit-constructible **deformation** of the
baseline weighting — a positive value means "emphasize, stabilize, make available," a negative
value "de-emphasize, inhibit, distance," zero "leave untouched" — renormalized under your fixed
focus capacity. The paper offers a couple of candidate formulas and stresses that **none is a
foundation commitment**; they are just application-level ways of writing one claim:
*self-directed focus changes the comparative weighting of the present field under a fixed
capacity bound.*

> **In plain terms —** what you actually control is *what stands out*, not each downstream
> effect. You don't reach in and re-wire particular beliefs one by one. You choose a **way of
> looking** — what to hold, compare, dwell on, push away — and the rest follows.

So **what is chosen is a filter profile, not a list of edits.** You enact the way-of-looking;
the theory's existing update rules do the connection-level work.

## Focus moves neighborhoods, not points

Leaning on one content almost never touches only that content. Contents are *coupled* — by
shared paths in your integration network, by common tools and inferences, by learned
co-activation, by similarity and contrast, by attributed source, by feeling and bodily
association, by what the current task makes relevant. So a filter that emphasizes one region
raises (or lowers) the pressure on a whole **correlated neighborhood** around it.

The paper summarizes this coupling with an applied "correlation kernel" — how a push at one
spot spreads to another — and insists it is **not a new primitive**: it is a summary *induced
by* relational structure the theory already has (integration graphs, toolkit, co-activation
history, salience, somatic associations, context). The only architectural commitment is modest
and almost undeniable: **relationally coupled contents need not update independently.**

> **In plain terms —** attention has a spread. Focus hard on one memory and the feelings,
> associations, and half-thoughts wired to it light up too — not because you aimed at them,
> but because they're near it in your inner web. You choose the *center of a splash*; the
> ripples are the theory's old dynamics, not extra choices you made.

This is held as **CONJECTURED, though strongly structurally motivated** — it follows naturally
from machinery already in place, but showing it empirically (beyond mere shared exposure)
needs subject-specific relation-mapping and matched controls.

## The old dynamics do the rest

Once the filter has re-weighted the field, **the frozen architecture takes over unchanged.**
The existing focus-driven update rules simply receive the self-directed contribution the filter
supplies and run as they always did — no new update law, just the self-directed part of the
focus the old law was already asking for. And because integration reshapes your future network
and toolkit, the loop closes on itself:

$$f_t \;\to\; \mathsf{Foc}_t \;\to\; \text{update} \;\to\; G_{t+1} \;\to\; \text{response, and the geometry of tomorrow's choosing}.$$

**Choice changes not only the current trajectory but the shape of later choices** — today's
chosen way of attending alters what will be easy, salient, or even *available* tomorrow, the
agency-side face of habit-formation and [compiled transport](app-this-is-water.md). (This does
*not* imply all learning is voluntary — much stays osmotic and contact-driven; deliberate
filtering is one channel over the always-running background, not a replacement for it.)

## Perceptual momentum and response leverage

Now the paper's central new *phenomenological* construct — and it flags the whole cluster as
**CONJECTURED** from the outset.

**Perceptual momentum** is the resistance of your current perceptual-and-bodily trajectory to
being re-weighted. It is measured by *control cost*: how big a filter-deformation you'd need to
make a given response likely.

- **Low momentum:** a small lean makes the target response dominant.
- **High momentum:** it takes a strong, sustained, or specially structured lean.
- **Captured / unreachable:** no filter you can currently build makes the target live at all.

Its partner is **response leverage** — how much response-change you get per unit of lean. High
leverage: a small filter-shift moves the response a lot. Low leverage: the current trajectory
absorbs your lean and little changes. Informally leverage runs *inverse* to momentum, though
the paper won't call them identical without a specific implementation.

Two disciplines the paper insists on. First, **perceptual momentum is not a new force** — it
just summarizes resistance the theory already generates (contact-driven salience, entrenched
integration, bodily and affective state, habit, fatigue, substrate capture, toolkit limits,
competing goals). It is an applied *diagnostic*, not an added substance. Second, it is **not a
personality trait**: it is target-, state-, toolkit-, and time-relative — the *same* person,
in the *same* moment, can find one basin easy and another nearly immovable. And it must be kept
distinct from [contact (κ)](three-coordinates.md), [truth-status (φ)](three-coordinates.md),
[identity (ρ)](three-coordinates.md), felt effort, ownership, and moral worth — a high-momentum
act can still be owned and good; a low-momentum one alienated and bad.

> **In plain terms —** perceptual momentum is how *stuck on its current course* you are toward
> some particular thing. Right now, lifting your coffee is low-momentum (the smallest intention
> does it); not checking your phone while anxious might be high-momentum (it takes real,
> repeated effort); and mid-panic, "just calm down" can be off the table entirely. It's not
> willpower-as-a-score. It's a reading that changes target by target, minute by minute.

## First-order willing, and the two depths of an act

This is the payoff that ties the arm back to the theory. TLICA already talks about
[orders of willing](app-agency-architecture.md) — first-order at focus, second at thought,
third at bodily action, and constructed orders reaching through other people and institutions.

> **In plain terms —** first-order is choosing where to look, second-order is choosing what to think about, third-order is choosing what your body does — like glancing at a clock (focus), choosing to work through a worry instead of a memory (thought), and standing up and leaving the room (body).

The refinement sharpens what "first-order" *means*:

> **First-order willing is when one chosen filter-shift has enough leverage to redirect the
> response — no intermediate re-representation loop required.**

For the neutral arm movement, momentum toward moving is low, so the chosen filter tilts the
field enough that the substrate follows into the motor basin. The elegant consequence: **the
same act can be first-order in control and third-order in realization.** The *body* moving is a
deep, third-order output — but the *willing* that produced it was a single first-order lean. A
choice's order is about the **minimal control needed to select it**, not how anatomically deep
the result runs.

$$\text{first-order in control} \quad\text{can be}\quad \text{third-order in bodily realization}.$$

And the "do not move" filter that raised the resting basin's stability shows the mirror point:
**negative choice is not pointwise negation.** "Don't answer yet," "refuse," "wait," "stay with
the discomfort," "withhold assent" — from outside these look like omissions, but inside they are
*positive configurations of the field*: stabilizing a region, not selecting every point in the
complement.

> **From the inside —** deciding "no" or "not yet" doesn't feel like listing everything you're
> *not* doing. It feels like *settling* — planting a flag and letting the ground firm up around
> it. Restraint isn't emptiness; it's a shape you're holding.

Generalizing the arm point, the refinement types every act by **two** numbers instead of one:
**control depth** (the minimum endogenous filter/feedback stages needed to make the act live and
selected) and **realization depth** (the order at which it actually gets expressed — focus,
thought, body, or through other people and structures). The old layered route was right; the
missing fidelity is that **where an effect shows up is not the same as how much control it took
to select it.** A low-momentum request can be first-order in control yet reach through a whole
institution; a hard inner reframe can be high in control depth while realizing only as a shift
in thought. Both the first-order account and this two-depth typing are held as **interpretive /
conjectured refinements, empirically UNVERIFIED** — they must earn their keep by predicting
latency, effort, and error across matched overt actions, not by sounding right.

> **In plain terms —** "how far out into the world did it reach?" and "how hard was it to
> decide?" are different questions. Snapping your fingers reaches only your hand but is
> effortless to will; forgiving someone reaches only your own heart but can be the hardest
> thing you do all year. Depth-out and depth-of-control are two dials, not one.

## The six conditions still hold

The existing [free-will paper](app-free-will.md) lists **six conditions** a choice must meet to
count as free — real contrastive options, evaluative engagement, operator-bundle selection,
non-preempted substrate coupling, non-confabulatory reflective (Mode B) availability, and
ownership-integration. The refinement maps cleanly onto all six and — the paper is emphatic —
**adds no seventh.** It only fills in the fine-grained semantics: options become
response-equivalence classes of filters; selection becomes *the filter deformation becoming
operative* (again, no extra selector behind it); substrate coupling gains perceptual momentum
as a way to describe *degrees* of non-preemption; and reflection and ownership now take hold of
the operative filter itself. Every condition keeps its old job.

> **In plain terms —** none of the earlier account of *free* choice is torn up. The six boxes
> that had to be ticked still have to be ticked. The refinement just describes, in higher
> resolution, what's happening inside each box.

## Reading the variations through filter geometry

One of the paper's richest sections re-reads the whole catalogue of agency variations in the
new vocabulary — and it is careful to present these as **candidate translations, not
replacements** for the existing taxonomy. A few, to give the flavor:

- **Flow / skilled action** — a compiled filter with high task-leverage and low explicit
  control cost; reflection stays available under intact conditions.
- **Habit** — past filter trajectories have reshaped the baseline weighting, so the response
  now activates cheaply, almost on its own.
- **Akrasia** (acting against your better judgment) — evaluation stabilizes one filter, but the
  operative selection settles into *another*, even though the alternative stayed live.
- **Addiction-like capture** — momentum toward the captured basin is so high (or away from it
  so high) that the alternative filter ceases to be sufficiently *inhabitable* under current
  capacity.
- **Manipulation** — the agent genuinely applies and owns a filter, while the *upstream* field
  — the baseline, the correlations, the available filters, the apparent options — was shaped
  from outside. Real, owned choice over a **rigged field**: the paper's existing
  ownership-versus-sourcehood dissociation, made more legible.

The discipline attached to the table matters: these readings are worth keeping *only if* they
preserve the distinct signatures the taxonomy already carries — they **must not** flatten every
failure of agency into "not enough leverage." Manipulation especially shows why "choice is a
filter" does **not** imply unlimited freedom: you can only ever choose among the filters
constructible over the field you actually have.

> **In plain terms —** the same lens re-describes flow, habit, weakness of will, addiction, and
> being manipulated — often quite vividly. But it's offered as a *possibly better translation*,
> not a verdict, and with a warning built in: don't let a single tidy phrase ("low leverage")
> swallow real differences between very different predicaments.

## What would test it — and what would sink it

The refinement only earns its place if it distinguishes outcomes the coarser language can't. The
paper stakes out **testable predictions** and **discriminating probes**; a representative few:

- **State-dependent leverage.** Hold the *instructed* filter roughly fixed and independently
  vary resistance (momentum). The same intended reorientation should then produce *different*
  downstream response shifts.
- **Correlated-neighborhood update.** Focus on one content while holding exposure to its
  neighbors constant; more strongly coupled neighbors should show larger later change than
  equally-exposed but weakly-coupled controls.
- **Control-order ≠ realization-order.** The *same* overt movement should sometimes be
  first-order and sometimes need higher control depth, with control-depth predicting latency,
  interruptibility, effort, and error better than the bodily output alone.
- **Stable non-movement without constant re-vetoing.** After a stillness filter settles, staying
  still shouldn't require a running stream of conscious "don't move" tokens; outside salience
  should predict basin-exit better than the *absence* of repeated vetoes.
- **Options are resolution-dependent.** Choices subjects call "distinct" may collapse into one
  response-class at one scale, while one named option splits into several under finer measurement.

The paper also lists explicit **falsifiers** — what would cost it its explanatory weight: if
deliberate focus effects turn out strictly pointwise (never relational) once exposure is
controlled; if leverage doesn't vary with independently manipulated resistance; if the two
depths predict nothing beyond overt-action categories; if repeated filtering doesn't change
future control cost; if discrete options behave as invariant primitives across scales; if
filter-reports show no causal bite before closure and are pure after-the-fact narration; or —
the internal one — if the filter parameterization turns out to *require* focus states the frozen
architecture doesn't allow, breaking the conservative-refinement condition itself.

> **In plain terms —** this isn't just a nicer way of talking. It sticks its neck out: leaning
> should bite harder or softer depending on your state; focusing on one thing should stir its
> relatives; the "same" action should sometimes be easy and sometimes hard to *will*, in
> measurable ways. If those don't show up, the refinement was decoration, and the paper says so.

## The durable formulation

The paper's own highest-fidelity statement, paraphrased faithfully:

> **Choice is not the selection of isolated points from a ready-made option-set. You inhabit a
> toolkit- and substrate-bounded field of possible understandings and response-trajectories.
> Choosing is the endogenous application of a filter to that field. The filter changes which
> regions — and which correlated neighborhoods — receive focus, probing, and update pressure;
> the existing TLICA dynamics then determine the resulting integrations, disintegrations, and
> bodily trajectory. Apparently discrete options are macroscopic equivalence classes of filters
> that converge on the same response basin. First-order willing is when perceptual momentum is
> low enough that one chosen filter-shift produces high downstream response leverage.**

And the compact identity it ends on:

$$\text{choice} \;=\; \text{self-directed filter application} \;\neq\; \text{a hidden act prior to filter application}.$$

This is the whole contribution in one breath: **the frozen architecture keeps its shape; the
refinement supplies the missing fidelity of what selection *is*** — and holds every step of the
psychology at CONJECTURED / UNVERIFIED until the probes are run.
