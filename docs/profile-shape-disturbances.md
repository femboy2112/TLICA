# Profile-Shape Disturbances

[← Wiki home](README.md) · Source: [File 4, §10.5](../foundation/4_derived_concepts_and_predictions.md)

---

Because the theory's primary object is the [profile](identity-correlation-profile.md) —
the *shape* of integration across your field — disturbances of the self are **shape
disturbances**, not "too little self" or "too much." This is one of the architecture's
sharpest commitments: conditions often classed as mental illness are **not** scalar
ρ-deficits ("depression = low ρ"). The same average ρ can correspond to completely
different conditions; what matters is the **structure**.

**ρ (identity-correlation)** — in plain terms, how much of *you* is bound up in a thing;
how much its loss would actually change who you are. A **scalar ρ-deficit** would mean a
condition is just "less self overall," a single dial turned down. The theory denies this.
Two people can have the *same total amount* of self-investment spread across their lives and
yet be in utterly different conditions, because that investment is arranged into different
*shapes* — concentrated here, missing there, walled off, leaking, doubled-back.

> **In plain terms —** Mental illness isn't "you have less self." It's "the self you have
> is bent into a particular shape." Think of it like a body: two people can weigh the same
> and one is healthy while the other has a curved spine. The weight (the average) tells you
> almost nothing; the *posture* (the shape) tells you everything.

## Descriptive, not prescriptive

The architecture is **descriptive and non-moralizing**. It commits to a **developmental
window** — boundaries ill-defined but real — during which conscious direction is
insufficient to overcome substrate and environment. Patterns rooted there are **not the
I's chosen errors**.

The window's edges are *gradual*, not a cliff: the substrate (your underlying biological
wiring) matures continuously, the capacity for reflective thought switches on at different
times in different people, and self-directed attention grows by inches rather than all at
once. So there is no clean birthday after which you become "responsible." Many conditions
with childhood or adolescent onset, strong familial/genetic components, or roots in early
trauma originate squarely inside this window.

The window is not only chronological. Two classes share the architecturally-relevant
feature ("conscious direction couldn't overcome the substrate response at formation"):

- **Developmental conditions** — early life, before self-direction exists. The substrate is
  highly malleable, the I has no accumulated structure to steer with, and reflective
  thought is barely online. Childhood-onset PTSD, attachment disturbances, developmental
  trauma.
- **Situational-overwhelm conditions** — substrate response so intense that mature
  self-direction is overwhelmed *regardless of age*. Adult-onset PTSD from combat,
  assault, accident, sudden loss. The intensity of the substrate reaction simply outruns
  any capacity to steer it, exactly as it would in a child.

> **In plain terms —** There's a stretch of life — and a kind of moment at any age — where
> the wiring reacts so fast and so hard that "just choose differently" was never on the
> table. The theory refuses to blame you for what got carved in then. You didn't pick those
> patterns; they were laid down before, or faster than, you could steer.

Outside the window, in mature operation, patterns *may* carry contributions from the I's
own choices — and these can be **accidental** (you don't see what your choices are doing to
your own structure), **misintentional** (choices you later regret that feed a harmful
pattern), or in some cases more deliberate. But substrate constraints still limit how much
can be changed: you can't choose to have a different bandwidth or fundamentally different
wiring. The theory **doesn't assert universal innocence**; it says the window protects
against responsibility for patterns rooted there, substrate limits modification throughout
life, and the share of mature self-direction in any case is an empirical question it doesn't
pre-judge.

> **Extended self is baseline-normal, not a disturbance.** A self whose high-ρ
> integration extends broadly into family, community, land, lineage, or sacred objects is
> normal across many cultures and traditions. The theory **rejects** the
> Western-individualist reflex to pathologize this as enmeshment. Extension is
> structurally neutral; pathology arises only when it *combines* with other shape-features.

> **In plain terms —** Loving widely — feeling that your family, your land, your ancestors
> are *part of who you are* — is not a sickness. It only becomes a problem when it's paired
> with *another* shape-feature, like self-erasure or domination. The width of your self is
> not the diagnosis; the structure built across it might be.

## The disturbance vocabulary

Disturbances are described as configurations of the apparatus you've already met. Three of
them are the **modes** — Mode A (regulating the I/not-I boundary, who counts as "me"),
Mode B (reflective self-monitoring, the capacity to think *about* your own thinking), and
Mode C (positively identifying with experience as *yours*). Each can go wrong in two
opposite directions — by withering (**atrophied**) or by overgrowing (**hypertrophied**):

- **Mode A disturbances** — weak boundary (fluid identity, self/other confusion) or
  rigid boundary (nearly all not-I rejected); **hypertrophied PtCns** (others' frames
  treated as illegitimate) or **atrophied PtCns** (one's own frame deprivileged, deferring
  to others as authoritative). **PtCns (Prerogative to Construe-as-not-I)** — in plain
  terms, your built-in right to treat what clashes with your view as *the other guy's*
  inconsistency rather than your own. Hypertrophied: "any disagreement means *you're*
  wrong." Atrophied: "any disagreement means *I'm* wrong" — chronic self-doubt.
- **Mode B disturbances** — atrophied (concrete thinking, lack of insight — you can't get
  any distance on yourself) or hypertrophied (obsessive self-monitoring, watching yourself
  from outside until you feel unreal).
- **Mode C disturbances** — atrophied (can't integrate experience as one's own — things
  happen but don't feel like *yours*) or hypertrophied (merger, loss-of-self in
  relationships — no edge between you and another person).
- **Coordinate partitions** — dissociation as the profile **splitting**: some contents
  keep ρ-integration but lose **φ-access**, or vice versa. **φ (verifiability / conscious
  access)** — in plain terms, whether a content is available to be checked, owned, and
  worked with in the open; the difference between something you can *get at* and something
  walled off. The partition *itself* is the diagnostic feature.

> **In plain terms —** Almost every disturbance here is one of your normal faculties either
> shrunk down or cranked up: the dial for "where do I end and you begin," the dial for
> "watching my own mind," the dial for "this feeling is mine." Health is these dials in
> range; disturbance is one of them stuck high or low.

> **From the inside —** A withered Mode C feels like watching your own life through glass —
> things are happening *to* a body that doesn't quite register as you. An overgrown Mode B
> feels like a camera floating above your head, narrating you, never letting you just *be*
> in the moment.

To make the "shape, not magnitude" point concrete, two quick profile-shape pictures:

- **Anxiety** is not "too much self" or "too little." Its *shape* is **lopsided density**:
  the threat-related regions of the profile bulge — every threat-relevant content gets
  weighted heavy and verified over and over — while the rest of the profile **thins out**.
  It hits the third-order region hard (the bodily activation — racing heart, clenched gut)
  and drags second-order thought (rumination) into the threat zone. Same person could have
  the same *average* integration as a calm day; the difference is the lopsided heap-in-one-
  corner posture.
- **PTSD** has its own characteristic posture: very **high local ρ** for the trauma (the
  event is deeply self-relevant), a broken sense of pastness so the memory arrives wearing
  *present-time* contact and floods back as if happening now, a salience-gate recalibrated
  to flinch at trauma cues (hyperarousal), trauma content that won't settle into ordinary
  conscious structure (unstable φ), and a wall between ρ-integration and φ-access
  (dissociation). It's not "broken in general." It's this specific arrangement.

> **In plain terms —** Anxiety is a self piled up in the corner marked *danger*, gone thin
> everywhere else. PTSD is a self with one searingly bright, un-filed region that keeps
> bursting in the door as though it were happening right now. Different illnesses are
> different *postures*, even when the total amount of self is the same.

Many disturbances have a heavy **osmotic** component (trauma-cue dynamics, attachment
patterns, implicit-attitude dispositions) — patterns soaked in below the level of
deliberate thought, the way a smell or a tone of voice can flood you before you've decided
anything. **Osmotic imprinting** — in plain terms, learning that seeps in through
repeated contact rather than being consciously studied; it lives in the substrate, not in
your reasons. That's why these patterns persist and **resist purely cognitive engagement**:
you can't talk yourself out of something that was never argued *into* you. (See
[Substrate, Focus & Imprinting](substrate-focus-and-imprinting.md).)

> **In plain terms —** A lot of this stuff was absorbed, not decided — soaked into the
> wiring through sheer repetition. So reasoning with it doesn't reach it, any more than a
> good argument cures a flinch. It has to be worked at where it actually lives: in the body
> and the reflexes, the substrate.

## Worked example: severe depression

The foundation's most developed case shows the vocabulary in action. It comes in two
structural depths — a basic level and a severe level — and a structurally specifiable
recovery.

- **Basic level** — *collapsed support*: previously high-ρ contents lose integration; the
  network **narrows**. Things that used to be woven into who you are — work, hobbies,
  people — drop out of the weave. Often this involves **atrophied Mode C** (you can no
  longer positively identify with experience, so the self can't reach as far) and a
  **deprivileged frame** (depressive content gets treated as "more truly me" than the rest
  of you). The signature is structural **narrowing**, not low-ρ-everywhere. The
  third-order region (anhedonia, somatic flattening — the body going grey and unfeeling) is
  hit first and hardest; the second-order region follows as rumination narrows the
  cognitive field down.

> **In plain terms —** Depression at this level isn't the self dimmed evenly like a
> rheostat. It's the self **shrinking** — the web of things that used to be part of you
> coming loose and falling away — while a small dark patch starts being treated as the real
> you. The feeling goes out of the body first; then the thoughts close in.

- **Severe level** — a deeper disturbance: **functional collapse of the cogito/thought
  partition**. This needs care, because it's the heart of the case and easy to get wrong.

  Normally there's a gap — a **partition** — between two very different things. On one side
  is the **cogito** (written **ι̂ₘ**, "the I itself"), the bare fact that there is a someone
  here doing the experiencing; it sits at **structural ρ = 1**, the fixed anchor-point of
  the whole profile. **ι̂ₘ (the cogito-I)** — in plain terms, the plain *you-that-is-here*,
  the witness, the one to whom thoughts occur. On the other side are **thoughts** —
  contents the I *has*, which live out in the field with their own ρ-values. The normal form
  of the relation is **"the I thinks X"**: X is something you *hold*, structurally distinct
  from the you that holds it.

  In severe depression, depressive thoughts — "I am worthless," "I should not exist,"
  "things will never get better," "I am beyond help" — acquire **ρ approaching 1** and get
  treated as *constitutive of who the I is* rather than as contents the I holds. The gap
  closes. The constellation spreads: despair, worthlessness, hopelessness, anhedonia stop
  being felt as states you *have* and come to be felt as **what you are**. **Depression
  becomes functional proto-self** — the phenomenology people report as *"I am not myself, I
  am the depression."*

  Here is the crucial, compassionate point. Structurally, the cogito is **untouched** — it
  **cannot fail**, because any move that would deny it presupposes the very I doing the
  denying. The witness is still there; it is always there. What collapses is not the self
  but the I's *functional access* to the partition — the ability, *from inside this
  configuration*, to feel the difference between "I am here" and "I am worthless." The wall
  is still standing; the I just can't currently find the door in it.

> **In plain terms —** In severe depression the verdict — *I'm worthless, it's hopeless* —
> stops feeling like a thought you're having and starts feeling like *the truth of who you
> are*. The healthy gap between "me" and "this dark thought about me" closes. And here is
> what matters: **you are still there.** The real you, the bare fact of your being here,
> cannot be destroyed by depression — depression can't reach it. What's lost is your
> *access* to it, your ability to feel yourself as separate from the despair. You haven't
> stopped existing as yourself. You've lost the felt line between yourself and the illness
> wearing your voice.

  Once depression operates as proto-self, the network reorganizes **around** it. Within
  this disturbed profile, *represented* death-content can attain high ρ as the "natural
  completion" of the depressive proto-self — death-as-imagined gets integrated as the
  endpoint that *finishes* who-the-I-now-is, rather than as the not-I state that negates it.
  The [prerogative of continued existence](the-two-prerogatives.md) — the structural pull
  toward staying in existence — then operates over this distorted profile and can be
  **misdirected**: routed through a distorted preservation-ranking (**Π**, the situational
  ranking of which losses count as least self-destroying) and inaccurate projections of the
  future, so that self-cessation is *represented* as relief or as the only coherent
  continuation. **Π (preservation-ranking)** — in plain terms, your internal sense of
  "which outcome would destroy least of me"; in severe depression this ranking gets the
  facts wrong. (Note carefully: *actual* cessation is never integration and never
  continuation — it ends the I's operation entirely; only its *representation* can carry ρ.)

> **In plain terms —** The mind's normal pull to keep itself going doesn't switch off — it
> gets pointed the wrong way. On a self that has reorganized around the depression, the
> imagined ending starts to read as *completion* or *relief* rather than as annihilation,
> and the survival instinct, running on bad inputs, can end up aimed at the exit. This is a
> map error in a system that's still trying to protect you — not the person "choosing" to
> stop wanting to live.

- **Recovery** is structurally specifiable: functional access to the partition **reasserts**;
  depressive thoughts fall back from their near-cogito positions to **ordinary positions in
  the field**; the depression-as-self dissolves; and as the proto-self that pointed toward
  it comes apart, represented death-content **loses** its high-ρ grip. This matches the
  phenomenology of *"getting myself back"* — and the architecture is precise about what that
  phrase means: *the cogito was always there; what returns is access to it.*

> **From the inside —** Recovery isn't being handed a new self to replace a ruined one. It's
> the fog thinning until you can feel, again, the gap between *you* and the thought — until
> "I am worthless" goes back to being just a sentence that crossed your mind, not the
> bedrock of your being. The you that returns was never actually gone. You're getting the
> door back.

  A downstream implication, and a humane one: rational arguments against suicide often fail
  in severe depression **not because they're unsound** but because they're addressed to the
  I as it normally is — to the cogito *separated from* the depressive content. That
  separation is exactly what has functionally collapsed, so the argument can't reach its
  addressee: the I can't currently access itself as distinct from the despair holding
  near-cogito ρ. It's a failure of *address*, not of reasoning. The healthy mind the
  argument is speaking to is structurally present but, for now, functionally out of reach.

> **In plain terms —** Telling someone in severe depression all the good reasons to live
> often bounces off — not because the reasons are wrong, but because they're being spoken
> to a *healthy* version of the person that's still in there but can't be reached right now.
> The letter is true; the address is unreachable. This is why presence, time, and getting
> the substrate care it needs tend to matter more than winning the argument.

The architecture is explicit that this is a **structural** account. A clinical risk model,
treatment model, or normative claim belongs to a dedicated application paper that must
engage existing suicidology — *not* the foundation.
