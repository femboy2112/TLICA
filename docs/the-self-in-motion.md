# The Self in Motion

[← Wiki home](README.md) · Source: [File 3, §§8.9–8.11](../foundation/3_formal_apparatus.md) · [File 1, §2.10](../foundation/1_foundations.md)

---

Every page so far has drawn the [profile](identity-correlation-profile.md) as a **snapshot** —
your whole field, scored on the three coordinates, frozen at an instant. But you are never
actually still. This page is the theory's **equation of motion**: how the profile *moves*,
moment to moment, and why a living self never comes to rest. It stands to the static profile
the way a law of motion stands to a coordinate system.

> **In plain terms —** the rest of the theory maps *where you are*. This page is about
> *how you move* — and the surprising claim that you can never stop moving while you're alive.

*(This apparatus was added to the foundation in v5.4.0 and tightened under external audit in
v5.4.1, v5.4.3, and again in v5.4.4 — the last correcting the old "never settles" claim to a
bounded-orbit result; where it rests on a named guess rather than a forced result, this
page says so — see
[What's solid, what's a labeled guess](#whats-solid-and-whats-a-labeled-guess) at the end.)*

## You read yourself as a difference, never an absolute

Here is the first move, and everything hangs off it. **You never read your field in absolute
terms.** You only ever register a *difference* from where you currently sit.

Three pieces:

- **the occupied baseline** (`b`) — *where you sit right now* in profile-space: your current
  standing position.
- **the field-reading** (`f`) — what the world, read against you as you now are, is *pressing
  you toward*.
- **the differential** (`Δ = f − b`) — the gap between them. This, and only this, is what you
  actually read from the inside.

Why must it be a difference and not an absolute reading? Because **you have no frame outside
yourself to read an absolute against.** Any such frame would itself be part of your field —
one more thing read from your point of view — so there is no view from nowhere available to
you, even in principle. The [cogito](the-cogito.md) ("I am I") is your one fixed point, and
you cannot step off it.

> **In plain terms —** ask the universe how fast it's moving. You can't — there's no *outside*
> to measure against. A self is in the same position: it's its own only anchor, so all it can
> read is *change from where it is*, never an absolute level.

> **From the inside —** you never feel your mood on some absolute 0–100 scale. You feel it as
> *better or worse than a moment ago*, *lighter or heavier than usual*. The "than" is the
> whole point: the reading is always relative to your own baseline.

## Felt salience: how big the mismatch feels

The **felt magnitude** of that differential is what the theory calls **felt salience** (`V`):
a raw, pre-judgment *feeling* of how much is off. Formally

`V = τ · ‖Δ‖_ρ`

which unpacks into two dials:

- **weighted by ρ** — a mismatch on something you deeply *are* (high [identity-correlation](three-coordinates.md))
  is felt far more strongly than the same-size mismatch on something peripheral. Losing a
  core commitment reads as *loss of self*; a stranger's bad news barely registers.
- **scaled by τ (temperature)** — a reactivity dial. High `τ` and the same mismatch feels
  enormous; low `τ` and it barely lands. It's the "temperature of consciousness": how hot
  your reactions run.

> **From the inside —** the difference between a scratch on a rental car and the same scratch
> on the thing you built with your own hands. Same physical dent; wildly different felt
> salience, because ρ weights the second one as *you*.

Two honesty notes the theory insists on. First, **felt salience is not the same as
attention-grabbing salience** — the loud-bang "salience-capture" of the
[imprinting page](substrate-focus-and-imprinting.md) is about what *seizes focus*; felt
salience is the *felt size of a mismatch*. They're fenced apart on purpose. Second, this exact
formula (ρ-weighted size × temperature) is a **named posit** — a labeled, plausible guess the
architecture proposes, *not* something the rest of the theory forces. It's marked as such
wherever it appears.

## The self that never comes to rest

Now set it in motion. Your baseline **chases the field-reading**: you continually relax toward
what you're reading, closing the gap. Written as the law of motion, the baseline moves in
proportion to the mismatch (at some rate `γ`) — it slides toward `f`.

But here's the catch: **the field never stops moving.** Even in a sensory-deprivation tank, in
perfect silence and dark, your own heartbeat, breath, interoception and inner chatter keep
patterning you (see osmotic imprinting, [same page](substrate-focus-and-imprinting.md)). The
target is always drifting. So your baseline is forever chasing a thing that keeps moving — it
**never catches up to a fixed rest-point.** But not catching up is not the same as flailing:
chasing a moving target, the baseline settles into a *bounded orbit* around it — steady, often
repeating, its felt size held in check — rather than either freezing or wandering off. What is
ruled out is coming to a *dead stop*, not settling as such.

This is **allostasis** — *stability through constant change* — as opposed to homeostasis, rest
at a fixed point. A living self is not a still thing that occasionally gets disturbed; **it is
a thing in permanent motion** — but motion that settles into a bounded, often repeating *orbit*,
not a fixed stop. Disequilibrium in the sense of *no fixed rest-point* is its normal condition;
perpetual *restlessness* is not — the orbit itself can be perfectly steady.

> **In plain terms —** you're not a rock that sometimes gets pushed. You're a surfer on a wave
> that never breaks: the floor is always moving, so you never plant your feet and stop — but you
> *do* settle into a stable stance that rides it. "Standing perfectly still" was never on the
> menu; "a steady orbit on the moving water" is exactly what you find.

> **From the inside —** why "settled" never means "frozen." The calm you reach on a quiet
> evening isn't your inner life switching off; it's a low, steady hum you keep *actively*
> holding — a stance that rides the small ongoing drift rather than ending it. Settled is a
> stable *way of moving*, not a stop.

That the self never comes to *rest at a fixed point* is a **derived** result — it follows from
the setup, given that the drive keeps going. That it nonetheless *settles into a bounded orbit*
— its felt salience held below a bound, not growing without limit — is **also derived**, given
the relaxation law. (A brief instant where you exactly match the field *can* happen — a fleeting
"click" — it just can't *last* while the field keeps moving.) What is **not** claimed is that the
felt magnitude stays perpetually restless: the orbit can be steady, even constant. The exact
form of the relaxation law and its rate `γ`, on the other hand, are **posited**.

## What death is — and what it isn't

This forces a sharp correction to a tempting mistake. **Zero felt salience is not death.**
You can hit `V = 0` — a moment where the mismatch closes, nothing feels off — and be *fully
alive*; it's just the baseline instantaneously catching the field, a crossing on a moving orbit.

So what is death, in this picture? **Not a value of the feeling — the stopping of the motion
itself.** A living self is a **driven non-equilibrium steady state**: a pattern held together
by continuous throughput, like a flame or a whirlpool — take away the flow and the pattern
*ceases*, it doesn't "reach zero." Death is the substrate no longer sustaining the orbit at
all: no longer forming the reading, or no longer able to track, or no longer able to react.
The reactivity-fading-to-nothing (`τ → 0`) direction is the theory's **posited** model of the
*dying* — offered as a labeled hypothesis, not derived, and the architecture keeps treating
the *presence* of consciousness as a starting assumption, never something it reads off a number.

> **From the inside —** the relief where a long ache finally goes quiet is a *crossing*, not an
> ending. The theory is careful here precisely so that "feeling nothing" is never confused with
> "being gone."

## Slack: the budget to push against the current

So far the motion is **passive** — you slide toward the field, downhill, automatically. But you
can also push *against* the current: hold a position the pull is trying to move you off, spend
effort to go somewhere the automatic slide wouldn't take you. That costs a budget, and the
budget is **slack**:

`S = M − Pressure`

- `M` is your inherited **capacity** (the same fixed ceiling from the
  [focus page](substrate-focus-and-imprinting.md)).
- `Pressure` is what the current situation is *consuming* of that capacity (it rises with felt
  salience and with how sharply a response is demanded).
- `S` is what's left over.

The gate: when **`S > 0`**, you have room — the reflexive operator can move the baseline against
the pull, and *this is the felt sense of having chosen*. When **`S ≤ 0`**, there's no room —
you run on automatic, sliding downhill with the gradient.

> **From the inside —** the difference between a calm afternoon (you can sit with a hard feeling,
> turn it over, *decide*) and a screaming-deadline overwhelm (you just *react*, and only later
> think "that wasn't really me choosing"). Same person; different slack.

Two refinements. There's **perceived** slack versus **actual** slack — and their gap is the
maddening "**stuck but capable**" state: the room to act is genuinely there, but your self-model
never certifies it, so it sits unused. And this particular `S` is only **one margin of a whole
family** of them — time, regained ownership, meaningful engagement are *different* margins, not
one interchangeable fuel tank (the research note *A Life Between Frames* develops the family;
the [referent-routing page](app-referent-routing.md) uses it). The foundation formalizes only
the one that gates this reflexive push. The gate itself (the threshold sitting exactly at `S=0`)
is a **posit**, and the stronger prediction — that *felt choice* comes apart from *actual
behavior* right at the threshold — is flagged **UNVERIFIED**: a real, testable claim the theory
makes but has not confirmed.

## The push against the current is you re-writing yourself

What *is* that reflexive push, mechanically? Here's the elegant part: **it's the same mechanism
as osmotic imprinting — just sourced from you instead of from the world.**

The [imprinting page](substrate-focus-and-imprinting.md) described the world quietly writing your
ρ from outside: Pavlov's bell, the smell that carries dread, the accent that makes you brace —
patterns soaked in without your consent. The reflexive operator (**[Mode B](modes-of-development.md)**)
is that *very same writing*, sourced from the inside — **you imprinting a change on your own ρ.**
Pushing the baseline against the current just *is* the self re-carving its own landscape,
deliberately doing to itself what chronic exposure does to it from outside.

Two consequences fall out cleanly:

- **It's always running, but quiet at first.** Two things share the name *Mode B*, and it's
  worth keeping them apart: the *mechanism* (the self-writing process) and the recognizable
  *firing* it can produce — a real, effective moment of self-direction. Like osmotic imprinting,
  the **mechanism** is *always on* — which doesn't mean you're consciously reflecting every
  instant; actual firings are occasional. At the very first instant of a self there's nothing
  accumulated to work with, so ability is zero; a moment later the world's always-on imprinting
  has begun to lay down structure. Does that make the mechanism's ability nonzero *immediately*,
  or does it stay at zero until enough has piled up to cross a threshold? The theory *posits* the
  first — nonzero right away, just **swamped by noise** — and flags the second as a fair
  alternative; always-on accumulation alone doesn't settle it. Either way, what the foundation's
  [modes of development](modes-of-development.md) call Mode B being "not yet available" early is
  the recognizable *firing*: too little is built up, and the self-reading window too small, for
  it to rise above the din and actually steer your development. So what "grows up" isn't the
  mechanism switching on — it's the *signal rising out of the noise*.
- **"Escaping a rut" doesn't need a cliff.** Because the floor is always moving, breaking out of
  a stuck pattern can happen just by the field *outrunning* your position — no dramatic tipping
  point required. And some ruts are **sticky**: chronic conditions can displace your baseline
  *for good*, carving a landscape that doesn't spring back on its own. ("Relief is not
  resolution," made literal.) Re-carving one of those from the inside is exactly what the
  reflexive push is for.

> **From the inside —** white-knuckling through a craving, or choosing to reframe a loss instead
> of sinking into it, isn't fighting your wiring from *outside* it. It's re-wiring — running the
> same soak-it-in machinery the world used on you, pointed inward, on purpose.

## What's solid, and what's a labeled guess

The architecture is deliberately honest about which parts of all this are *forced* and which are
*proposed*, and this page keeps that line visible rather than smoothing it over:

- **Derived** (follows from the setup): that the reading is a *differential*, never absolute;
  that a living self never comes to *rest at a fixed point* while the drive persists; and that
  under bounded drive its felt salience stays *bounded* — settling into a driven orbit rather
  than growing or wandering without limit.
- **Posited** (named, plausible guesses — labeled, not smuggled): the exact felt-salience formula
  `V = τ‖Δ‖_ρ`; the specific relaxation law and rate `γ`; the death-as-vanishing-reactivity limit;
  the location of the slack gate at `S = 0`.
- **Unverified** (a real prediction, not yet tested): that felt choice dissociates from behavior
  right at the slack threshold.

The apparatus is also **not claimed to be new**: it re-describes, in the theory's own vocabulary,
structure already known to science — reference-dependent / predictive coding (the differential
reading), allostasis and active inference (the driven orbit), and dual-process self-regulation
(the slack gate). It's offered as a **better-grounded, cogito-up statement** of that shared
structure — built from "I am I" outward — not as a new mechanism.
