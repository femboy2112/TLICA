# Other Minds

[← Wiki home](README.md) · Source: [File 4, §9](../foundation/4_derived_concepts_and_predictions.md)

---

The architecture is built **from one perspective** — anchored to a single modeling I (the
particular cogniting self the whole picture is drawn around; in this theory written
**m**, and its innermost self-identity written **Î** (the cogito-I) — in plain
terms, the bare "I am here, doing the experiencing" that you can't doubt without already
doing it). So how do *other* minds appear in it? The answer is **perspectival realism**:
other minds are real, but from inside any one perspective they are reached only indirectly.

> **In plain terms —** Everything in this theory is drawn from *someone's* point of view —
> never from nowhere. So the question "where do other people fit?" isn't a footnote; it's
> built into the shape of the thing. The answer: they're really there, but you only ever
> reach them by inference, never by looking straight at the inside of them.

## Three levels

The theory keeps three things formally distinct, which ordinary talk runs together. When
you say "I know my friend," you're actually holding three very different things at once,
and the theory insists on prying them apart:

1. **The actual other I** — written **o** (one of the other potential I's; formally
   `o ∈ O_m`, a member of the set of other minds). If it's genuinely cogniting, it has its
   *own* core I in its own instantiation — its own private "I am here," its own cogito-I
   `Î_o`. From your perspective, that core is **not directly given** to you. You can't peer
   into someone else's first-person and *find* the bare fact of their being-someone the way
   you find your own.
2. **Content-about-the-other-I** — your inferences and beliefs *about* them, written
   `h^m_t(S)` (a hypothesis-content living in *your* field `𝖠^m_t`, at
   time *t*). "She's probably tired," "he didn't mean it," "they'd love this" — these are
   all yours. They have their own coordinates in *your* experience, not theirs.
3. **Apparent-I-as-presented** — written `a ∈ AppI^m_t` — the body,
   behavior, and communication you actually perceive: the face across the table, the tone
   of voice, the text on the screen. This is the *outside* of another mind, the only part
   that ever reaches your senses.

When you "know someone," you have levels 2 and 3 — your guesses about them, and the
surface they show you. Level 1 — their actual inner core, the lit-up "someone home" on
their side — you never have directly.

> **In plain terms —** There are three things, not one: the real person behind their eyes
> (which you can never get *inside* of), your private theory *about* that person, and the
> face-voice-words you can see and hear. "Knowing someone" is having the last two and
> betting they add up to the first.

> **From the inside —** I look at my partner across the kitchen and feel that I *know* them.
> But if I'm honest, what I actually have is their face, their sigh, the way they set the
> mug down — plus a running story in my own head about what all that means. The them on the
> inside, the one who's actually *there* the way I'm here, I infer. I never touch it.

## Realism, not solipsism

This is **not** solipsism. Solipsism is the lonely doctrine that maybe *I'm the only mind*
and everyone else is a kind of empty puppet. The architecture says the opposite: it
explicitly *permits* other minds to exist (in the theory's notation, it allows
`O_m ≠ ∅` — the set of other minds is allowed to be non-empty) and
permits every apparent other to be a genuine cogniting being.

The asymmetry is **epistemic, not metaphysical**. There *is* a gap between you and other
people — but it's a gap in *access*, not in *reality*. The theory writes this as
`Direct_m(o) < Direct_m(Î)` —

> **Direct (direct-givenness)** — in plain terms, how *immediately* a
> thing is handed to you, with no inference in between. Your own existence is handed to you
> at full strength; another person's inner life arrives only second-hand, through their
> face and words and your guesses. The inequality just says: *your own core is more
> directly given to you than anyone else's is.*

— and that's the whole of it. Other minds are "less directly given," not *less real*. You
have direct access to your own core and only inferential access to theirs; that's a fact
about the angle you're looking from, not a ranking of who counts as a real being.

> **In plain terms —** "I reach myself directly and reach you only by inference" is *not*
> the same as "I'm real and you might not be." The first is about *how* you get to
> something; the second is about *whether it's there*. The theory says the first, and flatly
> refuses the second.

And the form is **shared**: if other minds exist, each builds the same architecture for
itself. The theory calls this **form-invariance conditional on existence** —

> **form-invariance (conditional on existence)** — in plain terms: *if* there are other
> minds, they're not built on some alien plan; each of them has the same kind of inner
> setup you do — its own cogito, its own field, its own three-level view of *you* as one of
> *its* "other minds." The "conditional" part just flags that the theory can't prove from
> the inside that any particular other mind is actually running; it only says what shape
> they'd have *if* they are.

What's *not* shared is any lived self — there is **no global, shared lived-I network**.
Your accumulated sense of who-you-are and theirs don't merge into one big pool. Shared
form, separate instances. Two computers can run the same program and still have completely
separate memories; that's the picture.

> **From the inside —** It's strangely symmetric. I'm the center of my world and you're one
> of the figures at its edge — but from where *you* stand, *you're* the center and *I'm* a
> figure at the edge. Same architecture, two separate centers. Neither of us is the
> stand-in.

## The honest limit

Whether any particular apparent-other is *actually* a cogniting being is an
**outside-perspective question the modeling I cannot settle from inside**. No
single-perspective architecture could — by construction, you only ever have your own
field to look in, and the certificate "yes, there's really someone home over there" would
have to come from outside any single field. The theory **assumes** other minds exist
(perspectival realism) while **admitting** it can't verify this from within — and treats
that as a structural feature of the perspectival framing, not a defect to be patched. (See
the out-of-scope items in [Predictions & Exclusions](predictions-and-exclusions.md).)

> **In plain terms —** The theory is honest about the one thing it genuinely can't do from
> the inside: *prove* you're not surrounded by very convincing empties. It doesn't paper
> over this with a fake proof. It says — this is just what it's like to be a point of view;
> the doubt isn't a bug, it's the shape of being situated *somewhere*.

## Why two minds disagree

The [prerogative of consistency](the-two-prerogatives.md) gives the structural form of
intersubjective friction. To see why, start with what the prerogative is.

> **PtCns (prerogative of consistency)** — in plain terms, the standing, un-chosen way your
> own frame acts as the *measuring stick*. Anything you meet that doesn't fit gets felt as
> "off," and anything that fits gets felt as "of course." You don't *decide* to treat your
> frame as the standard — the very feeling of something being wrong *is* your frame quietly
> being the standard. It's at work everywhere, not just with people: a door that pushes when
> you expected pull, a fact that contradicts what you knew, a wrong answer, a vibe that
> something's not right.

When two minds meet, **two consistency-standards operate at once**, each treating the
other as not-I (as outside-the-self) from its own frame, each implicitly its own anchor.
Each one is, structurally and without noticing, holding *its own* frame up as the ruler and
measuring the other against it. The asymmetry isn't that one is correct and the other
wrong — it's that **each frame is its own standard, with no view-from-nowhere available to
either.** There's no neutral referee's frame hovering above the two of you to declare the
winner; there are just two anchored points of view, each anchored to itself.

> **In plain terms —** A real argument isn't two people failing to reach an obvious shared
> truth. It's two measuring-sticks pointed at each other. Each of you automatically treats
> *your own* sense of what's reasonable as the baseline and the other's as the deviation —
> not by choice, but because that's how having a point of view works. There's no overhead
> view that settles it, because every view is *somebody's*.

> **From the inside —** We're fighting about whose turn it was to call. To me it's obvious:
> I called last time, the pattern's clear, you're just not seeing it. And the maddening part
> is that across the table the *exact same* obviousness is running in reverse — to you *I'm*
> the one not seeing it. Neither of us is lying. We're each standing at the center of our
> own frame, and there's no third chair labeled "the truth" for either of us to sit in.

So disagreement, on this account, is built into the structure of meeting another mind, not
a failure of one party. Two anchored frames, each its own standard — friction is the
*normal* output of that geometry, not a malfunction in it.

This also reframes the [self-sacrifice](the-two-prerogatives.md) cases: from the inside,
"valuing another's life over your own" is a comparison of *represented* future-states —
because the other's continuation is something you *project*, never directly observe. You
can't watch their inner life carry on; you can only picture, from your own side, a world in
which it does. The dramatic outside description "they valued someone else's life more than
their own" is, from inside the one acting, a choice between two imagined futures — and in
one of them the person they love is pictured as still going on.

> **In plain terms —** Even the most outward-facing act — diving in front of a car for
> someone — is, from the inside, still happening inside *your* one perspective. You're not
> directly accessing their survival; you're comparing two pictures *you* hold of how things
> could go, and reaching for the one where they're still in it. Other-directedness all the
> way down, but still routed through a single point of view — which is exactly perspectival
> realism, not solipsism.
