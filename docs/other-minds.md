# Other Minds

[← Wiki home](README.md) · Source: [File 4, §9](../foundation/4_derived_concepts_and_predictions.md)

---

The architecture is built **from one perspective** — anchored to a single modeling I. So
how do *other* minds appear in it? The answer is **perspectival realism**: other minds are
real, but from inside any one perspective they are reached only indirectly.

## Three levels

The theory keeps three things formally distinct, which ordinary talk runs together:

1. **The actual other I** — if it's genuinely cogniting, it has its *own* core I in its
   own instantiation. From your perspective, that core is **not directly given** to you.
2. **Content-about-the-other-I** — your inferences and beliefs *about* them. These are
   contents in *your* field, with their own coordinates.
3. **Apparent-I-as-presented** — the body, behavior, and communication you actually
   perceive.

When you "know someone," you have levels 2 and 3. Level 1 — their actual inner core — you
never have directly.

## Realism, not solipsism

This is **not** solipsism. The architecture explicitly *permits* other minds to exist and
permits every apparent other to be a genuine cogniting being. The asymmetry is
**epistemic, not metaphysical**: other minds are "less directly given" only in the sense
that you have direct access to your own core and inferential access to theirs — not in the
sense that they're less real.

And the form is **shared**: if other minds exist, each builds the same architecture for
itself ("form-invariance conditional on existence"). What's *not* shared is any lived
self — there is **no global, shared lived-I network**. Shared form, separate instances.

## The honest limit

Whether any particular apparent-other is *actually* a cogniting being is an
**outside-perspective question the modeling I cannot settle from inside**. No
single-perspective architecture could. The theory **assumes** other minds exist
(perspectival realism) while **admitting** it can't verify this from within — and treats
that as a structural feature of the perspectival framing, not a defect to be patched. (See
the out-of-scope items in [Predictions & Exclusions](predictions-and-exclusions.md).)

## Why two minds disagree

The [prerogative of consistency](the-two-prerogatives.md) gives the structural form of
intersubjective friction. When two minds meet, **two consistency-standards operate at
once**, each treating the other as not-I from its own frame, each implicitly its own
anchor. The asymmetry isn't that one is correct and the other wrong — it's that **each
frame is its own standard, with no view-from-nowhere available to either.** Disagreement,
on this account, is built into the structure of meeting another mind, not a failure of one
party.

This also reframes the [self-sacrifice](the-two-prerogatives.md) cases: from the inside,
"valuing another's life over your own" is a comparison of *represented* future-states —
because the other's continuation is something you *project*, never directly observe.
