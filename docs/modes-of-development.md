# Modes of Development

[← Wiki home](README.md) · Source: [File 2, §4](../foundation/2_access_and_development.md)

---

The [cogito](the-cogito.md) is anchored from the start, but there is **no fully-formed
self at that moment**. The lived self *emerges*, through three modes of development.
[Access to Intrinsic Structure](access-to-intrinsic-structure.md) is about building the
reasoning toolkit; this page is about building the *self*.

## Modes are response-types, not predictors

This framing matters. The three modes are **categories of developmental response** to an
encounter — not a rule for *which* response will fire. The theory says what each mode
*does* if it operates; it does **not** predict which one operates for a given encounter
(that depends on substrate, history, environment, and chance it doesn't formalize). The
analogy: thermodynamics doesn't predict which way one molecule jumps, but it predicts
what kinds of motion are possible and the statistics over many. (See exclusion 17 in
[Predictions & Exclusions](predictions-and-exclusions.md).)

## Mode A — Adversarial differentiation

When you encounter something "other" enough, it registers as **discrepant** — foreign,
out of place, wrong-from-here. That registration **draws a boundary**. Mode A is the
mechanism by which an I/not-I distinction exists at all; without it there's no separable
self to build on.

Built into Mode A is the **prerogative of consistency**: the discrepancy isn't registered
neutrally — *your own frame is the implicit standard* against which the other thing
counts as discrepant. (See [The Two Prerogatives](the-two-prerogatives.md).)

**Mode A produces the boundary.**

## Mode C — Positive identification

When you encounter something that registers as *continuous with you* — comforting, close,
already-yours — it gets **folded inside** the boundary as part of the (proto-)self.

The diagnostic case: take candy from a baby and the baby grieves intensely — not as
denial of a wanted object, but as **loss of self**. The candy had been positively
identified as part of the proto-self, before a firm boundary existed to exclude it. The
adult, in whom Mode A has done its work, experiences the same loss as loss-of-*thing*.

**Mode C populates the inside.** Most of the lived self is built this way.

## Mode B — Reflexive meta-reasoning

You turn attention onto your **own structure** — the cogito-as-act actualizing. Mode B
**can't run at the very beginning**: there has to be *something accumulated* to reflect
on. Once Mode A has drawn a boundary and Mode C has populated some structure, reflexive
attention has material to work on, and it **refines the whole**.

Two sub-types by trigger: **internally-triggered** ("wait — what am I doing?" arising
unprompted) and **externally-triggered** (a question, a work of art, a vast landscape
that throws your own position into relief). Whether any given encounter sparks Mode B is
**stochastic, with environment-shaped odds** — settings rich in reflection-prompting
stimuli raise the per-encounter probability.

On *when* Mode B switches on across a population, the theory commits to three structural
constraints — **zero at the very start, non-zero later, varying between minds** — but
**deliberately does not fix a distributional shape** (bell curve, log-normal, etc. are
left open as an empirical question).

## Early development happens *to* you

A consequence the theory takes seriously: through the earliest phases, the I has **no
intentional capacity to direct its own development**. Mode A firings are triggered by what
the world presents; Mode C identifications are handed to you by your immediate
environment; Mode B isn't online yet. There is no self that pre-exists its own
development to steer it.

So **early variation between people is not anyone's choice** — it reflects a substrate
they didn't pick and an environment they didn't control. This grounds the theory's
**descriptive, non-moralizing** stance toward patterns formed in that window, including
many later classed as mental illness. See [Profile-Shape Disturbances](profile-shape-disturbances.md).

## Typical progression

- **Phase 0** (newborn): bare cogito; boundary undrawn; Mode C dominant by default;
  Mode A not yet firing; Mode B impossible.
- **Phase 1**: encounters with distant-enough not-I start firing Mode A; the boundary
  forms; Mode C continues; Mode B may become possible toward the end.
- **Phase 2**: A and C run continuously; the boundary sharpens; Mode B comes online at
  its typical onset.
- **Phase 3** (mature): all three operate; the I can deliberately seek out
  reflection-rich environments.

These are *typical* patterns. Atypical trajectories are statistical outliers, not
failures of the architecture.
