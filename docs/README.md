## One-paragraph reminder

Classical accounts of the self blur three things that come apart in real experience:
how strongly something is *in contact* with you, how *undeniably real* it seems, and
how much it is *part of who you are*. TLICA makes these three independent coordinates
and builds a self from them — starting from one undeniable postulate ("I am I") and the
claim that logical and mathematical structure is real in the world independent of any
mind. Everything else about you is *acquired*. The result is a map of how deeply each
content is bound into the network that is you, and an account of how that map forms,
shifts, and can be disturbed.
