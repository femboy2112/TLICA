# The Two Layers

[← Wiki home](README.md) · Source: [File 1, §2.1–2.6](../foundation/1_foundations.md)

---

The architecture divides everything into two layers. The name of the whole theory —
*Two-Layer* Identity-Correlation Architecture — comes from this split.

The split answers a simple question with two very different answers: *does this thing
hold whether or not anyone is around to think it?* Some things do — `1 + 1 = 2` doesn't
wait for a counter. Most things don't — your fear of dogs, the ache in your knee, the
person you love. The first kind go in the **intrinsic layer**; everything else goes in the
**asymptotic layer**. Almost your entire mental life lives in the second one.

> **In plain terms —** there's the handful of things that are just true no matter what
> (math, logic), and then there's everything you actually experience. Two bins. The
> theory is named after the line between them.

## The intrinsic layer

The intrinsic layer contains **mathematics, logic, and reason**. The theory treats these
as ***in re*** — a Latin phrase meaning "in the thing." In plain terms, the structure is
sitting *inside the world itself*, not pasted on by a mind. It holds whether or not any
mind is present to verify it.

> **In plain terms —** "in re" just means *out there in the world, not in your head*.
> Two plus two would still equal four in an empty universe with no one to add.

The diagnostic image is the **river and the stones**: when a river pushes one stone next
to another, there are now two stones. The arithmetic `1 + 1 = 2` is realized in the
situation itself, without anyone needing to count. The *cognized* instance — a mind
picking up the stones and reasoning "that's two" — is a separate event that depends on a
mind existing. The structure is in the world; the access to it is the mind's.

So math and logic are intrinsic in **two senses at once**, and it helps to keep them
apart:

- **Ontologically** — they are simply true, woven into how the world is put together,
  regardless of whether any "I" is there. (The two stones are two whether or not you
  count them.)
- **Universally accessible** — any thinking being could *in principle* reach them by
  reasoning, given enough developmental growth. (Anyone who learns to count arrives at
  the same two.)

The first claim is about the *world*; the second is about *reason*. The theory commits to
both.

> **From the inside —** you don't *decide* that contradicting yourself feels wrong, the
> way you decide you like a song. When something doesn't add up, the not-adding-up
> presses on you from outside, like a fact you bumped into rather than an opinion you
> formed.

This is a **naturalist-realist** commitment, deliberately weaker than **Platonism**.
(Platonism is the stronger old idea that numbers and forms live in their own separate,
non-physical realm.) The theory does **not** claim mathematical objects live in a separate
abstract realm. It claims only that the world the I is embedded in *contains pervasive
structure a mind can encounter* — which is what the empirical sciences find at every
scale: mathematical regularities in physics, chemistry, biology, statistics. (See
[Access to Intrinsic Structure](access-to-intrinsic-structure.md) for how a mind reaches
it.)

> **In plain terms —** the theory is *not* saying numbers float in a magic heaven. It's
> saying the world is shot through with reliable structure — patterns that keep showing
> up everywhere you look — and a mind can run into that structure and learn it. That's a
> much more modest, science-friendly claim than the heaven version.

Intrinsic items are **outside the φ-scale entirely**. **φ (phi)** — in plain terms, a
measure of *how indistinguishable-from-true something looks when you reason hard about
it*, how close it sits to undeniable truth (see [Three Coordinates](three-coordinates.md)).
Intrinsic items aren't "maximally true things measured on the same ruler as everything
else" — they *are* the ruler's reference point, the ontological standard the rest is
measured *against*. You don't measure the meter-stick in meters; it's what meters are
measured by.

> **In plain terms —** you can't ask "how true does `1 + 1 = 2` look?" because it's the
> thing you compare *other* stuff to. It's not high up on the scale — it's the zero-mark
> the scale is built on.

**A note on "intrinsic structure."** This phrase just means: *the real, mind-independent
patterning of the world* — the math and logic baked into how things actually are. Object
persistence (things keep existing when you stop looking) and cause-and-effect are early,
short-timescale examples a child runs into and learns from. They're "intrinsic structure"
because the pattern is in the world; the child's grasp of it is the *access*.

## The asymptotic layer

The asymptotic layer contains **everything else**: every content in the I's field that
doesn't hold by reason alone — perceptions, thoughts, emotions, memories, relationships,
bodily sensations — **conscious or unconscious**.

The word **"asymptotic"** comes from a curve that gets forever closer to a line without
ever touching it. In plain terms: these contents *approach* intrinsic truth without ever
reaching it. None of them is dead-certain in the way math is. The nearest any of them
gets is the [cogito](the-cogito.md) — "I am I" — which sits one infinitesimal step short.

> **In plain terms —** "asymptotic" = *almost, but never all the way there*. Everything
> you see, feel, remember, and believe lives here. It can be vivid, obvious, overwhelming
> — but never *math-certain*. There's always a hair's-breadth of "could be otherwise."

### The four content classes

A key subtlety the theory is careful about: the asymptotic layer is *not* the same as
"the conscious mind." It contains **four classes of content**, sorted by **two
independent questions**:

1. *Is it present to you right now?* — **phenomenal availability** (jargon for: is it
   actually showing up in your experience this moment?). The theory writes this as **A**,
   which is just 1 (yes, present) or 0 (no, not present).
2. *Can you build a path back to why it's true / where it came from?* — **φ-status** (can
   you trace a verification pathway with the mental tools you currently have?).

Cross those two yes/no questions and you get four boxes:

- **Conscious-clear** — present, *and* you can trace the pathway. *Examples:* worked-out
  arithmetic, an explicit argument you can spell out, a memory you can source.
- **Conscious-fuzzy / source-opaque** — present, but you *can't say why*. *Examples:*
  panic, dread, raw affect, a "bad feeling" about someone. Fully conscious — you are
  100% feeling it — just **opaque**. This is emphatically *not* "almost unconscious."
- **Latent / retrievable** — not present now, but *reachable* if you go get it.
  *Examples:* your phone number before you think of it, a dormant skill, a fact you know
  but aren't currently using.
- **Unconscious-operative** — not present *and* not traceable, yet still **shaping you**.
  *Examples:* an implicit bias, a repressed memory, a disposition you absorbed without
  noticing (an **osmotic** imprint — soaked in by repeated exposure rather than
  deliberately learned).

> **From the inside —** *conscious-clear* is "2 and 2 make 4, here's the work."
> *Conscious-fuzzy* is "I don't know why, but I don't trust this room." *Latent* is "wait
> — it'll come to me." *Unconscious-operative* is the flinch you didn't know you had until
> someone pointed it out.

> **In plain terms —** "is it in my awareness?" and "can I explain it?" are *separate*
> questions. You can feel something fully and have no idea where it came from. You can
> know something perfectly and not be thinking of it. The four boxes are just every
> combination of those two.

### Why φ can be undefined while κ and ρ still apply

Here's the payoff of the four classes. Three coordinates get placed on the contents of
experience (see [Three Coordinates](three-coordinates.md)):

- **κ (kappa, contact)** — in plain terms, *how strongly a thing is touching you right
  now*: the world pressing on your senses, the body in current sensation.
- **ρ (rho, identity-correlation)** — in plain terms, *how much of you is bound up in a
  thing*; the stuff whose loss would actually change who you are.
- **φ (phi, truth-indistinguishability)** — in plain terms, *how undeniable it looks when
  you reason about it*.

The crucial move: **φ only applies where you can build a verification pathway.** If you
*can't* trace why a content is true or where it came from, **φ is undefined** for it — there's
simply no path to measure along. But **κ and ρ are defined across the *whole* asymptotic
layer**, pathway or no pathway.

This is why the theory can speak of identity-correlation (ρ) for a **repressed memory you
can't access**: the memory isn't present (A = 0) and you can't trace it (φ undefined), yet
it can still be deeply part of who you are (high ρ) and can still shape you. Likewise a
**panic episode** can be slamming into you (extreme κ) and fully present (A = 1) while its
*source* stays opaque — so φ stays undefined even though κ is sky-high.

> **In plain terms —** "how true does this look?" is a question you can only ask about
> things you can reason your way back to. But "how much is this touching me?" and "how
> much is this *me*?" you can ask about *anything* in your mental life — even the buried
> stuff you'll never explain. That's why a memory you can't reach can still be load-bearing
> for who you are.

> **From the inside —** the dread in your chest at 3 a.m. is undeniably *here* (high
> contact) and undeniably *yours* (high identity-correlation), even though you couldn't
> pass a quiz on where it came from (no truth-pathway). The theory says: of course — those
> are three different rulers, and only one of them needs an explanation to work.

One more thing the theory is honest about: the "no pathway" judgment is *the I's own
view from inside*, not a verdict from outside. A content might have real verification
structure that's simply invisible to you right now. Whether it does is an
outside-perspective fact you can't reach from within — and the theory says so plainly.

## The boundary between them

The two layers meet at exactly one point: the [cogito](the-cogito.md). "I am I" is the
single indexed instance of universal reflexivity ("x = x") — one perspectival step out
of the intrinsic layer and into a particular point of view.

Unpack that. **Reflexivity** is the logical fact that everything equals itself: for any x,
`x = x`. That's universal, intrinsic, true of everything-in-general. The cogito takes that
universal rule and *indexes* it to one specific viewpoint: not "everything equals itself"
but "***I* am I**" — said from inside one particular here-and-now perspective. That tiny
move from *everything-in-general* to *this-one-me* is the single step from the intrinsic
layer into the lived world.

That step is what the symbol **φ = 1 − δ** measures. **δ (delta)** — in plain terms, the
*infinitesimal* (smaller than any ordinary number you could name) price of taking one
step into a point of view. So φ = 1 − δ means: *as close to bedrock truth as anything in
your experience can possibly get — short by the thinnest sliver imaginable, the sliver
that is "being a perspective at all."*

> **In plain terms —** "I exist, and I'm me" is the surest thing you've got — closer to
> rock-solid than any other thought you'll ever have. But it's still not *quite* the same
> as a flat logical law, because it's said from *somewhere*, by *someone*. That
> one-step-back-from-pure-logic is the seam where the math layer and your lived life are
> stitched together.
