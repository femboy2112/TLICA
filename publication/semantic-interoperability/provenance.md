# Provenance — Semantic Interoperability

The purpose of this file is to record, honestly, how the ideas behind Paper I were
developed, so that any authorship or disclosure claim made at submission is true.
The goal is **not** to manufacture a story of unaided creation. It is to be able to
say exactly what happened and to meet the real bar: understanding, verification,
personal prose authorship, and venue-compatible disclosure.

## How this work was developed

The interoperability material was developed through sustained interaction between
Leah and generative AI systems (primarily large language models, in a long series
of working conversations, plus AI-assisted editing of repository documents). That
interaction included, in varying combination:

- conversational elicitation and drawing-out of Leah's own ideas;
- structural decomposition and organization of those ideas;
- literature discovery (candidate sources and traditions to check);
- adversarial objection generation;
- formal notation and schematic formulas;
- drafting and rewriting of prose in the research notes;
- claim-ledger construction;
- synthesis across many conversations and across repository documents.

This is **not** equivalent to spellcheck or copy editing. Any account that treated
the existing research notes as purely human-written prose would be false. The
correct response is disclosure, not concealment (roadmap §5.3, §8).

## Idea-origin classification

Every claim that reaches [`claim_ledger.md`](claim_ledger.md) carries an origin
label. The categories, and what each requires:

| Origin | Meaning | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **Leah-originated** | Leah's own prior insight, formed before or independent of the AI dialogue | Still must be verified and personally restated; lowest provenance risk |
| **Dialogue-elicited Leah idea** | Leah's idea, drawn out or sharpened in conversation with AI | Leah's, but the eliciting interaction is logged; ordinary in the process |
| **Jointly synthesized** | Emerged from the back-and-forth; no clean single author | Requires strong independent reconstruction before it can be owned |
| **AI-proposed, later adopted** | First proposed by an AI system, subsequently understood, tested, and endorsed by Leah | Adoptable **only** after Leah can defend it under the operative test; disclosure-sensitive |
| **Literature-derived** | Traceable to a specific published source | Requires a verified citation and a personally read page reference (roadmap §5.2) |
| **Uncertain provenance** | Origin genuinely unclear | Not disqualifying, but triggers stronger independent reconstruction and explicit disclosure (roadmap Gate H2) |

Uncertain or AI-proposed origin does **not** bar a claim from the manuscript. It
raises the bar: the claim must pass the operative authorship test —

$$
\boxed{\text{Can Leah explain, defend, revise, or abandon this claim without outsourcing the judgment?}}
$$

— and its history must be logged in [`ai_use_log.md`](ai_use_log.md) and disclosed
where the venue requires it.

## What publication actually requires

A clean human rewrite is a method for establishing genuine authorship and
comprehension. It is **not** a method for pretending AI was never involved, and it
does not by itself make the workflow compatible with a venue that forbids the AI use
that occurred (roadmap §5.3, §8).

Publication of Paper I requires, jointly:

1. **Understanding** — Leah can reconstruct every argument independently.
2. **Verification** — every citation personally accessed, read, and checked
   (roadmap §5.2).
3. **Personal prose authorship** — the submission prose is deliberately written and
   revised by Leah; AI does not ghostwrite it.
4. **Venue-compatible disclosure** — the AI-use history is disclosed truthfully in
   the form and placement the venue requires, and the venue's policy actually
   permits the workflow that happened.

None of those four is satisfied by rewriting alone. All four are tracked in this
workspace.
