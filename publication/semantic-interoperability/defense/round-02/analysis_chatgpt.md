## 1. Executive verdict

Round 2 produced real conceptual progress. Leah did not merely defend the Round-1 wording;
she revised the object under pressure. Several previously fused variables are now visible
as distinct:

1. **Semantic reconstruction:** whether the receiver built a usable model of what the
   speaker meant.
2. **Epistemic adequacy:** whether that model and the speaker's claim answer correctly to
   reality.
3. **Commitment or embodiment:** whether the receiver or speaker organizes conduct around
   the represented model.
4. **Good-faith procedure:** whether the parties preserve enough calibrated humility and
   willingness to continue reconstructing one another under uncertainty.

The strongest Round-2 result is supplied unintentionally by Q9. Leah's nonpolitical
examples—a child telling a teacher exactly what the teacher wants to hear and an employee
saying “yes” to an impossible order under authority—are not clean cases of failed
understanding. They can be cases of **excellent understanding plus strategic assent,
coercion, resistance, or non-embodiment**. That means the earlier category “humoring
semantic regurgitation” cannot remain one undifferentiated kind of non-understanding.
Surface agreement can coexist with:

- misunderstanding;
- understanding without endorsement;
- understanding under coercion;
- strategic deception;
- malicious compliance;
- inability to act on what was understood.

This sharply strengthens the boundary:

> **understanding is not agreement, sincerity, embodiment, or behavioral conformity.**

Round 2 also clarifies that no behavioral probe gives direct access to another person's
understanding. Words and actions are evidence generated by a dynamic system, not a window
onto the state itself. Leah therefore moves toward a fallible, iterative probe model rather
than a decisive one-shot test.

At the same time, several new commitments need correction or further pressure:

- treating **reality itself** as the transported invariant conflicts with the paper's
  boundary that high interoperability can coexist with shared error;
- “syntactically correct” reasoning is not sufficient for reasonable or reality-respecting
  inference;
- the arithmetic example demonstrates equality after evaluation, not literally an
  isomorphism as currently stated;
- “every question deserves an answer” and “direct the person to the correct experiences”
  create resource, safety, reciprocity, and paternalism problems;
- all three verification functions are important, but they cannot all be constitutive of
  semantic interoperability without collapsing interoperability into truth and sincerity.

**Round-2 verdict:** consistency and precision **substantially advanced**, but not fully
closed. Several claims are now ownership-ready in core form, while their exact boundaries
remain unresolved. No claim is automatically promoted by this report.

## 2. Faithful reconstruction of each answer

The reconstructions below aim to preserve the structure of Leah's answers without turning
them into publication prose.

### Q1 — Reality, lossy dictionaries, and inferential equivalence

Leah relocates the invariant from the message to reality itself. Reality is what is; an
individual's internal dictionary is a lossy compression of the portion of reality locally
encountered. Different internal constructions can therefore look unlike one another while
preserving reproducible inferential relations relative to reality. Her arithmetic example
uses two differently written sums that evaluate to the same result.

**Reconstructed core:** semantic sameness need not be sameness of representation. It may
consist in differently formed models preserving equivalent relations under a common
reality constraint.

### Q2 — The access boundary

Leah rejects the demand for a decisive external classifier of “understood but rejected”
versus “did not understand.” Another person's internal state is not directly available.
Without hypothetical brain-reading instrumentation, one can only infer from what the person
says and does.

**Reconstructed core:** understanding may be a real internal distinction while remaining
underdetermined by externally available evidence.

### Q3 — Two good-faith concepts

Leah distinguishes:

1. **colloquial good faith:** the actor believes their own activity serves a benevolent or
   justified end;
2. **semantic-interoperability good faith:** granting the other mind a defeasible benefit
   of the doubt because one's own knowledge is incomplete and fallible.

The second is described as chronic, calibrated humility: confidence is permitted, but
certainty should not eliminate the hedge against being wrong. Hitchens and the religious
leaders can therefore be acting in colloquial good faith while failing semantic-
interoperability good faith.

### Q4 — Three verification functions

Leah accepts all three proposed senses of verification and assigns each a different job:

1. checking reconstruction is **error correction for a lossy protocol**;
2. checking warrant or truth is a **reality checksum**;
3. comparing words, commitments, and conduct provides indirect evidence about the
   speaker's internal meaning/truth configuration.

**Reconstructed core:** “verify” names a family of different checks, not one operation.

### Q5 — Inquiry before condemnation

Leah rejects gating knowledge. Her procedural instinct is that questions should receive a
serious answer; when a question appears to be moving toward a false, dehumanizing, or
malevolent view, the educator should investigate the person's semantic and analogical map
rather than respond only with anger or forced material. The educator should try to locate
the experiences and mappings from which a reality-connected realization can arise
naturally. An offensively framed question may reflect inherited environment rather than a
fully examined commitment.

### Q6 — Why coverage is insufficient

Leah identifies a separate blocking mechanism: false certainty controls salience and
priority. Both sides of the Hitchens case may have colloquial benevolent intent while
lacking semantic-interoperability good faith. Shared coverage therefore does not guarantee
that the other model remains live enough to reconstruct.

### Q7 — A defeasible, predeclared inference test

Leah accepts that the sender can name in advance the inferential consequences expected to
survive reconstruction, but adds a critical caveat: conversation is probabilistic and the
receiver may produce something that breaks or confounds the sender's own model. In that
case, the sender must reconsider both the model and what should count as understanding.

**Reconstructed core:** a communication test can begin with declared expectations without
making the sender's expectations incorrigible.

### Q8 — Dynamic and perturbative probing

Leah says the distinction between non-understanding and understood-but-rejected requires
deeper probing. The observed behavior might reflect active choice, failure of good-faith
use, or a deeper analogical capture absent from the observer's model. Any probe is itself a
communication that the other person processes; the state is dynamic and the act of probing
can change it.

### Q9 — Strategic assent under power

Leah supplies two nonpolitical cases:

- a child tells a disliked teacher exactly what the teacher wants to hear to end the
  interaction and return to prohibited activity;
- an employee tells a boss “yes” when refusing or openly challenging a delusional order is
  unsafe, then minimally complies, maliciously complies, or disobeys out of necessity.

These cases preserve the structure of surface agreement plus later divergence while
removing partisan content.

### Q10 — One layered semantic object

Leah rejects a hard split between token-to-experience mapping and inference engine. Lexical
associations arise from experiential prototypes that appear to work; inferential moves
arise by chaining those associations and prototypes in ways that also appear to work. The
blind-men-and-elephant analogy treats each component as a partial access route to one larger
object.

**Reconstructed core:** the “dictionary” is one learned, layered representational system,
with lexical, experiential, and inferential aspects generated through perceived practical
fit.

## 3. What Round 2 resolved

### 3.1 Understanding and embodiment are no longer the same variable

Q2 and Q8 preserve the ontological distinction; Q9 makes it unavoidable. A person can
understand an instruction or worldview and still:

- reject it;
- rank another value above it;
- lack permission or power to act openly;
- strategically echo it;
- comply minimally;
- act against it.

Actions remain evidence about understanding, but not a constitutive definition of it.
This is a much stronger resolution than merely deleting the political examples.

### 3.2 The evidence of understanding is necessarily fallible

Leah does not offer a magical external test. She recognizes an access gap: the internal
state is inferred through words, applications, corrections, and conduct. Q8 adds that the
probe changes the interaction. Therefore the strongest defensible claim is likely not:

> this behavioral test proves understanding,

but:

> a family of probes can increase or decrease warranted confidence that a usable
> reconstruction exists.

That distinction protects the paper from pretending to solve other-minds epistemology.

### 3.3 Verification has at least three targets

Q4 directly accepts a three-way decomposition:

- **semantic fidelity:** did I reconstruct the message/model?;
- **world adequacy:** is the represented claim warranted or true?;
- **speaker/commitment inference:** what do later words and actions suggest about the
  speaker's actual stance or internal organization?

This is one of the cleanest Round-2 gains. The remaining problem is not whether the three
exist, but which are constitutive of semantic interoperability and which are adjacent
checks.

### 3.4 Shared coverage is a capacity variable, not a sufficient cause

Q6 resolves a major ambiguity in the central chain. More shared conceptual material may
make reconstruction cheaper or more reachable, while false certainty, salience, identity,
power, or unwillingness can prevent the capacity from being exercised.

So the current thesis cannot defensibly be:

> coverage causes faithful transfer.

It may be closer to a conditional relation:

> coverage enlarges or lowers the cost of available reconstruction pathways, other
> blocking conditions held sufficiently open.

That is a model-generated reformulation, not Leah-owned wording.

### 3.5 Good faith now has a specifically epistemic candidate meaning

Q3 and Q6 distinguish sincere benevolent intention from a dialogical discipline of
calibrated humility. This is more precise than the Round-1 use. It also shows why Hitchens
and a religious interlocutor can care about humanity while remaining closed to revision.

The unresolved placement is whether this belongs inside the definition of semantic
interoperability or names an ethical protocol for using the capacity.

### 3.6 “Dictionary” is one layered system in Leah's current view

Q10 answers Claude's split directly. Leah treats lexical association, experiential
prototype, and inference as developmentally linked layers of one learned system. That is a
real authored answer, not an AI taxonomy.

The term may still be too broad for publication, but the underlying unity is now stated:
all three are learned by perceived success in coupling representation to reality.

## 4. The most important new finding: Q9 reverses the original diagnosis

The child and employee examples were offered to preserve the structure of “humoring
semantic regurgitation.” Under pressure, however, they reveal a different object.

Both agents may understand extremely well:

- the teacher's demand;
- the boss's intended result;
- the social consequences of open disagreement;
- the minimum signal required to end the encounter.

Their surface assent can be generated **because** they understand the other mind and the
power relation, not because they failed to reconstruct it.

This suggests that the earlier category should be split rather than defended as one thing:

1. **semantic echo without understanding** — rote or shallow reproduction;
2. **strategic echo with understanding** — deliberate assent used to manage another agent;
3. **coerced assent** — the receiver understands but lacks safe voice;
4. **understanding without embodiment** — the receiver reconstructs but rejects or
   deprioritizes the model.

These labels are ChatGPT-generated and unowned. The structural result is stronger and does
not depend on the labels:

> **Surface agreement is radically underdetermined. It may indicate understanding,
> misunderstanding, strategy, coercion, or identity/value conflict.**

This also introduces power into the communication model without requiring culture-war
examples. The party who can demand an ACK may misread compliance as understanding or
agreement.

## 5. New architecture surfaced by the answers

The following decomposition is diagnostic and **not yet Leah-owned**. It is offered because
it explains the answers with fewer contradictions than one undifferentiated notion of
“successful communication.”

### Layer A — Semantic capacity

Can the receiver construct and use a model sufficiently related to the sender's target?
This is where private dictionaries, shared coverage, analogy, and generative reconstruction
belong.

### Layer B — Epistemic adequacy

Do the reconstructed model and the claims made inside it answer correctly to reality? This
is the reality-checksum layer. High Layer-A performance can coexist with shared falsehood.

### Layer C — Dialogical protocol

Are the parties willing to keep the other model live, expose assumptions, tolerate
correction, and preserve calibrated humility? This is where Leah's semantic-
interoperability good faith may belong.

### Layer D — Commitment and embodiment

Does the person endorse, identify with, or act from the model? This is separate from
understanding.

### Layer E — Power and action conditions

Can the person safely disagree, ask questions, refuse, or act on what was understood? Q9
shows that communicative behavior is conditioned by authority and risk.

A paper does not need to formalize all five layers. The decomposition is useful because it
prevents evidence from one layer from being silently treated as proof of another.

## 6. Remaining contradictions and technical defects

### T1 — Reality as invariant versus interoperability without truth

Q1 says reality is the invariant. C-006/F-008 says people can interoperate highly while
sharing a false model. Both can be preserved only by distinguishing:

- **reality as the external constraint or referent**;
- **the target-relative structure reconstructed between people**;
- **the adequacy of that shared reconstruction to reality**.

Reality cannot literally be the required invariant *transmitted between minds* if two
people can faithfully share the same false model. The transported invariant must be
relative to the model or communicative task; truth remains a separate test against the
world.

### T2 — The arithmetic example is equality after evaluation, not yet an isomorphism

`1 + 1 + 1 + 2` and `3 + 1 + 1` are different expressions that evaluate to the same
number. The example supports different representations preserving a common value or
relation. Calling that “isomorphism” requires an explicitly defined structure and map that
the answer does not yet provide.

This is a terminology correction, not a rejection of the intuition.

### T3 — Syntactic correctness is insufficient for reasonable inference

Q7 says reason is reasonable, however unintuitive, so long as it is syntactically correct.
Syntactically well-formed or even deductively valid reasoning can begin from false premises,
equivocate between meanings, use a bad source map, or be irrelevant to the world.

The answer likely intends:

> an internally valid inference may be reconstructible and understandable even when its
> premises or world-map are rejected.

That narrower formulation would preserve understanding-without-agreement. The current
wording overreaches.

### T4 — All verification forms are important, but not all can define interoperability

If semantic interoperability requires a successful reality checksum, then two people
cannot semantically interoperate while sharing a false model—contradicting C-006/F-008. If
it requires behavioral commitment, then understanding collapses back into embodiment—
contradicting Q2/Q9.

Round 3 should force a constitutive-versus-adjacent decision:

- semantic verification may belong to interoperability itself;
- truth verification and commitment inference may be guardrails or downstream probes.

This is a recommendation for pressure, not a settled answer.

### T5 — Benefit of the doubt versus directed realization

Q3 favors chronic humility and a defeasible benefit of the doubt. Q5 proposes directing a
person toward the “correct experiences” so the hoped-for realization occurs naturally.
That can become covert steering: the educator may already have fixed the desired
conclusion while presenting the process as discovery.

The anti-indoctrination boundary therefore needs properties such as transparency,
reciprocal corrigibility, permission to reject, rights/safety constraints, and disclosure of
the educator's own standpoint. These are possible pressure dimensions, not answers to
insert.

### T6 — “Every question deserves an answer” is too absolute

Questions can be:

- repetitive harassment;
- bad-faith diversion;
- demands for private or dangerous information;
- impossible under finite time and attention;
- framed to force dehumanizing premises;
- outside the responder's competence.

The durable core may be that offensive or naive framing should not be treated as decisive
evidence of a person's settled character before inquiry. That is narrower than an
unconditional duty to answer every question.

### T7 — Coverage is not the whole causal story

Q6 correctly says false certainty weights salience and closes reconstruction. This means
F-009's compact chain is missing at least one access/use condition. The paper should not
claim that coverage alone produces transfer.

The causal architecture may need to distinguish:

- **available semantic resources**;
- **willingness or capacity to deploy them**;
- **social permission and safety to perform repair**.

### T8 — No direct access versus a falsifiable understanding test

Q2 says internal genuineness is not directly available. Q7 accepts a predeclared
consequence test. These are consistent only if the test supplies **defeasible evidence**,
not proof of the internal state.

The formal ledger should eventually say explicitly that F-010 is a behavioral probe whose
outcome updates confidence; it is not a necessary-and-sufficient definition of human
understanding.

### T9 — The quantum analogy must remain analogy

Q8's point—that interactive probing changes the conversational state—is sound at an
ordinary dynamical level. It does not establish quantum indeterminacy, a measurement-
problem analogue, or any physical quantum mechanism in communication.

The paper can retain the interaction insight without importing quantum language.

### T10 — Truth versus credence remains imprecise

Q3 says truth is epistemically probabilistic. A safer distinction is that the world or
proposition may be determinate while a person's **credence, warrant, or confidence** is
graded and fallible. The paper does not need to settle the metaphysics of truth to defend
calibrated humility.

### T11 — One layered dictionary still contains separable probes

Q10 gives a coherent developmental unity, but a unified object can still have analytically
separable layers. A lexical mismatch, prototype mismatch, inferential-role mismatch, and
value/salience mismatch may require different repairs.

Rejecting a hard ontological split does not require refusing all diagnostic distinctions.

## 7. Formal-model recommendations

Every item here is model-generated and unowned. No formal-ledger entry should change until
Leah adopts, edits, or rejects the underlying distinction.

### 7.1 F-003: separate model preservation from world adequacy

F-003 currently treats success as preservation of an invariant set `I`. Q1 suggests the
world is the common constraint. To preserve C-006/F-008, the formal story needs two
relations:

1. a **reconstruction relation** between sender and receiver models over a declared target;
2. a separate **adequacy relation** between either model and reality/evidence.

This prevents “we understood each other” from becoming “we are correct.”

### 7.2 F-010: allow productive disagreement and sender correction

Q7 adds a branch absent from the current round-trip test. The receiver may not return the
expected consequences because the receiver found a real contradiction or missing
variable. That outcome can evidence deeper understanding rather than failure.

A stronger future probe would therefore ask whether the receiver can either:

- reproduce or apply the declared inferential structure in a novel case; or
- produce a relevant counterexample that forces revision of the sender's model.

Success is not conformity to the sender's expectations. It is evidence that the target
model became usable enough for reproduction, transfer, or disciplined challenge.

### 7.3 Add epistemic status to F-010

The test result should be interpreted as increasing or decreasing confidence in
understanding. It should not claim transparent access to another mind.

### 7.4 Do not formalize the five-layer decomposition prematurely

The capacity/adequacy/protocol/embodiment/power split is useful diagnostically. It should
not become a new equation or TLICA extension merely because it organizes the round well.
Round 3 and prior-art work should first determine whether existing terminology already
handles these distinctions.

## 10. Unresolved objections

### 10.1 Prior art remains completely open

The developing account may overlap common-ground theory, inferential-role semantics,
prototype/exemplar theory, hermeneutics, Davidsonian interpretation, perspective-taking,
dialogical epistemology, philosophy of education, and pedagogies of critical inquiry.
Gate H2 demonstrates command; it does not establish novelty.

### 10.2 The externalist anchor may be stronger than the paper needs

The paper can argue that people learn from local reality and remain answerable to it
without proving a complete metaphysical realism or privileged access to “reality itself.”
Q1 should not force Paper I to settle that larger dispute.

### 10.3 Functional understanding and the LLM/parrot problem

If generative application, novel inference, and counterexample production count as
understanding evidence, sophisticated models can satisfy the behavioral test. The paper
must either accept a functional criterion at the tested level or explain what additional
human property matters without claiming direct access to it.

### 10.4 Paternalism and hidden steering

“Directing” a learner to experiences that produce the educator's hoped-for realization
can reproduce indoctrination through subtler means. The anti-brainwashing claim needs a
procedure that permits the educator to be wrong and the learner to return a corrective
countermodel.

### 10.5 Reciprocity and unequal burden

Q9 shows that people with less power may have to model authority precisely while authority
need not model them at all. A democratic or educational ideal of interoperability may need
reciprocity or burden sensitivity, even if the descriptive capacity does not.

### 10.6 Harmful and bad-faith questions

An inquiry-first posture is valuable, but no person has unlimited duties to educate,
absorb abuse, or provide dangerous information. The paper needs a boundary that protects
openness without demanding compulsory exposure.

### 10.7 Local knowledge and elite blind spots

Formal liberal education can increase repertoire while displacing practical, indigenous,
working-class, or local knowledge and generating a shared elite codec with correlated blind
spots. C-003 should eventually receive a case where fluency rises while source-map adequacy
falls.

### 10.8 Measurement remains absent

Shared coverage, translation cost, fidelity, and repair burden remain unoperationalized.
Round 2 improves the conceptual variables but not the empirical instrument.
