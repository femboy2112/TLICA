## Questions

**Q1** · *Target: central novelty claim; C-001/C-002/C-013; common-ground and charity rivals*

> Assume an existing conversational theory already says that speakers coordinate a
> common ground, repair mismatches, and interpret one another charitably. What specific
> phenomenon does **semantic interoperability** explain that this account still misses?
> Give one concrete case where ordinary common-ground repair is available but your
> additional concept is still needed.

**Q2** · *Target: C-003/C-006/F-008; Q1–Q10 pragmatism/realism wedge*

> Construct a community whose shared concepts let its members communicate fluently and
> coordinate successfully, while the shared model is systematically wrong about the
> world. In that case, do they have high semantic interoperability? Answer yes or no,
> then use the case to separate model-to-model fidelity from model-to-world truth.

**Q3** · *Target: Q10 one-layer dictionary; prototype/exemplar and inferential-role rivals*

> Suppose a rival account splits your “dictionary” into two mechanisms: learned
> prototypes linking tokens to experience, and inferential roles linking concepts to
> consequences. What explanatory loss occurs if we use those two mechanisms instead of
> your one layered object? Give one interaction that the separated account would miss.

**Q4** · *Target: F-010; understanding criterion; functionalist / LLM rival*

> A language model can paraphrase an idea, derive novel consequences, generate relevant
> counterexamples, and revise after correction. If it passes every behavioral probe you
> have proposed, does it understand the idea? If not, name the additional criterion and
> explain how it could be tested without direct access to its inner experience.

**Q5** · *Target: C-008/C-009; Q5 paternalism tension; philosophy-of-education rival*

> An educator must select materials and may hope that the learner reaches a particular
> realization. What observable procedural rule distinguishes legitimate curation from
> soft indoctrination when both deliberately shape the learner's available experiences?
