## Questions (reference — do not answer here; answer in the section below)

**Q1** · *ChatGPT · central novelty; C-001/C-002/C-013; common-ground & charity rivals*
> Assume an existing conversational theory already says that speakers coordinate a common
> ground, repair mismatches, and interpret one another charitably. What specific phenomenon
> does semantic interoperability explain that this account still misses? Give one concrete
> case where ordinary common-ground repair is available but your additional concept is still
> needed.

**Q2** · *ChatGPT · C-003/C-006/F-008; the pragmatism/realism wedge*
> Construct a community whose shared concepts let its members communicate fluently and
> coordinate successfully, while the shared model is systematically wrong about the world. In
> that case, do they have high semantic interoperability? Answer yes or no, then use the case
> to separate model-to-model fidelity from model-to-world truth.

**Q3** · *ChatGPT · Q10 one-layer dictionary; prototype/exemplar & inferential-role rivals*
> Suppose a rival account splits your "dictionary" into two mechanisms: learned prototypes
> linking tokens to experience, and inferential roles linking concepts to consequences. What
> explanatory loss occurs if we use those two mechanisms instead of your one layered object?
> Give one interaction that the separated account would miss.

**Q4** · *ChatGPT · F-010; understanding criterion; functionalist / LLM rival*
> A language model can paraphrase an idea, derive novel consequences, generate relevant
> counterexamples, and revise after correction. If it passes every behavioral probe you have
> proposed, does it understand the idea? If not, name the additional criterion and explain how
> it could be tested without direct access to its inner experience.

**Q5** · *ChatGPT · C-008/C-009; the Q5 paternalism tension; philosophy-of-education rival*
> An educator must select materials and may hope that the learner reaches a particular
> realization. What observable procedural rule distinguishes legitimate curation from soft
> indoctrination when both deliberately shape the learner's available experiences?

**Q6** · *Claude · C-013 (S.I. good faith) vs principle of charity / dialogical humility*
> Your S.I. good faith (Q3/Q6) — defeasible benefit of the doubt, chronic humility
> proportional to your own certainty, failure read as "false certainty weighting salience" —
> has named ancestors: Davidson/Quine's Principle of Charity, Grice's Cooperative Principle,
> Dennett's intentional stance, virtue-epistemology's epistemic humility (Fricker). State
> precisely what S.I. good faith has that the Principle of Charity does not — the
> proportional-to-certainty dial, the salience-weighting failure mode, its placement outside
> the semantic core, or nothing? Sharp enough that a Davidson-reader can't say "just charity,
> renamed." If you can't, say so.

**Q7** · *Claude · F-004/F-005 measurement gap vs common-ground / alignment psycholinguistics*
> F-005 (more shared coverage → lower expected translation cost) has an empirical rival that
> may already own the ground: common ground / audience design (Clark & Wilkes-Gibbs),
> interactive alignment (Pickering & Garrod), lexical/conceptual entrainment. Does your
> "translation cost" reduce to their measured quantities, or is there a quantity they don't
> measure? If it reduces, F-005 is a replication, not a conjecture (cite it). If not, name the
> missing quantity and how you'd operationalize it. Already established, or still UNVERIFIED —
> which, and on what evidence?

**Q8** · *Claude · is "understanding" the correct central construct? vs coordination & inferential-role*
> Two rivals dissolve "understanding": (a) common-ground / coordination (Clark; Lewis) says
> the phenomenon is successful coordination and "understanding" adds nothing measurable;
> (b) inferential-role semantics (Sellars, Brandom) says meaning just is a web of inferential
> commitments — so your Q2 test may already be inferential-role semantics renamed. Give one
> concrete case your account explains that BOTH rivals get wrong — or accept renaming the
> central construct to one of theirs.

**Q9** · *Claude · one-object dictionary + syntactic-vs-analogical validity vs prototype / inferential-role / analogy-as-cognition. (Overlaps ChatGPT Q3, different pressure.)*
> Q10 unified lexical/prototype/inferential into one object held by "what works." Rivals
> separate them: prototype/exemplar (Rosch), inferential-role semantics, and — closest to your
> whole picture, your own first required reading — analogy-as-cognition (Hofstadter & Sander;
> Gentner structure-mapping). (a) Does your one-object account predict anything the layered
> rivals don't, or is the unification stylistic? (b) Your Q7 said validity is "syntactically
> correct" — but analogy/structure-mapping validity is not syntactic. Syntactic or analogical?
> You can't have both; pick one, and say which rival that commits Paper I to.

**Q10** · *Claude · n=1 method exposure + scope discipline (narrowing rule; C-003/C-009).*
> (a) Method: the thesis derives from first-person introspection (Round-1 Q6) — n=1. Sort the
> paper: what is load-bearing on introspection (falls if it doesn't generalize) vs what stands
> on argument alone? Why does your inner report license a claim about interoperability
> *between* minds? (b) Scope: your roadmap bars culture-war / political-economy material, yet
> power/coercion keep surfacing (Q9 boss/employee; reciprocity; unequal burden). State the cut:
> which of { power asymmetry, coerced assent, reciprocity/burden, metaphysics of truth, quantum
> framing } is inside Paper I vs deferred to the applications — and the one-sentence rule that
> makes the cut principled, not convenient.
