# AI-provenance disclosure — template

> **Template only. Use exactly one version, adapted to the selected venue, and only
> if every sentence is factually accurate against** [`../ai_use_log.md`](../ai_use_log.md).
> Do not soften the description to obtain permission — a negative answer is a venue
> mismatch, not a moral judgment (roadmap §8.1). If a venue forbids the kind of AI
> use that occurred, rewriting the prose does not cure it (roadmap §5.3).

## Candidate disclosure (roadmap §8.2)

> During early-stage development, the author used generative AI systems for
> conversational brainstorming, structural organization, and adversarial exploration
> of objections. The submitted manuscript was subsequently written by the author, who
> consulted her own verified notes and source ledgers but did not copy AI-generated
> prose. The author independently reviewed the relevant literature, verified every
> citation and claim, and accepts full responsibility for the manuscript. No AI system
> is an author.

## Adapt before use

- [ ] Every clause is true against [`../ai_use_log.md`](../ai_use_log.md) as of the
      submission date (update the log first if any AI was used after the human draft).
- [ ] Disclosure **wording and placement** match what the venue requires (ask via the
      roadmap §8.1 inquiry if the policy is silent/ambiguous; archive the written answer).
- [ ] If any A3 red-teaming was used on the human draft, it is disclosed and the venue
      permits it.
- [ ] The claim "no AI system is an author" is literally true for the submitted text.

## What this disclosure is not

It is not a claim of unaided creation, and it is not a device for concealing the
development history. It is a truthful account of a human-authored manuscript built on
an AI-assisted research process (see [`../provenance.md`](../provenance.md)).
