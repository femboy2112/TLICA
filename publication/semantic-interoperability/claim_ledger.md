# Claim ledger — Semantic Interoperability

Every claim that might enter the manuscript lives here first. A claim is
**manuscript-eligible** only when Leah owns it: she can explain, defend, revise, or
abandon it without outsourcing the judgment (roadmap §5, Gate H2), its sources are
personally read (roadmap §5.2), and its characterization is no stronger than the
evidence supports.

**Status vocabulary:** `candidate` (proposed, not yet owned) · `owned` (passed the
ownership test) · `boundary` (a non-claim the paper explicitly asserts it is *not*
making) · `rejected` · `UNVERIFIED` (empirical, no evidence yet).

The seeded rows below are drawn from the AI-assisted research note
[`this_is_water_semantic_interoperability_2026-08-09.md`](../../research/this_is_water_semantic_interoperability_2026-08-09.md) §11.
They are **candidates only**: none is yet owned, every judgment column is pending
Leah's review, and none is manuscript-eligible until she completes the ownership
test. They are listed so the ledger has something to work against, not because they
are endorsed.

## Working discipline

- **Add** a candidate row the moment a claim is proposed for the paper; do not carry
  claims only in prose or in your head.
- A claim moves to `owned` only after the matching
  [`canonical_notes.md`](canonical_notes.md) entry has no `[ AWAITING LEAH ]` fields
  and Leah has answered the ownership test in her own words.
- "Required sources" must become "sources personally read" (with page references in
  [`reading_ledger.md`](reading_ledger.md)) before a claim is manuscript-eligible.
- A claim whose novelty collapses against the prior-art audit is **narrowed to its
  residual or dropped** — that is successful truth correction (roadmap §38), not
  failure.
- Every `UNVERIFIED` empirical claim must be labeled UNVERIFIED in the manuscript
  too.
